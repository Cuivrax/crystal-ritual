Crystals for Sleep

Crystals for Sleep & a Calm Bedtime Routine

The crystals most linked to sleep are amethyst, howlite, charoite, stilbite and prehnite, with moonstone, rainbow moonstone and celestite close behind. These links are beliefs, most of them modern, not tested effects on sleep. A stone on the nightstand can still anchor a steady bedtime routine, and routine is what the CDC's sleep advice is built on.

By Iris Holloway

Tradition

Why These Stones Get Linked to Sleep

The Story. Most of the sleep reputations on this list are young. A few have older roots, and those roots are more interesting than the modern claims.

  1. Biblical era Tradition

    Writing about the stones of the high priest's breastplate, George Frederick Kunz notes that the Hebrew name for amethyst, aḥlamah, shows the stone "was believed to possess the virtue of inducing dreams and visions" (The Curious Lore of Precious Stones, 1913, page 297). The Greek name, which means "not drunk", gets more attention.

  2. Crystal gazing Tradition

    Kunz describes people who stare for a long time at a crystal ball, an opal or a moonstone and "may become partially hypnotized or even fall into a profound sleep" (same book, pages 7 to 8). He credits a gleam of light that fixes the eye, not the stone itself.

  3. 1915, Paris Tradition

    A Parisian oracle on mystic subjects, as Kunz calls the Baroness d'Orchamps, recommended moonstone "for the manifold forms of nervousness" (The Magic of Jewels and Charms, 1915, pages 371 to 372).

  4. Today Modern Association

    The rest is recent. Howlite is named for Henry How, the Nova Scotia geologist who described it, and the IMA list of minerals dates it to 1868. Charoite reached Western markets only in the 1970s. Their bedtime meanings come from crystal shops and books, not from old lapidaries. Color helps too, since modern charts put purple stones at the crown chakra and guides tie anything that glows like moonstone to the moon.

What Research Says. No study we know of has tested a crystal for sleep. The closest is a 2025 randomized controlled study in CNS Spectrums that gave 138 adults, believers and nonbelievers in crystal healing, either rose quartz or a visually matched glass imitation for 14 days. Anxiety scores fell only among the believers, and it made no difference whether their stone was real (Escolà-Gascón and colleagues, 2025). It measured anxiety, not sleep. The routine itself has better support, though from children. In a three-week randomized trial with 405 mothers and their babies or toddlers, a consistent nightly bedtime routine led to faster sleep onset, fewer night wakings and better maternal mood, as the mothers reported them (Mindell and colleagues, 2009). It was a study of young children, not adults, and no stones were involved. So the honest case for a sleep stone is the routine around it.

Try This

A 10-Minute Bedtime Wind-Down

The Ritual. The CDC's advice for better sleep is plain. Go to bed and get up at the same time every day, keep the bedroom calm, quiet and cool, and turn off electronic devices at least 30 minutes before bedtime. A stone changes none of that. It can make the routine easier to start every night.

When to use it. 30 minutes before your usual lights-out, every night, weekends included.

You need. One stone from the list, a small notebook, a pen and, if you can manage it, a phone charger outside the bedroom.

Steps.

  1. 2 minutes

    Put your phone on its charger, screen down, and pick up the stone. That handoff is the signal that the day is over.

  2. 3 minutes

    Dim the lights. Hold the stone and take 10 slow breaths, letting each one out a little longer than you took it in.

  3. 3 minutes

    Write two lines in the notebook, whatever is still on your mind and the first task for tomorrow. Close it.

  4. 2 minutes

    Set the stone on the closed notebook on your nightstand, not under the pillow. Lights out at the same time as last night.

Why it helps. Whatever you believe about the stone, here is what the ritual does for sure. It moves screens out of the last half hour, it happens at the same time every night, and it gives your hands something to hold besides a phone. The stone is the bookmark, the routine is the point.

Try This Choose the stone by feel, not by meaning. A smooth tumbled amethyst or a howlite palm stone is easier to hold in the dark than a sharp raw point.

Can You Sleep with Crystals Under Your Pillow?

You can, but the nightstand is the better spot for most stones and the only spot in a home with small children. A tumbled stone under a pillow tends to slide into the sheets, drop onto the floor or end up in a toddler's hand.

Safety. Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are choking hazards for babies, young children and pets. Keep crystals out of cribs and toddler beds entirely, and never put a crystal necklace, bead bracelet or teething necklace on a sleeping child. The US Food and Drug Administration has received reports of death and serious injuries to infants and children, including strangulation and choking, from teething jewelry such as amber teething necklaces. More in our crystals and kids safety guide.

Some stones also do badly under a pillow. Celestite has a very low hardness of 3 to 3.5 and perfect cleavage, which makes it a collector's stone rather than a jewelry stone. Stilbite is brittle with perfect cleavage. Moonstone can break on a sharp knock. If you want a stone in the bed anyway, pick a smooth tumbled quartz such as amethyst, which rates 7 on the Mohs scale, and keep it for adults.

Crystals for Nightmares, Dreams & Lucid Dreaming

Crystal guides give sleep stones two jobs, settling the mind and working with dreams. Modern Association For dreams, the usual picks are amethyst (one hobby magazine calls darker stones dream crystals), charoite, prehnite, which one seller calls a stone of dreaming, and stilbite. For nightmares, celestite and amethyst come up most. Lucid dreaming gets attached to prehnite, celestite and rainbow moonstone.

Try This The practical half of dream work is the notebook. Keep it under the stone on your nightstand and write down whatever you remember before you look at your phone. The stone reminds you, the notebook keeps the dream.

Nightmares that keep coming back, at any age, are worth a conversation with a doctor.

The Sleep Stones, One by One

  1. Classic
  2. 1

    Amethyst

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Amethyst is purple quartz, colored by traces of iron and natural radiation in the rock, and it rates 7 on the Mohs scale.

    Modern Association Classic for sleep and the default bedside crystal in modern guides, usually a cluster or small geode on the nightstand. Its dream link is the oldest on this page, thanks to that Hebrew name.

    Care. Wash it with warm water, mild soap and a soft brush. Some amethyst color can fade with long exposure to intense light, so a nightstand beats a sunny windowsill.

  3. 2

    Howlite

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Howlite is a white borate mineral, often veined with dark threads that resemble the veining in turquoise, and it is frequently dyed blue to imitate turquoise. A blue howlite is a dyed howlite.

    Modern Association Classic for sleep. Crystal guides call it a calming stone, and some believers place it under an insomniac's pillow.

    Care. It has a low hardness, so keep it away from keys and harder stones. It dissolves in dilute acids, dyed pieces should skip steam and harsh cleaners, and our care data has no water rating for it yet.

  4. 3

    Charoite

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Charoite is a swirling purple rock made almost entirely of the mineral charoite, and it comes only from the Chara River area in Russia's Sakha Republic. It was first found in the 1940s as "lilac stone" and reached Western markets in the 1970s. GIA has documented plastic look-alikes, one sold as Royal Russianite.

    Modern Association Classic for sleep, and one crystal guide treats dark violet pieces as dream talismans. People keep a palm stone by the bed and hold it while they wind down.

    Care. Clean it with a soft brush, mild detergent and warm water, never steam. At 5 to 6 on the Mohs scale it scratches more easily than quartz.

  5. 4

    Stilbite

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Stilbite is a zeolite mineral that grows in fan, wheat sheaf and bow-tie shaped bundles, and its name comes from the Greek for "to glitter", after its pearly luster.

    Modern Association Classic for sleep. Shop guides say it encourages sweet dreams in the bedroom, and some people keep a cluster by the bed next to a dream journal.

    Care. It is brittle with perfect cleavage, so it can split along flat planes, and our care data rates heat and acids as ones to avoid. Treat it as a shelf piece.

  6. 5

    Prehnite

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Prehnite is a green to yellow calcium aluminum silicate named after Colonel Hendrik von Prehn, who first found it at the Cape of Good Hope. A dealer guide notes it is sometimes sold as "new jade", but it is not jade.

    Modern Association Classic for sleep and dreams. One crystal guide suggests a tumbled piece by the bed rather than a sharp raw one.

    Care. Keep it away from heat, since prehnite gives off water when heated, and a dealer guide warns against sudden temperature changes. It is soluble in acids and has no known toxicity.

  7. Widely Linked
  8. 6

    Moonstone

    Widely LinkedHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Moonstone is a feldspar with adularescence, a soft glow that seems to float under the surface.

    Modern Association Widely Linked to sleep. People keep it on the nightstand or wear it as a pendant for dream work, an echo of the hypnotic glow in Kunz.

    Care. A hardness of 6 and two perfect cleavage directions mean it scratches and can break on a sharp knock, which makes it a poor pillow stone. Warm soapy water is fine.

  9. 7

    Rainbow Moonstone

    Widely LinkedHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Rainbow moonstone is a trade name. GIA's lab reports this material as labradorite, a different feldspar, known in the trade as rainbow moonstone.

    Modern Association Widely Linked to sleep, mostly on moonstone's reputation. Jewelry and crystal guides suggest keeping it near the bed for restful sleep and dream recall.

    Care. Cleaning in water is no problem, but the stone abrades over time, and labradorite in general chips on impact. High heat or a sudden temperature change can break moonstone.

  10. 8

    Celestite

    Widely LinkedHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Celestite is a strontium sulfate, often sky blue. The Crystal Cave at Put-in-Bay, Ohio, is a geode 35 feet wide lined with celestite crystals up to 18 inches across.

    Modern Association Widely Linked to sleep. Crystal guides call the bedroom its most common home and tie it to calm nights and fewer nightmares. One guide suggests giving children a small piece to hold, which we would not do, because small pieces are a choking hazard. In a child's room, a geode on a high shelf is the safer version.

    Care. It is very soft and light sensitive, and dealer guides say it is slightly soluble in water. Keep it out of the sun, clean it with a quick soft brush and warm water only, and store it apart from other stones.

When a Stone Is Not Enough

A stone can make a bedtime routine feel like yours. It does not treat insomnia, nightmares or any sleep disorder. The CDC advises talking to a healthcare provider if you have problems sleeping, and a provider can run tests, including sleep studies, to check for a sleep disorder. Do not stop, skip or delay any sleep treatment your doctor prescribed because of a crystal. If a child has frequent nightmares or night terrors, ask your pediatrician. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org, any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

Crystals for Love

Crystals for Love & Relationships

The crystals most linked to love are moonstone and rose quartz, followed by strawberry quartz, sugilite, green aventurine, green quartz, morganite and pink opal. Their love meanings come from color, old wedding and gift customs, and modern crystal books. No stone can make someone love you, but one makes a fine reminder or a thoughtful gift.

By Iris Holloway

Crystals for Love at a Glance

Modern crystal guides tie these stones to love most firmly, in this order. All eight are Classic, the top of a scale that runs down through Widely Linked, Often Linked, Sometimes Linked and Rarely Linked. It measures how common the belief is, not whether it works.

Tradition

Why These Stones Get Linked to Love

The Story. Love stones are mostly pink or green. Here is what each layer of the belief left in writing.

  1. 1913 Tradition

    George Frederick Kunz wrote that "as a gift for lovers the moonstone takes a high rank, for it is believed to arouse the tender passion." Lovers who held it in the mouth at the full moon were said to read their future together (The Curious Lore of Precious Stones, pages 97 to 98). Please keep yours out of your mouth.

  2. 1915, China Tradition

    At important Chinese weddings the priest carried a "marriage sword" whose sheath was studded with pink stones, the favorites being "pink tourmaline, rubellite from the Shan Mountains, or rose-quartz", with a piece of green jade at the hilt. The groom held the sword, the bride the sheath, and the two were joined as the ring went on her finger. Kunz adds that the pink was often deepened with paste or foil set beneath the stones (The Magic of Jewels and Charms, pages 384 to 385).

  3. 1977 Modern Association

    Modern chakra charts make green the heart chakra's color, with pink as its partner, so pink and green stones inherit love by color. That chart is younger than it looks. The Theosophical Society's Quest magazine credits Ken Dychtwald with first printing the rainbow colors alongside the familiar chakra qualities, in 1977. More on our heart chakra stones page.

  4. Today Modern Association

    Some love stones are new to everyone. Morganite was named in 1910 by Kunz himself, for the banker J. P. Morgan. Sugilite was first found in Japan in 1944 and named in 1976. When a crystal guide calls sugilite "a love stone for the modern age", it is simply accurate.

You will also read that rose quartz was stained pink by Aphrodite's blood, or that Egyptians and Romans used it in face masks. Treat both as modern tales. Pliny the Elder's Natural History, read in the Bostock and Riley translation, does not describe rose quartz under any name in its books on stones and gems. Its rose-colored stone is a kind of iaspis, ranked second best, which the translators read as jasper (Book 37, chapter 37).

Self-Love or Attracting Someone?

Modern guides treat rose quartz as a self-love stone as much as a romantic one, and one stresses that the love it stands for reaches beyond romance. That fits what a stone can honestly do. It can remind you to go easy on yourself on a hard day. It cannot change how another person feels.

People often ask what it means to put rose quartz under someone's pillow. In modern guides, a piece by the bed or under the pillow is a charm for intimacy and rest. Our house rule is simple. Put one under your own pillow if you like, never under someone else's without them knowing. The same goes for a stone slipped into a partner's bag or car to sway their feelings. If a stone is meant for someone, give it openly, with a card.

Safety. If you make a love jar or spell jar with small chips, close it tightly and keep it away from small children and pets, since chips are a choking hazard. Leave out pink opal if the jar holds salt.

What Research Says. A 2025 randomized controlled study in CNS Spectrums gave 138 adults, some who believed in crystal healing and some who did not, either rose quartz or a visually matched glass imitation for 14 days. Anxiety scores fell only among the believers, real stone or not (Escolà-Gascón and colleagues, 2025). It tested anxiety, not love, and we know of no study that has tested a love stone.

Try This

A Weekly Love Note Ritual

The Ritual. Whatever you believe about the stone, here is what the ritual does for sure. It sets aside 5 minutes a week to say what usually goes unsaid.

When to use it. One fixed evening a week, after dinner, phones in another room.

You need. One stone you both like, a few index cards and a pen.

Steps.

  1. 1 minute

    Put the stone on the table between you.

  2. 2 minutes

    Whoever holds the stone names one specific thing the other did this week that they were glad about. Specific beats grand. "You took the trash out in the rain" counts.

  3. 1 minute

    Pass the stone and swap.

  4. 1 minute

    Write both lines on one card and tuck it under the stone. The stack of cards becomes a record of an ordinary good year.

Try This On your own, the same ritual works as a self-love practice. Write one line about something you handled kindly this week, including kindness to yourself, and put it under the stone.

Try This Before a first date, a pocket stone can start a small pre-date ritual. Hold it, breathe out slowly three times, pick one question you really want to ask, then put the stone away and go.

What Research Says. The card ritual is close to a gratitude exercise, and those have been studied. In three randomized studies, two with college students (192 and 157 people) and one with 65 adults living with neuromuscular disease, those asked to list things they were grateful for reported better well-being than comparison groups on several measures, and the clearest gain was in positive mood (Emmons and McCullough, 2003). The results were self-reported, the studies did not look at couples, and no stones were involved, so take it as a reason to try the cards, not proof that the ritual works.

Crystal Gifts for Love That Get It Right

  • A wedding or a new couple. Rose quartz, with the Chinese marriage sword story written on the card.
  • An anniversary. Rose quartz appears on anniversary lists for the fifth wedding anniversary, and morganite for the ninth.
  • A June birthday. Moonstone shares June with pearl and alexandrite on the modern birthstone list.
  • An engagement ring. Morganite is the second most popular colored gem for engagement ring center stones, and it is durable enough for daily wear. Moonstone can break on a hard knock, and sugilite needs a protective setting.
  • A friend after a breakup. A tumbled rose quartz or pink opal and a short card. Write what the stone is and what people associate with it, gentleness, including toward yourself.

The Love Stones, One by One

  1. Classic
  2. 1

    Moonstone

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Moonstone is a feldspar with adularescence, a soft glow that seems to float under the surface.

    Modern Association Classic for love. Crystal guides call it a traditional wedding and engagement gift and suggest a small pendant worn close to the heart.

    Care. A hardness of 6 and two perfect cleavage directions mean it scratches and can break on a sharp knock, so protect it from bumps. Warm soapy water is fine.

  3. 2

    Rose Quartz

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Rose quartz is pink quartz, usually translucent and massive rather than in clear crystals. Its color comes mostly from tiny fibers of a dumortierite-like mineral. It usually gets no treatment, though it is occasionally irradiated to deepen the color.

    Modern Association Classic for love and the first stone most guides name, for romance, self-love and forgiving yourself.

    Care. Does rose quartz fade in the sun? Ordinary rose quartz lacks the light sensitivity of the rarer transparent pink quartz, which can lose color fast and should be stored in the dark. Wash it in warm soapy water and skip ultrasonic and steam cleaners. More on rose quartz and on rose quartz fading.

  4. 3

    Strawberry Quartz

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact According to one dealer, strawberry quartz is quartz with sparkling red iron oxide inclusions, mostly from Russia, Kazakhstan and Brazil. Some products sold under the name are man-made, usually colored glass. Ask the seller where it was mined, and see our page on cherry quartz if yours looks too even.

    Modern Association Classic for love, and one seller describes it as a nurturing stone of love, emotional healing and inner joy.

    Care. Our care data treats it as quartz, so warm soapy water is fine and ultrasonic and steam cleaners are not, the same advice as for rose quartz.

  5. 4

    Sugilite

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Sugilite is a rare pink to purple silicate mineral colored by manganese. Most gem material comes from mines in South Africa's Kalahari Manganese Field, and imitation sugilite is powdered sugilite bound with resin, often sold without saying so.

    Modern Association Classic for love, and one seller frames it as a love stone for the modern age, less tied to tales of romance.

    Care. Clean it with warm water and mild detergent. It is a fragile gemstone that needs a protective mounting in rings and bracelets.

  6. 5

    Green Aventurine

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Green aventurine is a translucent quartz rock whose shimmer and color come from flakes of fuchsite, a chromium-rich mica. Goldstone glass and dyed quartz are its usual imitations.

    Modern Association Classic for love as a heart chakra stone, though it is better known for luck. One guide says it helps you fall in love with your life.

    Care. A dealer guide recommends warm soapy water and a soft cloth, and no harsh household chemicals or prolonged extreme heat. More on green aventurine, and on its money side in crystals for money.

  7. 6

    Green Quartz

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Most green quartz sold today is prasiolite, made by heating certain amethyst to roughly 752 to 932°F (400 to 500°C). "Green amethyst" is an incorrect name, and the US Federal Trade Commission's guides call it misleading.

    Modern Association Classic for love, as a heart chakra stone said to encourage harmony between partners.

    Care. Heat-treated prasiolite can fade in strong sunlight and some artificial light, so store it in the dark and keep it away from heat and hot cars. Our care data has no water rating for it yet.

  8. 7

    Morganite

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Morganite is the pink to orange-pink variety of beryl, the mineral of emerald and aquamarine, colored by traces of manganese. Light stones are often heated or irradiated to deepen the pink, a common and permanent treatment that sellers should disclose.

    Modern Association Classic for love, with popular links to compassion, romance and harmony.

    Care. It has the hardness and durability for daily wear in any kind of jewelry. A soft brush, mild detergent and warm water always work, and mechanical cleaners only if a gemologist confirms there are no inclusions or fractures.

  9. 8

    Pink Opal

    ClassicHow We Rate

    Mineral Fact Pink opal is common opal, the kind without play of color, mixed with other minerals, and its pink is thought to come from organic compounds called quinones. Pink opal from Peru is sold as Peruvian opal.

    Modern Association Classic for love, especially for healing heartbreak and self-acceptance.

    Care. Opal in general is sensitive to moisture, heat and temperature changes, and our care data rates salt as one to avoid, so leave it out of salt cleansing. If a pink opal egg paled on a windowsill, light is the likely suspect, and heat from intense light can craze opal.

When a Stone Is Not Enough

Many of these stones are given after a breakup or a loss. A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. If heartbreak or grief turns into weeks of low mood, poor sleep or trouble getting through the day, talk to a doctor or a therapist. If a relationship leaves you feeling afraid or unsafe, reach out to someone you trust or a local support service. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org, any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

Crystal Care Checker for Water, Sun & Salt

By Iris Holloway

The Crystal Care Checker answers the crystal care question people ask most, whether a stone can take a rinse, a soak, sunlight or salt before you cleanse it. Pick up to six stones and a method, and each stone gets OK, Brief Only, a warning that names the test such as Keep Dry or No Salt, or Not Enough Data, with the reason and the source behind it. It looks at physical care only, never at what a method does for a stone's energy.

      How the Checker Works

      1. Choose your stones

        Type a name, or another name the stone goes by. "Tiger eye" finds tiger's eye, "rock crystal" finds clear quartz and "gypsum" finds selenite. You can add up to six stones, or tap one of the popular picks under the search box.

      2. Choose a method

        The first group is the contact methods, Rinse Under the Tap, Soak in Water, Leave in Sunlight and Bury in Salt or Salt Water. The second group is the dry methods, Moonlight, Sound, Smoke, Selenite Plate and Brown Rice.

      3. Read your results

        A short summary covers all your stones at once, then each stone gets its own card with the verdict, the reason in one or two sentences and links to the pages that reason comes from.

      If a stone comes back with a warning such as Keep Dry, or Not Enough Data, a Try Moonlight Instead button switches you to a dry method in one tap. Save as Image draws a tall care card with your stones and verdicts, handy to keep on your phone next to your shelf. The page address updates as you choose, so you can bookmark a result or send it to a friend who just got her first selenite lamp.

      What the Ratings Mean

      Rating What it means
      OK Our sources call this kind of contact fine
      Brief Only Our sources allow short contact only, so keep it quick
      Keep Dry Our sources warn against it. The warning names the test, such as Keep Dry for water or Keep Out of Sun for sunlight
      Not Enough Data No source we trust covers it yet. Until one does, a dry method like moonlight or sound is the gentle choice

      The word always shows next to the color, so the rating reads the same on any screen and to anyone who cannot tell the colors apart.

      Not Enough Data is an honest answer, not a warning. It means no source we trust has covered that stone and that kind of contact yet. Black tourmaline, for example, has no water rating in our sources, so the checker says so instead of guessing from its hardness or its chemistry.

      The Safety Rules Behind Every Rating

      These rules decide what you see. They lean cautious on purpose, because a cautious answer costs you a few seconds and a wrong one can cost you a stone.

      Every rating rests on a quote. Each one comes from a sentence we checked against the source page it links to. We never work a rating out from hardness or from what a stone is made of.

      Soak is Brief Only unless a source clears it. Most reference guides describe washing a stone, warm water, mild detergent and a soft brush, and say nothing about leaving it in a bowl. So when a stone is OK for a rinse, the checker still shows Brief Only for a long soak, unless the source itself says soaking is fine. Today that is true for three stones, black agate, flower agate and grape agate, through a dealer's care table for agate in general.

      An encyclopedia-only OK is not used. An open online encyclopedia is a useful lead, not a source we publish on its own. If it is the only page saying a contact is fine, the rating drops to Not Enough Data. A warning that rests on it alone stays, because caution is the safe side to err on, and the card says plainly how thin the support is. Orange calcite and water is one of those.

      A source has to fit the stone. When the only source does not match the stone or the use, we leave the rating out. Our only sunlight source for rainbow moonstone was about orthoclase moonstone, and rainbow moonstone is a labradorite. Our only salt source for clear quartz was a ritual page, not gem care advice. Both show Not Enough Data.

      Dealer guides are labeled. Some ratings come from shops that sell stones. Their links carry a small "dealer guide" tag, so you can weigh them yourself.

      Dry methods get the same line every time. Moonlight, sound, smoke, a selenite plate and brown rice are practices people follow, and our sources document no physical contact risk for any of them. The checker shows each stone's water rating beside them for comparison, and it never says a method cleanses energy.

      Three Results Worth Knowing

      • Selenite and water. Brief Only. Gypsum, the mineral behind selenite, is water soluble, and it should be cleaned by hand with warm water only. A quick wipe, then dry it.
      • Amethyst and sunlight. Brief Only. Some amethyst color can fade with prolonged exposure to intense light. A morning on the sill is not the same as a month on it.
      • Fluorite and a soak. No Soaking. Fluorite is slightly soluble in water, so it should never be submerged.

      What the Checker Does Not Cover

      Water you drink. The checker rates contact with water, not whether water a stone sat in is safe to swallow, and our data clears no stone for that. Crystal healers should avoid making potions with toxic gems. Our full water safety table lists the stones to keep out of drinking water, and our moon water guide keeps the stone beside the jar.

      Dyed, coated or stabilized pieces. Ratings describe the stone itself. A dye, a metal coating or a resin can change the answer, and a dyed stone should never go in water you drink. The Label Decoder helps you work out what a tag really means.

      Stones you cannot name. If you do not know what a stone is, or the search cannot find it, treat it as Not Enough Data and keep it dry.

      Salt, mostly. Salt is the thinnest part of our data. Gem references rarely discuss it, so most stones show Not Enough Data for salt. Sunlight is better covered, and water best of all.

      Heat, chemicals, storage and toxicity live in the same care data and appear on each stone's own page. For the method by method picture, read how to cleanse crystals without damaging them.

      Try This Before your next cleansing night, put every stone you plan to use into the checker in one go and save the care card. Stick it inside the cupboard door where you keep the stones, and the question is answered before you reach for the tap.

Browse by Color

Color is the first thing you notice and the least reliable clue to what a stone is. One mineral can come in many colors, and quartz alone gives purple amethyst, yellow citrine, pink rose quartz and colorless clear quartz. Different minerals can share a color, so a pink tourmaline and a rose quartz can sit side by side in the same bowl. And some color is added. Most citrine on the market is heated amethyst.

Mineral Fact Every stone page says what causes its color. Amethyst owes its purple to traces of iron and natural radiation from the surrounding rock, and chrysoprase owes its green to nickel.

Browse by Hardness

Hardness, on the Mohs scale from 1 to 10, tells you how easily a stone scratches. The Atlas sorts stones into three groups.

  • Soft, under 5. Selenite (gypsum, the 2 on the Mohs scale), amber, pearl, calcite and fluorite. Give each its own pouch.
  • Medium, 5 to 6.5. Moonstone, obsidian, opal, moldavite and hematite.
  • Hard, 7 and up. The quartz family at 7, from amethyst to clear quartz, and pink tourmaline at 7 to 7.5.

Hardness is not the whole story. Moonstone can break on a sharp knock along its cleavage, and hardness says nothing about whether a stone is safe in water. Check that in the Crystal Care Checker.

Types of Crystals, by What They Really Are

The honest way to sort types of crystals is by what each one is, and every stone page opens by saying which kind it belongs to.

Kind What it means Examples
Mineral species A mineral with its own chemistry and structure, on the official IMA list Calcite, fluorite
Variety A color or form of a species with a name of its own Amethyst and citrine (quartz), chrysoprase (chalcedony), moonstone (feldspar)
Rock A mix of minerals Jade, lapis lazuli
Natural glass Melt that cooled too fast to form crystals Obsidian, moldavite
Organic Made by living things Amber (fossilized tree resin), pearl
Treated A real stone changed by heat, dye or a coating Aqua aura quartz, quartz with a metal coating
Trade name A selling name that hides what the stone is Rainbow moonstone, Herkimer diamond

The International Mineralogical Association's list of July 2026 recognizes 6,226 mineral species. Many of the best-loved stones are not on it, because they are varieties, rocks, glasses or organic gems.

Sunstone Meaning, Properties & Real-Life Uses

By Iris Holloway

Sunstone is a feldspar that glitters, because thin flat flakes of copper or hematite inside it catch the light and flash as you tilt it. Modern crystal guides link it with confidence, warmth and good luck, and those meanings are beliefs. Oregon sunstone is sold untreated, while glittery goldstone glass is the common imitation.

Modern Association

What People Use Sunstone for Today

In modern crystal guides, sunstone is a Classic stone for confidence and Widely Linked to money and abundance. Joy, leadership and courage come close behind. Most guides place it on the sacral or solar plexus chakra, many pair it with Leo, though one large encyclopedia ties it to no zodiac sign at all. Fire is the usual element.

The "why" is easy to trace. A warm orange stone that throws sparks reads as sunlight, and sunlight reads as energy and good cheer.

What Does Sunstone Do?

As a mineral, sunstone flashes in the light. Everything else people say it does is belief, about the person wearing it. A few sites promise better metabolism or relief from seasonal low mood. No mineral or medical reference we know of supports those claims, and one crystal guide states plainly that sunstone is not a treatment for low mood or depression.

Sunstone & Moonstone, the Sun & Moon Pair

Moonstone is the partner guides name most often, sun and moon, action and reflection. Some practitioners hold one in each hand as a balance meditation. The pair has mineral logic, since both are feldspars, and history, since Kunz's papal pair was a moonstone that waxed with the moon and a sunstone that followed the sun. More on our moonstone page.

Try This

How to Use Sunstone in Everyday Life

The Ritual. The stone will not do the work, but a small object you reach for at the same moment each day is an easy anchor for a habit.

Daytime

A Pocket Stone for the Interview

Try This Put a tumbled sunstone in your pocket on the morning of an interview, a presentation or a first day somewhere new. In the lobby, hold it, tilt it once until it flashes, and say your opening sentence in your head. Then put it away and walk in.

Daytime

Gray Winter Mornings

Try This Keep a sunstone on the windowsill nearest your coffee. When you pick up the first cup, open the curtains, turn the stone toward the light and look outside for one minute before you look at a screen. Bring it in from any hot, sunny sill (see care below).

Evening

Sun in the Morning, Moon at Night

Try This Give the pair a job each. Sunstone sits by your keys and goes in your bag when you leave. Moonstone waits on the nightstand. Swapping them is the cue that the day is starting or that it is done.

For Someone Else

A Gift for a First Job

Try This For a graduate or a friend starting a new role, wrap a tumbled sunstone with a card. Write what it is (feldspar with tiny metal flakes that flash in the light) and what people associate with it (confidence and a warm start). More in our crystal gift ideas.

Safety. Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

What Research Says. In a small 2017 experiment with 48 mostly undergraduate participants, Nicholas Hobson and colleagues found that people who practiced an invented ritual at home for a week then showed a smaller brain response to their own mistakes on a task, though they performed no better (DOI 10.7717/peerj.3363). Their self-reported feelings did not change and no stone was involved, so tying this to a pocket sunstone is our extrapolation.

When a Stone Is Not Enough

Sunstone is sometimes sold as a cure for winter blues. A bright stone on the sill can be a nice cue, but it is not care. If low mood, poor sleep or low energy lasts or feels heavy, talk to a doctor or a therapist. If you are in crisis in the US, call or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org.

Mineral Fact

What Sunstone Actually Is

The Stone. Sunstone is not one mineral. It belongs to the feldspar group, minerals found throughout Earth's crust in rocks such as granite and gneiss. Both the orthoclase and the plagioclase feldspars have a sunstone variety, and the same group gives us moonstone, labradorite and amazonite.

The first stones called sunstone were oligoclase, a plagioclase feldspar. As other feldspars turned up with the same strong flash, labradorite and orthoclase among them, the name went with it. Older names you may see on a label are heliolite and, more often, aventurescent feldspar.

Feldspar With Aventurescence

Why Sunstone Glitters

The flash has a name, aventurescence. Thin, flat, highly reflective plates of copper, hematite or goethite sit inside, all lined up in one direction, so light bounces off them together and you see a burst of glitter at the right angle. Move the stone, the light or your head to trigger it.

Some plates are too small to see and simply tint the stone. Cutters set the plates parallel to the top of a cabochon for the strongest, most even flash.

What Color Is Sunstone?

Sunstone runs from colorless through yellow, orange and red, and a few exceptional stones are deep green or blue. In Oregon stones the copper platelets give the pink and red, and color can shift across one stone, pink on one side and orange on the other, or green patches against red.

Oregon Sunstone

The most famous US sunstone comes from basalt lava flows in Lake County and Harney County, Oregon, where it sits as larger crystals scattered through the rock. Oregon sunstone is labradorite with copper inclusions. Its colors do not come from treatment, and every Oregon stone carries a copper sheen even when the inclusions are too small to flash.

You can dig your own. The Bureau of Land Management keeps a public collecting area in Lake County where anyone may look for sunstone and keep what they find for personal use, not for trade.

Sunstone From Other Places

In Norway, sunstone occurs as masses in quartz veins at Tvedestrand and Hitterö. Tumbled sunstone from India is thought to carry hematite rather than copper. Tanzania yields a gold-sheen sunstone with hematite flakes that can top a millimeter. Most rough is badly cracked, so most stones are small.

Tradition

Where the Meaning Comes From

The Story. The sun in sunstone is all about that flash, which reminded people of sunlight, so the stone was believed to carry the sun's warmth and good fortune. Here is what each era actually left in writing.

The Timeline, Norse Legend, Undated to Today

  1. Norse legend, undated Tradition

    Some believe sunstone served Norse sailors as a "sun compass" to locate the sun on cloudy days. In 2011, lab experiments led by Guy Ropars found that a rotated crystal of Iceland spar can find the sun's direction within a few degrees, even in dim twilight (DOI 10.1098/rspa.2011.0369). In 2013, Albert Le Floch and colleagues showed that a calcite crystal recovered from the 1592 Alderney shipwreck could have worked as an optical sun compass (DOI 10.1098/rspa.2012.0651). Together the papers show that a sunstone was physically possible for navigation, not that Vikings used one. Iceland spar is transparent calcite, not feldspar. So the sunstone of Norse legend and the sunstone in your bracelet may share only a name.

  2. Undated, Oregon Tradition

    Native Americans collected Oregon sunstone, traded it widely and sometimes buried it with the dead.

  3. 1500s, Rome Tradition

    Pope Clement VII was said to own a stone with a golden spot that moved across it in step with the sun across the sky. Kunz calls the tale one of the "undoubtedly fables" spread to boost the fame of two fine stones, a moonstone and a sunstone, in the papal treasury (The Magic of Jewels and Charms, 1915, pages 386 to 387).

  4. 1800s to 1900s

    Russian deposits near Lake Baikal were mined in the early 1800s and Norwegian ones later that century. In the early 1900s, Tiffany & Company opened the first commercial US sunstone mine near Plush, Oregon, and called the clear stones "Plush Diamonds."

  5. 1987, Oregon

    The Oregon legislature named Oregon sunstone the official state gemstone.

  6. Today Modern Association

    Modern crystal enthusiasts see sunstone as a gem of positivity and empowerment. One large online crystal encyclopedia quotes the crystal author Robert Simmons for its link to Ra, the Egyptian sun god.

What About Greek Sun Gods & Warrior Legends?

You will find pages saying the ancient Greeks dedicated sunstone to Helios or Apollo, that it fell from the sun during an eclipse, or that a wounded warrior's blood turned Oregon's stones red. We found no reference work or old text that records those stories, so treat them as modern retellings.

Old books also use the word loosely. In Kunz's summary of a medieval Spanish lapidary, the ruby is "more especially a sunstone," a stone ruled by the Sun (The Curious Lore of Precious Stones, 1913, page 348). That is astrology, not feldspar.

Is My Sunstone Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone.

Natural, Untreated

Oregon sunstone is not treated to improve its appearance.

Natural, Treated

Pale feldspar from other areas might be enhanced to improve its color. In the early 2000s, copper-bearing feldspar like Oregon's was reported from new sources in Congo, Tibet or elsewhere in China, and its origin and natural color were widely questioned. A 2011 research chronology concluded that much of the red andesine on the market was pale yellow andesine from Inner Mongolia with copper diffused into it in a lab. GIA researchers have also published on copper-diffused labradorite and andesine (2023).

Lab-Grown

We found no reference source describing lab-grown sunstone for sale. The glittery stand-in you are likely to meet is goldstone, which is glass, so it belongs under imitation.

Imitation

Goldstone, a glass with small flat copper crystals, is the common imitation buyers should know about. See is goldstone real for its own story.

Trade Name

Red Oregon sunstone is often marketed as "spinel red," a color comparison, not a spinel. Clear plagioclase from Oregon with no visible flash has been sold as "Oregon Sunstone," and yellow plagioclase from Mexico without flash as "Golden Sunstone." Both are real feldspar, minus the flash that has traditionally defined sunstone.

Three checks you can do at home, with their limits.

  1. Tilt it

    Move the stone, the lamp or your head. Aventurescent sunstone flashes at a matching angle. This shows the flash only, not that the stone is natural, and goldstone glitters too.

  2. Use a 10x loupe

    Under magnification you can see that the flakes in Indian sunstone are not copper. This tells you the inclusion type, not whether the color was treated.

  3. Look for color zones

    Natural Oregon stones can shade from pink to orange or show green beside red. Zoning fits a natural stone, but that does not mean a treated stone can never show it.

Three questions to ask the seller.

  1. Is this feldspar sunstone or goldstone glass?
  2. Where was it mined, Oregon, India, Norway or somewhere else?
  3. Has the color been treated, including by copper diffusion? For a pricey red or green stone, ask for a lab report, which names any detectable treatment.

How to Care for Sunstone

Sunstone is durable enough for daily wear if you avoid rough handling. It has perfect cleavage in two directions, so earrings, brooches and small pendants suit it best, and a ring is safer in a bezel, flush or recessed setting.

Can Sunstone Go in the Sun?

Gem reference works do not settle it yet. Guides often charge sunstone in sunlight, which fits the name, while a couple warn that long exposure may discolor it. Our Care Checker shows sun as Unknown, so until a reference source covers it, keep sun sessions short and skip the hot windowsill.

Sunstone vs Look-Alikes

Goldstone

Glass with copper crystals, the imitation buyers should know about, not feldspar at all.

Moonstone

A feldspar cousin and the usual partner in sun and moon pairings.

Labradorite

Oregon sunstone is itself labradorite with copper inclusions.

Andesine

Red andesine feldspar, much of it on the market colored by copper diffused into pale stones (see above).

Price Guide

Sunstone fits almost any budget. Small cabochons with a pleasant orange color and good flash can cost under $50, while nicely faceted bright red and bicolor stones can sell for over $1,000 per carat. Price climbs from colorless and yellow through pink, orange and red, and transparent stones with strong flash cost the most. Deep red, deep green and intense bicolors are the most sought-after colors, and large carved stones are rare.

In the listings we checked, round and oval faceted orange sunstones of about 2 to 4 carats ran about $13 to $37 a stone. Prices from US-dollar listings at an online gem dealer in November 2026.

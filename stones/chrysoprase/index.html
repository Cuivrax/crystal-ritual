Chrysoprase Meaning, Properties & Real-Life Uses

By Iris Holloway

Chrysoprase is the apple-green variety of chalcedony, a quartz made of microscopic crystals, and nickel gives it its color. Modern crystal guides tie it to the heart, hope and fresh starts, which are beliefs. The honest note is that a lot of "chrysoprase" for sale is ordinary chalcedony dyed green.

Modern Association

What People Use Chrysoprase for Today

In modern crystal guides, the heart chakra comes up almost everywhere. Chrysoprase is Widely Linked to love, to money, to focus and to confidence, and Often Linked to grief. Hope, forgiveness, calm and fresh starts round it out. Several guides call it a stone of Venus, and zodiac picks split between Libra, Taurus and Cancer.

The "why" is mostly color. One guide compares its green to new leaves and early spring, and green is the color modern chakra charts give the heart.

What Does Chrysoprase Do?

As a mineral, it is a hard gem with no cleavage, fit for any jewelry. Everything else people say it does is belief. Some sites promise detox, liver or heart benefits, and one German guide credits healing claims to Hildegard of Bingen while noting she may have meant a different green chalcedony. No mineral or medical reference we know of supports those claims.

How to Use Chrysoprase in Everyday Life

The Ritual. Whatever you believe about the stone, a ritual gives you a pause you chose, with something in your hand to mark it.

After a Breakup or a Hard Year

Try This Keep a tumbled chrysoprase on your nightstand for a month. Each night, hold it and name one thing you are done carrying and one small thing you are looking forward to. Say them out loud. Then set the stone down.

Daytime

A Fresh-Start Line at Your Desk

Try This Set the stone beside your keyboard. Before you open email, write one line on a sticky note, the one thing you would like to begin fresh today, a project, a conversation or simply a kinder tone with yourself. Stick it under the stone.

Evening

The Evening Jewelry Box

Try This Since sun can fade it, make putting it away part of the evening. When you take off a chrysoprase ring or pendant, wipe it with a soft cloth and lay it in a closed box, not on the sunny dresser. Taking off the day can be literal.

For Someone Else

A Gift for a Move or a New Job

Try This Wrap a tumbled stone with a card that says what it is (green quartz colored by nickel, mined mostly in Australia) and what people associate with it (fresh starts and an open heart). Add one care line, keep it out of the sun.

Safety. Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

What Research Says. No study we know of tested chrysoprase, so treat these as personal practices.

When a Stone Is Not Enough

Chrysoprase is often chosen for heartbreak and grief. A stone can be a comfort, but it is not care. If sadness, anxiety or trouble sleeping lasts or feels heavy, talk to a doctor or a therapist. If you are in crisis in the US, call or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org.

Mineral Fact

What Chrysoprase Actually Is

The Stone. A variety of chalcedony, chrysoprase is a cryptocrystalline quartz, made of quartz grains far too small to see, and it ranges from nearly opaque to nearly transparent. It ranks as the second most valuable chalcedony, after gem silica.

How to Pronounce Chrysoprase

Say KRIS-uh-prayz, with the stress on the first syllable. The name comes from Greek words for "golden apple" or "golden leek," and the second half goes back to the Greek práson, leek.

What Gives Chrysoprase Its Green?

Nickel. It may sit in the quartz as tiny amounts of nickel silicate, as dispersed nickel oxide or as free nickel ions, and more nickel means deeper green. Pale yellowish stones hold about 0.2 to 1 percent nickel oxide by weight, deep green ones up to 5 percent. A 2026 GIA study confirmed nickel as the coloring element in new Australian material, with no chromium, iron or copper detected (Gems & Gemology, Spring 2026). That matters, because some other green chalcedony gets its color from chromium instead.

Where It Forms

Chrysoprase forms at shallow depths where nickel-bearing rocks such as peridotite, dunite and serpentinite weather. It fills cavities, veins and fractures that are rarely more than a few inches wide, so it is mined by the kilogram, not by the ton. Yet sites near Kalgoorlie, Western Australia, have yielded nodules of about 1,540 and 3,240 pounds (700 and 1,470 kg).

Where It Comes From

Most chrysoprase sold today comes from Australia, and much of the best is mined in the Marlborough Creek District of Queensland. Earlier, much of the world's supply came from the Szklary deposit in Poland, worked on and off from the Middle Ages until 1980, with new activity since 2010.

The newest chapter is very recent. Since mid-2024, chrysoprase found by small-scale gold miners in Western Australia has reached the gem markets of Bangkok, and GIA's Bangkok lab described it in its Spring 2026 issue.

Tradition

Where the Meaning Comes From

The Story. Chrysoprase has one of the richer paper trails in gem lore, and one of the most borrowed.

The Timeline, Ancient Egypt to Today

  1. Ancient Egypt Tradition

    Kunz describes an Egyptian woman's amulet necklace with large beads of carnelian, chrysoprase and malachite among its charms (The Curious Lore of Precious Stones, 1913, pages 36 to 37).

  2. Greece and Rome

    The stone was known, and a few pieces turn up among their artifacts, but it was not commonly used.

  3. The Bible Tradition

    The Septuagint, the old Greek translation of the Hebrew Bible, renders the precious stone of Genesis 2:12 as chrysoprase (Kunz, The Magic of Jewels and Charms, 1915, page 277). In Revelation's list of foundation stones of the New Jerusalem, chrysoprase is the tenth, in a place where the older breastplate list had an agate (Kunz 1913, pages 305 and 319).

  4. Christian symbolism Tradition

    One commentator saw in chrysoprase "the work of the blessed martyrs and their reward." Another gave it to St. Thaddaeus, a stone "more brightly tinged with a golden hue than gold itself." A third, reading every stone as Christ, made the "harsh-colored Chrysoprase" stand for sternness toward sinners (Kunz 1913, pages 305, 312 and 313).

  5. About 1250, Germany Tradition

    The poet Volmar wrote that a thief sentenced to hang who put chrysoprase in his mouth would escape his executioners. Kunz guesses the stone was thought to make him invisible (1913, pages 67 to 68 and 373).

  6. 1500s, Prague

    A huge "emerald" nine inches across in the chapel of St. Wenceslaus may have been chrysoprase, Kunz suggests, since the chapel walls are inlaid with it (1913, pages 259 to 260).

  7. 1500s, pharmacies

    "Yellow chrysoprase," really a stained chalcedony, was often swapped for jacinth in tonics meant to protect the heart (Kunz 1915, pages 122 to 123). Dyed stand-ins are an old trade.

  8. 1700s, Europe

    Chrysoprase first became popular in Europe after Polish deposits were found.

  9. Iron Age Japan Tradition

    Curved magatama amulets found in graves were sometimes carved from chrysoprase (Kunz 1913, page 265).

  10. Today Modern Association

    The heart chakra, hope and fresh starts (see below).

Did Alexander the Great Wear Chrysoprase?

Maybe not. Shop pages say Alexander wore chrysoprase in battle and never won again after losing it. Kunz's version, from the medieval scholar Albertus Magnus, has Alexander wearing a "prase" in his girdle until a serpent bit it off while he bathed in the Euphrates. Albertus himself admitted it seemed like a fable, and Kunz adds that "prase" was used so loosely that this victory stone may have been an emerald or jade (1913, page 68). The "never won again" ending is not in his account.

Is Chrysoprase a Birthstone?

Not on the modern US list, which gives May to emerald. Some crystal sites call it an alternative May stone. In older schemes that matched the foundation stones to months, chrysoprase belonged to December, but the later month list put ruby there instead (Kunz 1913, page 316). Today's December stones are on our December birthstone page.

Is My Chrysoprase Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone.

Natural, Untreated

Most chrysoprase sold today is Australian, and natural, untreated Australian stones will not fade in sunlight.

Natural, Treated

Dyed chrysoprase exists, and it may fade with sun or heat.

Lab-Grown

Labs can grow quartz, chalcedony included, and that material does appear in jewelry, though buyers are more likely to meet dyed look-alikes.

Imitation

Ordinary chalcedony and agate dyed green, often sold as "chrysoprase," "chalcedony chrysoprase" or "chrysoprase chalcedony." All of these are misnomers, and the honest name is dyed green chalcedony.

Trade Name

See the box below.

Trade Name

"Lemon" or "citron chrysoprase" is milky yellow-green magnesite, a magnesium carbonate, better called lemon magnesite. Aquaprase, a blue-green chalcedony found in Africa in 2015, is not a variety of chrysoprase. And fine, saturated chrysoprase has itself been sold as "imperial jade." "Prase" is an old name for almost any green quartz.

Three checks you can do at home, with their limits.

  1. Compare the beads

    Real chrysoprase usually varies in color, saturation or translucency and often contains inclusions. Perfectly matched beads are dyed, imitation or of fantastic quality. This raises suspicion, it proves nothing.

  2. Ask about dye, in writing

    When a geologist messaged online sellers whose "chrysoprase" listings never mentioned dye and asked "are these beads dyed?", they usually answered yes. It depends on the seller's honesty.

  3. Watch the color over time

    A stone that pales in sunlight could be dyed or European, since natural untreated Australian stones do not fade. That is a clue after the fact, not a test before you buy.

A gem lab can confirm it. The 2026 GIA study recorded a refractive index of 1.53 to 1.54, an aggregate reaction in the polariscope and nickel in the chemistry. Ask the seller whether the color is natural, where it was mined and whether it has been dyed or treated. The Label Decoder turns a tag into these questions.

Can Chrysoprase Go in Water?

Yes, for washing. Clean it with mild detergent, warm water and a soft brush. For faded European stones, the International Gem Society even suggests storage with a moist cloth to bring the color back. Washing is covered, long soaks are not, so our Care Checker rates soaking Brief only.

Can Chrysoprase Go in the Sun?

Better not. Color in some chrysoprase is unstable, and prolonged sunlight can fade it. Dyed and natural European stones fade, while natural untreated Australian ones do not. You usually cannot tell which you have by eye, so our care data marks it Keep Out of Sun. A few guides suggest charging chrysoprase in strong midday sun. Here the care data wins, and the evening jewelry box is the safer habit.

Chrysoprase vs Look-Alikes

Chrysoprase vs Jade

They can look alike, and very fine chrysoprase has been misidentified, or deliberately sold, as "imperial jade." Jadeite costs much more, and despite appearances gemologists can readily tell the two apart by their properties. If a green stone is priced as jade, ask for a lab report. More on the real thing on our jade page.

Aquaprase vs Chrysoprase

Aquaprase is a bluish green to turquoise chalcedony sold under that trade name since its discovery in Africa in 2015. It is its own variety of chalcedony with distinct properties, not a chrysoprase. See our aquaprase page.

Other Green Look-Alikes

Lemon Magnesite

Opaque and milky, and often found in the same nodules as chrysoprase, sometimes one wrapped around the other.

Chrome Chalcedony

Called mtorolite in Zimbabwe and chiquitanita in Bolivia, colored by chromium, darker grayish green and nearly opaque.

Bloodstone

Dark green opaque chalcedony with red spots, and its color does not come from nickel.

Price Guide

Chrysoprase is a valued chalcedony, yet rarely seen in mass-market jewelry, because stones of consistent color in standard sizes are hard to get in quantity. It turns up mostly in designer, limited-run and one-of-a-kind pieces. The 2026 GIA report found that the new Western Australian material has drawn connoisseurs despite its limited volume, especially in markets with an appetite for fine green translucent gems.

What sets the price is mostly color, translucency and whether the color is natural. Chrysoprase runs from apple to olive green and from nearly opaque to nearly transparent, and very fine, highly saturated stones have been sold as "imperial jade," a far pricier gem. Origin claims matter too, since untreated Australian stones hold their color in sunlight while European ones can fade. Dyed green chalcedony is not chrysoprase at any price, so ask the questions above before you look at the tag. We give no price figures here, since the current listings we found did not make clear whether prices were per stone or per carat.

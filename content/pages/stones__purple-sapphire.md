# Purple Sapphire Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Purple sapphire is the purple variety of corundum, the same mineral as ruby and blue sapphire, and it rates 9 on the Mohs hardness scale. Crystal sellers tie it to calm, intuition and the crown chakra, and those meanings are beliefs. The honest note is that much sapphire on the market is heated, diffusion treated or lab-grown, so the label matters.

Under a loupe, curved growth lines point to a lab stone. Natural versus lab-grown, and heated versus unheated, is a question for a gem lab report.

## What Purple Sapphire Actually Is

**The Stone.** All red gem-quality corundum counts as ruby, and every other color counts as sapphire. On the market, blue stones are simply called sapphires, while the others are named by color and grouped as "fancy sapphires." Purple sapphire is one of them, the same crystal as a blue sapphire with different trace elements.

Corundum is made only of aluminum and oxygen and needs a growth environment free of silicon, which makes natural corundum relatively uncommon. Pure corundum is colorless, our [white sapphire](/stones/white-sapphire/). Sapphire has a hardness of 9, a specific gravity of 4.00 and a refractive index of 1.762 to 1.770. Only diamond is harder among natural gems, and with no cleavage planes, sapphire makes a superb everyday jewelry stone.

### What Gives Purple Sapphire Its Color?

Trace elements, and the exact mix is less tidy than the sales copy suggests. Iron and titanium make corundum blue, and chromium causes the red of ruby and the pink of pink sapphire. Purple sits between the two, and natural purple sapphire may show a combination of the ruby and the sapphire absorption spectrum.

One gem auction site credits vanadium instead, an element tied to color change, so treat any single-element answer as a simplification.

### Purple, Violet or Color-Change?

Sellers use purple, violet and plum for overlapping shades. Purple sapphire is medium to dark in tone, with small contributions of red or violet allowed. Color-change sapphire is the special case. These stones most often go from blue in daylight or fluorescent light to purple under incandescent light, and one documented 1.33-carat stone from the Umba Valley in Tanzania does exactly that.

### Where Purple Sapphire Comes From

Fancy sapphires became widely known in the 1990s, when discoveries in East Africa and Madagascar increased the supply of yellows, oranges, pinks and purples. Madagascar, Tanzania, Sri Lanka, Myanmar and Australia are among the sources of both blue and fancy sapphires, and Madagascar also yields purple and pink rough.

Montana has a purple chapter too. A 2020 lab note in *Gems & Gemology* describes an unheated 10.47-carat Montana sapphire of "saturated purple color," and notes that Yogo Gulch has produced fine blue and purple gems, though cut stones over one carat are rare.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** Purple sapphire has almost no lore of its own, and one gem dealer says it is usually understood within the history of sapphire in general. What it does have is a long record of mixed-up names.

- **Ancient Rome.** **Tradition.** Pliny the Elder's amethystos may not be one stone. The footnotes of his translators, John Bostock and H. T. Riley, say it may cover quartz amethyst, violet sapphire, violet fluorite and possibly garnet. Pliny reports that magicians claimed these stones prevent drunkenness, and he wrote that "the falsehoods of the magicians would persuade us that these stones are preventive of inebriety" (*Natural History*, Book 37, chapter 40).
- **Pliny's "sapphire."** **Tradition.** His sapphirus, azure and only rarely purple, is lapis lazuli, not corundum, say the same translators (Book 37, chapter 39). Sapphire was long linked with royalty and said to protect against poison and fraud.
- **The "Oriental amethyst."** **Tradition.** Old jewelers called purple corundum Oriental amethyst. George Frederick Kunz lists it with ruby, sapphire and Oriental topaz among the corundum gems (*The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, 1915, page 58), and he notes that the amethyst of the Hebrew breastplate was "not, however, the Oriental amethyst, a variety of corundum," but a dark blue or purple quartz (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, page 297).
- **Old color symbolism.** **Tradition.** In one scheme Kunz describes, violet stood for sober judgment, industry and gravity in a man, and for high thoughts and religious love in a woman. It was the color of Jupiter and of Thursday, its animal was the bull, and "the sapphire was conceived to present violet most attractively" (1913, pages 32 to 33).
- **18th century.** **Tradition.** A sapphire once at the South Kensington Museum in London looked rich blue by day and violet by artificial light, like an amethyst. The writer Mme. de Genlis made it the theme of a story, "Le Saphire Merveilleux," in which the color change served as a test of a wearer's faithfulness. Kunz adds that it was for a time in the collection of the Duke of Orleans, known as Philippe Égalité in the French Revolution (1913, pages 105 to 106).
- **Zodiac verse.** **Tradition.** An old list of zodiacal gems that Kunz reprints gives the sapphire to Taurus (1913, page 346), which may be where today's Taurus link comes from.
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Dealer guides call purple sapphire a crown chakra stone and the "Stone of Awakening." One of them adds that in Hindu astrology a rare blue-violet sapphire with crimson hints, khooni neelam, is a stone for Saturn and Mars.

### Is the Delhi Purple Sapphire Really a Sapphire?

No. The stone usually called the Delhi Purple Sapphire is an amethyst. The Natural History Museum in London, which holds it, lists it as "The Cursed Amethyst," an oval amethyst set in a silver ring shaped like a snake. The story says it was looted from the Temple of Indra in Cawnpore during the Indian uprising against British rule and that later owners suffered disaster.

Its last private owner, Edward Heron-Allen, sealed it in a safety deposit box with a warning letter saying it should not be seen again until 33 years after his death. His daughter waited 12 months and gave it to the museum in 1944, and rumor has it that he invented the curse to promote his short story "The Purple Sapphire." The best-known "purple sapphire" in the world is a case of mistaken identity, a useful lesson for any buyer.

## What People Use Purple Sapphire for Today

**Modern Association.** Purple sapphire has no Classic association in modern crystal guides. It is only Sometimes Linked with easing anxiety, with focus, with confidence and with protection. Dealer pages call it a stone for worry and overthinking, for intuition, and for "quiet confidence," individuality and dignity.

The "why" has two roots. One is color. One dealer points to purple's old links with refinement, ceremony, imagination and spiritual reflection, which suits the [crown chakra](/chakras/crown/), shown in violet or white on today's chakra chart. The other is family. Sapphire is often used to stand for commitment and marks the 5th and 45th anniversaries, so purple inherits that.

### What Does Purple Sapphire Symbolize?

In today's crystal culture, calm thinking, intuition and a quiet kind of confidence. In older symbolism, violet meant sober judgment, ruled by Jupiter. Even that dealer calls these cultural and personal associations rather than scientific properties, which is the right way to hold them. One listicle goes further, saying purple sapphire improves circulation and treats gallstones, hepatitis and eye disorders. Gem science and medicine do not back those claims.

## How to Use Purple Sapphire in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** Purple sapphire lives on a hand or at a collarbone, not in a pocket, so its rituals ride along with the jewelry.

### The Lamp-Light Check-In

**Try This.** When you switch on the warm lamps in the evening, look at your stone for a moment. Note how its color looks in the new light, since some purple sapphires shift noticeably, then name one thing that shifted in your day too. Mme. de Genlis turned the same change into a test of faithfulness. Here it is just a nightly pause.

### A Quiet Confidence Cue Before You Speak

**Try This.** Guides tie purple sapphire to quiet confidence. Before a meeting or a hard phone call, turn the ring once and say your main point to yourself in one sentence. The stone is the cue. The sentence is the preparation.

### A Sapphire Anniversary Note

**Try This.** Sapphire marks the 45th anniversary, and purple is a lovely change from blue. Put a handwritten note in the box with one moment from the early years that still makes you smile and one thing you still admire about your partner.

**Safety.** Loose stones and small studs are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

**What Research Says.** In three experiments, people who wrote gratitude letters underestimated how positive their recipients would feel and overestimated how awkward the letter would seem (Kumar and Epley, 2018, *Psychological Science*, [doi.org/10.1177/0956797618772506](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618772506)). The letters were written because researchers asked for them, the study measured reactions to one letter, and no stone or ring was involved, so tying it to an anniversary note is our extrapolation. The note is the part that may matter, not the sapphire.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Purple sapphire is sold by some shops for worry, stress and anxiety, and by a few for physical complaints. A ring can be a comfort to wear, but it is not care. If anxiety, stress or a health problem lasts or feels heavy, talk to a doctor or a therapist, and never use a stone in place of treatment. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My Purple Sapphire Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone. Purple sapphire can be any of them, and the price should follow the label.

- **Natural, untreated.** Mined and never heated or diffused, like the Montana stone above, and fine unheated sapphires see a big bump in their per-carat values. One dealer claims purple sapphires rarely need enhancement, but GIA is more cautious, so ask rather than assume.
- **Natural, treated.** "A large amount of the sapphire on the market, both blue and fancy colors, has been treated to alter its color by heat or by lattice diffusion," says GIA. Lattice diffusion uses heat and chemicals to push an element into the stone, and the added color can be shallow or go all the way through.
- **Lab-grown.** Corundum was first grown in the early 1900s by flame fusion, and many sapphires on the market today are lab-grown. See [lab-grown vs natural crystals](/real-or-fake/lab-grown-vs-natural-crystals/).
- **Imitation.** Glass, plastic and cubic zirconia, which can be made in almost any color. Amethyst sold as sapphire belongs here too, as the Delhi stone shows.
- **Trade names.** "Violet sapphire" and "plum sapphire" are color names. "Oriental amethyst" is an old name for purple corundum, not amethyst, and "khooni neelam" is an astrology name.

### Is Heat-Treated Purple Sapphire Real?

Yes. It is real corundum, mined and then heated, so it sits in the natural, treated group. Heating is an accepted treatment, so common that it does not lower value much, while other treatments do. One twist matters for purple. Heated purple sapphires can take on a more valuable pink or red color, so a heated stone may not stay purple at all.

### What Does Lab-Created Purple Sapphire Mean?

Real corundum grown in a laboratory instead of mined, so close to natural that it is difficult for all but the most skilled professionals to identify. Lab-made purple corundum also plays the opposite role, as a durable amethyst imitation. A lab label is fine if the price matches it.

### Three Checks, With Their Limits

1. **Compare daylight and lamplight.** A clear shift from blue toward purple suggests a color-change sapphire. Lab-grown color-change sapphires exist too, so this says nothing about origin.
2. **Look inside with a loupe.** Curved growth lines, called curved striae, appear only in synthetic sapphires and rubies, while many natural sapphires hold fine rutile needles called silk. A clean stone proves nothing either way.
3. **Ask for a lab report.** "When in doubt, get a lab report," says GIA. A report can tell natural from lab-grown and list detectable treatments, though GIA does not grade colored stones for quality. Labs also use ultraviolet light, and synthetic violet sapphire glows strong greenish blue under shortwave, where natural violet sapphire glows weak light red.

Four questions to ask the seller. Natural or lab-grown? Heated, diffused or filled? Which lab wrote the report? Where was it mined? The [Label Decoder](/tools/crystal-label-decoder/) turns the words on a tag into what they usually mean.

## How to Care for Purple Sapphire

**Mineral Fact.** Corundum is relatively hard and tough, a great choice for jewelry worn every day. Some sapphires receive oil treatments, and inclusions can weaken their normally tough structure.

- **Water.** OK. Warm water, gentle soap and a soft brush. Warm soapy water is always safe.
- **Soaking.** Brief only. The guidance covers washing, not a long soak.
- **Sun, heat, salt and chemicals.** Unknown. There is no reliable guidance for purple sapphire yet, so keep it away from harsh cleaners and strong heat and ask a jeweler before anything stronger than soapy water.
- **Filled or dyed stones.** Fracture-filled, cavity-filled or dyed material should only be cleaned with a damp cloth.
- **Scratches and storage.** Brief only. Very hard and durable, but not immune to damage. Only diamond is harder among natural gems, and no care source gives storage advice for this stone.
- **Toxicity.** OK to wear and handle. No known toxicity risk is listed for sapphire.

### Can Purple Sapphire Go in Water?

For a wash, yes. A long soak is rated Brief only because no care source covers soaking, and an oiled or filled stone is the last one you want sitting in water. Keep every stone out of drinking water. Check your piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/), which shows OK, Brief only, Avoid or Unknown with the reason and the source.

## Purple Sapphire vs Look-Alikes

- **Amethyst.** Purple quartz, 7 on the Mohs scale against sapphire's 9. Quartz has a specific gravity of 2.6 to 2.7 against 4.00, and a refractive index near 1.54 to 1.55 against about 1.76. Amethyst is mistaken for purple sapphire more often than the reverse. See [amethyst](/stones/amethyst/) and [purple sapphire vs amethyst](/compare/purple-sapphire-vs-amethyst/).
- **Spinel and rhodolite garnet.** Rarely purple, but they can pass for amethyst, with a refractive index and specific gravity higher than amethyst's, so telling them from sapphire takes a gemologist.
- **Pink sapphire.** The same mineral one shade over, and far more sought after. See [pink sapphire](/stones/pink-sapphire/).
- **Blue sapphire.** Blue sapphire ranges from greenish blue to violetish blue, so violet-blue versus purple is partly a naming question. Our [sapphire page](/stones/sapphire/) covers the family.

## Price Guide

Is purple sapphire expensive? The International Gem Society says purple sapphires are "not nearly as sought-after as pinks," tend to be darker in tone, and compete directly with amethyst, which is low-cost and can be vividly colored. Prices vary too much by stone to quote a single figure, so ask any seller for the weight, color and treatment in writing and compare like with like.

What drives the price.

- **Color.** The most important factor. Strong saturation sits at the top of the range, and each fancy color has its own value range. Purple allows small touches of red or violet.
- **Treatment.** Heating is standard and does not lower value much, while fine unheated stones sell for more, beryllium lattice diffusion is a drastic treatment that reduces value, and radiation treatment is unstable and often reversible. Lattice-diffused sapphire generally costs less than heated sapphire, and an independent lab report adds to the value of exceptional stones.
- **Clarity.** Eye-clean stones command more, and fractures and inclusions lower value. Price can also drop if inclusions threaten the stone's durability.
- **Cut.** A poor cut sells at a discount, while skilled cutting can hide color zoning.
- **Color zoning.** A noticeably patchy stone is worth less than an evenly colored one.
- **Size.** Above five carats value jumps significantly with good color and clarity. One dealer says most purple sapphires sold are just under 1 carat.

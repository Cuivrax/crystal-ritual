## How the Birthday Moon Tool Works

Pick a date and press Show the moon. The tool works out where the moon stood in its cycle at 9 PM your time that day, then draws it, names the phase and gives the share of the disk in sunlight. When one of the four main phases falls on that day, it also gives the exact time.

The math runs in your browser, with no account and nothing to install. It follows the method in Jean Meeus's *Astronomical Algorithms* (1998), and when we checked it against the US Naval Observatory phase times for 2026 and 2027, it agreed to within about a minute.

Three details explain most surprises.

- **Your time zone sets the date.** The main phases are instants that happen at the same moment for everyone on Earth. The calendar date of that instant depends on where you are, and the tool uses the time zone of your device. The Cold Moon of 2026 comes on the evening of December 23 in New York and on December 24 in Tokyo, so a December 23 birthday reads Full Moon in New York and Waxing Gibbous in Tokyo. Born far from where you live now? Check the day before and the day after too.
- **A phase name covers the whole day.** Like a printed calendar, the tool gives the day of a main phase that phase's name, and every other day the in-between name. The moon can look full for several nights in a row, so a birthday next to the full moon still had a nearly round moon.
- **The drawing follows your hemisphere.** The lit side is drawn as it looks from the Northern Hemisphere, a waxing moon lit on the right, and turned around when your time zone is in the Southern Hemisphere. The tool reads this from the time zone, not from your location.

**Try This.** The Save as image button makes a 1000 by 1500 pixel picture of your birth moon with its phase, the date and the share lit. It fits a Pinterest board or the inside of a birthday card.

## The Eight Moon Phases, Explained

One full cycle of phases, called a lunation, takes 29.5 days on average. The four main phases are moments. The four others are the stretches between them, waxing when the lit part grows and waning when it shrinks.

| Phase | What you see | Share lit |
|---|---|---|
| New Moon | Nothing, the unlit side faces us | 0% |
| Waxing Crescent | A sliver that grows each night | Under 50% |
| First Quarter | Half the disk | 50% |
| Waxing Gibbous | More than half, still growing | 50 to 100% |
| Full Moon | The whole disk | 100% |
| Waning Gibbous | More than half, shrinking | 100 to 50% |
| Last Quarter | Half the disk, lit on the other side | 50% |
| Waning Crescent | A sliver shrinking toward new | Under 50% |

The percentages follow the Naval Observatory's definitions.

### What Moon Was I Born Under?

The moon you were born under is the phase the moon was in on your birth date, the one the tool above shows. Some people mean the traditional name of the full moon in their birth month instead. These are the North American names listed by the Old Farmer's Almanac, which our [full moon calendar](/moon/full-moon-calendar/) also uses.

| Month | Full moon | Month | Full moon |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | Wolf Moon | July | Buck Moon |
| February | Snow Moon | August | Sturgeon Moon |
| March | Worm Moon | September | Corn Moon |
| April | Pink Moon | October | Hunter's Moon |
| May | Flower Moon | November | Beaver Moon |
| June | Strawberry Moon | December | Cold Moon |

A second full moon in the same month is a Blue Moon, as on May 31, 2026. Reading a personality into the moon phase of a birth is a modern idea, not an old tradition, and nothing in the astronomy behind this tool tests it.

**Mineral Fact.** Moonstone earned its name honestly. It is a feldspar, and its soft glow that seems to float under the surface, called adularescence, comes from light scattering between thin layers inside the stone.

**Tradition.** Long before phone apps, European writers tied a handful of stones to the moon. George Frederick Kunz collected the stories in 1913 and 1915.

- **Moonstone.** An old print of the Roman days of the week pairs Diana and the sign of Cancer with the moonstone for Monday, and in Siam white clothes and moonstones were prescribed for Monday (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, pages 332 and 335). Kunz also reports a belief that lovers could read their future by placing a moonstone in the mouth while the moon was full (pages 97 to 98). Please admire it from a safe distance instead.
- **Selenite.** In the 1500s Antoine Mizauld described a "selenite or moonstone" whose white mark grew and shrank with the moon, and Rabelais imagined a temple column of selenite with the moon moving inside it (same book, pages 98 and 349).
- **Pearl.** In the color symbolism Kunz summarizes, white was tied to the moon and to Monday and was represented by the pearl. In the old list of planetary stones, the moon controls the beryl, rock crystal and pearl (pages 30 and 347 to 348).
- **A fable, by Kunz's own account.** Pope Leo X was said to own a stone that brightened as the moon waxed. Kunz files it among the "fables that were circulated intentionally" (*The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, 1915, pages 386 to 387).

**Modern Association.** Today's crystal guides pair stones with phases rather than days. Moonstone is the stone modern guides pair most often with both the new moon and the full moon, along with its trade cousins peach, black and [rainbow moonstone](/stones/rainbow-moonstone/) (the last one is really a labradorite). For the full moon, obsidian, strawberry quartz and citrine come up too. For the waxing and waning moon, the guides agree on no stone, so the tool shows none rather than invent a list.

**Safety.** Our care data has no moonlight warning for these stones, but the morning sun is another matter. Some citrine can fade with prolonged exposure to intense light, so bring a full-moon citrine in before the sun reaches the sill. Selenite is a form of gypsum, which is water soluble, so keep it off damp porches. Not sure about your stone? Run it through the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## A Five-Minute Birthday Moon Ritual

**Try This.** On the evening of your birthday, look up your birth moon above, then find tonight's moon on [Tonight's Moon](/moon/full-moon-calendar/). Compare the two drawings.

1. **1 minute.** Step to a window or outside and find the real moon, or the patch of sky where it should be.
2. **2 minutes.** On a small card, write one thing that grew in you since your last birthday and one thing you are ready to let shrink.
3. **1 minute.** Set a stone you like on the card. A moonstone suits the theme, any pebble with a story works as well.
4. **1 minute.** Save your birth moon picture and keep the card with it.

It is a personal practice, not a proven effect. What it gives you for sure is a quiet pause on a loud day and a date to meet again next year.

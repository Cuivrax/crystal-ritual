# Cancer Crystals & Birthstones, Plus Where the Links Come From

Cancer has no single official birthstone, because birthstones belong to months and the zodiac sign Cancer runs from about June 21 to July 22. That gives pearl, alexandrite or moonstone for a June birthday and ruby for a July one. As a zodiac stone, a 1913 list gives Cancer the emerald, while today's crystal guides favor opal, moonstone and morganite, all beliefs rather than mineral facts.

## What Is the Cancer Birthstone?

The honest answer has two parts, the month stone and the sign stone.

**The month stone.** On the modern list of the [American Gem Society](https://www.americangemsociety.org/birthstones/), June has three birthstones, pearl, alexandrite and moonstone, and July has one, ruby. The 1912 jewelers' list gave June the pearl with moonstone as its alternate, and July the ruby (Kunz 1913, pages 319 to 320). Alexandrite joined June in 1952, when the Jewelry Industry Council of America revised the list. Our [June](/birthstones/june/) and [July](/birthstones/july/) pages go stone by stone.

Kunz disliked two of those choices. He objected to the ruby moving from December to July (page 320), and he thought the pearl, "not a stone in any sense of the word," should not be on the list at all (page 321).

**The sign stone.** **Tradition.** In the verse list of zodiac gems that George Frederick Kunz printed in 1913, Cancer gets the emerald. "If born in Cancer's sign, they say, / Your life will joyful be alway, / If you take with you on your way / An emerald" (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, page 346). An old Spanish list that Kunz thought probably reflected Arab tradition gives Cancer agate and beryl (page 347), and his own calendar of the months gives June, with Cancer as its sign, the agate and the honeysuckle (pages 328 to 329). So the agate of [Gemini's](/zodiac/gemini/) 1913 verse turns up for Cancer in two other lists.

## What Color Is the Cancer Birthstone?

The Cancer birthstone colors are mostly pale and milky, with one red exception. Pearl is best known in white and cream, moonstone glows with a white to silvery sheen, and alexandrite looks green to bluish green in daylight and red to purplish red under incandescent light. July's ruby is the red, from orangy red to purplish red. The 1913 emerald adds green, and the modern picks stay soft, from opal to pink morganite and the pale calcites.

## Cancer Crystals in Today's Guides

**Modern Association.** Guides describe Cancer as "the moon-ruled water sign traditionally associated with emotion, family, and intuition," in the words of one German guide. "Both Cancer and moonstone are governed by the moon," another says, and that line explains much of the list. Crystals for Cancer tend to be milky and pale, and few are hard gems. Here are the usual Cancer stones, with what each really is.

### Opal

**Widely Linked.** Opal is hydrated silica with no crystal structure, holding 3 to 21 percent water, and it rates 5 to 6.5 on the Mohs scale. It chips and scratches more easily than most gems and dislikes water and heat, so it suits a pendant better than a ring. It is October's birthstone. One guide says opal has no sign of its own but a strong affinity with "the maternal sign" of Cancer. [Blue opal](/stones/blue-opal/), Often Linked, is usually Peruvian common opal with no rainbow flashes, and it wants a damp cloth, with any soak kept brief. Our [types of opal](/stones/family/opal/) page sorts the rest.

### Moonstone

**Widely Linked.** [Moonstone](/stones/moonstone/) is a feldspar with adularescence, a soft glow that seems to float under the surface, caused by light scattering between thin feldspar layers. It rates 6 to 6.5 and has two cleavage directions, so a sharp knock can break it. **Tradition.** It also has the clearest old Moon link of any Cancer stone. In an old print of the Roman day gods, Diana stands with the sign of Cancer and the moonstone for Monday (Kunz 1913, page 332), the day the Romans called dies Lunae (page 137). Peach, blue and [rainbow moonstone](/stones/rainbow-moonstone/) share the link, though rainbow moonstone is technically labradorite.

### Morganite

**Widely Linked.** [Morganite](/stones/morganite/) is the pink to orange-pink variety of beryl, colored by manganese and rated 7.5 to 8, hard and durable enough for daily wear. It is the same mineral as the 1913 emerald. Light stones are often heated or irradiated, a common, permanent and undetectable treatment.

### The Calcites

**Widely Linked** for honeycomb calcite and **Often Linked** for yellow, orange and pink calcite. All are calcite, the soft calcium carbonate of limestone and marble, about 3 on the Mohs scale, and vinegar dissolves them. They are shelf stones, never bath stones, and [orange calcite](/stones/orange-calcite/) has the details.

### Emerald, Pearl & Alexandrite

**Sometimes Linked.** [Emerald](/stones/emerald/), the 1913 stone, still turns up in some Cancer lists, and one guide calls it "the traditional zodiac stone for those born under the sign of Cancer in early summer." It is green beryl, rated 7.5 to 8, and most cut stones are oiled or filled. [Pearl](/stones/pearl/) is an organic gem made by a mollusk, only 2.5 to 3 on the Mohs scale, and cultured pearls are real pearls. One guide ties it to Cancer as "a water sign that astrologers say is ruled by the moon." [Alexandrite](/stones/alexandrite/), the color-change chrysoberyl, appears in a few Cancer lists but more Gemini ones.

### Ruby for July Cancers

**Rarely Linked.** [Ruby](/stones/ruby/) is red corundum colored by chromium, rated 9, and heat treatment is the norm. Many famous historic "rubies," the Black Prince's Ruby among them, turned out to be red spinels. Few crystal guides tie ruby to Cancer. For anyone born between July 1 and July 22, though, it is the one official birthstone, and Kunz, who wanted it back in December, still called the ruby "the gem of summer" (1913, page 323).

## Where the Cancer Links Come From

**The Story.** Cancer's old stones belong to the Moon.

- **Medieval Jewish tradition.** **Tradition.** The tribe of Reuben was paired with Cancer (Kunz 1913, page 314).
- **Old print.** **Tradition.** Diana, for Monday, stands with Cancer and the moonstone (page 332).
- **Old planetary lists.** **Tradition.** The Moon "controls the beryl, the rock-crystal and also the pearl" (page 348).
- **Old weekday rituals.** **Tradition.** Monday was the day for the "Works of Divination and Mystery," when the high priest wore a triple necklace of pearls, "crystals" and selenites (page 336), an old name Kunz also uses for moonstone (page 98).
- **Old talisman lists.** **Tradition.** An eagle, Aquila, engraved under the sign of Cancer "preserves former honors, adds new ones, and helps to victory" (page 353).
- **1913.** **Tradition.** Kunz's verse list names the emerald (page 346), and the old Spanish list agate and beryl (page 347).
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Opal, moonstone and morganite lead the modern lists.

So the old Moon stones were beryl, rock crystal and pearl. The old Cancer lists give emerald, which is green beryl, and beryl itself, and today's favorites include moonstone and morganite, a pink beryl. The thread is our reading, but each piece of it comes from Kunz.

## How to Use Cancer Stones in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** Whatever you believe about the stones, these give a pause you chose and something in your hand to mark it.

### A Monday Night Check-In

**Try This.** The old print gave Monday to the Moon and Cancer. On Monday evening, set a moonstone on your nightstand and send one message to someone in your family or circle you have not talked to that week. Moonstone has two cleavages, so it rests on a soft dish rather than rattling in a drawer.

### A Full Moon Pause

**Try This.** Guides tie Cancer to the Moon and to emotion. On the night of each full moon, check the [full moon calendar](/moon/full-moon-calendar/), hold a stone for one minute and write one line about how the month felt. After a year, you have the whole year in your own words.

### A Birthday Card with the Old Verse

**Try This.** For a June Cancer, give a pearl and add Kunz's grumble that it is "not a stone in any sense of the word." For a July Cancer, give a ruby and copy the 1913 emerald verse inside the card.

**Safety.** No stone goes in water you drink. Calcite and pearl dissolve in acids, so keep vinegar sprays away from them. Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them. These are personal practices, not treatments.

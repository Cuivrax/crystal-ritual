# April Birthstone Meaning, History & Honest Buying Tips

The April birthstone is diamond, the only stone the American Gem Society lists for the month. Diamond is pure carbon, the hardest natural gem, and April's only stone on the US jewelers' list since 1912, though most older European lists gave April the sapphire. A lab-grown diamond is a real diamond too, so the paperwork matters as much as the sparkle.

## What Is April's Birthstone?

Diamond, on every modern US list. The older lists tell a different story.

| List | April stone | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Old lists Kunz attributes to the Jews, Romans, Arabians, Russians, Italians and Isidore of Seville | Sapphire | Kunz 1913, page 315 |
| Polish list | Diamond | Kunz 1913, page 315 |
| 15th to 20th century European usage | Diamond, sapphire | Kunz 1913, page 315 |
| US jewelers, Kansas City, August 1912 | Diamond alone | Kunz 1913, page 319 |
| Kunz's list of American gems | Sapphire from Montana and Idaho | Kunz 1913, page 323 |
| American Gem Society and GIA today | Diamond | AGS, GIA |

### Modern vs Traditional April Birthstones

"Modern" means diamond. So does "traditional" if you mean the 1912 jewelers' list, as on our [birthstone chart](/birthstones/). Go back further and the traditional April stone is sapphire, named for April in seven of the eight old lists George Frederick Kunz compared in 1913, against two for diamond (page 316).

### Why Does April Have Only One Birthstone?

GIA's birthstone exhibit says months gain extra stones mostly because "some gemstones are too rare and expensive, and others just aren't as popular." Popularity is not diamond's problem. In 2018 the US imported about $26 billion of diamonds for consumption, against about $2.0 billion of all other gemstones.

## How April Lost Its Sapphire

**Tradition.** Month stones follow the foundation stones of the New Jerusalem in Revelation, counted from March, so the second stone, the sapphire, went to April (Kunz 1913, pages 307 to 308). That ancient "sapphire" was probably lapis lazuli, Kunz notes (page 319).

Diamond came later. When wearing month stones caught on, probably in Poland, a few stones were swapped, notably "the diamond for April" (page 308). The 1912 Kansas City list then made diamond April's only stone and moved the sapphire, "properly the gem for April," to [September](/birthstones/september/) (page 320).

Kunz went further. Since the diamond "does not appear to have been known to the ancients," he suggested dropping it from the list and using diamonds as a border around the month's own stone (page 321).

## Diamond, the April Birthstone

**The Stone.** [Diamond](/stones/diamond/) is a mineral made entirely of carbon and the 10 on the Mohs scale of scratch resistance, the hardest natural substance known. It forms about 100 miles down in the mantle and reaches the surface in deep-source volcanic eruptions, not from coal. Some diamonds are more than three billion years old.

Most diamonds are brown or yellow. Jewelers favor colorless stones, and only about one diamond in 10,000 has a color vivid enough to be called fancy. The sparkle comes from a refractive index of 2.417, a measure of how strongly a gem bends light, and high dispersion, which splits light into flashes of color called fire.

Diamond has perfect cleavage, planes along which it can split, in four directions, so a sharp knock at the wrong angle can chip it.

India may have traded diamonds as early as the fourth century BCE. Brazil became an important source in the early 1700s, as India's supplies declined, and South Africa followed after diamonds were found there in 1866. In the US, the diamond pipe at Murfreesboro, Arkansas, sits on government land where visitors pay a small fee to dig, and it gave up the 40.23-carat "Uncle Sam."

**The Story.**

- **2nd century.** **Tradition.** A poet held that diamond warded off the evil eye. Kunz doubted the ancients knew diamond at all and read the adamas, the Greek word behind "diamond," in a 2nd-century Orphic poem as white sapphire or quartz (1913, pages 72 to 73).
- **Middle Ages.** **Tradition.** Its virtues were "almost all directly traceable either to its unconquerable hardness or to its transparency and purity," Kunz explains, so it was thought to bring victory, strength and courage (pages 69 to 70).
- **A gift, not a purchase.** **Tradition.** A diamond's talismanic power was said to vanish if the stone was bought, and to work only as a gift (page 73).
- **1477.** The earliest written record of a diamond engagement ring dates from this year, when the future Holy Roman Emperor Maximilian had a diamond ring made for his betrothal to Mary of Burgundy.
- **1913.** **Tradition.** Kunz's April verse calls the diamond an "emblem of innocence" and ends "The diamond or the sapphire is her dower" (page 328).

## Sapphire, the Traditional April Birthstone

**The Stone.** [Sapphire](/stones/sapphire/) is gem corundum in any color except red, which is ruby. Its hardness is second only to diamond among natural gems, and with no cleavage it is superb for everyday wear. Heating has been used for decades to improve its color, and corundum has been grown in labs since the early 1900s, so many sapphires sold today are lab-grown. Colorless [white sapphire](/stones/white-sapphire/) is the classic diamond stand-in.

**The Story.**

- **Antiquity.** **Tradition.** Damigeron wrote that kings wore sapphire at the neck as a defense from harm, and that it kept off envy and drew divine favor (Kunz 1913, page 104).
- **12th century.** **Tradition.** Sapphire was coming to be seen as the stone most fitting for clergy rings (page 104).

## What April Birthstones Mean Today

**Modern Association.** GIA's birthstone exhibit ties the great age of diamonds to endurance. Diamond is the gem of the 60th and 75th anniversaries and, since that 1477 ring, the classic engagement stone.

Modern crystal guides tie diamond to abundance, love and clarity, and most place it on the crown chakra. Sapphire's guides favor the throat and third eye chakras, and in our rankings it is Widely Linked to focus and protection. Some sites list health benefits for both stones. Nothing in the mineral or medical sources behind this page supports them.

### What Is the April Birthstone Color?

Colorless, or "white" in jewelry talk, even though most mined diamonds are brown or yellow. Sapphire would make April blue.

April splits between [Aries](/zodiac/aries/), until about April 19, and [Taurus](/zodiac/taurus/). Kunz's zodiac verses give Aries the bloodstone and Taurus the sapphire (pages 345 to 346).

## How to Choose an April Birthstone

### Natural, Lab-Grown or Simulant?

- **Natural diamond.** Mined. The FTC's Jewelry Guides define a diamond as "a mineral consisting essentially of pure carbon crystallized in the isometric system."
- **Lab-grown diamond.** Grown in a press (HPHT) or a vacuum chamber (CVD) over days to weeks, with the same chemistry as a mined diamond and physical properties that match it very closely. The FTC expects "laboratory-grown," "laboratory-created" or a maker's name plus "created" right before the word diamond.
- **Simulant.** A look-alike of a different material, such as cubic zirconia or moissanite, which should be called "imitation" or "simulated." "Faux" is not enough.

### Which Stone Fits Your Day & Budget

| Colorless stone | What it is | Mohs hardness |
|---|---|---|
| Diamond, natural or lab-grown | Carbon | 10 |
| Moissanite | Lab-made simulant | 9¼ |
| White sapphire | Colorless corundum, natural or lab-grown | 9 |
| Cubic zirconia | Lab-made simulant | 8 to 8½ |
| White topaz | Colorless topaz, which cleaves easily | 8 |
| Clear quartz | Rock crystal | 7 |

- **An everyday or engagement ring.** Diamond or sapphire. Even a diamond can chip at its girdle, the thin outer rim, so a setting that protects the edge is worth asking for.
- **A first piece for an April baby.** A tiny diamond or white sapphire pendant to keep for later. Small diamonds cost less per carat than large ones of equal quality.
- **A gift on a budget.** White sapphire, white topaz or [clear quartz](/stones/clear-quartz/) give the colorless look honestly, if the label says what they are. Kunz grouped rock crystal and white sapphire with diamond among his gems of winter (page 326).
- **An inherited ring.** Have a jeweler check the prongs, and ask whether the stone could be fracture-filled before any repair or machine cleaning.

### What to Ask Before You Buy

1. **Is it natural or laboratory-grown?** Ask for that word on the receipt.
2. **Has it been treated?** Laser drilling is permanent. Fracture filling and coatings are not, and they need special care.
3. **Is there a grading report, and from which lab?** Check the report number on the lab's website.
4. **What are its 4Cs?** Color, clarity, cut and carat weight, GIA's grading system from the 1950s.
5. **Will you write all of it down?** The FTC calls it deceptive to hide a treatment that is not permanent, needs special care or significantly affects value.

### What Drives a Diamond's Price

Price follows the 4Cs. The most colorless stones fetch the most, inclusions, the tiny crystals and cracks inside a stone, lower value, cut decides the sparkle, and price per carat, a unit of one fifth of a gram, climbs with size, since large stones are rare. Treated diamonds may cost substantially less than untreated ones. When two look-alikes cost very differently, the paperwork usually explains why.

**Try This.** The old belief held that a diamond worked only as a gift. If you give one, write that on the card, add what the stone is, natural or lab-grown, and the moon phase of their birth night from our [Birthday Moon](/moon/birthday-moon/).

**Safety.** Small stones and loose earrings are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

## Is My April Birthstone Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone. A lab-grown diamond is not fake, but it is not natural either.

- **Natural, untreated.** A mined diamond with nothing done but cutting.
- **Natural, treated.** Laser drilling opens tiny holes so dark inclusions can be bleached. Fracture filling hides cracks with a glass-like filler that steam, acid and ultrasonic can damage. HPHT and irradiation change color and are stable. Coatings can wear off. GIA does not grade fracture-filled or coated diamonds.
- **Lab-grown.** Usually impossible to tell from mined by eye. Labs use instruments that reveal growth patterns.
- **Imitation.** Moissanite, cubic zirconia, white sapphire, white topaz, glass and lab materials called YAG and GGG.
- **Trade name.** A "[Herkimer diamond](/stones/herkimer-diamond/)" is double-pointed quartz from New York, and sellers must say so.

### Why Home Diamond Tests Fail

GIA says there are "no easy and reliable at-home tests." The fog test gives inconsistent results and treats mined and lab-grown diamonds the same. Sparkle proves nothing, since moissanite and cubic zirconia sparkle too. Never try the sandpaper or scratch test, which damages stones and settings. A handheld thermal tester can mistake moissanite for diamond.

### Checks That Do Help

1. **Find the paperwork.** A report from an independent lab names the stone as natural or lab-grown and lists treatments.
2. **Ask to see any inscription.** Labs laser-inscribe the girdle of some lab-grown and color-treated diamonds. The jeweler can show it to you under magnification.
3. **Look for doubling.** With a 10x loupe, an inexpensive jeweler's magnifier, look down through the top at the back facet edges. Moissanite is strongly doubly refractive, so they can look doubled. Diamond does not double. It is a hint, not proof.
4. **Ask for a lab.** A jeweler can send the stone to an independent lab and handle shipping and insurance.

For diamond jewelry, GIA suggests the soak below once or twice a week, and leaving ultrasonic machines to a jeweler, since they can shake stones loose.

### How to Clean a Diamond or Sapphire Ring at Home

1. Soak the ring briefly in a small bowl of warm water with a few drops of mild dish soap.
2. Brush under and around the stone with a soft toothbrush kept only for jewelry. The back of the stone collects the most oil and dirt. Go gently on old or delicate prongs.
3. Rinse with the drain closed, then dry with a soft, lint-free cloth.
4. Never use chlorine bleach, toothpaste or household cleansers.
5. Never put a fracture-filled or coated stone in an ultrasonic, and skip machines for sapphire until it has a rating.
6. Have a jeweler check the prongs and any loose stones from time to time.

### How to Store April Birthstones

1. Give each piece its own pouch or compartment. Diamond scratches every other gem, and sapphire scratches most others.
2. Take rings off for heavy work and the gym, where a knock on the girdle can chip even a diamond.

**Where the beliefs and the care data disagree.** Some crystal guides say diamond is fine in salt water, and others clean it with steam or ultrasonic. Our data has no rating yet for salt, soaking or machines, and steam and ultrasonic can ruin a fracture-filled stone, so check the report first. Moonlight or sound is the gentle choice.

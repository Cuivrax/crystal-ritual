# Pink Amethyst Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Pink amethyst is a pink quartz that grows as crystals inside geodes, first sold in 2017 from the El Choique mine in Patagonia, Argentina. Researchers who described it call it a new color variety of quartz. Crystal guides tie it to calm, compassion and gentle love, and those meanings are modern beliefs. The stone is hard and durable, but strong light and heat can fade amethyst.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Natural pink amethyst is glassy pink quartz, often as crystal points lining a geode. A hot, candy pink, or color that sits darkest in cracks, suggests dye. "Pink amethyst" on a faceted ring stone may mean pale violet amethyst instead. Ask where it was mined, and get the answer on the receipt.

## What Pink Amethyst Actually Is

**The Stone.** Pink amethyst is quartz, silicon dioxide, the same mineral as clear rock crystal and purple amethyst. Geodes from the El Choique mine in Argentina first appeared on the market in 2017 at a gem fair in Brazil. In 2020, Ana L. Rainoldi and three colleagues described them in *Mineralogical Record* as geodes lined with various minerals, most notably pink amethyst crystals.

As quartz, it rates 7 on the Mohs scale, the hardness scale from 1 for talc to 10 for diamond, and it does not break by cleavage, meaning it has no built-in planes where it splits.

### Is Pink Amethyst Real Amethyst?

It is real quartz and a natural stone, and amethyst is the name its describers chose. Strictly speaking, though, amethyst is quartz in colors from pale lilac to deep reddish purple, and other colors are simply colored varieties of quartz. So think of pink amethyst as the name of a new pink quartz from Argentina, not a shade of purple [amethyst](/stones/amethyst/).

> **Trade Name.** "Pink amethyst" names the Patagonian geode quartz. Rose de France is something else, the trade name for the lightest, pinkish violet amethyst, often faceted for jewelry, which the International Gem Society calls "a clever bit of marketing." Some sellers also say "lavender amethyst." "Lithia amethyst" is a wrong name for [kunzite](/stones/kunzite/), a pink spodumene named after George F. Kunz, whose lore books this page quotes.

### What Makes It Pink?

In ordinary amethyst, trace iron is built into the growing quartz, and gamma rays from radioactive minerals in the surrounding rock later act on that iron to produce purple. We found no reference source that explains what makes the El Choique crystals pink.

### Is Pink Amethyst Rare?

It is new rather than rare. The 2020 study expects the El Choique mine's substantial remaining reserves to keep yielding attractive specimens for years to come.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** Pink amethyst has no history of its own. Everything older belongs to purple amethyst, and some of it points the other way.

- **Ancient Greece.** **Tradition.** Amethyst was believed to prevent intoxication, and its Greek name means "not drunk."
- **1st century CE, Rome.** **Tradition.** Pliny the Elder was not convinced. "The falsehoods of the magicians would persuade us that these stones are preventive of inebriety," he wrote (*Natural History*, Book 37, chapter 40, Bostock and Riley translation, via Perseus). His translators note that his amethystos may have covered other stones besides quartz.
- **French verse, retold 1913.** **Tradition.** In a legend Kunz records, Bacchus's tigers chased a maiden named Amethyst, Diana turned her into a pure white stone, and Bacchus poured grape juice over it, giving the stone its violet hue (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, pages 58 to 59).
- **15th century.** **Tradition.** Amethyst was said to quicken the intelligence, control evil thoughts and sober not only drinkers but also "those over-excited by the love-passion" (Kunz 1913, page 58). The old amethyst cooled love down, while today's pink one is sold to open the heart.
- **St. Valentine.** **Tradition.** An engraved amethyst with a little Cupid was said to have been St. Valentine's ring, a story Kunz calls "somewhat doubtful" (1913, page 257).
- **2017 to today.** **Modern Association.** The Patagonian geodes reach the market, and crystal guides give the new pink stone a softer, heart-centered meaning.

One shop claims royalty once prized pink amethyst as an omen of luck and long life. For a stone first sold in 2017, that story has no source.

## What People Use Pink Amethyst for Today

### What Does Pink Amethyst Mean?

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal guides, pink amethyst means calm, compassion and gentle, self-directed love. It rates Classic for [anxiety](/crystals-for/anxiety/) in our ranking, Widely Linked with [love](/crystals-for/love/), and Often Linked with sleep, focus and grief. Guides put it on the heart chakra most often, with the crown and third eye after it, and the few that name a sign choose Aquarius or Pisces.

Pink stands for affection, and the amethyst name carries purple amethyst's calm reputation. Guides describe it as the softer, more feminine side of amethyst, one sorting purple for mental clarity and pink for emotional healing.

### Pink Amethyst Metaphysical Properties, Honestly

Guides also credit pink amethyst with sensing other people's dishonest intentions and absorbing bad energy, and one says it is not "self-cleansing" like purple amethyst. These are beliefs, and young ones.

Some pages go further, listing high blood pressure, stress-related gut trouble, headaches, and the heart, lungs and immune system. Mineralogy and medicine do not support those claims.

## How to Use Pink Amethyst in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** None of these routines depends on believing anything about the stone.

### The Worry Page

**Try This.** Before something that scares you, an exam, a job interview or a medical appointment, sit down for ten minutes. Write every worry about it on one page, as plainly as you can. Fold the page, set the pink amethyst on top and leave both on your desk.

### A Point on the Nightstand

**Try This.** Keep a small pink amethyst point or cluster on your nightstand, away from the window. When you turn off the lamp, hold it for three slow breaths and say one thing from today you handled better than you feared. Then put it back in the same spot.

### A Gift for a Hard Season

**Try This.** For a friend going through a breakup or a loss, give a small pink amethyst with a card. Write what it is (a pink quartz from Patagonia, first sold in 2017) and one care line, keep it out of the sun.

**Safety.** Small points, tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys, so keep them away from small children and pets. Keep pink amethyst out of drinking water, since soaking is Brief Only in our data and dyed fakes exist.

**What Research Says.** In two laboratory and two classroom experiments, students who did a short writing task about their worries right before a high-stakes exam scored better than students who did not, especially those who usually get anxious about tests ([Ramirez and Beilock, *Science*, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1199427)). The studies tested students and exams, the paper carries a published correction, and none of it involved stones. The writing is the researched part.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Pink amethyst is often chosen for anxiety, grief, heartbreak and sleep. A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. If anxiety, grief or poor sleep lasts or feels heavy, talk to a doctor, a therapist or a grief counselor. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My Pink Amethyst Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone.

- **Natural, untreated.** Patagonian pink amethyst described in the 2020 study is natural crystal in geodes.
- **Natural, treated.** Heat and irradiation can change the color of amethyst, and heat can turn it green, blue or yellow-orange, but no reference describes pink amethyst as a treatment product. Dyed quartz is the treatment to watch for, covered below.
- **Lab-grown.** Laboratories grow amethyst hydrothermally, and synthetic rough is sometimes sold as natural rough.
- **Imitation.** Crystal sellers warn that glass and dyed quartz are sold as pink amethyst.
- **Trade names.** Rose de France, lavender amethyst and lithia amethyst, covered above.

### Pink Amethyst Jewelry, What You Are Buying

The Patagonian material comes as geodes and crystal specimens. A faceted pink or lilac stone in a ring labeled "pink amethyst" may be pale amethyst sold as Rose de France, a perfectly good stone, but not the same one. Either way, amethyst's hardness of 7 and lack of cleavage make it a sound choice for rings. Ask the seller in writing whether it is Patagonian pink amethyst or Rose de France, and whether the color is natural and undyed.

Three checks you can do at home, with their limits.

1. **Look into the cracks.** With a 10x loupe, follow any fissures. In a dyed quartzite examined by GIA, the dye gathered in fissures and between grains, so color pooling in cracks is a warning sign. Even color does not prove the stone is undyed.
2. **Judge the pink.** Sellers say a vivid hot pink is a common sign of dye. This is a hint, not a test.
3. **Check the luster at an edge.** With the loupe, look for a tiny existing chip where two facets meet or where a cabochon's flat base meets its dome. Quartz looks glassy there, and purple chalcedony, sometimes improperly called amethyst, looks dull. Never chip a stone to test it.

Natural or lab-grown, and treated or not, are questions for a gem laboratory. The [Label Decoder](/tools/crystal-label-decoder/) translates names like "Rose de France" and "lithia amethyst."

## How to Care for Pink Amethyst

**Mineral Fact.** At hardness 7 with no cleavage, pink amethyst is durable. Its weak spots are light and heat, not knocks.

- **Water.** Brief Only. A quick clean with warm water, mild detergent and a soft brush. No soaking.
- **Sun.** Brief Only. Some amethyst may fade with prolonged exposure to intense light.
- **Heat.** Keep Away From Heat. Amethyst is sensitive to extreme heat and can crack or fade at high temperatures.
- **Cleaning.** Brief Only, by hand. No Ultrasonic or Steam, since inclusions can make amethyst shatter in an ultrasonic.
- **Chemicals and salt.** Not Enough Data, so keep both away.
- **Storage.** OK. Store it apart from harder gems, and take jewelry off for the gym or the garden.
- **Toxicity.** OK to handle. The silica hazard concerns dust from cutting and polishing.

### How to Clean a Pink Amethyst Geode or Cluster

1. **Dust it dry.** Use a soft, clean makeup brush to sweep between the crystal points. Most of the time this is all it needs, and if the geode holds other minerals, stop here, since our care data covers only the quartz.
2. **Wash only if it is grimy.** Warm water, one drop of mild detergent and a soft toothbrush, for a minute or two. No soaking.
3. **Rinse and dry.** Rinse in warm water and pat dry with a soft cloth, then let it finish drying on a towel, crystals up.
4. **Put it back in the shade.** Choose a shelf that direct sun never reaches.

### Does Pink Amethyst Fade in the Sun?

It can. Some amethyst fades with prolonged exposure to strong light, and sellers warn that the pink can fade in direct sunlight. Keep it out of the sun and away from radiators.

### Can Pink Amethyst Go in Salt Water?

No, not by our data. One shop suggests a 24-hour saltwater bath or a bed of Himalayan salt. Salt is Not Enough Data and soaking is Brief Only, and our care data wins over the guides. Sound keeps the stone dry, and so does moonlight on an indoor sill if you bring it in before the morning sun. Check your piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

| Mohs hardness | 7 | 7 | 7 | 6.5 to 7 |
| Typical form | Crystal points lining geodes | Massive and cloudy, crystals rare | Often faceted, the lightest pinkish violet | Blade-like crystals, pastel pink to violet |

### Pink Amethyst vs Rose Quartz

They are the same mineral and look different up close. [Rose quartz](/stones/rose-quartz/) is usually found as massive material with no crystal faces, and microscopic mineral inclusions, fibers close to dumortierite, give it its cloudy pink. Pink amethyst grows as distinct crystal points inside geodes. Cloudy and chunky, think rose quartz. Glassy points in a geode, think pink amethyst. See [amethyst vs rose quartz](/compare/amethyst-vs-rose-quartz/) for purple amethyst.

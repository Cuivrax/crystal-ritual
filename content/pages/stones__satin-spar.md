# Satin Spar Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Satin spar is the fibrous variety of gypsum, a soft white mineral with a silky glow, and it is the stone behind many wands, towers and lamps sold as "selenite." Crystal lovers give it selenite's meanings of cleansing and calm, and those are modern beliefs. Like all gypsum, it dissolves in water and dislikes heat.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Satin spar is white and silky, with fibers you can see along its length. If you can see through it like a windowpane, it is selenite, the clear crystal form of the same mineral. Bright, even color can mean dye, which is common in gypsum. Calcite looks similar but is harder and heavier.

## What Satin Spar Actually Is

**The Stone.** Gypsum is calcium sulfate with water built into its crystal structure, and it is the most common sulfate mineral. It is an evaporite, meaning it settles out of water as that water evaporates, so it turns up in layered sedimentary rock next to rock salt (halite), anhydrite and calcite. Gypsum comes in several named forms, and our [gypsum page](/stones/gypsum/) covers the whole family.

- **Selenite** is the clear, colorless crystal form. It grows as flat crystals that can be split into thin sheets.
- **Satin spar** is the fibrous form, white or pink, with a silky luster. It forms as thin layers in beds of rock gypsum and in certain shales.
- **Alabaster** is massive, fine-grained gypsum, the kind carved into bowls and figures.
- **Desert rose** is gypsum, or barite, grown into rose-like clusters around grains of sand.

"Satin spar" refers to the satiny luster of the fibers.

### Why Satin Spar Glows

The fibers lie side by side, like threads in a bundle. Light catches along them and comes back as one soft band that slides across the stone when you turn it. Gemologists call this chatoyancy, the "cat's eye" effect, and some satin spar cabochons show it very strongly. Some gypsum also glows under an ultraviolet lamp, most often bluish, orange-yellow, yellow, greenish or brownish, but not every piece does.

### Is Satin Spar the Same as Selenite?

Same mineral, different form. Selenite is the clear crystal and satin spar the fibrous one, so a white, silky wand labeled "selenite" is satin spar by the mineral books' definitions. The care is identical.

One crystal seller writes that the overwhelming majority of "selenite" wands, towers, palm stones and plates on the market are actually satin spar, and names Morocco as a dominant source of the white and orange pieces. Our [selenite vs satin spar comparison](/compare/selenite-vs-satin-spar/) goes through the naming in more detail.

### How to Tell Satin Spar From Selenite at Home

You need a lamp, a printed page and, if you have one, a 10x loupe (a small magnifier sold for jewelry).

1. **Look through it.** Hold the piece over a printed page. Selenite is transparent, so you read the letters through it. Satin spar is translucent to opaque and white.
2. **Tilt it under the lamp.** A band of light that glides across the surface points to satin spar. Selenite shows a pearly shine on its flat faces instead.
3. **Look along the side with the loupe.** Satin spar shows long, parallel fibers. Selenite shows no fibers, only clear crystal, sometimes crossed by straight cleavage lines (the planes where a mineral splits most easily).
4. **Do not peel it to check.** Selenite splits into thin sheets along that cleavage, and splitting damages any piece.

These checks tell you which form of gypsum you have, not whether the color is natural.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** The moon lore belongs to selenite. Satin spar's own story is about luck and tourism.

- **Ancient Greece.** Greeks used clear selenite crystals for temple windows long before glass was invented. The name selenite, from the Greek for moon, refers to the pearly sheen of those clear crystals.
- **1913, Niagara Falls.** **Tradition.** George Frederick Kunz described satin spar cut in England or Russia, set in brass or gilded brass and sold at Niagara as luck stones. The sales story was that the stone had been taken from beneath the Falls "at great peril." Kunz adds that small deposits of this gypsum occasionally occur under the Falls (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, pages 79 to 80).
- **Egypt, by 1913.** **Tradition.** The International Gem Society writes that egg-shaped satin spar cabochons from Egypt were sometimes called "Pharaoh's Eggs" and said to be lucky, a story it credits to Kunz, and that the Niagara tourist stones were sometimes called "Niagara spar."
- **Russia, around 1915.** **Tradition.** Egg-shaped rhodonite was a favorite Easter ornament in Russia. For those who could not afford one, Kunz wrote, "a yellow fibrous gypsum or satinspar cut into a similar form may be substituted" (*The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, 1915, page 285).
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Satin spar is sold under the selenite name, so it inherits selenite's modern meanings of cleansing, clarity and protection.

## What People Use Satin Spar for Today

### What Does Satin Spar Mean?

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal guides, selenite is a Classic stone for [protection](/crystals-for/protection/), Widely Linked with focus and Often Linked with calm in anxious moments. Guides most often place it on the crown chakra. Crystal sellers also describe it as a stone that cleanses other crystals and needs no cleansing itself, which is why satin spar "charging plates" and bowls sit under so many collections.

The "why" is the look and the name. A white stone that seems lit from inside reads as clean and bright, and the moon name adds a link to cycles and night. Some pages go further and list serious illnesses. Nothing in the mineral sources behind this page supports those claims.

### Does a Satin Spar Plate Cleanse Other Crystals?

That is a belief, and no mineral process stands behind it. Physically, a plate holds stones, and at Mohs 2 it is softer than almost everything you set on it. Quartz, at 7, scratches it easily. If you use one, lay a thin cloth on top first, and keep anything wet off it.

## How to Use Satin Spar in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** None of these depend on believing anything about the stone.

### The Lamp That Ends the Screen Day

**Try This.** If you own a satin spar lamp, make switching it on your cue to stop screens for the night. Lamp on, phone onto its charger in another room, then read or stretch by the soft light. Use a low-heat LED bulb, the cautious choice for a heat-sensitive stone, and switch it off when you leave the room.

### A One-Minute Sunday Dust

**Try This.** Keep a clean, soft makeup brush next to your tower or wand. Once a week, brush the dust out of the grooves along the fibers and use that minute to clear the shelf around it. It doubles as the care satin spar needs.

### A Luck Stone Gift With Its Real Story

**Try This.** For a friend who likes old curiosities, give a polished satin spar egg or palm stone with a card. Write what it is (fibrous gypsum), the Niagara luck stone story, and one care line, keep it dry.

**Safety.** Small polished stones are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets, and keep satin spar out of anything you drink, since it dissolves in water.

**What Research Says.** In a study of 96 volunteers who repeated one daily behavior in the same setting for 12 weeks, the time for the habit to become close to automatic ranged from 18 to 254 days, and missing a single day did not materially hurt (Lally and colleagues, *European Journal of Social Psychology*, 2010, [doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)). The volunteers rated themselves and chose simple eating, drinking or activity habits, and the study had nothing to do with stones. Pairing a cue like a lamp with a fixed time is our extrapolation.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Satin spar and selenite are often chosen for stress, anxiety and sleep, and some sellers suggest them for grief or illness. A stone can be a calming object to look at, but it is not care. If anxiety, low mood or poor sleep lasts, talk to a doctor or a therapist. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My Satin Spar Real?

- **Natural, untreated.** Satin spar is natural gypsum, cut and polished into wands, towers, eggs, bowls and lamps.
- **Natural, treated.** Gypsum takes dyes and coatings very easily. Dyeing and bleaching are common, and gypsum is occasionally heated to improve color or prepare it for dye. Shops sell orange pieces widely, see [orange selenite](/stones/orange-selenite/), so ask whether a strong color is natural.
- **Lab-grown.** Gypsum is widely made as an industrial by-product, but there is no known jewelry use for it.
- **Imitation.** No documented imitation targets satin spar. Gypsum itself has been used in assembled cabochons made to imitate rainbow moonstone.
- **Trade names.** "Selenite" is the most common label, and it names the wrong form of the same mineral. "Niagara spar" and "Pharaoh's Eggs" are old names for lucky satin spar pieces. And "satin spar" can occasionally mean fibrous calcite or aragonite.

### How to Spot Calcite or Dye

1. **Weigh it in your hand against a known piece.** Calcite is denser than gypsum, 2.71 to 2.94 against 2.30 to 2.33, though only a gemologist's scale measures it properly.
2. **Try the fingernail test only on raw rough, never on a polished piece.** A fingernail rates about 2 to 2.5 on the Mohs scale, so it leaves a mark on gypsum (2) but not on calcite (3). Use it only where a mark will not matter.
3. **Ask about strong color.** No home test proves a color natural.

Three questions for the seller. Is this satin spar or selenite? Is the color natural, dyed or coated? Where was it mined? The [Label Decoder](/tools/crystal-label-decoder/) turns shop names like these into what they usually mean.

## How to Care for Satin Spar

**Mineral Fact.** Gypsum rates 2 on the Mohs scale and has perfect cleavage, so it scratches and splits easily. It is best kept for display, not jewelry. If you do wear a satin spar bracelet, take it off before you wash your hands.

- **Water.** Brief only. Gypsum is water soluble.
- **Soak.** Brief only. In practice, skip soaks entirely.
- **Sun.** Not Enough Data, so the cautious choice is a shelf out of direct sun.
- **Heat.** Keep Away From Heat. Gypsum is very heat sensitive. Its name comes from a Greek word meaning "to cook" or "burned," a nod to how it is prepared for plaster.
- **Chemicals.** No Chemicals. Not even detergent.
- **Salt.** Not Enough Data. Skip it as the cautious choice, and salt water is out anyway because of the water.
- **Cleaning machines.** No Ultrasonic or Steam. Never use anything that relies on heat or vibration.
- **Storage.** Handle Gently. Give it its own shelf or pouch, away from harder stones.
- **Toxicity.** Not Enough Data. Keep it out of drinks and food.

### How to Clean Satin Spar at Home

1. **Brush it dry.** A soft, clean makeup brush lifts dust out of the fiber grooves. Do this first, every time.
2. **Dab, do not scrub.** For a smudge, dampen a soft cloth with warm water, no soap, and press it on the spot. Do not rub.
3. **Pat it dry at once.** Use a towel, then leave the piece on a dry towel for an hour, away from radiators and sunny windows.

A shelf piece needs the dry brush about once a week. A piece you handle daily may want the damp cloth once a month at most.

### Can Satin Spar Go in Water?

Only for seconds, because gypsum is water soluble. Keep it out of crystal water bottles and set it beside the jar in our [moon water guide](/rituals/moon-water/), never inside. More stones like it are in [crystals that can't go in water](/care/crystals-that-cant-go-in-water/), and our [selenite in water test](/lab/selenite-in-water-test/) will show what happens over a day.

### How to Cleanse Satin Spar Without Damaging It

Moonlight, sound and smoke never touch the stone, so they suit it best. Skip water, salt and sun. More in [how to cleanse crystals without damaging them](/care/how-to-cleanse-crystals/).

## Satin Spar vs Look-Alikes

- **Selenite.** The clear crystal form of gypsum. See [selenite](/stones/selenite/).
- **Alabaster.** Also gypsum, but massive and fine-grained with no fibers. The word is sometimes used for opalescent glass, and occasionally for banded aragonite.
- **Calcite.** Harder (3), denser, and not flexible. See [clear calcite](/stones/clear-calcite/).
- **Desert rose.** Rosettes of gypsum, or of barite, which is harder (3 to 3.5) and much heavier (4.3 to 4.6). See [desert rose selenite](/stones/desert-rose-selenite/).

## Price Guide

Gypsum is common and has many sources around the world, so satin spar is not a rare stone. We found no current US listing for it with a clear price and size, so this guide gives no figures. These are the things to compare.

- **Size and carving.** Fibrous gypsum can be carved into pieces of several pounds, so a large lamp, bowl or tower costs more for its size and carving.
- **Color.** Ask whether orange or pink is natural before paying extra for it.

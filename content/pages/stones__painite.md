# Painite Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Painite is a rare borate mineral of calcium, zirconium and aluminum, deep red to brownish orange, found only in Myanmar and rated 8 on the Mohs hardness scale. For half a century only a few crystals were known, and it was once considered the rarest mineral in the world. Its crystal meanings are modern seller claims, and no treated or lab-grown painite is known.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Real painite comes only from Myanmar, and cut stones are usually small and included. Under a 10x loupe, round bubbles and swirl marks point to glass. Ruby and red garnet can look the same and weigh about the same, so only a gem lab can confirm painite. Ask for the report before you pay a rare-gem price.

## What Painite Actually Is

**The Stone.** Painite is a real mineral species, not a trade name or a variety of something else. It is a borate, meaning its structure is built around small groups of boron and oxygen atoms, here combined with calcium, zirconium and aluminum. A 2005 team that included Caltech and American Museum of Natural History researchers wrote it as CaZrBAl₉O₁₈, the same atoms also written CaZrAl₉O₁₅(BO₃). You may still see an older formula without zirconium in some references.

It is hard, at 8 on the Mohs scale (the 1 to 10 scale of scratch resistance), and it has a bright, glassy luster. Gem-quality rough is usually heavily included and fractured, though, so cutters tend to make small, shallow stones, which costs them some brilliance.

### What Gives Painite Its Color?

Traces of chromium and vanadium control the red to brown color. The Kachin State crystals analyzed in 2005 carried nearly ten times less of these elements and were pale pink, while the classic crystals from Ongaing, near Mogok, are orange-red to brownish red.

Painite is also pleochroic, which means it shows different colors depending on the direction light travels through it. One direction looks deep ruby red, the other pale brownish orange. Under a loupe you may see tiny cavities in thin sheets and small six-sided flakes of phlogopite, a mica.

### Where Painite Comes From

Myanmar is the only known source. The type locality, the place where the mineral was first described, is Ongaing, a village near Mogok. Kyauk-Pyat-Thet in Mogok and Namyazeik in Kachin State have produced gem-quality material too. In Kachin State, about 190 miles (300 km) northwest of Mogok, a 2005 study found it as slightly water-worn crystals in gravels with spinel, corundum and zircon.

The Mogok and Kachin State deposits are both known for ruby and both come from the marbles of the Mogok Belt, and one Mogok specimen shows a painite crystal surrounded by dozens of rubies.

### Is Painite Still the Rarest Gem?

Not as a mineral, but good gems are still very rare. Arthur C. D. Pain, a British gemologist, found the first crystal near Mogok in the early 1950s, and it was named painite in 1957. For decades only two crystals were known, weighing 1.7 and 2.1 grams, and both are now at the Natural History Museum in London. A third crystal later turned up in a batch of gem rough at GIA.

Things changed in 2001, when an 11 gram (55 carat) crystal was found near Mogok and confirmed as painite at a Caltech laboratory. A faceted stone of about 2.5 carats was identified in Thailand soon after. Since then more than a thousand crystals and fragments have been recovered, but most of them cannot be faceted.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** Painite has no ancient lore, because nobody knew it existed before the 1950s, so any "ancient painite legend" is a modern invention. Its real story is the discovery history above, plus one museum twist. In 2007 a brown specimen at the Natural History Museum in London, first identified as tourmaline with rubies from Mogok, turned out to be painite.

## What People Use Painite for Today

**Modern Association.** One gem marketplace's guide says painite works on all seven chakras rather than a single one, and lists it as a zodiac stone for Pisces and Aquarius. The same guide credits it, like other red stones, with passion, vitality and motivation, gives brown-toned crystals a grounding role, and says painite is believed to help you leave old habits behind. It also suggests painite as an unusual stand-in for July's ruby or January's garnet, though the modern US birthstone list does not include it.

The "why" is mostly color, since red reads as passion in modern crystal guides. These are modern beliefs, not effects.

## How to Use Painite in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** Faceted painite is rare, and most recovered material cannot be cut, so the painite people actually own is usually a small rough crystal.

### A Specimen Box With Its Story

**Try This.** Keep a small rough painite in a padded box with a card inside the lid. Write where it was found, the year the mineral was named (1957) and the lab report number if you have one. The card keeps the facts with the stone if you ever sell it or pass it on.

### The Look-Twice Habit

**Try This.** The Natural History Museum's painite was once labeled tourmaline. Before you buy any rare stone online, write down the seller's claim, then check one fact yourself, the locality or the lab. Make it a rule for every purchase over an amount you set in advance.

**Safety.** Small crystals are a choking hazard. Keep them away from small children and pets.

## Is My Painite Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone.

- **Natural, untreated.** Presumably all of it, since no gem treatments are known.
- **Natural, treated.** None known.
- **Lab-grown.** No synthetic painite is known.
- **Imitation.** We found no lab report on a painite imitation. Dark red look-alikes are easy to find, though. Besides ruby and red garnets (see below), synthetic spinel can be made to look like ruby, strontium titanate is made in dark red and brown, and glass comes in any color.
- **Trade name.** None. "Painite" is the mineral's real name.

### How to Check a Painite at Home

1. **Check the origin.** Myanmar is the only known source. A "painite" said to come from anywhere else needs a very good explanation.
2. **Use a 10x loupe.** Glass usually shows round bubbles and swirl marks, and molded glass can have rounded facet edges or slightly hollow facets. Painite's own inclusions include sheets of tiny cavities and small mica flakes. A clean stone still proves nothing.
3. **Touch it.** Glass tends to feel warmer to the touch than most of the gems it imitates. Treat this as a hint, not a test.
4. **Compare size, clarity and price.** Because painite rough is so included and fractured, cut stones are usually small. A large, clean, bright faceted "painite" at a modest price deserves suspicion.

The limit is honest. Painite's refractive index differs from ruby's, and its double refraction and absorption spectrum separate it from garnets, but those readings need a refractometer and a spectroscope, which means a gemologist. Researchers also recognize it by its distinctive Raman spectrum, a laser test done in a lab.

### What to Ask the Seller

1. Which gem lab identified this stone as painite, and can I see the report?
2. Where in Myanmar was it found?
3. Will you write "natural painite" and the lab report number on the receipt?

## How to Care for Painite

**Mineral Fact.** At hardness 8, painite resists scratches well. Its inclusions and fractures are the weak point, making it vulnerable to knocks, to heat and to the vibration of mechanical cleaners. For a rare set stone, ask a gemologist to check it before choosing a setting or a cleaning method.

### How to Clean a Painite at Home

1. Mix warm water with a drop of mild dish soap.
2. Brush the stone gently with a soft toothbrush.
3. Rinse quickly in plain warm water and pat dry with a soft cloth.
4. Skip ultrasonic and steam cleaners, and keep it away from heat.

- **Water.** OK for a quick wash with mild soap. For long soaks, the cautious choice is No Soaking.
- **Knocks and storage.** Handle Gently. Store it in its own padded box, away from other stones.
- **Sun, salt and toxicity.** Not Enough Data. The cautious choice is no salt cleanse, no long days on a sunny sill, and never in anything you drink.

## Painite vs Look-Alikes

- **Ruby.** Similar color and density, and found in the same ruby deposits of Myanmar, but a different refractive index. See [ruby](/stones/ruby/).
- **Red garnets.** Almandine and [spessartine](/stones/spessartine/) overlap with painite in color and density, and a gemologist separates them by double refraction and spectrum. More in [types of garnet](/stones/family/garnet/).
- **Brown tourmaline.** The mix-up behind the Natural History Museum's relabeled specimen.

## Price Guide

Painite's price follows rarity, color, clarity and size. Eye-clean stones are scarce because the rough is so fractured, and most recovered material cannot be faceted at all. One gem marketplace says the best stones are deep red or vibrant garnet red, and that raw crystals too poor to cut sell for a tiny fraction of faceted prices.

For any faceted painite, the lab report is part of the value. Without one you may be paying a rare-gem price for a garnet or a ruby.

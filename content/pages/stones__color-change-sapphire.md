# Color Change Sapphire Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Color change sapphire is sapphire, the gem form of the mineral corundum, that looks blue to violet in daylight and turns purple to reddish purple under warm indoor light. Natural stones exist but usually change more gently. A bold shift, with blue and purple showing at the same time, is typical of lab-grown sapphire, which is made very inexpensively.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Look at it by a window, then under an incandescent bulb or a candle. A gentle shift from blue toward purple fits natural stones, and a loud one with both colors at once fits lab-grown sapphire. Only a gem lab can say natural or lab-grown for sure.

## What Color Change Sapphire Actually Is

**The Stone.** The name sapphire covers corundum of every color except red, which is ruby. Color change sapphire is not a separate mineral. GIA calls these stones "corundum's chameleons." The rest of the family is on our [sapphire page](/stones/sapphire/).

### How the Color Change Works

Daylight and lamplight are not the same mix of colors. Daylight is richer in blue and green, and an old-style incandescent bulb is richer in red. A color change sapphire lets two bands of light through, one in the blue-green and one in the red, and absorbs the yellow in between. In daylight the blue band wins. Under a lamp, the extra red pushes the stone toward purple.

Gemologists call this the alexandrite effect, after the gem that made it famous. Vanadium can cause color change in sapphire, and natural color change corundum also holds chromium, iron and titanium. How strong the change is depends on how much of these elements a stone holds. A 1982 review in *Gems & Gemology* found that sapphire from Thailand, with less chromium, changes only weakly, while blue sapphire from the Umba Valley in Tanzania, with more chromium, turns violetish red under a lamp.

### Where Natural Color Change Sapphire Comes From

The Umba Valley in Tanzania is a documented source, including a 1.33-carat stone that is violet-blue in daylight and flashes deep purple under incandescent light. The 1982 study also describes natural color change corundum from Thailand and Colombia, and a 9.54-carat stone said to come from Sri Lanka.

Alexandrite-like sapphire has been known since at least the 1950s, and the American Museum of Natural History put one on display in 1960.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** The lore of this stone turns on the lighting.

- **16th century, Ceylon (Sri Lanka).** **Tradition.** A purple-red sapphire was called nilacandi, which Kunz renders "sapphire-ruby." These stones were purple-red by day, but "artificial light kills the blue and they appear red." Kunz notes they were frequently called phenomenal sapphires or alexandrite sapphires (*The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, 1915, page 402).
- **18th century.** **Tradition.** A sapphire later shown at the South Kensington Museum in London was rich blue by day and violet by lamplight. The writer Mme. de Genlis made it the center of her story "Le Saphire Merveilleux," in which the stone tested a woman's faithfulness. To prove a woman innocent, she wore it for three hours of daylight. To condemn her, the test began in daylight and ended after the candles were lit. Kunz adds that the stone later belonged to the Duke of Orleans (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, pages 105 to 106). The verdict depended on the clock, not the wearer.
- **1830.** Alexandrite was found in Russia's Ural Mountains, and its green-to-red change later gave the effect its name.
- **1950s and 1960s.** Jewelry with lab-grown color change sapphire was popular in those decades, according to one antiques dealer.
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Calm, protection and focus, borrowed from sapphire.

## What People Use Color Change Sapphire for Today

### What Does Color Change Sapphire Mean?

**Modern Association.** Color change sapphire is usually given sapphire's modern meanings. Modern crystal guides Widely Link sapphire with calm in anxious moments, [protection](/crystals-for/protection/) and focus.

A stone that changes with its surroundings reads as adaptable, and older sapphire lore adds royalty and protection against fraud. Lab-grown color change corundum is also sold as an inexpensive look-alike for alexandrite, the June birthstone, while color change sapphire already counts as sapphire for [September](/birthstones/september/).

## How to Use Color Change Sapphire in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** The stone gives you two natural cues a day, daylight and lamplight.

### The Two-Light Check-In

**Try This.** Wear the ring or pendant all day. When you switch on the first lamp in the evening, look at the stone as its color shifts, and write one line in your phone about something that went better today than you expected. Then put the phone away. The color change is the reminder, the line is the habit.

### A Gift for Someone Starting Over

**Try This.** For a new job, a move or a 45th anniversary, give a small color change sapphire with a card. Write what it is (sapphire that shifts with the light), the Saphire Merveilleux story, and whether the stone is natural or lab-grown.

**Safety.** Small loose stones are a choking hazard. Keep them away from small children and pets.

**What Research Says.** In three randomized studies with 192 and 157 students and 65 adults with neuromuscular disease, people asked to list things they were grateful for reported better well-being than comparison groups, most clearly in positive mood (Emmons and McCullough, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2003, [doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.84.2.377](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.84.2.377)). The outcomes were self-reported, two samples were college students, and nobody used a stone. Tying the habit to a lamp is our extrapolation.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Like sapphire in general, color change sapphire is often chosen for stress and anxiety. A stone can be a pleasant object to look at, but it is not care. If anxiety lasts or feels heavy, talk to a doctor or a therapist. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My Color Change Sapphire Real?

- **Natural, untreated.** Natural color change sapphires exist. We found no gem reference that says how often they are treated, so ask, using the questions below.
- **Natural, treated.** Sapphire in general receives many treatments, including heating, lattice diffusion and glass filling. Under the FTC's Jewelry Guides, a seller must disclose a treatment that is not permanent, needs special care or significantly affects value.
- **Lab-grown.** Corundum was first grown in labs in the early 1900s by flame fusion, and the color change kind dates from the same period. High vanadium gives lab stones a change stronger than most natural sapphires show, often with warm purple and cool violet-blue visible at the same moment. A lab-grown stone has essentially the same properties as natural sapphire, but it must be sold with a word like "laboratory-grown," "laboratory-created" or "[maker]-created" right before the name, and never as "real," "natural" or "genuine."
- **Imitation.** Glass, plastic and other gems are used to imitate sapphire in general.
- **Trade names.** "Alexandrite sapphire" usually means a lab-grown sapphire with an alexandrite-like change. Lab-grown color change sapphire was also used in place of alexandrite and is sometimes mislabeled "synthetic alexandrite." Under section 23.25(c) of the FTC guides, a word like "synthetic" or "laboratory-created" may go with a stone's name only when the product has essentially the same optical, physical and chemical properties as that stone, and sapphire does not have alexandrite's. One antiques dealer tells the International Gem Society that the mix-up was helped along by Russian-made lab sapphire sold through Alexandria, Egypt, after World War II.

### How to Tell Natural From Lab-Grown

You need a window, a warm lamp or a candle, and a 10x loupe, a small jeweler's magnifier.

1. **Compare two lights.** Look at the stone by a window in daytime, then under an incandescent bulb. GIA describes the shift between daylight-type light (fluorescent or daylight LED) and incandescent light.
2. **Watch for both colors at once.** Bold purple and bold blue together point to lab-grown, a strong hint, not proof.
3. **Check the price against the claim.** Lab-grown color change corundum is made very inexpensively, so a low price on a stone sold as natural is a reason to ask for a lab report.
4. **Look for curved lines with the loupe.** Curved growth lines, called striae, occur only in lab-grown sapphires and rubies, never natural ones. They are hard to spot, and not every lab stone shows them.

Modern lab methods can copy natural colors and even inclusions so closely that only skilled professionals can tell. A lab uses tools such as a spectroscope, which shows a line at 4740 in lab-grown color change sapphire, and ultraviolet glow.

Five questions for the seller, with the answers on the receipt.

1. Is it natural or lab-grown?
2. Has it been heated, filled or diffusion-treated?
3. Is it sapphire, or alexandrite, garnet or spinel?
4. Can you show me a video of it in daylight and under incandescent light?
5. For a natural stone at a serious price, is there a lab report?

## How to Care for Color Change Sapphire

**Mineral Fact.** Sapphire is second only to diamond in hardness among natural gems and has no cleavage, which makes it superb for everyday wear. A heavily included or fractured stone is less stable. Our care data for sapphire covers storage only, so the rest below is GIA's published care advice for sapphire, with the cautious choice where it is silent.

- **Water.** Not Enough Data in our care data. Warm, soapy water is always safe.
- **Sun and heat.** Not Enough Data. Corundum is stable under normal wear, resistant to heat, light and common chemicals.
- **Chemicals.** Not Enough Data. Boric acid powder etches even untreated sapphire, and filled or dyed stones can be damaged by mild acids like lemon juice.
- **Salt.** Not Enough Data. Skip it.
- **Cleaning machines.** Ultrasonic and steam cleaners are usually safe for untreated, heated and lattice diffusion-treated stones. Fracture-filled, cavity-filled or dyed stones get a damp cloth only. If you do not know which you have, skip the machines.
- **Storage.** OK. At 9 it scratches softer stones, and diamond can scratch it, so give it its own pouch.
- **Toxicity.** Not Enough Data. Keep loose stones out of drinks.

### How to Clean a Color Change Sapphire Ring at Home

1. **Mix.** A bowl of warm water with a drop of mild dish soap.
2. **Brush.** Dip the ring and brush under the stone with a soft toothbrush, where lotion and dust collect.
3. **Rinse and dry.** Rinse under warm running water with the sink plugged, then dry with a lint-free cloth.
4. **Filled stone?** If your receipt says fracture-filled or you are not sure, wipe with a damp cloth instead.

Once a month suits a ring worn daily. Check the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/) for the stones you keep with it.

## Color Change Sapphire vs Look-Alikes

- **Alexandrite.** A different mineral, chrysoberyl, that turns from bluish green in daylight to red or purplish red under incandescent light. Natural alexandrite is rare and valuable. See [alexandrite](/stones/alexandrite/).
- **Color change garnet.** Chromium-rich garnets turn from bluish green in daylight to wine red or reddish violet under a lamp, and alexandrite-like garnets also come from the Umba Valley. See [color change garnet](/stones/color-change-garnet/).
- **Color change spinel.** Rare. The one described in 1982 went from violet-blue in daylight to red-violet in artificial light. See [spinel](/stones/spinel/).
- **Purple sapphire.** Purple is always its dominant color. See [purple sapphire](/stones/purple-sapphire/).

## Price Guide

The strength of the color change, judged as weak, moderate or strong, is the most important quality factor in a color change sapphire's value. The usual sapphire factors, color, clarity, cut and carat weight, come after it. Lab-grown color change corundum is produced very inexpensively, which is why the "natural or lab-grown" question comes before any other. We found no current US listing for color change sapphire with a clear price and size, so this guide gives no figures.

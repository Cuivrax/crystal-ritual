# Spessartine Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Spessartine is the manganese garnet, an orange to reddish orange member of the garnet family and one of the garnets behind the January birthstone. The International Gem Society knows of no gem treatments for it, and its most vivid orange form is sold as Mandarin garnet. Its links to confidence and creativity are modern beliefs, built mostly on its color.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** The International Gem Society knows of no gem treatments for spessartine, and GIA says most garnets are not treated, so the real question is whether a stone is spessartine at all. Color alone cannot name a garnet, and orange look-alikes include hessonite, the lab-made garnet YAG and even lab-made corundum. Naming the exact garnet blend takes a gem lab.

## What Spessartine Actually Is

**The Stone.** Spessartine is the manganese aluminum garnet, Mn₃Al₂(SiO₄)₃, one of the main species in the [garnet](/stones/garnet/) group ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/garnet/gem-overview), [Handbook of Mineralogy](https://www.handbookofmineralogy.org/pdfs/spessartine.pdf)). GIA's [January birthstone page](https://www.gia.edu/birthstones/january-birthstones) says it comes in "exciting oranges and yellows."

Like every garnet, it is never found pure. It forms a series with almandine, the iron garnet, and usually comes blended with almandine or pyrope ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/spessartite-garnet), Handbook of Mineralogy). The closer a stone is to pure spessartine, the lighter orange it looks. More almandine pushes the color toward red and red-brown and raises the refractive index (International Gem Society). Spessartine and andradite garnets have been found up to 95 percent pure ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/garnet-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)).

The name comes from the Spessart Mountains of Bavaria, Germany, its type locality (Handbook of Mineralogy, [Minerals.net](https://www.minerals.net/mineral/spessartine)). The IMA List of Minerals dates the species to 1832.

### Spessartine or Spessartite?

Both names are correct. The International Gem Society says British gemologists first favored "spessartite" and Americans "spessartine," and either is accepted today. One catch. "Spessartite" is also the name of a lamprophyre, an igneous rock named after the same mountains, so context matters.

### What Color Is Spessartine?

Orange is the signature, leaning toward yellow in some stones and toward red in others (GIA, International Gem Society), and the *Handbook of Mineralogy* adds red, yellowish brown, reddish brown, brown and black. GIA's [quality factors page](https://www.gia.edu/garnet-quality-factor) describes spessartine as "a variety of orange colors." Large stones are very rare and usually quite dark (International Gem Society), so a big, bright orange spessartine is uncommon.

### How Is Spessartine Formed?

**Mineral Fact.** Spessartine is common in granite pegmatites, granites and rhyolites. It also forms in skarns and in manganese-rich rocks altered next to igneous intrusions (Handbook of Mineralogy). [Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/garnet.shtml) sums it up as an orange garnet found as crystals in granite pegmatites. Crystals can grow large. A few Brazilian spessartines have weighed several pounds while staying transparent, and faceters have cut gems of more than 100 carats from Brazilian and Madagascar rough (International Gem Society).

### Where Spessartine Comes from

Namibia, Nigeria and Tanzania are major sources, including of Mandarin garnet (International Gem Society), and GIA names Namibia and Tanzania as key sources of rich orange-to-yellow spessartine. In the US, San Diego County, California, especially Ramona, has produced fine orange gems, and Amelia Court House in Virginia gives "fine, gemmy orange to deep brownish material" (International Gem Society). Brazil, Madagascar, Pakistan and China are on the list too (International Gem Society, Minerals.net).

### What Is Spessartine Used for?

Mostly as a gem. Minerals.net calls it the most widely used orange and reddish-orange garnet. With a hardness of 7 to 7.5 and no cleavage, it makes a durable stone for any jewelry, engagement rings included (International Gem Society). Crystal specimens, especially recent finds from Tanzania, are popular with collectors (Minerals.net). Industrial garnet abrasive is another story, since almandine is the garnet most used in industry (Geology.com).

### Mandarin, Malaya & Other Trade Names

**Trade Name.** Several names on garnet labels point to spessartine or to blends that contain it.

- **Mandarin garnet.** Spessartine with the highest spessartine content, 85 to 95 percent, and vivid orange colors (International Gem Society). Collectors seek it out.
- **Malaya or malaia garnet.** The name first described garnets that "didn't fit into standard categories." Gemologists now treat them as blends of pyrope and spessartine, in orange, red-orange, peach and pink (International Gem Society), and GIA calls malaia/malaya a trade name for orange pyrope-spessartine garnets. It originates in the Umba River Valley of Tanzania and Kenya (Minerals.net).
- **Kashmirine.** A market name for a rare spessartine from Azad Kashmir, Pakistan, slightly yellowish orange to red-orange. Clean gems over 1 carat are rarely seen (International Gem Society).
- **Color-change garnets.** Most are pyrope-spessartine blends (International Gem Society).

### What Is Flame Spessartine?

"Flame spessartine" is not a species, and the International Gem Society's spessartite article does not use the term. Read "flame," like other color words in a listing, as a seller's description of the color, not a separate kind of garnet, and ask which garnet it is.

## Where the Meaning Comes from

**The Story.** Spessartine has no ancient lore of its own. It was described as a species only in 1832, so its older story is garnet's story.

- **Before 1832.** **Tradition.** The old lore belongs to garnet in general, and mostly to red stones. The name "carbuncle," a glowing coal, covered rubies and "certain fine garnets" (Kunz, *The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, pages 292 to 293). A lion engraved on a garnet was said to guard its owner "from all perils in travelling" (pages 133 to 134). None of these stories names spessartine.
- **1832.** **Mineral Fact.** Spessartine is described as a species and named for the Spessart Mountains (IMA List of Minerals, Handbook of Mineralogy).
- **Late 1990s, China.** Spessartine is found in Tongbei and Yunling, in Zhangzhou Prefecture, often as small gemmy crystals coating smoky quartz (Minerals.net).
- **2008, Tanzania.** Bright orange crystals from Nani, near Loliondo in the Arusha Region, reach the market (Minerals.net), while the bright orange crystals that once came from Marienfluss in Namibia have become very hard to find.
- **Today.** Minerals.net says spessartine was once much rarer, until abundant finds in Tanzania, China and Pakistan "really put Spessartine on the map." Its modern meanings are just as new.

### Is Spessartine a January Birthstone?

Yes, as a garnet. Garnet is the only January stone on the [American Gem Society](https://www.americangemsociety.org/birthstones/) list, and GIA's January page lists spessartine among the garnet colors. Our [January birthstone guide](/birthstones/january/) covers the whole family, and [types of garnet](/stones/family/garnet/) sorts the species by color.

## What People Use Spessartine for Today

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal guides, spessartine is Widely Linked with confidence. Creativity, courage and motivation are close behind, and one guide offers it to anyone who struggles to be heard in a group. It is Often Linked with protection and only Sometimes Linked with love, focus, money or sleep. The sacral chakra is the link most guides make, with the solar plexus and root also named, and fire is the element they pick.

The "why" is mostly color. One guide says plainly that spessartine's meaning builds on garnet's heritage but takes its direction from its color, and notes that orange spessartine is more often linked with the sacral chakra while deep red garnets go to the root. The protection theme is the part with old roots, in garnet amulets like the lion for travelers.

These meanings are recent. One crystal encyclopedia credits its spessartine entry to modern crystal authors, among them Melody (author of *Love Is in the Earth*, 1991), Judy Hall (*The Crystal Bible*, 2003) and Robert Simmons and Naisha Ahsian (*The Book of Stones*, 2005). The zodiac is not settled. Guides split between Leo, Aquarius and other signs.

### Does Spessartine Have Healing Properties?

Not in any way that has been shown. Some sites say spessartine helps fertility, digestion, food intolerances or weight loss. No mineral or medical reference supports that, and one crystal guide says plainly that crystal practices are not a substitute for medical care.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** If you are dealing with fertility questions, digestive trouble or a food intolerance, a stone can be a comfort, but it is not care. Talk to a doctor, and do not delay or replace any treatment.

## How to Use Spessartine in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** None of these depends on believing anything about the stone. Each one uses it as a cue, something orange and solid that you see or touch at the same moment every day.

### The First Line

**Try This.** Crystal guides tie spessartine to creative drive, so give it a job at your desk. Make a plan in one sentence, "When I sit down to work, I pick up the stone and write one line before I open any messages." Then do exactly that, every day, in the same place. The stone marks the moment, the line is the habit.

### One Thing to Say

**Try This.** Before a meeting where you tend to stay quiet, decide on one point you will make. Put a small spessartine in your pocket and touch it when you make the point. That is all. One sentence spoken is the win.

### A Spot in the Sun

**Try This.** Garnets are stable to light (GIA), so unlike sun-shy stones, spessartine can sit in a sunny window. Set it where the morning light hits it, and make opening the blinds your cue to stand up, stretch and plan your first task of the day.

### A January Gift With a Note

**Try This.** For a January birthday, give spessartine as an orange take on the birthstone. Write on the card what it is (a natural manganese garnet, almost never treated) and that it is named for a German mountain range. A garnet that is not red makes a good surprise.

**Safety.** Small tumbled stones and loose gems are a choking hazard. Keep them away from small children and pets.

**What Research Says.** The first-line ritual is a habit, and habits take time. In a study of 96 volunteers who each picked one eating, drinking or activity behavior to repeat daily in the same setting for 12 weeks, the time to reach 95 percent of each person's plateau in automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days, and missing a single day did not materially hurt (Lally and colleagues, 2010, *European Journal of Social Psychology*, [doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)). It was a small volunteer study with self-rated automaticity and simple behaviors, the model fit only some people, and it tested no stones. Treat the rituals above as personal practices.

## Is My Spessartine Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone.

- **Natural, untreated.** The norm. The International Gem Society knows of no gem treatments for spessartine, and GIA says most garnets are not treated.
- **Natural, treated.** Rare for any garnet. GIA says some garnets might be fracture filled with a glass-like substance to improve their clarity, and such stones need special care.
- **Lab-grown.** Spessartine has been synthesized for research (International Gem Society). "Synthetic spessartite" jewelry online may be something else entirely (see Imitation).
- **Imitation.** YAG, a lab-made garnet, can be made in orange and reddish orange. Some online sellers also call colored cubic zirconia or synthetic corundum "synthetic spessartite," which is wrong, since those are different materials. In at least one case, lab-created corundum was sold as natural spessartine rough (International Gem Society).
- **Trade names.** Mandarin, malaya and Kashmirine are names for real garnets, though malaya can be more pyrope than spessartine.

Three checks you can do at home, with their limits.

1. **Look for inclusions.** GIA says orange garnets like spessartine and hessonite often have eye-visible inclusions. A large, flawless, cheap "spessartine" deserves questions. The limit is that clean spessartine does exist.
2. **Look at any break.** Spessartine has no cleavage (International Gem Society). A stone that splits along flat planes is probably something else.
3. **Ask for the species, in writing.** Gemologists cannot identify garnet by color alone (International Gem Society). Spessartine can be virtually indistinguishable from almandine where the two occur together, and grossular and andradite take complex methods to separate (Minerals.net). Even GIA's lab relies on chemical analysis so that unusual garnets "are not misidentified" ([Gems & Gemology](https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/spring-2018-labnotes-unusual-orange-pyrope-spessartine-grossular-garnet)).

Three questions for the seller. Which garnet species or blend is it? Has it been filled or treated in any way? Where was it mined? For a significant purchase, GIA's [garnet buyer's guide](https://www.gia.edu/garnet/buyers-guide) suggests an independent laboratory report.

## How to Care for Spessartine

**Mineral Fact.** Garnets have fair to good toughness, durable enough for all jewelry styles, but they should not take hard blows or rough wear. GIA puts spessartine on the harder side of the garnet range ([GIA care guide](https://www.gia.edu/garnet-care-cleaning)).

- **Water.** OK. Warm soapy water is always safe for cleaning garnets (GIA). Keep soaks brief.
- **Cleaning machines.** Brief only, because the sources differ. The International Gem Society says to avoid mechanical cleaning systems, since spessartine tends to contain inclusions. GIA says ultrasonic cleaners are usually safe for garnets except stones with fractures. Hand washing is the safe choice.
- **Sun.** OK. Garnets are stable to light (GIA).
- **Heat.** Brief only. GIA advises against steam cleaning and gives no other heat guidance.
- **Chemicals.** Brief only. Garnets are stable to chemicals but can be attacked by hydrofluoric acid (GIA).
- **Salt.** Unknown. We found no reference source on salt and spessartine.
- **Knocks and storage.** Brief only. Durable, but no hard blows.
- **Toxicity.** Unknown in our care data.

### Can Spessartine Go in Water or the Sun?

A wash, yes, and a sunny shelf, yes, both from GIA's garnet guidance. A long soak is a different matter, since GIA covers washing, not soaking, so our [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/) keeps soaks to Brief only. If your stone might be fracture filled, GIA says to clean it only with warm soapy water.

**Where the beliefs and the care data disagree.** Crystal guides suggest cleansing spessartine with sound, moonlight, smoke or a brief rinse, and one warns against long salt soaks for set jewelry. Salt is Unknown in our data, so leave it out. Sound and moonlight keep the stone dry.

## Spessartine vs Look-Alikes

- **Hessonite.** The "cinnamon stone," a grossular garnet ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/garnet-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). GIA groups it with spessartine as an orange garnet that often has eye-visible inclusions. Telling grossular from spessartine takes complex methods (Minerals.net). See [hessonite](/stones/hessonite/) and [grossular](/stones/grossular/).
- **Almandine.** The iron garnet. A redder color in a spessartine points to more almandine in the blend (International Gem Society). See [almandine](/stones/almandine/).
- **Pyrope.** Usually a deeper red (Minerals.net). Orange pyrope-spessartine blends are sold as malaya. See [pyrope](/stones/pyrope/).
- **YAG, cubic zirconia and synthetic corundum.** Lab-made materials that can be orange, sometimes mislabeled as spessartine (International Gem Society).

## Price Guide

What drives the price, from GIA and the International Gem Society.

- **Color.** Vivid orange is the prize. Mandarin garnets are "very much in demand" and can exceed other spessartines in price, and before they were found in the 1990s, bright orangish reds were the most valuable colors. Saturated reddish orange "aurora red" is prized too (International Gem Society). GIA calls color the most important quality factor for garnet ([GIA buyer's guide](https://www.gia.edu/garnet/buyers-guide)).
- **Clarity.** Most orange spessartines have eye-visible inclusions (International Gem Society, GIA), so clean stones stand out.
- **Size.** Large stones are very rare and usually quite dark, and clean Kashmirine over 1 carat is rarely seen (International Gem Society).
- **Cut.** Poorly cut garnets are much less marketable and sell at a discount ([GIA buyer's guide](https://www.gia.edu/garnet/buyers-guide)).
- **Treatment.** Not a factor in most cases, since spessartine is sold untreated.

Whatever the price, GIA says an independent laboratory report can confirm that the garnet is what the seller says it is.

# Orbicular Jasper Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Orbicular jasper is jasper patterned with round orbs, rings or "eyes," best known as red poppy jasper from Morgan Hill, California, and multicolored ocean jasper from Madagascar. Crystal lovers treat it as a calm, patient stone. The honest note is that much of what is sold under the name is translucent chalcedony or hardened volcanic rock, not textbook jasper.

## What Orbicular Jasper Actually Is

**The Stone.** Jasper is an opaque, solid or patterned form of quartz made of crystals far too small to see, colored by other minerals mixed in. Its names come from a color, a place or a pattern, and orbicular is a pattern name, like poppy and leopardskin. So orbicular jasper is not its own mineral, just jasper with circles in it.

Very fine-grained quartz is called chalcedony, and the [jasper family](/stones/jasper/) is its opaque, iron-stained form, so an orb stone that lets light through is closer to chalcedony.

### How the Orbs Form

The orbs usually begin in volcanic rock. When lava cools quickly, some of its glass turns crystalline in rounded masses of radiating needle-like crystals, often quartz or feldspar. Geologists call them spherulites, from Greek roots meaning "ball or orb" and "stone," and they range from microscopic to many inches (tens of centimeters) across.

Later, silica soaks through and hardens the rock, which is then called silicified. Cut open, each sphere shows as a circle, often with concentric bands that shift slightly in color. One gem dealer, looking at ocean jasper under a polarizing microscope, describes its orbs as fibrous chalcedony spherulites.

### Red Orbicular Jasper & Poppy Jasper

A 1957 US Geological Survey bulletin called the Morgan Hill district of Santa Clara County, California, one of the most famous jasper localities. "Here the jasper is bright red containing circular areas of white and gray, and therefore is known as orbicular jasper."

Locally it is Morgan Hill poppy jasper, the city's official stone and the namesake of the Poppy Jasper International Film Festival. The Morgan Hill Historical Society says it turns up in the hills and along Llagas Creek and is found only in the Morgan Hill area. If a tag says poppy jasper from elsewhere, ask what the seller means.

### Ocean Orbicular Jasper From Madagascar

Geologist Hobart King of Rocktumbler.com describes ocean jasper as a trade name for a silicified rhyolite, a silica-rich volcanic rock, from the northwest coast of Madagascar. He notes generally translucent orbs on a green, brown, yellow, cream, red or pink background, and small holes often lined with drusy quartz, a crust of tiny sparkling crystals. "Ocean jasper isn't a true 'jasper' because much of the material is translucent," he writes. He also found that most pieces glow to some degree under an ultraviolet mineral lamp, though a party "black light" may show nothing.

As dealers tell it, a slab owned by the German collector Richard Baldauf came with a 1927 receipt calling it Augenjaspis, German for "eye jasper." The outcrop was rediscovered in 1999, visible only at low tide, and the stone reached the Tucson Gem and Mineral Show in 2001.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** Orbicular jasper has no ancient lore of its own, and neither of George Frederick Kunz's books on gem lore mentions it. What it inherits is the long story of jasper.

- **The name.** **Tradition.** Jasper goes back through Latin to the Greek iaspis, often translated "spotted stone."
- **77 CE.** **Tradition.** Pliny the Elder wrote that "throughout all the East, it is the custom, it is said, to wear iaspis by way of amulet" (*Natural History*, Book 37, chapter 37). His iaspis was mostly green and often transparent, so it only partly matches modern jasper.
- **Fourth century.** **Tradition.** Jasper "had great repute in ancient times as a rain-bringer," praised in the Greek poem *Lithica* (Kunz, *The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, page 90). Ocean jasper's water meanings, by contrast, are modern.
- **Middle Ages.** **Tradition.** Bartholomaeus Anglicus wrote that jasper "hath as many vertues as dyvers coloures and veines." To old writers color mattered as much as chemistry, Kunz explains, so the many colors and markings of jaspers pointed to many uses (1913, page 91).
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Calm, patience and gentle cheer, much of it written for ocean jasper.

## What People Use Orbicular Jasper for Today

### What Does Orbicular Jasper Mean?

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal practice, orbicular jasper means calm, patience and gentle cheer. In modern crystal guides it is a Classic stone for [anxiety](/crystals-for/anxiety/), Widely Linked with [grounding](/crystals-for/grounding/) and confidence, and Often Linked with protection and focus. Ocean jasper is nicknamed the "stone of emotional peace," and guides place it on the heart, solar plexus and throat chakras.

The "why" is the pattern. Circles inside circles read as cycles, so guides suggest it for slow stretches like a long course or waiting on a job decision. Some see community in it, separate circles held together in one stone, which makes it a popular gift for groups. The ocean name adds water and going with the flow. Only one guide links it with Cancer, Pisces and Libra.

### Orbicular Jasper Metaphysical & Healing Properties

In crystal guides, "metaphysical properties" means beliefs about a stone's energy, not traits anyone can measure. Some pages promise cellular regeneration, immune support, detoxification or help with depression. No reliable evidence backs those claims, and one gem dealer labels its own meanings section "pseudo-scientific and based on cultural beliefs."

## Is My Orbicular Jasper Real?

- **Natural, untreated.** A natural rock whose orbs grew in it.
- **Natural, treated.** In jasper in general, dyeing is common and stable, and heating, which changes the color, is common, stable and undetectable. How often orbicular jasper is treated is not documented.
- **Lab-grown.** No lab-grown orbicular jasper is documented.
- **Imitation.** No documented imitation targets it.
- **Trade name.** "Ocean jasper" is Madagascar's trade name and "poppy jasper" Morgan Hill's local one. Both describe real natural rocks, and underneath either name the mineral is quartz.

### How to Tell Orbicular Jasper From Look-Alikes

1. **Read one orb with a 10x loupe.** Natural orbs show rings inside rings that shift in color, and Madagascar pieces often hold pockets of drusy quartz. The limit is that other orb rocks, such as Kambaba stone, share the look.
2. **Shine a flashlight through a thin edge.** In a dim room, press a phone flashlight to the edge. Textbook jasper stays dark, while ocean jasper's orbs often let light through. This tells you which name fits, not whether the stone is natural.
3. **Check the color against the place.** Red with white and gray points to Morgan Hill, greens, creams and pinks to Madagascar, a hint rather than proof. Electric blue or purple is a reason to ask about dye. Subtle dye needs a gemologist with a spectroscope or a Chelsea filter, a small color filter used in gem testing.

Three questions for the seller. Where was it mined? Has it been dyed or heated? Would you call it jasper, chalcedony or rhyolite, and why? The [Label Decoder](/tools/crystal-label-decoder/) turns names like ocean jasper into what they usually mean.

## How to Care for Orbicular Jasper

**Mineral Fact.** Jasper rates 7 on the Mohs scratch-hardness scale, where talc is 1 and quartz 7, and it is hardy enough for all jewelry uses. Most of our care data here is written for ocean jasper.

- **Water.** Brief only. A quick rinse is fine. Soaking is Brief only too, so no long soaks.
- **Sun.** Brief only. Avoid prolonged exposure.
- **Heat.** Not Enough Data. Heat changes jasper's color, so keep it away from candles, radiators and hot dashboards.
- **Chemicals.** Not Enough Data. Skip household cleaners, bleach and acids, the cautious choice.
- **Salt.** Not Enough Data. Skip it, the cautious choice.
- **Cleaning.** Brief only. Running water or a soft cloth.
- **Storage.** OK. Give it its own pouch, since at 7 it scratches soft stones such as calcite, which rates 3.
- **Toxicity.** OK to wear and handle. The standard gem toxicity table rates jasper Low, with silicosis from cutting dust as the hazard.

### How to Clean Orbicular Jasper at Home

1. **Dust it first.** A soft, dry brush, such as a clean makeup brush, lifts grit out of crystal pockets before anything rubs it in.
2. **Rinse briefly.** If it is grimy, hold it under lukewarm running water for a few seconds and wipe it with a soft cloth. A drop of mild dish soap helps on an undyed piece.
3. **Dry it at once.** Pat it with a towel, then leave it on the towel for an hour so water drains out of pockets and cracks.
4. **Skip the machines.** A dealer guide rules out ultrasonic cleaners for dyed jasper, and you may not know if yours is dyed.

Once a month is plenty for a shelf stone, and a daily bracelet needs only a dry wipe.

### Can Orbicular Jasper Go in Water?

Briefly, yes. One gem dealer says ocean jasper is generally safe in water for short periods, so our Care Checker rates water as Brief only. Raw or cracked pieces need extra care, because water sits in the gaps. Keep it out of drinking water and set it beside the glass, as in our [moon water guide](/rituals/moon-water/).

### Can Orbicular Jasper Go in the Sun?

Briefly. Sun is Brief only in our care data, and some collectors keep dyed pieces out of strong sun, since dye can fade. A morning on the sill fits the rating, a summer in the window does not.

### How to Cleanse Orbicular Jasper

Cleansing is a belief, not a mineral process. Moonlight, sound and smoke involve no water or salt, so they suit it best. More in [how to cleanse crystals without damaging them](/care/how-to-cleanse-crystals/), and check your piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Orbicular Jasper vs Look-Alikes

- **Ocean jasper.** Less a look-alike than the best-known orbicular jasper. More on the [ocean jasper page](/stones/ocean-jasper/).
- **Kambaba stone.** Green with dark, irregular orbs, a volcanic rock rather than a true jasper, according to one dealer. Dealers note it is often confused with ocean jasper from Kabamby despite looking very different. See [Kambaba jasper](/stones/kambaba-jasper/).
- **Leopardskin jasper.** Another spotted, orb-patterned stone with its own trade name. See [leopardskin jasper](/stones/leopardskin-jasper/).

## Price Guide

Jaspers are very common, so most of a piece's value lies in the saturation of its color, the beauty of its pattern and the skill of the cutting. Some types, Madagascar jasper among them, command premium prices because they are relatively rare. For orbicular jasper, pay for crisp orbs with strong contrast and an undyed stone, and ask where it was mined before paying extra for a famous name.

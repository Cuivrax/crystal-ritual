# Goddess Stone Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Goddess stone is a shop name, not a mineral, for natural stone lumps whose rounded curves recall a small female figure. Shops sell them as menalite, which sellers describe as a chalky, calcium-rich concretion, or as menilite, a gray to brown opal. The fertility and divine feminine meanings are modern beliefs.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** There is no lab definition of goddess stone to check against, so the useful question is what your piece is made of. Ask the seller whether it is menalite or menilite, since their care differs. Under a 10x loupe, a natural concretion often shows growth rings or layers. Only a lab can confirm the material.

## What Goddess Stone Actually Is

**The Stone.** We found no gem lab or mineral reference that defines "goddess stone." It names a shape, not a substance. Sellers say the curves recall the Stone Age figures nicknamed Venus figurines, such as the Venus of Willendorf.

### What a Concretion Is

A concretion forms when minerals dissolved in groundwater settle around a fragment buried in sediment, such as a leaf, shell, bone or fossil, and cement it into a hard lump. That cement makes the lump tougher than the rock around it, so when weather wears the softer rock away, the concretion appears and often breaks free.

Quebec's Abitibi lakes are a good example. The concretions found on the shores of lakes Malartic, Lamotte, Carpentier and Abitibi are mostly fine sand, muddy loam and cemented limestone, and several grew around plant remains such as a horsetail stem. The Abitibi mineral museum links them to the retreat of the glaciers, and each ring marks a stage of growth. They are known as fairy stones, and one seller lists fairy stone and goddess stone as names for the same kind of lump. The fairy stone name has its own tangle, since George Frederick Kunz used it in 1913 for staurolite crystal crosses from Patrick County, Virginia. Our [fairy stone page](/stones/fairy-stone/) covers both.

### Menalite or Menilite?

Two names, one letter apart, and sellers disagree about them.

- **Menilite** is a real opal name. It is an opaque gray to brown opal with a concretionary structure. Shops trace the name to Ménilmontant, a neighborhood of Paris.
- **Menalite** appears in no reference source we found. Sellers describe it as a chalky, cream, calcium-rich concretion, and one lists a Mohs hardness (the 1 to 10 scratch scale) of 3 to 3.5 while another lists 9. Both cannot be right. One gem shop suggests menalite is simply a misspelling of menilite, while another insists they are two different stones, and a third sells its goddess stones as menilite opal.

The practical upshot is that two very different materials travel under one name, with different care. More on the opal side in our guide to [types of opal](/stones/family/opal/).

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** No old text mentions goddess stones. The goddess name and the divine feminine meaning come from modern crystal shops and books. The closest old tradition, the eagle-stone, is a parallel, not this stone's history. It was another kind of concretion that people gave a womanly meaning, and Kunz traced it in *The Magic of Jewels and Charms* (1915, pages 173 to 178).

- **77 CE, Rome.** **Tradition.** Pliny the Elder wrote that the eagle-stone was found in eagles' nests and that its special virtue was to prevent miscarriage, because the stone "was as though pregnant, for when it was shaken another stone rattled within it, as though in a womb" (Kunz, page 173).
- **What it really was.** Kunz explains that eagle-stones are hollow ironstone concretions holding a loose kernel, which Italians called the *bambino*, or "babe." So they were neither menalite nor menilite. They turn up on every continent, many fine ones in Delaware, and measure 1 to 6 inches across.
- **1742, England.** **Tradition.** After earlier misfortunes, Martha Postlethwayte wrote to her daughter advising her to "wear the eagle-stone" (page 177).
- **Early 1900s, Italy.** **Tradition.** Eagle-stones were still called *pietre gravide*, "pregnant stones." Owners rented them out for the nine months of a pregnancy for five lire, with a pledge of 100 lire (page 178).

One seller also says Native American cultures near the Great Lakes saw menalite as a protective talisman, without naming a source.

## What People Use Goddess Stone for Today

**Modern Association.** Crystal sellers present goddess stone as a stone of the divine feminine and of the passages of a woman's life, with periods, fertility, pregnancy and menopause among them. They add divination, rebirth and a link to the "Earth Mother." Sellers place it on the sacral or the root chakra, and one adds Cancer as its sign.

Some sellers go much further, listing PMS, menopause symptoms, night sweats, fertility and help in labor. No evidence supports those claims.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Goddess stone is often chosen for fertility, pregnancy, periods and menopause. A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. Talk to a doctor, a gynecologist or a midwife about fertility, pregnancy, painful periods or menopause symptoms, and never let a stone delay that conversation.

## How to Use Goddess Stone in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** A stone shaped like a figure makes an easy marker for a habit you want to keep.

### A Marker for a New Chapter

**Try This.** At a turning point, a new job, a move, a milestone birthday, set the stone on a shelf you pass every morning. Write one line on a card about what you want this chapter to hold and tuck it under the stone. On the same date each month, read the card again and rewrite it if it changed.

### A Gift With an Honest Card

**Try This.** For a friend at a turning point, give the stone with a card that says what it is, menilite opal or a concretion, whichever the seller confirms.

**Safety.** Small stones are not toys. Keep them away from babies, small children and pets who might swallow them, and never put one in a crib.

## Is My Goddess Stone Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone.

- **Natural, untreated.** A concretion or a menilite nodule as it was found, perhaps cleaned.
- **Natural, treated.** We found no reference source on treatments for goddess stones.
- **Lab-grown.** None documented.
- **Imitation.** No documented imitation. A carved figure sold under the same name is a different thing from a natural lump, so ask.
- **Trade name.** Goddess stone, fairy stone and menalite are trade names. Menilite is a real opal name.

Three questions to ask the seller.

1. Is this menalite or menilite, and what is it made of?
2. Where was it found?
3. Is the shape natural, or was it carved or ground?

## How to Care for Goddess Stone

**Mineral Fact.** Because a goddess stone can be calcium carbonate or opal, the care below is the cautious choice that suits both. Calcite rates only 3 on the Mohs scale. Opal rates 5.5 to 6.5, is sensitive to moisture and changes in temperature, and chips more easily than most gems.

- **Water.** Keep Dry. A damp cloth for dust, never a soak.
- **Heat.** Keep Away From Heat. Opal is very sensitive to sudden temperature changes, which can craze its surface with fine cracks.
- **Chemicals.** No Chemicals. Calcite reacts with acid, so keep vinegar, lemon juice and bathroom cleaners away.
- **Salt.** No Salt. Not covered for either material, so leave it out.
- **Sun.** Not Enough Data. A shelf out of direct sun is the cautious choice.
- **Storage.** Handle Gently. Give it its own spot or pouch, away from harder stones that could scratch it.

### How to Clean a Goddess Stone at Home

1. **Dust it.** Use a soft, dry brush, such as a clean makeup brush, to lift dust out of the folds.
2. **Wipe it if needed.** Use a cloth barely damp with lukewarm water, then pat it dry at once.
3. **Skip the machines.** No ultrasonic or steam cleaner, and no soaking.

Once every few months is plenty for a shelf stone. Our [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/) does not list goddess stone yet. If you know your piece is calcite or opal, check that material there. For cleansing, methods that never touch the stone, such as moonlight or sound, suit it best.

## Price Guide

No reference source prices goddess stones, so we give no figures. Since the name sells a shape, two answers change what you are paying for, the material (a calcium carbonate concretion or menilite opal) and whether the shape is natural or carved. Ask both before paying extra for the shape, and get the seller's answers on the receipt.

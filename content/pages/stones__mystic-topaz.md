# Mystic Topaz Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Mystic topaz is natural colorless topaz with a metal film, most often titanium, deposited on the back of the cut stone so it flashes rainbow colors. The color is a coating and must be disclosed. Its meaning is modern and borrowed from topaz, and the coating, not the stone, sets the care rules.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Tilt the stone under a lamp. The rainbow should slide and change as you move it. With a loupe, look at the back facets for scratches or worn spots, where plain colorless stone shows through. Then ask the seller one question, topaz or quartz underneath? ## What the Name Actually Covers

**Trade Name.** "Mystic topaz" is a trade name, not a gem variety. The [International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/topaz-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/) uses it for surface-treated topaz showing multiple colors, and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topaz) describes it as colorless topaz "artificially coated via a vapor deposition process" [TO CHECK upstream source]. The topaz is real. The rainbow is added.

The same product goes by other names. Skyjems, a gem dealer with an online encyclopedia, lists Mystic Fire, Rainbow topaz and Caribbean topaz, depending on the producer and color balance ([Skyjems](https://skyjems.ca/pages/encyclopedia-mystic-topaz)). The International Gem Society's [treatments cheat sheet](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/common-gemstone-treatments-cheat-sheet-2/) says metallic coatings create both mystic and azotic topaz, and Mystic Quartz is the same coating on colorless quartz (Skyjems).

On our five-step ladder, mystic topaz is **Natural, treated (coated)**, sold under a **Trade name**. Disclosure of the coating is required under AGTA, CIBJO and FTC guidelines, Skyjems writes, and a buyer should never be told the color is natural. Honest sellers say "coated topaz" or give the trade name with that disclosure.

## What Mystic Topaz Actually Is

**The Stone.** Under the film is ordinary topaz, a silicate of aluminum and fluorine. It rates 8 on the Mohs scale and has a specific gravity of 3.4 to 3.6, says [Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/topaz.shtml), and most topaz crystals are colorless. Brazil is the principal source of gem-quality topaz (International Gem Society). Our sources do not say where the colorless rough for mystic topaz is mined or where it is coated [TO CHECK].

Colorless topaz is cheap, and treatment is what sells it. Most topaz in commercial jewelry has been heated, irradiated or coated, Geology.com notes, and the most affordable, most often bought topaz is the blue and mystic material that got its color from treatment.

### How the Coating Is Made

The topaz is faceted first, usually as an oval, round, cushion or pear, and only then coated. In a vacuum chamber, titanium is sputtered onto the back facets, called the pavilion, and some producers use other metals such as gold or chromium. The film is nanometers to a few hundred nanometers thick (Skyjems). Light enters through the untreated top, bounces off the coated back and comes out tinted with rainbow color.

Sources do not describe the process the same way. The International Gem Society calls it chemical vapor deposition, Skyjems describes physical vapor deposition, and Geology.com says some topaz is heated and then coated with a metallic oxide. GemSelect, another dealer, simply calls it thin-film deposition, the same family of technology used for lens coatings and computer chips ([GemSelect](https://www.gemselect.com/english/help/faq/mystic-topaz-gems.php)). For you as a buyer, the details matter less than the result, a real stone wearing a very thin metal skin.

One dealer encyclopedia credits the coating to a company called Azotic and dates the gem to about 1998 [TO CHECK with a reference source].

### Why the Colors Shift

The colors are thin-film interference. Light reflects from the outer surface of the film and from the topaz below, and the two reflections interfere to make visible colors that depend on film thickness and viewing angle (Skyjems). It is the same kind of color you see on a soap bubble. Tilt the stone and the color slides from green through blue, violet, magenta and orange.

Some producers coat the top facets too. Those stones look more even, but the coating there faces every knock and scrape.

### Mystic Topaz vs Mystic Quartz

Same coating, different stone. Topaz is harder, Mohs 8 against 7 for quartz, and heavier, with a specific gravity of 3.4 to 3.6 against 2.6 to 2.7 ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/quartz.shtml)). In a pendant the coating is what you see either way. A gemologist confirms the base with refractive index, specific gravity and a look at the inclusions (Skyjems), so the seller's label is your best guide.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** There is no old lore for mystic topaz, because it only reached the market in the late 1990s. The crystal guides we read say so plainly and borrow the meaning of topaz in general. That older meaning comes with a catch, because "topaz" did not always mean topaz.

- **Ancient world.** **Tradition.** Pliny told of an island in the Red Sea called Topazos, from a Greek word for "to conjecture," because it was hard to find. His topazius was greenish and usually what we call peridot (Kunz, *The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, page 291). The International Gem Society adds that before the 20th century, all yellow, brown and orange transparent gems were called topaz ([Topaz Symbolism and Legends](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/history-legend-topaz-gems-yore/)).
- **13th century.** **Tradition.** A falcon engraved on a topaz was said to win the goodwill of kings, princes and magnates (Kunz 1913, pages 133 to 134). The International Gem Society also records a popular belief that topaz could make its wearer invisible.
- **Middle Ages.** **Tradition.** St. Hildegard recommended leaving a topaz in wine for three days and nights and rubbing the eyes with it for dim vision (Kunz 1913, page 389). History, not advice.
- **15th century, Rome.** **Tradition.** A physician was said to cure plague sores by touching them with a topaz that had belonged to two popes. Kunz suspects the patients' faith "may really have helped" (1913, pages 389 to 390).
- **13th century, India.** **Tradition.** The Hindu physician Naharari wrote that anyone who wears topaz "will be assured of long life, beauty and intelligence" (Kunz, *The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, 1915, page 158). Hindu astrology ties topaz to Jupiter (International Gem Society).
- **Late 1990s.** Coated "mystic" topaz reaches the market (Skyjems).
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Guides keep the topaz themes and add what the rainbow suggests, as the next section shows.

## What People Use Mystic Topaz for Today

**Modern Association.** One crystal guide says mystic topaz meaning "centers on joy, clarity and optimism" ([crystalgemstones.net](https://crystalgemstones.net/mystic-topaz-meaning-and-spiritual-properties.html)). An Australian jeweler's blog lists balance, creativity, intuition and manifestation ([Shiels](https://www.shiels.com.au/a/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-mystic-topaz)). Chakra links vary, with crown and third eye in one guide and all chakras in another, "precisely because it shows the whole spectrum."

The "why" is mostly the rainbow. One guide calls it a stone "for seeing one situation from many angles," because the color changes with your point of view. The same guide reads the rainbow as an old symbol of hope after a storm, and the many colors as embracing every side of who you are. Artists and writers come up in more than one guide, and GemSelect suggests the name itself may come from the "deep, mysterious" color shifts.

Practitioners do not agree on the coating. Some feel a treated stone carries less of the earth, the first guide notes, while others are happy to inherit the topaz meaning. Mystic topaz is not on any official birthstone list, though that guide says it is often given as a November gift.

A few associations are marginal, like specific star signs linked through Jupiter. The jeweler's blog also repeats old claims about respiratory problems, then states that healing properties are not scientifically proven. Nothing in the mineral sources behind this page supports them either.

## How to Use Mystic Topaz in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** Whatever you believe about the stone, here is what the ritual does for sure. It turns a stone that changes as you move into a reason to look at one problem from more than one side.

### The Three-Angle Decision

**Try This.** Stuck on a choice? Hold your pendant or ring under a lamp and tilt it until one color takes over. Name one way of seeing the problem. Tilt again for a second color and a second view, then a third. Write the three down and only then decide. One guide suggests nearly this exact practice, and it works with or without belief.

### A Creative Cue on the Desk

**Try This.** Set a loose stone in a small dish where you write, draw or plan. When you catch yourself playing it safe, tilt the dish and jot one idea you would normally rule out. Leave it in the dish, not in a pocket with keys, which scratch the coating.

**Try This.** Topaz is the [November birthstone](/birthstones/november/) on the American Gem Society's list. A mystic topaz makes an honest, affordable gift if the card tells the truth. Write what it is (real topaz with a rainbow coating, made since the late 1990s) and one line of lore, like Pliny's island that was hard to find.

**Safety.** Loose stones and small jewelry are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

## Is My Mystic Topaz Real?

Here "real" means honest. Is it coated topaz, as the tag says, or coated quartz, or something sold as naturally rainbow-colored?

- **Natural, untreated.** Not possible under this name. Without the coating it is colorless, or white, topaz.
- **Natural, treated.** This is mystic topaz, natural topaz with a metallic coating. Some topaz is also heated before coating (Geology.com).
- **Lab-grown.** Topaz has been synthesized in labs but is not usually commercially available (International Gem Society), so the base is almost certainly natural topaz or quartz.
- **Imitation.** Mystic Quartz is the same coating on quartz, fine if labeled. [TO CHECK whether coated glass is sold as mystic topaz.]
- **Trade names.** Mystic, Mystic Fire, Rainbow topaz, Caribbean topaz and azotic topaz all describe coated stones.

### Is Mystic Topaz Natural?

The topaz is natural, the rainbow is not. Skyjems is blunt that a buyer should not be told a mystic topaz is naturally colored, and no source behind this page describes topaz that grows with this coloring.

Three checks you can do at home, with their limits.

1. **Tilt it under magnification.** The coating shows interference colors that respond to the viewing angle (Skyjems). This confirms a coating, not that topaz is under it.
2. **Check the back facets.** The International Gem Society advises checking the pavilion for scratches in the coating, and worn spots reveal the colorless stone underneath (Skyjems). A new stone may show no wear at all.
3. **Leave immersion to a gemologist.** The International Gem Society says the coating can be detected by immersion. Gemologists do it with special liquids and equipment, so it is not a kitchen test.

Mystic topaz is not normally sent for lab origin or treatment reports beyond coating disclosure (Skyjems). Three questions to ask the seller. Is the base topaz or quartz? Is the coating on the back only, or on the top too? How should I clean it, and can it be re-coated if scratched? ## How to Care for Mystic Topaz

The care rules come from two facts. Topaz has perfect cleavage, so a hard knock or heat can split it, and the color is a film only nanometers thick.

**Mineral Fact.** Topaz rates 8, but because of its perfect cleavage the International Gem Society warns that both vibrations and heat could cause it to split. The coating is permanent under careful wear but not as robust as the topaz beneath, according to Skyjems. GemSelect calls the film hard and durable on the back, while the International Gem Society says the coating "can easily be scratched" and Geology.com says these coatings can wear through in normal wear. We follow the cautious side.

- **Water.** OK. Mild soap, warm water and a soft brush (Skyjems and the International Gem Society). Keep soaks brief, since our sources cover washing, not long soaks.
- **Ultrasonic and steam.** Avoid. Both can damage the coating, and vibration and heat can split topaz.
- **Heat.** Avoid. Keep it away from heat and from a jeweler's torch.
- **Chemicals.** Avoid. Strong chemicals can damage or remove the coating (Skyjems). One crystal guide adds jewelry dips and household cleaners to the list.
- **Sun.** Unknown. None of our sources covers sunlight for mystic topaz.
- **Salt.** Unknown. One crystal guide warns against burying it in salt or soil, since the grit can scratch the surface.
- **Scratches.** Brief only. A scratched coating cannot be repaired in the field and must be re-coated by the producer or a specialist (Skyjems).
- **Storage.** Give it a soft pouch of its own, away from harder stones and metal.
- **Wearing it.** Take it off for gardening, cleaning, the gym and swimming, as one crystal guide advises. For a ring worn daily, GemSelect suggests a protective setting.
- **Repairs.** Never repolish or re-cut it, which removes the coating. Setters must avoid burnishing the coated surface (Skyjems), so tell your jeweler what it is.
- **Toxicity.** OK to wear and handle. The International Gem Society [toxicity table](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/gemstone-care-guide/) lists no known risk for topaz.

### Can Mystic Topaz Get Wet?

A quick wash, yes. A soak, a salt bowl or a dip in cleaner, no. Warm water with a little mild soap and a soft brush is the method every source agrees on. If you make moon water, set the stone beside the jar. ## Mystic Topaz vs Look-Alikes

- **Mystic quartz.** The same coating on quartz, softer and lighter (see above).
- **Aqua aura and other aura quartz.** Quartz with a vapor-deposited metal film too, covered on our [aqua aura quartz page](/stones/aqua-aura-quartz/).
- **Blue topaz, including London blue.** Treated color that runs through the whole stone, from irradiation and heating, not a surface film (Geology.com). See [London blue topaz](/stones/london-blue-topaz/).
- **Coated pink topaz.** Colorless topaz heated and coated with a metallic oxide to look pink, and Geology.com notes the coating can wear thin where it rubs.
- **White topaz.** The uncoated starting material, colorless topaz.

## Price Guide

### How Much Is Mystic Topaz Worth?

Not much, and that is part of its appeal. Retail prices are typically under fifty US dollars per carat for one-carat stones and rise moderately with size, according to Skyjems, which calls it affordable fashion jewelry rather than fine jewelry. Geology.com notes that most topaz in department and mall jewelry stores at low to moderate prices has been treated in a laboratory.

What you pay for is the setting, the cut and the look, not rarity. If a seller prices mystic topaz like a rare natural gem, ask why. If a scratched stone needs re-coating, ask a jeweler about the cost before you assume it can be done.

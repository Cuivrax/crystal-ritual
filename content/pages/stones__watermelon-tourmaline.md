# Watermelon Tourmaline Meaning, Properties & Real-Life Uses

Watermelon tourmaline is a single tourmaline crystal that grew pink or red at its core and green around the outside, so a thin slice looks like the fruit. Modern crystal guides tie it to the heart, love and the meeting of opposites. In 1915 it was called the Peace Stone. It rates 7 to 7.5 on the Mohs scale.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Look at where the colors meet. Natural zones usually blend, though some real stones carry a crisp colorless ring. A slice that is flawless and cheap deserves a second look, since most natural pieces show inclusions. Glass and plastic fakes exist. Only a gem lab can confirm a valuable piece.

## What Watermelon Tourmaline Actually Is

**The Stone.** "Watermelon tourmaline is pink in the center and green around the outside," says the [Gemological Institute of America](https://www.gia.edu/tourmaline-description), and crystals are typically cut in slices to show it off. Some have a colorless ring between the two colors, according to the [International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/tourmaline-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/). It is a pattern, not a species of its own.

Tourmaline is a supergroup of more than 30 species with the same trigonal structure and wildly different chemistry. In 1890 John Ruskin said its chemistry was "more like a medieval doctor's prescription than the making of a respectable mineral" (International Gem Society). Most gem tourmaline is elbaite, rich in sodium, lithium and aluminum (GIA), and watermelon stones usually are too, according to the dealer guide at [Gem Rock Auctions](https://www.gemrockauctions.com/learn/a-z-of-gemstones/watermelon-tourmaline).

### How a Crystal Grows Two Colors

The colors come from trace elements. GIA says iron, and possibly titanium, gives tourmaline its green and blue, while manganese produces pinks and reds. A watermelon crystal changed its recipe while it grew, starting pink and finishing green, so each slice is a record of that change, as Gem Rock Auctions puts it.

Most of this happens in granite pegmatites, rare igneous rocks with scattered open pockets. GIA notes that different pockets in one pegmatite can hold tourmaline of very different colors. The most spectacular crystals form when hot water and vapor carry the right elements into those spaces ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/tourmaline.shtml)).

Tourmaline can also be zoned along its length instead of from the center out. Those stones are sold as bi-color, tri-color or parti-color (International Gem Society). A pink rind around a green core also exists, and dealers call it reverse watermelon (Gem Rock Auctions).

### Where It Comes From

Brazil has been the world's leading tourmaline source for nearly 500 years, and its Minas Gerais pegmatites have produced much bicolor material (Geology.com). The American chapter is older than you might think. The first commercial gemstone mine in the United States followed an 1821 tourmaline find near Paris, Maine, and southern California later became the country's most important source (Geology.com). Gem Rock Auctions adds Afghanistan, Madagascar, Namibia, Nigeria and others to the list.

### Is Watermelon Tourmaline Rare?

It depends on quality. Tourmaline in general is abundant and affordable (International Gem Society). George Frederick Kunz called the watermelon kind "rare" but "not especially costly" in 1915 (*The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, page 59). Today Gem Rock Auctions says quality material is rare and that prices have climbed as mines are depleted. Gems over 4 carats are scarce, the same dealer notes.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** Watermelon tourmaline has no ancient lore, because tourmaline was not recognized as its own mineral until 1793 (Geology.com). Its story is modern, and some of it is surprisingly scientific.

- **1500s, Brazil.** Portuguese explorers sent green and blue tourmaline home thinking it was emerald and sapphire (Geology.com).
- **Holland.** **Tradition.** Kunz tells of Dutch jewelers who put tourmaline in the fire and saw it attract and repel the ashes. Lapidaries nicknamed it Aschentrekker, the "Ash-Attractor." The name tourmaline comes from turmali, used in Ceylon (1915, pages 52 to 56). That trick is the pyroelectric effect, a real property (International Gem Society).
- **1759.** **Tradition.** Benjamin Franklin wore a tourmaline in a ring on a pivot, and noted that the warmth of his finger was enough to charge it (Kunz 1915, pages 57 to 58).
- **1910 and 1911.** The name "watermelon tourmaline" was coined by the naturalist George Robley Howe of Norway, Maine, and appeared in a local paper in 1910, according to Gem Rock Auctions.
- **1915.** **Tradition.** Kunz calls the green and red crystal with a white zone between them the Peace Stone, "sometimes called the 'Watermelon Stone,'" found in Paris, Maine, San Diego County and Brazil. He counts it among the stones of sentiment, a symbol of "universal peace" (1915, pages 58 to 59). He even explains the white band. Manganese makes the red, iron makes the green, and where they meet they cancel out. GIA's modern account names the same two elements.
- **1915, fiction.** **Tradition.** Kunz retells "My Tourmaline," a story by Saxe Holme (Helen Hunt Jackson) about a Mount Mica crystal, red at one end, green at the other, with a white band, that comforts a girl laid up with a sprained leg (1915, pages 51 to 52). A tale, not a test, and an early sign of the gentle reputation.
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Crystal guides read the pink core as love and tenderness and the green rind as growth, and the meeting of the two as harmony between differences ([Crystal Gemstones](https://crystalgemstones.net/watermelon-tourmaline-meaning-and-spiritual-properties.html)). Kunz's Peace Stone said much the same thing a century earlier.

## What People Use Watermelon Tourmaline for Today

**Modern Association.** Modern crystal guides place watermelon tourmaline on the heart chakra, a fit one guide traces to chakra color symbolism (Crystal Gemstones). Love and courage come up most, with compassion, joy and confidence close behind. A German encyclopedia site adds understanding, joy and a feeling of being safe ([Edelsteine.net](https://www.edelsteine.net/wassermelonen-turmalin/)).

Real-life uses in the guides are gentle. People give it between partners and close friends, or keep it close during a stretch when they want to treat themselves more kindly (Crystal Gemstones). For [love](/crystals-for/love/), one guide pairs it with [pink tourmaline](/stones/pink-tourmaline/) to double the tenderness (Crystal Gemstones).

Zodiac links are thin. Gemini, the sign of the twins, is the one that repeats, fitting for a two-colored stone. Gem Rock Auctions adds Virgo, plus Saturn and Venus, and one shop lists Libra, Chinese zodiac signs and the number 2. Treat those as marginal.

Some sites go much further. One dealer lists immune support, metabolism and pain relief, and one shop calls it a talisman for serious heart and nervous system conditions. Nothing in the mineral or medical sources behind this page supports those claims. One crystal guide states that it is not a substitute for medical or mental health care, and we agree.

## How to Use Watermelon Tourmaline in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** A slice is easy to read at a glance, two colors and a line where they meet. That makes it a good cue for small moments of choosing.

### The Peace Stone on the Table

**Try This.** Keep a slice on the kitchen table or your desk, wherever the hard conversations happen. Before you answer in a disagreement, look at the colorless band and name, out loud or to yourself, one thing you and the other person both want. Then answer. Kunz's peace symbol, put to work.

### Pink & Green at Dinner

**Try This.** Pass the slice around at dinner. Whoever holds it answers two short questions. Pink, who was kind to you today? Green, what is growing, a plant, a skill, a plan? It works with kids, roommates and tired couples alike.

### A Window Slice

**Try This.** Stand a thin slice in a window where light comes through it. Each morning when you open the blinds, let the color be your cue to pick one thing to do with care that day. GIA calls tourmaline generally stable to light, so the sill is fine for most pieces, but skip a spot over a radiator.

### A Gift for Two

**Try This.** For an October birthday, a pair of best friends or a couple, give a slice with a card. "Pink and green in one crystal. In 1915 it was called the Peace Stone." Gem Rock Auctions also names tourmaline as the 8th wedding anniversary stone.

**Safety.** Small slices and tumbled stones are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them, and watch thin edges, which can be sharp.

**What Research Says.** In three randomized studies, two with college students (192 and 157 of them) and one with 65 adults living with neuromuscular disease, people asked to list what they were grateful for reported better well-being than comparison groups on several measures, and the clearest gain was in positive mood (Emmons and McCullough, 2003, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, [doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.84.2.377](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.84.2.377)). The results were self-reported, the gains did not show on every measure, and no stones were involved. Treat the habits above as personal practices.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Watermelon tourmaline is often chosen for heartbreak, old hurts and low moods, and some sites make claims about heart and nerve conditions. A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. If sadness, anxiety or a health worry lasts, talk to a doctor or a therapist. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States.

## Is My Watermelon Tourmaline Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone.

- **Natural, untreated.** We found no reference source giving the share of watermelon tourmaline on the US market that is untreated, and the International Gem Society says natural tourmalines frequently receive enhancements, so ask.
- **Natural, treated.** GIA names heating and irradiation as the two most important tourmaline treatments. Heat changes are stable and undetectable, and irradiation color can fade with heat or bright light. The [International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/tourmaline-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/) calls both common and notes that irradiation can produce red, deep pink and parti-colors. Gem Rock Auctions adds laser drilling and fracture filling for clarity, which lower value.
- **Lab-grown.** Not on the market. The International Gem Society says no commercially available lab-created tourmaline exists for gem or jewelry use, since it is expensive and difficult to make. A "simulated" tag online often means a fake.
- **Imitation.** Fakes are usually glass, plastic or cheaper stones (Gem Rock Auctions).
- **Trade name.** "Watermelon" is an honest pattern name. "Reverse watermelon" is the same idea turned inside out.

Three checks you can do at home, with their limits.

1. **Look at the boundary.** Natural stones usually fade from color to color, while imitations tend to show a sharp line (Gem Rock Auctions). The limit is real, since some natural stones have a crisp colorless ring and parti-colored tourmaline can have "striking boundaries" (International Gem Society).
2. **Look for inclusions.** Almost all watermelon tourmaline shows eye-visible inclusions, so a flawless stone is suspect, according to Gem Rock Auctions. Flawless natural stones are not impossible.
3. **On a crystal or slice, look at the shape.** Tourmaline crystals often have triangular or six-sided cross-sections with rounded edges and fine lines running their length. Its cleavage is indistinct, so a piece that splits cleanly along flat planes is probably not tourmaline (Geology.com). Tumbled and faceted stones lose these clues.

A price far below the market is one more warning sign. Ask the seller three questions. Is this natural tourmaline? Has it been heated, irradiated or filled? Where was it mined? The [Label Decoder](/tools/crystal-label-decoder/) explains trade names and gives you the questions to copy.

## How to Care for Watermelon Tourmaline

**Mineral Fact.** With a hardness of 7 to 7.5, no cleavage and only slight brittleness, tourmaline makes a good stone for everyday jewelry (International Gem Society). GIA rates its toughness as fair.

- **Water.** Unknown for soaking. GIA's advice covers washing only.
- **Cleaning.** OK. "Warm, soapy water is the best method for cleaning tourmaline," says [GIA](https://www.gia.edu/tourmaline-care-cleaning), and ultrasonic and steam cleaners are not recommended.
- **Sun.** OK. GIA says tourmaline is generally stable to light. The exception is color created by irradiation, which can fade with bright light or heat.
- **Heat.** Avoid. High heat can alter the color, and sudden temperature change can crack the stone (GIA).
- **Chemicals.** OK. GIA says tourmaline is not affected by chemicals. A mild wash is still all it needs.
- **Salt.** Unknown. We found no reference source on salt and tourmaline, so skip it.
- **Storage.** OK for everyday wear, with one caution for slices. The International Gem Society's [care guide](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/gemstone-care-guide/) warns that bi-colored crystals sometimes split where the colors meet, and that pink tourmaline is often flawed. Store slices flat in their own pouch.
- **Toxicity.** None known, per the same International Gem Society table.

### Can Watermelon Tourmaline Go in Water or Sun?

A wash in warm soapy water, yes. A long soak or a monthly session under running water, as one German site suggests, has no reference guidance behind it, so our Care Checker rates water Unknown. Sun is the easier one. GIA calls tourmaline generally stable to light, so a few hours on the sill is fine unless the color came from irradiation. Skip salt, which is also Unknown, and skip drinking elixirs too. ## Watermelon Tourmaline vs Look-Alikes

- **Pink tourmaline.** The core color on its own. See [pink tourmaline](/stones/pink-tourmaline/).
- **Green tourmaline.** The rind color on its own. See [green tourmaline](/stones/green-tourmaline/).
- **Bi-color and parti-color tourmaline.** Same mineral, colors zoned along the length rather than from the center out (International Gem Society).
- **Trapiche tourmaline.** Cut across the crystal like watermelon, but showing spokes of inclusions between growth sectors (International Gem Society).
- **Glass and plastic.** Usually too clean and too sharply divided (Gem Rock Auctions).

## Price Guide

The most detailed published figures come from one marketplace dealer, Gem Rock Auctions, in a guide updated January 25, 2026. Rough runs about $3 to $8 per carat, faceted stones about $20 to $55 per carat (roughly $10 to $80 at the extremes), and cabochons about $15 to $40 per carat. The most coveted look, a green rind fading into a hot pink core, costs more than other patterns, and cat's-eye stones cut as cabochons fetch more too.

At the top end, tourmaline specimens with attractive color and crystal form can sell for thousands of dollars (Geology.com). For slices, tumbled stones and pendants we found no current US shop listings to quote, so judge them by what drives tourmaline prices in general. The International Gem Society names color, clarity and origin, with a premium for clean stones, particularly those over 10 carats. Treatment matters too, so ask whether a piece was heated, irradiated or filled.

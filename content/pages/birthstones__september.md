# September Birthstone Meaning, History & Honest Buying Tips

The September birthstone is sapphire, the blue variety of the mineral corundum and September's only stone on the modern US list from the American Gem Society. It has held the month since 1912, when US jewelers moved it from April and pushed out chrysolite, and some charts add lapis lazuli as an alternate. Sapphire rates 9 on the Mohs scale, and heat treatment is common, permanent and accepted.

## What Is the September Birthstone?

Sapphire, alone, on both the American Gem Society list and the US jewelers' list adopted in Kansas City in August 1912 (Kunz 1913, pages 317 and 319). Before 1912, September belonged to another stone.

### Why Is Sapphire the September Birthstone?

Because the 1912 list put it there. On the older lists Kunz compared, sapphire was April's stone on seven of eight, while chrysolite held September on six (pages 315 to 316). The Kansas City list gave April the diamond, moved sapphire to September and made chrysolite, under its modern name peridot, an alternate for August.

Kunz called it an "undesirable change" that took chrysolite "from the place it has always occupied as the gem of September," while "the sapphire, properly the gem for April, is made the birth-stone for September" (page 320). His zodiac verses still give the sapphire to Taurus, an April and May sign (page 346).

### Modern vs Traditional September Birthstones

Modern means sapphire, and so does traditional if a jeweler means the 1912 list. The older European lists mean chrysolite. Lapis lazuli is an alternate on some charts, such as the Burke Museum's, but not on the AGS list, and the 1912 list made it December's alternate, not September's. Its tie to sapphire is ancient. Every month is on our [birthstone chart](/birthstones/).

## Where the September Birthstone Comes From

**Tradition.** The month lists follow the twelve foundation stones in Revelation, starting with March (Kunz 1913, pages 307 to 308). The second stone, sapphire, fell on April, and the seventh, chrysolite, on September (pages 311 to 313).

That old "sapphire" was probably not our sapphire. Pliny the Elder describes his sapphirus as "refulgent with spots like gold" (*Natural History*, book 37, chapter 39), which fits lapis lazuli and its pyrite, not corundum. Kunz agreed. Of the Bible's stone lists he wrote that "the sapphire was the lapis-lazuli" (page 319), and he read the sapphire tablets of the Law of Moses as lapis too (page 104). So the September alternate is, in a sense, the original sapphire.

For his patriotic list of American gems, Kunz gave September [kunzite](/stones/kunzite/), a California stone named in his honor in 1903 (page 323).

## Sapphire, the Modern September Birthstone

**The Stone.** [Sapphire](/stones/sapphire/) is gem corundum, aluminum oxide, in any color but red, since red corundum is [ruby](/stones/ruby/). Trace elements such as iron and titanium color it. It rates 9 on the [Mohs scale](/identify/mohs-hardness-scale-for-crystals/) of scratch resistance, second only to diamond among natural gems, with excellent toughness and no cleavage, the tendency to split along flat planes.

Gem crystals turn up mainly in river gravels. Kashmir, Myanmar and Sri Lanka are the historic sources, and Kashmir is now exhausted, while Sri Lanka has produced sapphire for more than 2,000 years. Madagascar, Australia, Thailand and Montana add more.

### Sapphire Colors, From Blue to Padparadscha

Sapphires that are not blue are called fancy sapphires, from violet, green, yellow and orange to pink, purple, gray, black and brown. A rare pinkish orange kind is [padparadscha](/stones/padparadscha-sapphire/), from the Sinhalese for lotus blossom. Needles of rutile, called silk, can make a six-rayed [star sapphire](/stones/star-sapphire/) when the stone is cut as a dome.

Some sapphires change color, most often from blue in daylight to purple under incandescent light, because of traces of vanadium. Kunz describes a sapphire once in London's South Kensington Museum, rich blue by day and violet by lamplight, that the writer Mme. de Genlis turned into a test of female faithfulness (1913, pages 105 to 106).

### How Sapphires Are Treated

A treatment is any process that changes a gem's color or clarity after mining, and sapphires are often treated. Heating is common, permanent and well accepted, and milky Sri Lankan "geuda" sapphires have been heated to a rich blue for decades. Less common treatments, such as lattice diffusion, fracture filling and dye, may need special care, and filled or dyed stones can be damaged even by lemon juice.

**The Story.**

- **Antiquity.** **Tradition.** Damigeron wrote that kings wore sapphire at the neck as a defense from harm, and that it kept off envy and drew divine favor (Kunz 1913, page 104).
- **India.** **Tradition.** The Sanskrit name nîla means blue, and Hindus believed the sapphire made the planet Saturn favorable to its wearer (page 344).

## Chrysolite, the Old September Birthstone

**The Stone.** Chrysolite, Greek for "golden stone," is an old name for [peridot](/stones/peridot/), the green gem variety of olivine. Kunz writes "chrysolite (peridot)" (page 320), though the name wandered, and one old list used it for yellow chrysoberyl (pages 347 to 348). Peridot is mostly yellowish green, rates 6.5 to 7 and is now the [August](/birthstones/august/) birthstone.

**Tradition.** Kunz's calendar gave September the chrysolite and a verse promising that "follies and dark delusions flee afraid" (page 330).

## Lapis Lazuli, an Alternate September Birthstone

**The Stone.** [Lapis lazuli](/stones/lapis-lazuli/) is a rock, not a single mineral. It must be distinctly blue and hold at least 25 percent lazurite, with white calcite and brassy pyrite in the rest. Afghanistan's Badakhshan region is the leading and oldest source. It rates only 5 to 6, and because it is slightly porous, much of it is dyed to hide the calcite, then waxed or oiled.

**Tradition.** Kunz called lapis "the sapphire of the ancients," typical of heaven because its gold spots looked like stars on the blue (1915, page 282).

## What September Birthstones Mean Today

**Modern Association.** The September stone has traditionally stood for sincerity, truth, faithfulness and nobility, and Kunz called sapphire "a symbol of truth, sincerity, and constancy" (1913, page 324). Modern crystal guides tie sapphire to wisdom and loyalty and place it on the throat and third eye chakras. Our rankings rate sapphire Widely Linked for protection, focus and calm, and lapis lazuli Widely Linked for protection and focus.

September splits between [Virgo](/zodiac/virgo/), until about September 22, and [Libra](/zodiac/libra/). Kunz's verses give Virgo the carnelian and Libra the chrysolite (page 346).

### What Is the September Birthstone Color?

Blue. Sapphire is best known in a vivid, saturated blue, though it comes in every color but red. Lapis is an opaque deep blue with gold flecks, and chrysolite adds yellowish green. Medieval Europeans believed sapphire cured plague boils and eye disease. Nothing in the mineral or medical sources behind this page supports such claims.

## How to Choose a September Birthstone

Hardness is resistance to scratching, and toughness is resistance to chipping. Sapphire scores high on both.

- **For every day.** Sapphire, in a ring or anything else. Lapis is soft, so choose earrings or a pendant. Peridot can be scratched by household dust and wants a protective setting.
- **For a baby's first piece.** Buy it to keep, not to wear, since small stones are a choking hazard.
- **For an engagement or anniversary.** Princess Diana's ring, later Kate Middleton's, is a blue sapphire, and sapphire is the gem of the 5th and 45th wedding anniversaries.
- **For an inherited sapphire.** Ask a jeweler whether it is filled before using any cleaning machine.
- **For another color.** A [yellow sapphire](/stones/yellow-sapphire/) or another fancy sapphire.

**What drives a sapphire's price.** Color first, with strong to vivid saturation preferred in any hue. Then clarity, where blue sapphires usually show some inclusions, and size, though large blue sapphires are easier to find than large rubies. An origin claim that raises the price needs a lab report, since many stones are called "Kashmir" for their color alone.

### What to Ask Before You Buy

1. **Mined or lab-grown?** Many sapphires on the market are lab-grown. Under the FTC's Jewelry Guides, a lab-made sapphire needs a word like "laboratory-grown," "laboratory-created" or "[maker]-created" right before its name, and never "natural" or "genuine." Get the answer on the receipt.
2. **Treated, and by what method?** Heat is fine. Diffusion, filling or dye need gentler care, and the same guides say a treatment that is not permanent or needs special care should be disclosed.
3. **Is there a lab report?** Ask for one for any origin or "no heat" claim.
4. **Can I look with a 10x loupe?** Some inclusions are normal. A big, flawless, cheap blue stone deserves a lab check.
5. **How do I clean it?** Ask for the care steps in writing.

## Is My September Birthstone Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone.

- **Natural, untreated.** Mined and never heated.
- **Natural, treated.** Heated, the usual case, or diffused, filled or dyed. Treated is not fake.
- **Lab-grown.** First made in the early 1900s by flame fusion, now by several methods, and so natural-looking that only skilled professionals can tell. Curved growth lines appear only in synthetic sapphire and ruby, but their absence proves nothing.
- **Imitation.** Glass, plastic and other blue gems. See [tanzanite vs sapphire](/compare/tanzanite-vs-sapphire/).
- **Trade name.** "Fancy sapphire" honestly names colors other than blue. "Kashmir" for color alone proves nothing.

For lapis, dyed and waxed stones are common, dyed jasper is sold as "Swiss lapis," and [sodalite](/compare/sodalite-vs-lapis-lazuli/) is the natural look-alike. The acid and streak tests for lapis are destructive, so leave them to a gemologist. For chrysolite, green glass is the usual imitation.

## How to Use a September Birthstone

**Try This.** Kunz made sapphire the stone of truth and constancy. On your September birthday, hold it, write one true sentence about the year ahead and keep the note with the stone.

**Try This.** Lapis was read as a night sky. Before bed, find three gold flecks in a lapis bead and name three things that went right today.

**Safety.** Small stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Sapphire is often chosen for calm and focus, and two of Kunz's 1913 verses promise that sapphire and chrysolite cure "diseases of the mind" (pages 330 and 346). A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. If anxiety or low mood lasts, talk to a doctor or a therapist. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

### How to Clean a Sapphire Ring at Home

Our data has no water rating for sapphire yet, so these steps follow GIA's September care advice, with the cautious choice where it matters.

1. **Check the treatment** on the receipt or lab report, or ask your jeweler. Untreated, heated or lattice diffusion-treated stones can be washed. Filled, dyed or unknown stones get a damp cloth only.
2. **When it looks dull, brush it gently in warm water with a drop of mild dish soap,** using a soft toothbrush behind the stone, where lotion and dust collect.
3. **Rinse in a second bowl of clean water and pat dry** with a lint-free cloth.
4. **Skip ultrasonic and steam cleaners at home** unless the treatment is in writing, since they are usually safe only for untreated, heated and lattice diffusion-treated stones.
5. **Keep filled or dyed stones away from acids,** even lemon juice.

### How to Care for Lapis Lazuli

1. **Brush it quickly** with a soft brush, lukewarm water and a little mild soap, then dry it at once. Never leave it to soak.
2. **Never use steam, an ultrasonic cleaner or solvents.**
3. **Put it on after perfume and lotion,** and take it off for dishes and cleaning.
4. **Store it in its own pouch,** since sapphire, quartz and most other gems will scratch it.

**Where the beliefs and the care data disagree.** Some crystal guides cleanse lapis in salt water or sunlight. Our data keeps water brief for porous lapis and has no sun or salt rating, so use moonlight or sound and bring it in before morning.

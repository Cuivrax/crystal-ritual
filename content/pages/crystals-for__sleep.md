# Crystals for Sleep & a Calm Bedtime Routine

The crystals most linked to sleep are amethyst, howlite, charoite, stilbite and prehnite, with moonstone, rainbow moonstone and celestite close behind. These links are beliefs, most of them modern, not tested effects on sleep. A stone on the nightstand can still anchor a steady bedtime routine, and routine is what the CDC's sleep advice is built on.

## Crystals for Sleep at a Glance

**Modern Association.** Modern crystal guides tie these stones to sleep most firmly, in this order. The last column decides where each one belongs at night.

| Stone | How Often It Is Linked | What People Do With It | Bedside Care Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amethyst | Classic | A cluster or small geode on the nightstand | Washable, hardness 7, keep out of strong sun |
| Howlite | Classic | Held during a wind-down, sometimes under the pillow | Soft, often dyed blue |
| Charoite | Classic | A palm stone by the bed for dream work | Hardness 5 to 6, no steam |
| Stilbite | Classic | A cluster on the bedside table with a dream journal | Brittle, shelf only |
| Prehnite | Classic | A tumbled stone by the bed for dream recall | Keep from heat and acids |
| Moonstone | Widely Linked | On the nightstand or worn as a pendant | Can break on a sharp knock |
| Rainbow moonstone | Widely Linked | The same uses as moonstone | Really labradorite, chips on impact |
| Celestite | Widely Linked | A geode on a bedroom shelf | Very soft, fades in light, brief water only |

The labels run from Classic and Widely Linked down to Often Linked, Sometimes Linked and Rarely Linked. They measure how common the belief is, not whether it works. Before any stone meets water or a sunny window, look it up in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Why These Stones Get Linked to Sleep

**The Story.** Most of the sleep reputations on this list are young. A few have older roots, and those roots are more interesting than the modern claims.

- **Biblical era.** **Tradition.** Writing about the stones of the high priest's breastplate, George Frederick Kunz notes that the Hebrew name for amethyst, aḥlamah, shows the stone "was believed to possess the virtue of inducing dreams and visions" (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, page 297). The Greek name, which means "not drunk", gets more attention ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/amethyst/gem-overview)).
- **Crystal gazing.** **Tradition.** Kunz describes people who stare for a long time at a crystal ball, an opal or a moonstone and "may become partially hypnotized or even fall into a profound sleep" (same book, pages 7 to 8). He credits a gleam of light that fixes the eye, not the stone itself.
- **1915, Paris.** **Tradition.** A Parisian oracle on mystic subjects, as Kunz calls the Baroness d'Orchamps, recommended moonstone "for the manifold forms of nervousness" (*The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, 1915, pages 371 to 372).
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** The rest is recent. Howlite is named for Henry How, the Nova Scotia geologist who described it ([Amethyst Galleries](https://galleries.com/minerals/silicate/howlite/howlite.htm)), and the IMA list of minerals dates it to 1868. Charoite reached Western markets only in the 1970s ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/charoite-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). Their bedtime meanings come from crystal shops and books, not from old lapidaries. Color helps too, since modern charts put purple stones at the crown chakra and guides tie anything that glows like moonstone to the moon.

**What Research Says.** No study we know of has tested a crystal for sleep. The closest is a 2025 randomized controlled study in *CNS Spectrums* that gave 138 adults, believers and nonbelievers in crystal healing, either rose quartz or a visually matched glass imitation for 14 days. Anxiety scores fell only among the believers, and it made no difference whether their stone was real ([Escolà-Gascón and colleagues, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1092852925100515)). It measured anxiety, not sleep. The routine itself has better support, though from children. In a three-week randomized trial with 405 mothers and their babies or toddlers, a consistent nightly bedtime routine led to faster sleep onset, fewer night wakings and better maternal mood, as the mothers reported them ([Mindell and colleagues, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/32.5.599)). It was a study of young children, not adults, and no stones were involved. So the honest case for a sleep stone is the routine around it.

## A 10-Minute Bedtime Wind-Down

**The Ritual.** The CDC's advice for better sleep is plain. Go to bed and get up at the same time every day, keep the bedroom calm, quiet and cool, and turn off electronic devices at least 30 minutes before bedtime ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html)). A stone changes none of that. It can make the routine easier to start every night.

**When to use it.** 30 minutes before your usual lights-out, every night, weekends included.

**You need.** One stone from the list, a small notebook, a pen and, if you can manage it, a phone charger outside the bedroom.

**Steps.**

1. **2 minutes.** Put your phone on its charger, screen down, and pick up the stone. That handoff is the signal that the day is over.
2. **3 minutes.** Dim the lights. Hold the stone and take 10 slow breaths, letting each one out a little longer than you took it in.
3. **3 minutes.** Write two lines in the notebook, whatever is still on your mind and the first task for tomorrow. Close it.
4. **2 minutes.** Set the stone on the closed notebook on your nightstand, not under the pillow. Lights out at the same time as last night.

**Why it helps.** Whatever you believe about the stone, here is what the ritual does for sure. It moves screens out of the last half hour, it happens at the same time every night, and it gives your hands something to hold besides a phone. The stone is the bookmark, the routine is the point.

**Try This.** Choose the stone by feel, not by meaning. A smooth tumbled amethyst or a howlite palm stone is easier to hold in the dark than a sharp raw point.

## Can You Sleep with Crystals Under Your Pillow?

You can, but the nightstand is the better spot for most stones and the only spot in a home with small children. A tumbled stone under a pillow tends to slide into the sheets, drop onto the floor or end up in a toddler's hand.

**Safety.** Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are choking hazards for babies, young children and pets. Keep crystals out of cribs and toddler beds entirely, and never put a crystal necklace, bead bracelet or teething necklace on a sleeping child. The US Food and Drug Administration has received reports of death and serious injuries to infants and children, including strangulation and choking, from teething jewelry such as amber teething necklaces ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/safely-soothing-teething-pain-infants-and-children)). More in our [crystals and kids safety guide](/care/crystals-and-kids/).

Some stones also do badly under a pillow. Celestite has a very low hardness of 3 to 3.5 and perfect cleavage, which is why the [International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/celestite-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/) calls it a collector's stone rather than a jewelry stone. Stilbite is brittle with perfect cleavage, according to the [Handbook of Mineralogy](https://www.handbookofmineralogy.org/pdfs/stilbite-Ca.pdf). Moonstone can break on a sharp knock. If you want a stone in the bed anyway, pick a smooth tumbled quartz such as amethyst, which rates 7 on the Mohs scale, and keep it for adults.

## Crystals for Nightmares, Dreams & Lucid Dreaming

Crystal guides give sleep stones two jobs, settling the mind and working with dreams. **Modern Association.** For dreams, the usual picks are amethyst (darker stones are "dream crystals" in a [Rock & Gem](https://www.rockngem.com/amethyst-meaning-uses/) article), charoite, prehnite, which [Fire Mountain Gems](https://www.firemountaingems.com/learn/categories/essential-resources/gemstone-meanings/prehnite-gemstone-meanings.html) calls a "stone of dreaming", and stilbite. For nightmares, celestite and amethyst come up most. Lucid dreaming gets attached to prehnite, celestite and rainbow moonstone.

**Try This.** The practical half of dream work is the notebook. Keep it under the stone on your nightstand and write down whatever you remember before you look at your phone. The stone reminds you, the notebook keeps the dream.

Nightmares that keep coming back, at any age, are worth a conversation with a doctor.

## The Sleep Stones, One by One

### Amethyst

**Mineral Fact.** Amethyst is purple quartz, colored by traces of iron and natural radiation in the rock, and it rates 7 on the Mohs scale ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/amethyst/)). **Modern Association.** Classic for sleep and the default bedside crystal in modern guides, usually a cluster or small geode on the nightstand ([Crystals.com](https://www.crystals.com/blogs/news/amethyst-meaning)). Its dream link is the oldest on this page, thanks to that Hebrew name. **Care.** Wash it with warm water, mild soap and a soft brush ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/amethyst-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). GIA says some amethyst color can fade with long exposure to intense light, so a nightstand beats a sunny windowsill ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/amethyst-care-cleaning)). More on [amethyst](/stones/amethyst/).

### Howlite

**Mineral Fact.** Howlite is a white borate mineral, often veined with dark threads that resemble the veining in turquoise, and it is frequently dyed blue to imitate turquoise ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/howlite-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). A blue howlite is a dyed howlite. **Modern Association.** Classic for sleep. Crystal guides call it a calming stone, and [Fire Mountain Gems](https://www.firemountaingems.com/learn/categories/essential-resources/gemstone-meanings/howlite-gemstone-meanings.html) notes that some believers place it under an insomniac's pillow. **Care.** It has a low hardness, so keep it away from keys and harder stones ([Amethyst Galleries](https://galleries.com/minerals/silicate/howlite/howlite.htm)). It dissolves in dilute acids, dyed pieces should skip steam and harsh cleaners, and our care data has no water rating for it yet. More on [howlite](/stones/howlite/).

### Charoite

**Mineral Fact.** Charoite is a swirling purple rock made almost entirely of the mineral charoite, and it comes only from the Chara River area in Russia's Sakha Republic. It was first found in the 1940s as "lilac stone" and reached Western markets in the 1970s. GIA has documented plastic look-alikes, one sold as Royal Russianite ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/charoite-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). **Modern Association.** Classic for sleep, with dark violet pieces called "dream talismans" by [CrystalVaults](https://www.crystalvaults.com/crystal-encyclopedia/charoite/). People keep a palm stone by the bed and hold it while they wind down. **Care.** Clean it with a soft brush, mild detergent and warm water, never steam. At 5 to 6 on the Mohs scale it scratches more easily than quartz. More on [charoite](/stones/charoite/).

### Stilbite

**Mineral Fact.** Stilbite is a zeolite mineral that grows in fan, wheat sheaf and bow-tie shaped bundles, and its name comes from the Greek for "to glitter", after its pearly luster ([Minerals.net](https://www.minerals.net/mineral/stilbite)). **Modern Association.** Classic for sleep. Shop guides say it encourages sweet dreams in the bedroom ([Moonrise Crystals](https://moonrisecrystals.com/stilbite-meaning/)), and some people keep a cluster by the bed next to a dream journal ([Crystal Gemstones](https://crystalgemstones.net/stilbite-meaning-and-spiritual-properties.html)). **Care.** The Handbook of Mineralogy lists it as brittle with perfect cleavage, so it can split along flat planes ([Handbook of Mineralogy](https://www.handbookofmineralogy.org/pdfs/stilbite-Ca.pdf)), and our care data rates heat and acids as ones to avoid. Treat it as a shelf piece. More on [stilbite](/stones/stilbite/).

### Prehnite

**Mineral Fact.** Prehnite is a green to yellow calcium aluminum silicate named after Colonel Hendrik von Prehn, who first found it at the Cape of Good Hope ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/prehnite-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). A dealer guide notes it is sometimes sold as "new jade", but it is not jade ([Gem Rock Auctions](https://www.gemrockauctions.com/learn/a-z-of-gemstones/prehnite)). **Modern Association.** Classic for sleep and dreams. One crystal guide suggests a tumbled piece by the bed rather than a sharp raw one ([Crystal Gemstones](https://crystalgemstones.net/prehnite-meaning-and-spiritual-properties.html)). **Care.** Keep it away from heat, since prehnite gives off water when heated ([Amethyst Galleries](https://galleries.com/minerals/silicate/prehnite/prehnite.htm)), and a dealer guide warns against sudden temperature changes. The International Gem Society lists it as soluble in acids and lists no known toxicity ([IGS care guide](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/gemstone-care-guide/)). More on [prehnite](/stones/prehnite/).

### Moonstone

**Mineral Fact.** Moonstone is a feldspar with adularescence, a soft glow that seems to float under the surface ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/moonstone/gem-overview)). **Modern Association.** Widely Linked to sleep. People keep it on the nightstand or wear it as a pendant for dream work ([Lebensquelle Plus](https://lebensquelle-plus.de/blogs/magazin/mondstein-wirkung)), an echo of the hypnotic glow in Kunz. **Care.** A hardness of 6 and two perfect cleavage directions mean it scratches and can break on a sharp knock, which makes it a poor pillow stone ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/moonstone/)). Warm soapy water is fine ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/moonstone-jewelry-gem-information/)). More on [moonstone](/stones/moonstone/).

### Rainbow Moonstone

**Mineral Fact.** Rainbow moonstone is a trade name. GIA's lab reports this material as labradorite, a different feldspar, known in the trade as rainbow moonstone ([JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/labradorites-blue-rainbow-moonstone/)). **Modern Association.** Widely Linked to sleep, mostly on moonstone's reputation. Jewelry and crystal guides suggest keeping it near the bed for restful sleep and dream recall ([AJ Luxe](https://ajluxe.com/blogs/jewelry-guide/rainbow-moonstone-meaning), [Otter Spirit](https://otterspirit.com/blogs/otterspirit/rainbow-moonstone-meaning-properties)). **Care.** A designer quoted in JCK says cleaning in water is no problem, but the stone abrades over time, and labradorite in general chips on impact ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/labradorite/)). GIA's moonstone care page warns that high heat or a sudden temperature change can break moonstone ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/moonstone-care-cleaning)). More on [rainbow moonstone](/stones/rainbow-moonstone/).

### Celestite

**Mineral Fact.** Celestite is a strontium sulfate, often sky blue. The Crystal Cave at Put-in-Bay, Ohio, is a geode 35 feet wide lined with celestite crystals up to 18 inches across ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/celestite-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). **Modern Association.** Widely Linked to sleep. Crystal guides call the bedroom its most common home ([Crystal Gemstones](https://crystalgemstones.net/celestite-meaning-and-spiritual-properties.html)) and tie it to calm nights and fewer nightmares ([Energy Muse](https://energymuse.com/pages/celestite-meaning)). One guide suggests giving children a small piece to hold ([CrystalVaults](https://www.crystalvaults.com/crystal-encyclopedia/celestite/)), which we would not do, because small pieces are a choking hazard. In a child's room, a geode on a high shelf is the safer version. **Care.** It is very soft and light sensitive, and dealer guides say it is slightly soluble in water ([Gemstones.com](https://www.gemstones.com/gemopedia/celestite)). Keep it out of the sun, clean it with a quick soft brush and warm water only, and store it apart from other stones. More on [celestite](/stones/celestite/).

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** A stone can make a bedtime routine feel like yours. It does not treat insomnia, nightmares or any sleep disorder. The CDC advises talking to a healthcare provider if you have problems sleeping, and a provider can run tests, including sleep studies, to check for a sleep disorder ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html)). Do not stop, skip or delay any sleep treatment your doctor prescribed because of a crystal. If a child has frequent nightmares or night terrors, ask your pediatrician.

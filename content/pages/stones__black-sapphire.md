# Black Sapphire Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Black sapphire is corundum, the same mineral as blue sapphire and ruby, in a tone so dark that light barely passes through it. Modern crystal guides link it with protection and grounding, and those meanings are beliefs. Black is the lowest-valued color among star sapphires, and some black star stones hide glass or varnish in pits on the back.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Turn the stone over. On a black star sapphire, look at the back with a loupe for pits filled with a brown material, or a glassy layer with tiny gas bubbles. Natural black sapphire does not glow under a UV flashlight, so a bright orange glow on the back is worth asking about. These checks raise questions. Only a gem lab can answer them.

## What Black Sapphire Actually Is

**The Stone.** Sapphire is gem-quality corundum. All red corundum counts as ruby, and every other color counts as sapphire. Blue is the classic, but sapphires can even be gray, black or brown. A black sapphire is a real sapphire.

**Mineral Fact.** Corundum is aluminum oxide with a hardness of 9 and a specific gravity of about 4.00. Among natural gems only diamond is harder, and sapphire has no cleavage planes, the flat directions along which some crystals split. It is superb for everyday wear, though a heavily included or fractured stone is less stable. That caveat matters for black stones.

Sapphire colors come from trace elements such as iron, titanium and chromium. Black can have a different cause. In a black star sapphire from Thailand described in *Gems & Gemology* in 2018, Wim Vertriest and Simon Bruce-Lockhart traced the black to a very high concentration of iron-rich particles, identified as hematite and ilmenite, packed so densely that they completely masked the stone's bluish green body color. Other black sapphires are simply very dark stones, as the next section explains.

### What Color Is Black Sapphire?

Black to the eye, and often something else underneath. The term generally covers very dark sapphires of several hues, such as blue, brown or green, which look black because light barely travels through them. Truly black sapphires occur naturally too, with Australia as their primary source.

So most "natural black sapphire" is just that, natural sapphire whose color is too deep to read. If a jeweler says yours is "really a very dark blue," that is not a sales trick.

### Black Star Sapphire

Some black sapphires carry a star. Needle-like inclusions aligned with the crystal reflect light into a star when the stone is cut as a dome, called a cabochon, and these stars show six or twelve rays. Sources disagree on what makes the star in black stones. The International Gem Society ties star sapphire stars to aligned rutile needles, the usual explanation for star sapphires. In a 2020 *Gems & Gemology* note, Richard W. Hughes and E. Billie Hughes say the star in black stones generally comes from ilmenite and/or hematite, iron-bearing minerals that separate out inside the crystal, and not from rutile. A lab can identify the inclusions in a particular stone. Black star sapphires come mainly from Kenya, Australia and Thailand.

The best-known example, the Black Star of Queensland, weighs 733 carats. More on our [black star sapphire page](/stones/black-star-sapphire/).

### Natural Black Sapphire vs Lab-Grown

Corundum was first synthesized in the early 1900s, and today many sapphires on the market are grown in labs. Synthetic sapphire is durable enough for supermarket scanner windows and spacecraft. Lab-grown stones share the chemistry of natural ones, so "is it sapphire?" and "did it grow in the ground?" are two questions. All gem varieties of corundum can be synthesized, but we found no reference source on how often lab-grown black sapphire is sold.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** Black sapphire has almost no lore of its own. What it inherited comes from sapphire in general and from star sapphires, and both stories were mostly told about other colors.

- **Ancient world.** **Tradition.** The word sapphire is often traced to the Hebrew sapir. Ancient "sapphire" may really mean lapis lazuli, so the oldest beliefs may not be about corundum at all.
- **Centuries of lore.** **Tradition.** Sapphire has long been linked with royalty and said to protect against poison and fraud, and star sapphires were credited with power to divine the future.
- **Old Hindu astrology.** **Tradition.** Kunz reports that Hindus believed sapphire made the planet Saturn favorable, and sorted sapphires into four classes from light blue to dark blue, matched to the four castes (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, page 344). Black is not one of them.
- **Around 1000 CE.** **Tradition.** The scholar Al-Berûnî (973 to 1048) listed the colors of yakut, a name that covered all corundum, and black was among them, next to red, yellow, gray, green and white, as Kunz reports (*The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, 1915, page 403). Black corundum was known, even if nobody wrote lore for it.
- **Sri Lanka.** **Tradition.** The moving star was believed by the Sinhalese to guard against witchcraft. Kunz adds that star sapphires rarely have fine blue color, and gray or nearly white stones often show the sharpest star (1913, page 106).
- **Travelers' talismans.** **Tradition.** The explorer Sir Richard Francis Burton called his large star sapphire his talisman, because it brought him good horses and prompt service, and the 17th-century writer De Boot said Germans called such a stone a Siegstein, a victory stone (Kunz 1913, page 106). Kunz also records the three crossed lines read as Faith, Hope and Destiny (page 107).
- **Gazing.** **Tradition.** Kunz describes a belief that staring at a gleaming stone such as a star sapphire could bring on a trance and a glimpse of the future, and that in the East a living spirit was supposed to dwell in stones with a moving light (1913, pages 7 to 8). Both ideas survive today on a dealer's black sapphire page.
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** A witchcraft blog credits the occult writer Scott Cunningham, author of *Cunningham's Encyclopedia of Crystal, Gem & Metal Magic* (1988), with calling the star sapphire the most potent sapphire in magic. Protection, grounding and the root chakra are now applied to black sapphire as a stone of its own.

## What People Use Black Sapphire for Today

**Modern Association.** Protection is Widely Linked with black sapphire, and grounding is Sometimes Linked. One dealer guide sums the stone up as protection, prestige and power, and calls it a root chakra stone that keeps you "stable and grounded." A rockhound blog adds insight, and the dealer guide adds staying calm in stressful moments.

The "why" is mostly color. The dealer guide says that "being a black gemstone, it inherently offers protective and balancing benefits," the same color logic crystal guides apply to obsidian, a Classic protection stone. Some newlyweds choose it for engagement rings, the guide adds. A witchcraft blog ties sapphire to finding and keeping a job, and wrote about black sapphire in winter as "a time of contemplation and a dark time of year."

Some links are marginal. A single dealer page pairs it with Leo and offers it as a stand-in for black onyx as a "mystical December birthstone." Neither stone is on the American Gem Society's December list, which names tanzanite, turquoise and zircon. Sapphire is September.

The dealer guide also lists help with pain, circulation and bone injuries. Gemological references do not support those claims.

## How to Use Black Sapphire in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** Whatever you believe about the stone, here is what the ritual does for sure. It gives a small habit a fixed place in your day, and a ring is the easiest place of all, because it is already on your hand.

### The Boundary Ring

**Try This.** When someone asks for your time and you feel the automatic yes coming, turn the ring once before you answer, then say "let me check and get back to you." Protection, in everyday terms, is often a little room to think.

### Before an Interview

**Try This.** On the morning of an interview, put on the ring or pendant and say your answer to "tell me about yourself" out loud twice. On the way in, touch the stone once as a cue to slow your first sentence down.

### A Fifth Anniversary Gift With a Note

**Try This.** Sapphire is the gem of the 5th anniversary, and black is an unusual, affordable way to keep the tradition. Write on the card what it is (a real sapphire, just very dark) and one line of lore, like Burton's star sapphire that brought him good horses wherever he went.

**Safety.** Loose stones and small jewelry are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

**What Research Says.** The boundary ring is an if-then plan with the ring as the cue. A 2006 meta-analysis by Peter Gollwitzer and Paschal Sheeran, pooling published lab and field studies, found that people who made an if-then plan ("if situation X happens, then I will do Y") were more likely to act on a goal than people who only intended to ([DOI 10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601%2806%2938002-1)). It pooled studies of everyday goals, so publication bias is possible, and none of them tested stones. The plan does the work, and the black sapphire is only the reminder.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Black sapphire is sometimes chosen for stress, anxiety or low moods. A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. If worry, sadness or trouble sleeping lasts or feels heavy, talk to a doctor or a therapist, and never use a stone in place of treatment. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My Black Sapphire Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone.

- **Natural, untreated.** Black corundum straight from the ground, with Australia the main source of truly black stones. Untreated sapphires usually sell for more than treated ones.
- **Natural, treated.** Corundum is very often treated, and has probably been treated for more than 1,000 years. Dye can be deposited in fractures of low-quality stones, fractures can be filled with oil or glass, and sapphires that are too dark can be lightened by heating below about 1,830 to 2,010 °F (1,000 to 1,100 °C).
- **Lab-grown.** Synthetic corundum has existed since the early 1900s, and all gem varieties of corundum can be synthesized. We found no reference source on how common lab-grown black sapphire is, so ask the seller and get the answer in writing.
- **Imitation.** Any gem can be imitated. Glass, plastic and cubic zirconia, which comes in almost any color, are used for sapphire lookalikes, and colored cubic zirconia makes good imitations of black gemstones. Assembled stones are another trap, and GIA's Fall 1985 Lab Notes include a black star sapphire doublet, a stone assembled from two parts. We found no reference source on which black imitations turn up most often in the US.
- **Trade name.** None. "Black sapphire" is the correct name for black corundum.

### The Back of a Black Star Sapphire

This is where buyers get surprised. Black star sapphires have a weak plane along the base of the crystal, called parting, and it leaves holes in the back. In Thailand, "one often sees cavities on the back of black star sapphires filled in with brown dopping varnish," Hughes and Hughes write. The 75-carat stone they tested had both glass over about two-thirds of its base and varnish in its pits, in a color that matched the stone.

Three checks you can do at home, with their limits.

1. **Look at the back with a loupe.** GIA's team saw gas bubbles between the glassy layer and the sapphire. A glassy patch with bubbles, or a pit filled with a duller brown material, is worth a question. A clean back is reassuring, not proof.
2. **Try a UV flashlight in a dark room.** Natural black sapphire is inert under UV, meaning it does not glow. The varnish in GIA's stone glowed bright orange under long-wave UV. A glow does not prove a filling, and no glow does not rule one out.
3. **Check for dye and foil.** Dye gathers in fractures and stains a solvent-soaked swab wiped across the surface, and foil behind a stone in a closed-back setting usually looks crumpled under a loupe.

None of this tells natural from lab-grown. Doubtful stones belong in a laboratory, and a lab report is worth asking for on any stone of real value.

Three questions to ask the seller. Is it natural, and treated in any way, including fillings on the back? Is it black, or a very dark blue, brown or green? Where was it mined, and is there a lab report? The [Label Decoder](/tools/crystal-label-decoder/) gives you these questions to copy.

## How to Care for Black Sapphire

**Mineral Fact.** At Mohs 9 with no cleavage, sapphire resists scratches and knocks better than almost any gem. Black sapphires need a little more care than that suggests. Abundant inclusions can weaken them, so handle them carefully and choose protective settings.

- **Water.** OK. Warm water, detergent and a soft brush. Keep soaks brief, since the care guidance covers washing, not long soaks.
- **Sun.** Brief only. Naturally colored sapphires may fade in intense artificial light and usually regain color in sunlight or UV, though that guidance does not cover black stones specifically.
- **Heat.** Brief only. Treated corundum may not withstand heat in a jewelry workshop, and fracture-filled stones can be damaged by standard workshop techniques. Tell your jeweler about any treatment before a repair.
- **Chemicals.** Unknown. We found no reference source on cleaners or acids for black sapphire.
- **Salt.** Unknown. We found no reference source on salt yet.
- **Cleaning machines.** Skip them for fractured stones and black star sapphires, as the dealer guide advises. Oil-treated sapphires may also have weaker inclusions.
- **Storage.** Give it its own pouch. At hardness 9 it scratches softer gems, and an included stone can still chip.
- **Toxicity.** OK to wear and handle. Sapphire carries no known risk.

### Can Black Sapphire Go in Water?

For a wash, yes. For a long soak, not by the Care Checker. Warm soapy water and a soft toothbrush are what both the care references and the dealer guide recommend. Salt has no source either way, so we would skip salt cleanses until a reference source covers them. ## Black Sapphire vs Look-Alikes

Black gems are hard to tell apart by eye, because the main color clue is missing. These are the differences that matter.

- **Black spinel.** A different mineral, hardness 8, usually cheaper than other spinels. See [black onyx vs black spinel](/compare/black-onyx-vs-black-spinel/).
- **Black onyx.** Strictly, chalcedony with straight, parallel layers. Most solid black chalcedony sold as onyx today is dyed.
- **Black tourmaline.** Colored by high iron, hardness 7 to 7.5, so softer than sapphire.
- **Hematite.** Heavy for its size. Magnetic "hematine" is a simulant.
- **Jet.** Light and warm in the hand, and soft.
- **Black diamond.** Hardness 10, but abundant inclusions make it prone to splitting.

## Price Guide

### What Is Black Sapphire Worth?

Usually much less than blue. Among star sapphires, "the color that is consistently valued lowest is black," the International Gem Society writes in its 2026 guide to phenomenal sapphire prices. Thailand and Australia have plenty of this material, and it may sell for just dollars per carat. Black stars with golden bands are worth more than those with white bands, though both stay affordable.

Treatment still matters, because untreated sapphires usually sell for more than treated ones. If a black sapphire is priced like a fine blue one, ask why, and get the treatment in writing.

In the listings we checked, loose black star sapphires of about 3 to 4 carats, roughly 9 to 11 mm long, ran about $49 to $69 a stone. One listing we found, a 6-carat stone about 11 mm long, was about $120, and another, a 41-carat stone about 20 mm long, was about $533. The listings did not say stone by stone whether each was natural or lab-created, or treated, so ask before you buy. Prices from US-dollar listings at an online gem dealer in November 2026.

# Libra Crystals & Birthstones, Plus Where the Links Come From

Libra has no single official birthstone, because birthstones belong to months and Libra runs from about September 23 to October 22. That gives sapphire for a September birthday and opal or tourmaline for an October one. As a zodiac stone, a 1913 list gives Libra the chrysolite, the name Kunz used for peridot, while today's crystal guides favor lepidolite, prehnite, opal and rose quartz, all beliefs rather than mineral facts.

## What Is the Libra Birthstone?

The honest answer has two parts, the month stone and the sign stone.

**The month stone.** On the modern list of the [American Gem Society](https://www.americangemsociety.org/birthstones/), September has one birthstone, sapphire, and October has two, opal and tourmaline. The 1912 jewelers' list already gave September the sapphire and October the opal, with tourmaline as its alternate (Kunz 1913, pages 319 to 320), and pink tourmaline joined October in 1952. Kunz disliked both months. He wanted September to keep the chrysolite, and he wrote, "For October neither the tourmaline nor the opal is as appropriate as the beryl" (page 320). Our [September](/birthstones/september/) and [October](/birthstones/october/) pages go stone by stone.

**The sign stone.** **Tradition.** The traditional Libra zodiac stone is the chrysolite, from the verse list of zodiac gems that George Frederick Kunz printed in 1913. "Through Libra's sign it is quite well / To free yourself from evil spell, / For in her gem surcease doth dwell, / The chrysolite" (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, page 346). An old Spanish list that Kunz thought probably reflected Arab tradition gives Libra the [jasper](/stones/jasper/) instead (page 347).

Which stone is the chrysolite? In his birthstone chapter Kunz writes "chrysolite (peridot)" (page 320), and he calls the finest chrysolites "peridots, or olivines" (page 66). He also warns that the old names wandered. The name chrysolite, "golden-stone," was once used for our topaz (pages 297 to 298), and in one planetary list it meant the yellow chrysoberyl of Brazil (pages 347 to 348). The safest reading is peridot, which is August's birthstone today. The verse's "evil spell" fits older lore too, since chrysolite was believed to dissolve enchantments and put evil spirits to flight (page 67).

### What Color Is the Libra Birthstone?

There is no single Libra birthstone color. Sapphire is best known in blue but comes in every color except red. Opal can have any body color, and precious opal adds a flashing play of color. Tourmaline occurs in more colors than any other mineral group, black most commonly. The 1913 chrysolite, read as peridot, is mostly yellowish green.

Modern guides lean pink and lilac, with rose quartz and lepidolite, and pale green, with prehnite. One guide notes that some astrologers match pink to Libra, "which is often tied to love and harmony."

## Libra Crystals in Today's Guides

**Modern Association.** Most lists of crystals for Libra come from modern crystal guides, which describe Libra as a sign that seeks balance and harmony and name Venus as its ruling planet. One guide ties rose quartz to Taurus and Libra because both "have Venus as their ruling planet."

### Lepidolite

**Classic.** [Lepidolite](/stones/lepidolite/) is a rare lithium-rich mica, usually pink, red or purple and often grayish. The color comes from manganese, not from lithium, as many people assume. It is very soft, 2.5 to 3.5 on the Mohs scale, and gem lepidolite is sometimes impregnated with quartz to make it more durable. One shop guide says lepidolite "is mainly assigned to Libra."

### Prehnite

**Widely Linked.** [Prehnite](/stones/prehnite/) is a calcium aluminum silicate, popular as a green cabochon and sometimes faceted. It is named after Colonel Hendrik von Prehn, who first found it at the Cape of Good Hope. It forms at low temperature from groundwater in basaltic rocks, rates 6 to 6.5 on the Mohs scale, and can give off water when heated and cannot take it back.

### Opal for October Libras

**Widely Linked.** [Opal](/stones/opal/) is hydrated silica with no crystal structure, holding 3% to 21% water. Its play of color comes from light diffracted by tiny stacked spheres of silica, and Australia is the leading source. It rates 5 to 6.5 on the Mohs scale and chips, scratches and shatters more easily than most gems.

### Rose Quartz

**Widely Linked.** [Rose quartz](/stones/rose-quartz/) is pink quartz, usually translucent and massive rather than in clear crystals, at 7 on the Mohs scale. Its color usually comes from fibrous inclusions of a dumortierite-like mineral. Common rose quartz is generally stable in light and heat. The rarer transparent pink quartz is the kind that fades, as our [rose quartz fading](/care/rose-quartz-fading/) guide explains.

### Sapphire & Tourmaline

**Often Linked.** [Sapphire](/stones/sapphire/), 9 on the Mohs scale, is superb for everyday wear.

**Sometimes Linked.** [Tourmaline](/stones/tourmaline/) rates 7 to 7.5 with no cleavage, so it suits daily jewelry, and one guide calls it "the zodiac stone for those born under the sign of Libra." Kunz thought it "out of place" among birthstones, since it was only known as a gem from the 18th century (1913, page 321).

### Peridot, the 1913 Stone

**Rarely Linked.** Modern Libra lists hardly mention [peridot](/stones/peridot/), the green gem variety of olivine. Its oldest known source is Zabargad, an island in the Red Sea, and Kunz writes that the finest chrysolites from that region reached the cathedral treasuries of Europe, where they were "generally called emeralds" (1913, page 66).

## Where the Libra Links Come From

**The Story.** Libra's old stones come from star tables, talisman lists and planetary lists.

- **Medieval Jewish tradition.** **Tradition.** The tribe of Ephraim was paired with Libra (Kunz 1913, page 314).
- **Old star lists.** **Tradition.** A list of fixed stars gives the topaz to the wings of the Raven at 8° of Libra, and the emerald and the jasper to Spica Virginis at 17° of Libra (page 351).
- **Old talisman lists.** **Tradition.** Two engraved constellation figures fall under Libra. Corona Australis, an imperial crown, "augments wealth and makes the wearer gay and happy," and the Centaur gives constancy (pages 354 to 355).
- **Crystal gazing.** **Tradition.** One writer on scrying called Libra "the sign controlling the virtues of the beryl" (page 212).
- **Old planetary lists.** **Tradition.** Venus held the sapphire, the carbuncle, coral and pearl, usually the emerald too (page 348). Another list puts both the chrysolite and the jasper under Venus and Mercury (page 350).
- **1913.** **Tradition.** Kunz's verse list names the chrysolite (page 346), the old Spanish list the jasper (page 347), and his calendar gives September the chrysolite, the sign of Libra and the morning-glory (page 330).
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Lepidolite, prehnite, opal and rose quartz lead the modern lists of zodiac stones for Libra.

## How to Use Libra Stones in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** Whatever you believe about the stones, these give a pause you chose and something in your hand to mark it.

### A Two-Stone Decision Dish

**Try This.** Stuck between two options? Set two tumbled stones on a small dish, a rose quartz for one choice and a prehnite for the other. Under each, put a slip of paper with the single best reason for that option. Pick a deadline, and when it comes, keep the stone you chose and put the other away. Guides describe Libra as seeking balance, and a deadline is how the scales finally settle.

### An Opal Habit for October

**Try This.** Give an opal ring one fixed spot, a small dish by the sink, and take it off there before washing hands or dishes. Opal is sensitive to moisture and temperature changes, so the habit protects it.

### A Birthday Card with Two Stones

**Try This.** For a September Libra, give a sapphire and copy the 1913 chrysolite verse inside the card. For an October Libra, give an opal or a pink tourmaline and add one line about Corona Australis, the old crown talisman of Libra.

**Safety.** Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them. These are personal practices, not treatments.

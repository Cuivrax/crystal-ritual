# June Birthstone Meaning, History & Honest Buying Tips

The June birthstones are pearl, moonstone and alexandrite, the three stones the American Gem Society lists for the month. Pearl is an organic gem grown inside a mollusk, moonstone is a feldspar with a soft floating glow, and alexandrite is a rare chrysoberyl that looks green in daylight and red under lamplight. Pearl and moonstone were on the 1912 US jewelers' list, and alexandrite joined in 1952.

## What Is the June Birthstone?

June has three birthstones, pearl, moonstone and alexandrite, and you can pick any of them. The older lists look nothing like today's.

| List | June stone | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Jewish, Roman, Italian and Isidore of Seville lists | Emerald | Kunz 1913, page 315 |
| Arabian, Polish and Russian lists | Agate, chalcedony, and pearl on the Arabian list | Kunz 1913, page 315 |
| 15th to 20th century European usage | Cat's-eye, turquoise, agate | Kunz 1913, page 315 |
| US jewelers, Kansas City, 1912 | Pearl, alternate moonstone | Kunz 1913, page 319 |
| Jewelry Industry Council of America, 1952 | Alexandrite added | IGS |
| American Gem Society today | Pearl, alexandrite, moonstone | AGS |

### Modern vs Traditional June Birthstones

"Traditional" usually means pearl, the June stone of the 1912 list, with moonstone as its alternate. "Modern" means today's list, which adds alexandrite, a 1952 addition by the Jewelry Industry Council of America.

Go back further and the pearl almost vanishes. Of the eight older lists Kunz compared, only the Arabian one names it, while emerald and agate each appear in four (pages 315 to 316). Kunz's own 1913 calendar still gave June the agate. "With ring of agate on her hand / Can health, long life, and wealth command" (pages 328 to 329).

### Why Does June Have Three Birthstones?

GIA's birthstone exhibit says tradition plays a part, but "the more significant reason is that some gemstones are too rare and expensive, and others just aren't as popular." In Germany and Scandinavia, moonstone is the favorite June stone.

## Where the June Birthstones Come From

**Tradition.** Kunz, who printed the 1912 list, objected that "there should be no duplication or triplication of birth-stones for any given month" (pages 320 to 321). He would have swapped the moonstone for the asteria (page 320), his word for the star sapphire (page 107). He was harder on the pearl, "which is not a stone in any sense of the word," though it could be worn as "an emblem of purity" (page 321). The Russian list even had alexandrite, for August (page 315). More on our [birthstone chart](/birthstones/).

## Pearl, the June Birthstone That Is Not a Mineral

**The Stone.** [Pearl](/stones/pearl/) is the only June birthstone made by an animal. When an irritant gets so deep into a mollusk that it cannot push it out, the animal coats it with nacre, the lustrous layer that lines most shells. The irritant is usually microscopic, far smaller than a grain of sand. Nacre is aragonite, a calcium carbonate mineral, bound with an organic binder called conchiolin. Aragonite is on the IMA List of Minerals (July 2026 edition), and pearl is not.

Natural pearls are now extreme rarities. In the wild, the International Gem Society says, only one mollusk in several million makes one. The modern cultured pearl industry began in Japan around 1910, and a cultured pearl is still a real pearl. A farmer inserts a bead or a piece of tissue, and the mollusk coats it with nacre for one to six years.

**The Story.** **Tradition.** White "was associated with the moon and with Monday and was represented by the pearl" (Kunz 1913, page 30), and Kunz called the pearl the "queen-gem," an emblem of "modesty and purity" (pages 69 to 70). In 1917 Pierre Cartier traded a double strand of natural pearls for a mansion on Fifth Avenue. Pearl is the gem of the 3rd and 30th anniversaries.

## Moonstone, the Glowing Feldspar

**The Stone.** [Moonstone](/stones/moonstone/) is a variety of orthoclase, a feldspar. As it forms, orthoclase and albite separate into layers about as thin as a wavelength of light, and light scattered between them makes a glow that seems to float under the surface, called adularescence. Very thin layers glow blue, thicker ones white. The flashing white stones sold as "rainbow moonstone" are a different feldspar, labradorite, as our [rainbow moonstone page](/stones/rainbow-moonstone/) explains.

**The Story.** **Tradition.** In India moonstone was sacred, sold only on a yellow cloth, and a lovers' gift believed to "arouse the tender passion" (Kunz 1913, pages 97 to 98). In early 1900s Siam, white fabrics and moonstones were the prescribed wear for Monday (page 335).

## Alexandrite, Green by Day & Red by Lamplight

**The Stone.** [Alexandrite](/stones/alexandrite/) is the very rare color-change variety of chrysoberyl, which gem lovers call "emerald by day, ruby by night." It needs beryllium, one of the rarest elements on Earth, and chromium, two elements that rarely meet in the same rocks, and most cut stones weigh under 1 carat.

Alexandrite absorbs yellow light in a band near 580 nanometers, and that band lets its color shift with the light source, green to bluish green in daylight, red to purplish red under an incandescent bulb. The closer the shift comes to pure green and pure red, and the more of the stone that changes, the more it is worth.

**Try This.** Check the change at home.

1. Look at the stone by a window in daylight.
2. Close the curtains and look again under a warm incandescent bulb, since other lamps can show other colors.
3. Note how far the color moved and how much of the stone changed. Lab-grown alexandrite changes too, so this cannot prove natural origin.

**The Story.** **Tradition.** Kunz tells that it was found in 1831 on the day the future Czar Alexander II came of age, and that Russians took it as a good omen because red and green were their national colors (1913, pages 54 to 55). Alexandrite is the gem of the 55th anniversary.

## What June Birthstones Mean Today

**Modern Association.** In our rankings, moonstone is Classic for love, protection and calm in anxious moments, and Widely Linked for sleep. Pearl is Often Linked to protection and money. Alexandrite is Widely Linked to money, and its guides lean on change and balance. Some sites promise help with fertility or circulation. Nothing in the mineral or medical sources behind this page supports that.

### What Is the June Birthstone Color?

Cream, milky white and a color change. Pearl is best known in white and cream, moonstone runs from colorless to peach with a silvery to blue glow, and alexandrite turns from green to red. June splits between [Gemini](/zodiac/gemini/), until about June 20, and [Cancer](/zodiac/cancer/), and Kunz's zodiac verses give them the agate and the emerald (page 346).

## How to Choose a June Birthstone

### Which June Birthstone Fits Daily Wear

The Mohs scale ranks resistance to scratching from 1 to 10, with diamond at 10. Toughness is resistance to chipping and breaking, and cleavage is a tendency to split along flat planes when struck.

- **Alexandrite, for a ring you never take off.** Mohs 8.5, excellent toughness, no cleavage.
- **Moonstone, for earrings and pendants.** With two directions of perfect cleavage, one hard knock can split it. A ring needs a protective setting and calm days.
- **Pearl, for pendants, earrings and strands.** It scratches easily, though its toughness is usually good. Keep pearl rings for occasions, not dishes or the gym.

### June Birthstones for Real Situations

- **One ring for every day.** Alexandrite, natural or lab-grown sold as lab-grown.
- **A first piece for a baby.** One pearl or moonstone pendant, kept in the jewelry box until the child is older, since small pearls and beads are a choking hazard.
- **A tight budget.** Moonstone. Indian moonstones with strong body color are plentiful and affordable.
- **An inherited strand.** A strand older than the cultured industry may be natural, so get a lab report before you insure, sell or restring it. Antique jewelry is also now the main source of large natural alexandrites.

### What to Ask Before You Buy

1. **Is this pearl natural, cultured or imitation?** Under the FTC's Jewelry Guides, a cultured pearl must be sold as "cultured" or "cultivated," and a fake as "imitation," "simulated" or "artificial." Plain "pearl" means natural.
2. **Is this alexandrite natural or lab-grown?** A lab-grown stone must carry a word like "laboratory-grown" or "laboratory-created" before its name. For a natural one, ask for a gem lab report.
3. **Has it been treated, and how?** The FTC guides call for disclosure when a treatment is not permanent, needs special care or significantly affects value.
4. **Is this true moonstone, or a doublet?** A doublet is a quartz cap glued over a thin slice of moonstone.
5. **Will you write that on the receipt?**

Price follows rarity. Pearls are valued on luster, nacre thickness, shape, surface, color and size, with natural far above cultured. Alexandrite is priced first on its color change, then on size, and the International Gem Society warns that a bargain alexandrite is "likely not natural and possibly not an alexandrite."

**Try This.** Siam kept moonstones for Monday. Wear yours on Mondays, and as you put it on, name the one thing the week has to hold.

**Try This.** For an anniversary pearl, add a card. "A mollusk built this one thin layer at a time."

**Safety.** Pearls, small stones and bead strands are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Moonstone is often chosen for sleep, anxiety and fertility. A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. If poor sleep, anxiety or low mood lasts, talk to a doctor or a therapist, and bring fertility or pregnancy questions to a doctor or a midwife. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My June Birthstone Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone, and for pearl, "cultured" takes the place of lab-grown.

### Real or Fake Pearl

- **Natural, untreated.** Extremely rare.
- **Treated.** Bleaching is common, stable and undetectable. Dyeing is common too, and gamma radiation can turn pearls gray to blue-gray.
- **Cultured.** Real pearls grown on farms, with the same chemistry as natural ones.
- **Imitation.** Glass, shell or plastic with a pearly coating.
- **Trade name.** Akoya is the best-known cultured pearl, and Tahitian is used for black pearls.

1. **The tooth test.** Rub the pearl gently along the edge of your front teeth. A real pearl, natural or cultured, feels slightly gritty, because the edges of its tiny aragonite plates make the surface rough. Most imitations feel smooth.
2. **The drill hole.** Under a 10x loupe, a color layer below the nacre points to dye.

Only a gem lab, sometimes with X-rays, tells natural from cultured.

### Real or Fake Moonstone

- **Natural.** Under magnification, look for "centipedes," short paired cracks with smaller cracks branching off like legs, typical of moonstone.
- **Lab-grown.** Lab-created moonstone has not entered the market.
- **Imitation.** Milky chalcedony, some synthetic spinel, and quartz-moonstone doublets. In 1915 Kunz described US beach pebbles of quartz being cut and sold as moonstones (pages 19 to 20).
- **Trade name.** "Rainbow moonstone" is labradorite.

### Real or Fake Alexandrite

- **Natural.** Usually untreated, though some fractures are filled.
- **Lab-grown.** Made since the 1960s. It is real alexandrite, not natural, and much cheaper. Labs can sometimes spot it by curved growth lines, bubbles or specks of platinum.
- **Imitation.** Synthetic corundum with an alexandrite-like color change, made very cheaply.
- **Trade name.** Not every color-change chrysoberyl counts as alexandrite, and Kunz's "alexandrite sapphires" were sapphires (1915, page 402).

## How to Care for June Birthstones

| | Pearl | Moonstone | Alexandrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Washing | Not Enough Data | OK. Warm soapy water | OK. Warm soapy water, soft brush |
| Soaking | Not Enough Data | Brief Only | Brief Only |
| Heat | Not Enough Data | Not Enough Data | Keep Away From Heat |
| Chemicals | No Chemicals. Attacked by acids | Not Enough Data | Not Enough Data |
| Cleaning | Not Enough Data | OK. Skip ultrasonic and steam | OK. Ultrasonic per its instructions |
| Knocks and storage | Not Enough Data | Handle Gently. Scratches, splits on impact | OK. Durable in any setting |

Sun, salt and toxicity are Not Enough Data for all three.

### Why Pearl Needs Different Care

Pearl is organic and soft, so habits that suit a hard mineral can ruin it. Our data rates only chemicals so far, and GIA's published advice fills the gaps. Many chemicals and all acids damage pearl, as do hair spray, perfume, cosmetics and perspiration. High heat can burn or crack it, and heat from intense light can dry out the nacre.

### How to Clean a Pearl Ring or Strand at Home

1. **After each wear,** wipe the pearls with a very soft, clean cloth and store them in a soft pouch or case.
2. **Now and then,** dampen the cloth in warm water with a drop of mild soap and wipe each pearl. Never soak them.
3. **Let a strand dry** on a soft towel, and wear it only when the string is completely dry.
4. **Never use** an ultrasonic or steam cleaner, jewelry dip, vinegar, lemon juice or household cleaners.
5. **Put pearls on last,** after hair spray, perfume and makeup, and take them off before a workout.
6. **Restring when the silk darkens, stretches or wears thin.** Knots between pearls mean a break loses only one.

### How to Clean & Store Moonstone & Alexandrite

1. Wash the stone briefly in warm, soapy water with a soft brush, then rinse and dry it with a soft cloth.
2. Keep moonstone out of ultrasonic and steam cleaners. Alexandrite can take an ultrasonic cleaner but not steam, and only warm, soapy water if fracture-filled.
3. Keep alexandrite away from high heat. GIA calls it stable in normal wear, but the International Gem Society warns of extreme heat, hence the Keep Away From Heat rating.
4. Store each piece in its own soft pouch. At Mohs 8.5, alexandrite can scratch moonstone and pearl.

**Where the beliefs and the care data disagree.** Some crystal guides leave pearls in salt water overnight or charge June stones in the sun. Our data has no salt or sun rating for any of the three, so skip both, and keep pearls away from hot, dry spots like a sunny dresser. For a cleansing ritual, moonlight or sound keeps all three dry.

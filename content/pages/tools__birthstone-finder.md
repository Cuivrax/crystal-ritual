# What's My Birthstone? Birthstone & Zodiac Stone Finder

Your birthstone, or birth gemstone, is the gem of your birth month. On the modern US list from the American Gem Society, January is garnet, February amethyst, March aquamarine or bloodstone, April diamond, May emerald, June pearl, alexandrite or moonstone, July ruby, August peridot, sardonyx or spinel, September sapphire, October opal or tourmaline, November topaz or citrine and December tanzanite, turquoise or zircon. Your zodiac sign gives a second answer from a separate tradition, such as bloodstone for Aries or amethyst for Pisces in the verse list George Frederick Kunz printed in 1913.

Enter your birth date, or pick a month or a sign, and the finder shows both answers next to the older lists.

## How the Birthstone Finder Works

The finder looks up a stone by birth date with the same two charts as our [birthstones by month](/birthstones/) and [zodiac stones](/zodiac/) pages.

1. **The month gives your birthstone.** You get that month's row of the chart in three columns, the modern US list, the 1912 jewelers' list and older European usage. The day does not change it.
2. **The day gives your sign.** The finder matches the day to a sign by the dates most modern guides give, then shows its dates, its 1913 stone and the stones today's crystal guides link most, strongest first.
3. **Edges get a note.** A birthday on the line between two signs is flagged.
4. **The moon comes along.** A link opens the [Birthday Moon](/moon/birthday-moon/) for that date, the moon phase on the night you were born.

A month or a sign alone gives half the answer. Under every result sit the full 12-month and 12-sign charts, handy for checking a friend's birthday gemstones.

## Do Birthstones Go by Month or Zodiac Sign?

By month. A birthstone belongs to a month and a zodiac stone belongs to a sign, and Kunz already kept them apart in 1913, calling the sign stones "astral" or "zodiacal" stones. Every sign straddles two months, so each sign inherits two sets of month birthstones.

That is why "the [Capricorn](/zodiac/capricorn/) birthstone" has a two-part answer. A December birthday gets tanzanite, turquoise or zircon and a January one gets garnet. The sign itself gets ruby in the 1913 verses.

## Why Does My Month Have More Than One Birthstone?

Mostly price and taste. GIA's birthstone exhibit notes that differing traditions are often cited, but says "the more significant reason is that some gemstones are too rare and expensive, and others just aren't as popular." A second or third stone gives buyers another price and another look.

The trade added stones in waves. In 1952 the Jewelry Industry Council of America added alexandrite to June, citrine to November, pink tourmaline to October and zircon to December. Tanzanite joined December in 2002, and in July 2016 the American Gem Trade Association and Jewelers of America named spinel a birthstone for August. That is how [December](/birthstones/december/) ended up with three blue stones, while [January](/birthstones/january/) stays with garnet, which already comes in almost every color.

## Modern vs Traditional vs Zodiac Birthstones

The finder keeps five lists apart.

| List | Where It Comes From | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Modern US list | The American Gem Society, the current US trade list | Months |
| 1912 list | The National Association of Jewelers, Kansas City, August 1912 | Months |
| Older usage | Kunz's summary of European usage from the 15th to the 20th century | Months |
| 1913 zodiac stone | The verse list Kunz printed in *The Curious Lore of Precious Stones* | Signs |
| Linked Most Today | Today's crystal guides, strongest link first | Signs |

Most jewelers mean the 1912 list when they say traditional. Older writers like Kunz meant the European lists before it, so a December "traditional birthstone" can be turquoise on one site and ruby on another. The 1912 list moved the ruby to July, and Kunz objected that "there should be no duplication or triplication of birth-stones for any given month."

**Tradition.** The sign lists disagree even more. Kunz's verses give Sagittarius the topaz and Capricorn the ruby.

**Modern Association.** Today's guides often pick by color or by a stone's modern meaning. Neither sign list is a mineral fact, and no list is the correct one.

## What Is My Birthstone If I Was Born on a Cusp?

Your month birthstone stays the same, since it follows the calendar, and only the sign stone moves. Guides disagree by a day at the edges, and Kunz's 1913 tables draw some lines elsewhere. He runs [Aquarius](/zodiac/aquarius/) from January 21 to February 20, where most modern guides give about January 20 to February 18, and he starts Capricornus at the winter solstice.

The finder uses the modern dates and flags a birthday on a boundary. If yours is flagged, read both neighboring signs in the chart below, check the dates your own horoscope source uses, or wear the month stone and skip the question.

Astronomers also count a thirteenth zodiac constellation, Ophiuchus, between Scorpius and Sagittarius. Astrology's twelve signs leave it out, and so do these lists.

## Which Birthstone Should I Choose for a Ring or a Gift?

Pick the list you like, then think about wear. These notes use the same care data as the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

- **For a ring worn every day.** Sapphire, spinel, alexandrite and tourmaline all suit everyday wear.
- **With a protective setting.** Aquamarine resists everyday scratching very well but is brittle. Emerald is fragile despite its hardness, and zircon chips at the facet edges.
- **Better in earrings or a pendant.** Opal, tanzanite, turquoise and moonstone scratch, chip or break more easily, so they last longer away from a hand's daily knocks. Opal is also sensitive to moisture, so keep it dry.

Two care notes surprise people. Heat-treated blue zircon can turn brown in minutes under UV light, so take a blue zircon ring off before a tanning bed or a nail lamp. Some amethyst and citrine can fade with prolonged exposure to intense light, so a jewelry box beats a sunny dresser.

**Try This.** For a gift, copy the finder's result onto the card, the modern stone, the older one and the 1913 sign stone, then add the moon phase from the Birthday Moon. It turns one small gift into three stories.

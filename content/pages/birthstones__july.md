# July Birthstone Meaning, History & Honest Buying Tips

The July birthstone is ruby, the red variety of the mineral corundum and the only stone the American Gem Society lists for the month. Ruby is second only to diamond in hardness, and unless a seller says otherwise, assume it has been heated. It became the July stone in 1912, while older European lists gave July the onyx or the turquoise.

## What Is the July Birthstone?

Ruby, and only ruby, on today's US list. Older lists differ.

### Modern vs Traditional July Birthstones

July is one of the few months where "modern" and "traditional" agree. Today's list says ruby, and so did the 1912 list most jewelers mean by traditional. The older alternatives, turquoise and onyx, come from European usage before 1912. Onyx is the old favorite, on five of the eight lists Kunz compared (page 316).

### Does July Have More Than One Birthstone?

Not on today's US list. For a second stone, choose turquoise or onyx from the older lists, or a zodiac stone. Our [birthstone chart](/birthstones/) shows every month.

## Why Ruby Moved to July in 1912

**Tradition.** For centuries ruby belonged to December. When the National Association of Jewelers met in Kansas City in August 1912, it moved the ruby to July and gave its December slot to turquoise. The argument was that the warm red suited July, while pale turquoise suited a winter month "when the sun's rays are feeble" (Kunz 1913, page 320).

Kunz disagreed. "It is in winter that we seek for warmth," he wrote, and he called the swap a return to the old Polish list (page 320). Yet he named ruby "the gem of summer" among his seasonal gems (pages 323 to 324), and his own 1913 calendar kept turquoise for July. "The heav'n-blue turquoise should adorn / All those who in July are born" (page 329).

## Ruby, the July Birthstone

**The Stone.** [Ruby](/stones/ruby/) is gem-quality red corundum, an aluminum oxide. Pure corundum is colorless, and traces of chromium make it red. Every other color of gem corundum is sapphire, and gemologists do not agree on exactly where ruby ends and pink sapphire begins. Fine ruby is a pure, vibrant red to slightly purplish red, and it can glow in sunlight thanks to red fluorescence, the same glow that powered the first laser in 1960.

Myanmar's Mogok valley has been famous for rubies for centuries, and Mozambique, Vietnam, Sri Lanka, Tajikistan and East Africa produce them too. Fine rubies over 1 carat are very rare, and ruby can command the highest price per carat of any colored stone.

### Ruby Treatments in Plain Words

A treatment is any process that changes a gem's color, clarity or durability after it is mined.

- **Heat.** Assume it unless the seller says unheated in writing. Heating can dissolve the fine needles called silk and improve the color. It is stable and accepted when disclosed.
- **Lattice diffusion.** Color diffused into the stone, sometimes only near the surface, so chips or recutting can remove it.
- **Glass filling.** Surface-reaching fractures are filled, mostly with lead glass. There are large numbers of these rubies on the market, and they need extra care.
- **Dye.** Less common, and also needs gentle care.

**The Story.**

- **India.** **Tradition.** In Sanskrit ruby is ratnaraj, "king of precious stones" (Kunz 1913, page 101).
- **Medieval France.** **Tradition.** The *Lapidaire* of Philippe de Valois called the ruby "the lord of stones" and "the gem of gems" (page 102).
- **Burma.** **Tradition.** A ruby inserted in the flesh was believed to protect its bearer from spear, sword or gun (page 103).

## Turquoise & Onyx, the Older July Stones

**Turquoise.** [Turquoise](/stones/turquoise/) is an opaque copper aluminum phosphate. It is porous, most of it is treated, and since 1912 it has been a December stone, covered on our [December page](/birthstones/december/). **Tradition.** Kunz's list of American gems paired July with turquoise from New Mexico, California and Arizona (page 323).

**Onyx.** [Onyx](/stones/onyx/) is chalcedony, a quartz made of microscopic crystals, in straight parallel bands, classically black and white. Sardonyx is onyx with red and white layers. **Tradition.** Onyx was said to provoke discord and separate lovers, perhaps because of its stark black and white layers (Kunz 1913, pages 98 to 99), and its Arabic name, el jaza, means "sadness" (page 159).

## What July Birthstones Mean Today

**Modern Association.** In our rankings, ruby is Classic for love, protection and confidence and Often Linked for calm in anxious moments, with the heart and root chakras named most often. The protection thread is old, since the medieval ruby kept its owner from "all perils" (Kunz 1913, page 103). Turquoise is Classic for protection, and onyx is Classic for protection and calm and Widely Linked to grief.

Some sites say ruby helps the heart or circulation. Nothing in the mineral or medical sources behind this page supports that.

### What Is the July Birthstone Color?

Red. Ruby runs from orangy red to purplish red, and the trade calls the most coveted shade, a very slightly purplish red with vivid saturation, "pigeon's blood." July splits between [Cancer](/zodiac/cancer/), until July 22, and [Leo](/zodiac/leo/), and Kunz's zodiac verses give them the emerald and the onyx (page 346).

## How to Choose a July Birthstone

### Which July Birthstone Fits Daily Wear

The Mohs scale ranks resistance to scratching from 1 to 10, with diamond at 10. Toughness is resistance to chipping and breaking, and cleavage is a tendency to split along flat planes when struck.

- **Ruby, for a ring you never take off.** Mohs 9, excellent toughness and no cleavage, as long as it is not glass-filled.
- **Onyx, for rings and beads.** Chalcedony is tough and suits any jewelry.
- **Turquoise, for pendants and earrings.** It is soft and porous. In a ring, choose a high bezel, a metal rim that guards the sides and face, and wear it occasionally.

### July Birthstones for Real Situations

- **Born in July, one ring for every day.** Ruby. A heated ruby is durable, and accepted as long as the heating is disclosed.
- **An anniversary.** Ruby is the gem of the 15th and 40th anniversaries, turquoise of the 11th.
- **A tight budget.** A lab-grown ruby sold as lab-grown costs far less than a natural one and can look identical. Ask whether a cheap "natural" ruby is glass-filled, since many are sold.
- **An inherited ring.** Some heirloom ruby rings hold flame-fusion lab-grown stones, worth more in memories than in money.

### What to Ask Before You Buy a Ruby

1. **Is it natural or lab-grown?** Under the FTC's Jewelry Guides, a lab-grown ruby must carry a word like "laboratory-grown" or "laboratory-created" right before the name.
2. **Has it been heated?**
3. **Is it glass-filled, diffused or dyed?** The FTC guides call for disclosure when a treatment is not permanent, needs special care or significantly affects value.
4. **Is there a lab report?** A gem lab can say whether a ruby is natural or lab-grown, and whether it shows signs of treatment.
5. **Will you write all of that on the receipt?**

Color matters most to a ruby's price, followed by size, clarity, treatment and origin. Unheated stones with vivid color sell for more than heated ones, and Myanmar stones usually fetch the most.

### How to Check an Inherited Ruby Ring

1. Hold the ring over white paper under a lamp, for soft, even light, and look through the stone with a 10x loupe, a small jeweler's magnifier.
2. Look for growth lines. Natural corundum shows only straight ones, while flame-fusion stones show curved ones, though the curve is subtle.
3. Look for silk and tiny crystals. Lab-grown corundum usually lacks them, but heat can dissolve silk, so a clean stone proves nothing.
4. Take it to a gem lab for a firm answer, since modern lab-grown rubies can fool trained professionals.

**Try This.** The medieval ruby was said to reconcile disputes. Before a hard conversation, turn your ruby ring once, take one slow breath, then say your first sentence.

**Safety.** Small stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

## Is My July Birthstone Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone.

### Real or Fake Ruby

- **Natural, untreated.** Rare and worth more. Heating shows only when inclusions carry signs of heat stress, so a truly clean stone gives no clue.
- **Natural, treated.** Heated, diffused, glass-filled or dyed.
- **Lab-grown.** Made since the late 1800s. Auguste Verneuil published his flame-fusion method in 1904, and flux and hydrothermal growth are also used. Recent ones can have natural-looking color and inclusions.
- **Imitation.** Red spinel and garnet have long passed for ruby, and the Black Prince's Ruby in the British crown jewels is a red spinel. Blood-red cabochons of all three were once called carbuncles. See [garnet vs ruby](/compare/garnet-vs-ruby/).
- **Trade name.** "Pigeon's blood," "Burmese" and "Thai" describe colors, not proof of origin. Ruby zoisite is a rock with opaque ruby in green zoisite.

Rubies glow red under a UV flashlight, Myanmar stones strongly and iron-rich Thai stones hardly at all. That is a clue to origin, not a test of real or fake.

### Real or Fake Turquoise & Onyx

- **Turquoise.** Most is stabilized with wax, oil, resin or epoxy. Reconstituted turquoise is ground turquoise mixed with plastic, Gilson made a lab-grown version in 1972, and blue-dyed howlite is a common fake.
- **Onyx.** Black onyx is dyed chalcedony as a rule, and dyeing should be disclosed. Kunz noted that so-called "Mexican onyx" is not onyx at all but an aragonite stalagmite (page 251).

## How to Care for July Birthstones

| | Ruby | Turquoise | Onyx |
|---|---|---|---|
| Washing | Not Enough Data | Brief Only. Porous | OK. Warm water, mild soap |
| Soaking | Not Enough Data | Brief Only | Brief Only |
| Sun and light | Not Enough Data | Brief Only. Store away from bright light | Not Enough Data |
| Heat | Not Enough Data | Keep Away From Heat | Keep Away From Heat |
| Chemicals | Not Enough Data | No Chemicals | No Chemicals |
| Cleaning | Not Enough Data | Brief Only. Damp cloth, very mild soap | OK. Skip ultrasonic and steam |
| Knocks and storage | Not Enough Data | Brief Only. Store apart | OK. Tough |

Salt and toxicity are Not Enough Data for all three.

### Why Ruby Care Depends on Its Treatment

Our data has no ruby ratings yet, so here is GIA's published advice. Untreated and heated rubies are very durable, and corundum resists heat, light and common chemicals in normal wear. Glass-filled, cavity-filled and dyed rubies are another story, since even mild acids like lemon juice can damage them.

### How to Clean a Ruby Ring at Home

1. **Find out what you have.** Check the receipt or lab report for heating, diffusion, glass filling or dye.
2. **Untreated, heated or diffused.** Wash it in warm, soapy water with a soft toothbrush, rinse and dry. Ultrasonic and steam cleaners are usually safe for these.
3. **Glass-filled, dyed, or not sure.** Wipe with a damp cloth only. No machines, no lemon juice, no household cleaners.
4. **Store it apart.** At Mohs 9, ruby will scratch turquoise, onyx and other softer gems.

### How to Clean Turquoise & Onyx

1. **Turquoise.** Wipe with a soft cloth dampened in a very mild soap solution, then with a cloth dampened in plain water, and let it dry. Put it on after lotion and perfume, and store it in a box away from bright light.
2. **Onyx.** Wash in warm water with mild soap and a soft brush, rinse and dry. Skip ultrasonic and steam cleaners, and keep it away from heat, which one seller says can make it lose its bands.

**Where the beliefs and the care data disagree.** Some crystal guides cleanse ruby under running water and then in the sun, or in salted water. Our data has no ruby rating for water, sun or salt, and glass-filled rubies can be harmed by chemicals, so skip the salt bowl. Turquoise rates Brief Only for water and sun, so skip running water and the sunny sill. For a cleansing ritual, moonlight or sound keeps all three dry.

# Sagittarius Crystals, Birthstones & Where the Links Come From

There is no official Sagittarius birthstone, because birthstones belong to months and Sagittarius runs from late November to December 21. The stones most often named are topaz, which an old zodiac rhyme gives to the sign, and turquoise, a December birthstone that modern crystal guides tie to Sagittarius more than to any other sign. Both links are beliefs, from astrology and from modern crystal books.

## What Is the Sagittarius Birthstone?

Birthstones and zodiac stones are two different lists, and Sagittarius sits on the seam. If you were born in the last days of November, your birthstone is [topaz or citrine](/birthstones/november/). From December 1 to 21 it is [tanzanite, turquoise or zircon](/birthstones/december/), the modern US list from the American Gem Society.

Zodiac stones follow the sign instead of the month. **Tradition.** In the zodiac rhymes George Frederick Kunz printed in 1913, the verse for Sagittarius runs from November 21 to December 21 and says "their true gem should ever show a topaz" (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, page 347). An old Spanish list he reproduced on the same page, which he believed reflected Arab tradition, gives Sagittarius the emerald instead.

So when a site names one Sagittarius birthstone, it is choosing. Topaz has the best-known old claim. Turquoise is both a birthstone of Sagittarius season and a favorite of modern crystal guides.

### A Quick Note on the Dates

Kunz's table runs the sign from November 21 to December 21, and horoscope sources do not all use the same dates, so check the ones you follow. The sign is also not the same thing as the constellation. Astrology's signs were mapped long ago, and because of a slow shift called precession, they no longer line up with the star patterns that share their names. The constellation Ophiuchus sits between Scorpius and Sagittarius. Western astrology ties its signs to the seasons instead, starting Aries at the March equinox, so your sign stays the same either way.

## Where the Sagittarius Links Come From

**The Story.** The links between Sagittarius and stones come from two very different eras, an old astrological layer and a young crystal one.

- **The breastplate and the months.** In the 1st century, the historian Josephus wrote that the twelve stones of the Jewish high priest's breastplate could stand for the months or for the twelve signs of the zodiac (Kunz 1913, pages 309 to 310). That idea is the root of both birthstones and zodiac stones.
- **An early horoscope gem.** Kunz describes a banded agate, then in the Metropolitan Museum of Art, engraved with Sagittarius as a centaur surrounded by the stars of the constellation. The collector C. W. King called it the earliest horoscope gem he knew (Kunz 1913, page 342).
- **Planets and their stones.** **Tradition.** An old print Kunz reproduced pairs Jupiter with Sagittarius and Pisces, and the carnelian, for Thursday (page 332). In the old planetary lists, Jupiter controlled the emerald, sapphire, amethyst and turquoise (page 348). Modern crystal guides still name Jupiter as the planet of Sagittarius, which gives turquoise a second reason to show up.
- **The modern layer.** **Modern Association.** Most of today's Sagittarius stones come from crystal books and websites of recent decades, built on the sign's personality in modern astrology and on the colors of the November and December birthstones.

### How Modern Guides Describe Sagittarius

**Modern Association.** Crystal guides describe Sagittarius as optimistic, adventurous and hungry for knowledge and truth, a fire sign that loves to travel. Guides then pick stones for two opposite jobs. Some are said to feed those traits, like azurite for the sign's quest for truth in one French guide. Others are said to steady them, like smoky quartz for what one guide calls the sign's "grounding drive," or silver sheen obsidian, which another says brings a sometimes naive optimism back to reality. One guide even says charoite helps "this flighty sign to ground down."

That split explains why lists disagree so much. A stone can be "for" Sagittarius because it matches the sign or because it balances it.

## Which Stone Should a Sagittarius Choose?

Start with the birthday. A late November Sagittarius gets the warm side of the season, topaz and [citrine](/stones/citrine/). A December Sagittarius gets the blues, tanzanite, turquoise and zircon. If you want one stone for the whole sign, topaz works twice, as the November birthstone and as Kunz's zodiac stone.

Then think about wear. Topaz is the hardest of the group at 8 on the Mohs scale, but it can split along its cleavage, so a protective setting helps. Citrine is a quartz, and most citrine on the market is heated amethyst, which is a treatment, not a fake. Turquoise, sodalite and azurite are softer and happier in pendants and earrings than in a ring you wear every day.

Before any cleansing ritual, look the stone up in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/). Azurite slowly loses its blue in air, heat and light, blue zircon can turn brown under UV, and turquoise does not like long soaks.

## How to Use Sagittarius Stones in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** Whatever you believe about the stars, here is what a small ritual does for sure. It gives a busy season a few pauses you chose yourself.

### The Holiday Travel Stone

**Try This.** Sagittarius season is travel season for a lot of people. **Tradition.** From the 1200s, European writers said turquoise protected its wearer from falls, especially from horseback (Kunz 1913, pages 108 to 109). Borrow the idea for a modern trip. Keep a small turquoise in your coat pocket, and every time your fingers find it at a gate or a station, check the three things you always forget, like your ID, charger and keys. The stone is the reminder, the checklist does the protecting. Turquoise is porous, so wipe it dry if it gets damp.

### One Question a Week

**Try This.** For a sign that guides call hungry for knowledge, keep a sodalite or a piece of lapis on the nightstand with a small notebook. Each Sunday night, write one question you actually want answered. Look it up during the week and write the answer under it. By the next birthday you have a notebook of things you learned.

### A Birthday Card with Both Lists

**Try This.** Shopping for a Sagittarius? Give the birthstone of their month and tuck in a card with the zodiac story. "Your birthstone is turquoise. Your old zodiac stone is topaz, from a rhyme printed in 1913." It turns one stone into two stories.

**Safety.** Azurite and lapis lazuli never go in water you drink. Azurite is a copper mineral, and its cutting dust is toxic, though wearing finished azurite is considered safe. Small tumbled stones are not toys, so keep them away from small children and pets.

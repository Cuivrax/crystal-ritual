## Tonight's Moon

The panel names tonight's phase, shows how much of the moon is lit and counts the days to the next full moon. The lit share is the same everywhere on Earth at a given moment. Only the clock time changes.

The new moon, first quarter, full moon and last quarter are single moments, and the panel gives that whole day the phase's name, as printed almanacs do. The moon also looks full to the eye for about three nights in a row. At 9 PM New York time on December 22, 2026, the night before the Cold Moon, it is already more than 98 percent lit.

Times follow the method of Jean Meeus in *Astronomical Algorithms* (1998) and agree with US Naval Observatory times to within about a minute.

## Stones Tied to the Moon

No old source we know of gives each phase its own stone. They do record a handful of stones tied to the moon itself, many of them pale, glowing or clear.

### What the Old Books Say

**Tradition.** Every row comes from George Frederick Kunz's two books on gem lore (1913 and 1915), now in the public domain.

| Stone | What the old record says | Where |
|---|---|---|
| [Moonstone](/stones/moonstone/) | Sacred in India and sold there only on a yellow cloth. As a lovers' gift, it was believed to show a couple their future if held in the mouth at the full moon | *The Curious Lore of Precious Stones* (1913), pages 97 to 98 |
| Moonstone | An old print pairs Diana, the sign of Cancer and the moonstone with Monday. In Siam, white clothes and moonstones were worn on Mondays | 1913, pages 332 and 335 |
| Moonstone | Pope Leo X was said to own one that brightened as the moon waxed and dimmed as it waned. Kunz calls such stories fables | *The Magic of Jewels and Charms* (1915), pages 386 to 387 |
| [Selenite](/stones/selenite/) | Antoine Mizauld kept a "selenite or moonstone" for a lunar month and watched a white mark on it grow and shrink with the moon | 1913, page 98 |
| Selenite | Rabelais gave an oracle's temple a column of transparent selenite with the moon inside it, "full and new, waxing and waning" | 1913, page 349 |
| Rock crystal ([clear quartz](/stones/clear-quartz/)) | Old astrologers saw an affinity between the moon and rock crystal, and the waxing moon was said to be the best time for crystal gazing | 1913, page 212 |
| Rock crystal, pearl & beryl | In a list of planetary stones, the moon "controls the beryl, the rock-crystal and also the pearl" | 1913, pages 347 to 348 |
| White jade | Chosen to adorn the Manchu emperors' girdles for the ceremonies at the Altar of the Moon | 1913, page 245 |
| Turquoise | A Persian saying held that seeing the new moon reflected on a friend's face, a Koran or a turquoise brought good fortune | 1913, page 111 |
| Lapis lazuli | A Babylonian hymn calls the moon god Sin "bright as lapis-lazuli" | 1913, page 92 |

**Safety.** Please keep any stone out of your mouth, full moon or not. Small stones are a choking hazard.

Two honest notes. The old "selenite" may not be the gypsum sold under that name today, since older writers used the word for more than one moon-like stone. And Kunz blamed cleavage planes for moonstone's silvery light (1913, page 334). **Mineral Fact.** [GIA](https://www.gia.edu/moonstone/gem-overview) explains it as light scattering between microscopic layers of feldspar, an effect called adularescence.

### What Crystal Guides Say Today

**Modern Association.** Rated on our five-facet scale, which shows how widely a belief is held, not whether it works ([how we rate](/sources/#how-we-rate)).

- **Full moon.** Moonstone is the Classic full moon stone. [Rainbow moonstone](/stones/rainbow-moonstone/) and peach moonstone are Widely Linked, often through the same Kunz stories retold. Obsidian and strawberry quartz are Widely Linked too, but mostly as stones to "recharge" in full moonlight. [Citrine](/stones/citrine/) is Sometimes Linked, the same way.
- **New moon.** Moonstone, black moonstone and peach moonstone are Sometimes Linked, as stones for an intention. Rainbow moonstone is Rarely Linked.
- **Waxing and waning moon.** No stone is linked with any consistency, so the panel says so rather than inventing a list.

Most guides split the month in two, the full moon for release and the new moon for intentions. One label to know before you buy. Rainbow moonstone is technically a variety of labradorite, not moonstone, according to the [International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/moonstone-jewelry-gem-information/).

### Before You Leave a Stone on the Sill

Moonlight is gentle, but the morning sun on the same sill is not. Bring stones in before it arrives, since some [amethyst](/stones/amethyst/) and citrine can fade with prolonged exposure to intense light, according to GIA ([amethyst care](https://www.gia.edu/amethyst-care-cleaning), [citrine care](https://www.gia.edu/citrine-care-cleaning)). Outdoors, dew counts as water, and selenite, a form of water-soluble gypsum, stays inside ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/gypsum-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). Moonstone can break on a sharp knock because of its perfect cleavage, so set it down gently ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/moonstone/)). Check your own stone in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Moon Water, the Safe Way

**Safety.** Never put a stone in water you drink. Set it beside the jar.

Fill a clean glass jar, close it, set it in the moonlight with your stone beside it and bring everything in the next morning. Our [moon water guide](/rituals/moon-water/) lists the stones that must never touch water you drink, malachite and pyrite among them, and the ones water can harm, such as selenite and opal.

## Four Looks a Month

**Try This.** One minute on each principal phase day in the strip above.

**You need.** A window, a small notebook, and the stone you keep for the moon, if you have one.

1. **Find the moon**, or the patch of sky where it should be. On the new moon there is nothing to see, which is fine.
2. **Take three slow breaths** with the stone in your hand, letting each one out longer than you took it in.
3. **Write one line.** New moon, what you want to start. First quarter, what is in the way. Full moon, what you are done carrying. Last quarter, what you will keep.

**Why it helps.** Whatever you believe about the stone, here is what the ritual does for sure. It gives you four quiet minutes a month and a short record of how the month went. **Modern Association.** Starting at the new moon and letting go at the full moon follows the split most crystal guides use.

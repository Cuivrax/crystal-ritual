# Crystals for Money & Abundance with a Weekly Budget Ritual

The crystals most linked to money are green aventurine, pyrite and citrine, followed by ammolite, epidote, moss agate and jade. Their wealth reputations come from gold and green colors, names and old luck customs, not from any effect on a bank balance. One on your desk makes a good cue for a weekly 10-minute money check-in.

## Crystals for Money at a Glance

**Modern Association.** Modern crystal guides tie these stones to money, luck and abundance most firmly, in this order. All of them are Classic, the top of a scale that runs down through Widely Linked, Often Linked, Sometimes Linked and Rarely Linked. It measures how common the belief is, not whether it works.

| Stone | How Often It Is Linked | Why People Link It | Care Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green aventurine | Classic | Green, and called the stone of opportunity | Washable, no harsh chemicals |
| Pyrite | Classic | It looks like gold | Brittle and heat sensitive, tarnishes |
| Citrine | Classic | Golden yellow, nicknamed the merchant's stone | Mostly heated amethyst, intense light can fade it |
| Ammolite | Classic | A rainbow fossil sold as a prosperity stone | Thin and delicate, never in water you drink |
| Epidote | Classic | Green, and read as growth | Sensitive to heat and acids |
| Moss agate | Classic | Called the gardener's stone in modern lore | No care data yet |
| Jade, green & blue | Classic | Chinese emblems of riches and long life | Ask about dye and resin |

Green jade and blue jade rank separately in our data. They share one card here, since both are jade. Before any stone meets water, salt or a sunny window, look it up in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Why These Stones Get Linked to Money

**The Story.** Money stones are mostly gold or green. Here is what each layer of the belief left in writing.

- **Gold.** **Mineral Fact.** Pyrite earned its reputation honestly, by looking like gold. Its nickname is fool's gold ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/pyrite-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)), yet some pyrite holds 0.25 percent gold or more by weight, enough to make it worth mining for that gold ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/pyrite.shtml)). Citrine's golden color earned it the nickname merchant's stone in shop guides ([Fire Mountain Gems](https://www.firemountaingems.com/learn/citrine-gemstone-meanings.html)). Some sellers add that Roman merchants carried it for trade, but that story is modern. Pliny the Elder, whose *Natural History* gives a whole book to gems, describes no stone that can be shown to be citrine, and his 19th-century translators read his nearest yellow gems as other stones ([Book 37, Bostock and Riley translation, Perseus, Tufts University](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0137%3Abook%3D37%3Achapter%3D42)).
- **1913, China.** **Tradition.** Kunz describes an early 18th-century carving in kingfisher-green jadeite of a "hand-of-Buddha" citron, which the Chinese regarded as "a most felicitous emblem, denoting at once a long life and abundance of riches for its enjoyment." A bat carved on the leaves added good fortune, since the Chinese character fu means both bat and happiness (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, page 85).
- **Green and growing.** **Modern Association.** Moss agate's plant-like patterns have made it the gardener's stone of modern crystal lore ([Crystal Gemstones](https://crystalgemstones.net/green-moss-agate-meaning-and-spiritual-properties.html)), and shop guides carry the same idea of slow, steady growth over to business.
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Feng shui-inspired guides place a prosperity stone in the home's "wealth corner", but they do not agree on where that is. One says the far left corner from the front door ([Crystals.com](https://www.crystals.com/blogs/news/citrine-meaning-properties)), others say the southeast ([Solacely](https://www.solacely.co/en-eu/blogs/crystals/pyrite-meaning), [Crystal Gemstones](https://crystalgemstones.net/ammolite-meaning-and-spiritual-properties.html)).

**What Research Says.** No study we know of has tested a stone for money, luck or wealth. In a 2025 trial on anxiety, scores fell only among people who believed in crystal healing, and a look-alike stone did as well as rose quartz ([PubMed 40855750](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40855750/)). If a money stone helps, it helps through what you do while it sits on the desk. For the wider history of luck charms, see [lucky crystals](/crystals-for/luck/).

## A 10-Minute Weekly Money Check-In

**The Ritual.** Whatever you believe about the stone, here is what the ritual does for sure. It puts your money in front of you once a week, before small problems grow.

**When to use it.** The same day and time every week. Sunday evening is an easy slot.

**You need.** Your banking app or statements, a notebook, a pen, an index card and one stone from the list.

**Steps.**

1. **1 minute.** Pick up the stone, sit down and open your account. No other tabs.
2. **3 minutes.** Look at what came in and what went out this week. Write three numbers, money in, money out and money left.
3. **3 minutes.** Circle one purchase you are glad you made and one you would skip next time. Notes, not guilt.
4. **2 minutes.** Check the next 7 days for bills and due dates, and set one reminder.
5. **1 minute.** Choose one small move for the coming week, such as moving a little into savings, canceling a subscription or calling about a bill. Write it on the card and set the stone on top.

**Why it helps.** The stone sits on that card all week, so every time you see it you see your one move. It holds your place, you do the work.

**Try This.** Before a salary talk or a call to dispute a charge, give yourself a 1-minute pre-call ritual. Hold the stone, breathe out slowly three times, say your number or your request out loud once, then put the stone down and dial.

**What Research Says.** Tying the check-in to the turn of the week borrows from research on fresh starts, not on stones. Three archival field studies, which tracked Google searches for "diet", visits to a university gym and commitments made on a goal-setting website, found that people took up their goals more often right after dates that feel like a clean slate, such as a new week, month or year, a birthday or a holiday (Dai, Milkman and Riis, 2014, *Management Science*, [doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901)). The data were observational, so they show a pattern rather than a cause, and the studies did not test stones or money rituals.

## Crystals for a Money Bowl, Wallet or Cash Drawer

For a money bowl, a wallet or a cash drawer, pick hard stones that do not mind coins and keep the fragile ones on a shelf. Shop guides suggest a tumbled citrine or green aventurine in the wallet or near the point of sale ([Crystals.com](https://www.crystals.com/blogs/news/citrine-meaning-properties)), and citrine rates 7 on the Mohs scale, green aventurine 6.5 to 7. Jade is even tougher, and GIA calls it very resistant to breaking or chipping ([GIA](https://gia.edu/jade-care-cleaning)).

Leave ammolite, whose thin layers can separate with a jolt, and epidote, which has perfect cleavage, out of a bowl of loose coins. Pyrite tarnishes, so a dry spot is the cautious choice, and citrine is best kept out of a sunny window.

**Safety.** Coins and small tumbled stones are choking hazards for babies, young children and pets, so keep the bowl out of reach. Pyrite and ammolite never go in water you drink. If a pet chews on pyrite, keep it away from the stone and ask your vet, since our care data has no toxicity rating for pyrite yet.

## The Money Stones, One by One

### Green Aventurine

**Mineral Fact.** Green aventurine is a translucent quartz rock that sparkles with flakes of fuchsite, a chromium-rich mica, and India is by far its most important producer. Goldstone, a man-made glass with metal flecks, is its most common imitation, and dyed quartz is sold as aventurine too ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/aventurine/)). **Modern Association.** Classic for money, "one of the most sought-after" crystals for luck and abundance, kept in a wallet or on a desk ([Crystals.com](https://www.crystals.com/blogs/news/green-aventurine-meaning-properties)). Gem Rock Auctions passes on the idea that a stone in your left pocket helps you win the lottery ([Gem Rock Auctions](https://gemrockauctions.com/learn/a-z-of-gemstones/aventurine-stone)). We would keep it for the budget, not the ticket. **Care.** Warm soapy water and a soft cloth, no harsh household chemicals and no prolonged extreme heat ([GemSelect](https://www.gemselect.com/gem-info/aventurine/aventurine-info.php)). More on [green aventurine](/stones/green-aventurine/) and on [goldstone](/real-or-fake/goldstone/).

### Pyrite

**Mineral Fact.** Pyrite is a brass-yellow iron sulfide with a bright metallic luster and the most common sulfide mineral. Its name comes from the Greek for fire, because it throws sparks when struck ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/pyrite.shtml)). **Tradition.** Kunz notes that some North American Indians used pyrite crystals as amulets, and that ancient Mexicans polished it into mirrors (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, page 99). **Modern Association.** Classic for money, called "the Stone of Abundance", with a cube on the desk or in a business space ([Crystals.com](https://www.crystals.com/blogs/news/pyrite-crystal-meaning)). **Care.** The International Gem Society calls it very brittle and heat sensitive. Our Care Checker has no water rating for pyrite yet, but Geology.com notes that it tarnishes easily and oxidizes when exposed to air and moisture, so a cube that darkens over time is not unusual. A dry shelf and a dry cloth are the cautious choice. More on [pyrite](/stones/pyrite/).

### Citrine

**Mineral Fact.** Citrine is yellow to brownish orange quartz colored by a trace of iron, and most citrine on the market is heated amethyst ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/citrine/gem-overview)). The International Gem Society calls that heat treatment stable and fully accepted in the gem trade. It shares November with topaz on the modern birthstone list ([American Gem Society](https://www.americangemsociety.org/birthstones/)). **Modern Association.** Classic for money. Shop guides call it the merchant's stone and suggest a tumbled piece in the wallet, near the point of sale or in the wealth corner ([Crystals.com](https://www.crystals.com/blogs/news/citrine-meaning-properties)). **Care.** GIA says some citrine color can fade with long exposure to intense light ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/citrine-care-cleaning)), and the International Gem Society says heat-treated stones may fade with heat, so skip steam cleaning and the sunny sill ([IGS](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/citrine-jewelry-gemstone-information/)). More on [citrine](/stones/citrine/) and on [real citrine vs heated amethyst](/real-or-fake/citrine/).

**Mineral Fact.** Ammolite is gem material made from the fossil shells of certain ammonites, found only in the Bearpaw Formation, mainly in southern Alberta. Its colors come from light interfering in thin layers of aragonite, and it was recognized as an organic gem only in 1981. Because the colored layer is so thin, it is often sold as a doublet or triplet ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/ammolite-jewelry-gem-information/)). **Tradition.** The International Gem Society gives its name in Siksika, the Blackfoot language, as aapoak, "small, crawling stone." The Galt Museum & Archives in Lethbridge, Alberta, says the Blackfoot have known these iridescent fossils for hundreds of years, called the material iniskim, "buffalo stone," and used it as a talisman ([Galt Museum](https://galtmuseum.com/articles/2017/08/ammolite-history.html)). Dealers, including Korite, the largest commercial supplier, retell it as a talisman of good fortune ([Korite](https://korite.com/ammolite/)). **Modern Association.** Classic for money, often called a prosperity stone in feng shui-inspired practice ([Crystal Gemstones](https://crystalgemstones.net/ammolite-meaning-and-spiritual-properties.html)). **Care.** Keep it away from acids, perfume and hairspray, prolonged sun and high heat, and do not soak it, since our care data covers only a quick wash with warm soapy water. The International Gem Society's toxicity table rates ammolite's risk as high ([IGS care guide](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/gemstone-care-guide/)), so it stays out of drinking water and out of children's hands. More on [ammolite](/stones/ammolite/).

### Epidote

**Mineral Fact.** Epidote is a green silicate mineral that owes its color to iron, and it is mostly a collector's stone ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/epidote.shtml)). In shops you also meet it inside unakite, a rock of green epidote, quartz and pink orthoclase that is widely cut into cabochons and spheres ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/epidote-group-of-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). **Modern Association.** Classic for money, read as a stone of "growth, abundance and amplification" ([Crystal Gemstones](https://crystalgemstones.net/epidote-meaning-and-spiritual-properties.html)). **Care.** The International Gem Society says epidote group gems are sensitive to heat and acids, and their perfect cleavage makes earrings and pendants safer than rings. Clean it with a soft brush, mild detergent and warm water only. More on [epidote](/stones/epidote/) and [unakite](/stones/unakite/).

### Moss Agate

**Mineral Fact.** Moss agate is chalcedony, a fine-grained form of quartz, with mineral oxide inclusions in plant-like patterns. The "moss" only gives the impression of vegetation, says the International Gem Society, which lists India, Scotland and the northwestern United States among its sources ([IGS](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/agate-gem-information/)). **Modern Association.** Classic for money, a stone said to draw "new business and gradual expansion" ([CrystalVaults](https://www.crystalvaults.com/crystal-encyclopedia/moss-agate/)), with the gardener's-stone lore above behind it. **Care.** Our care data has no water, sun or salt rating for moss agate yet, so a soft dry cloth is the cautious choice until the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/) has one. Agate in general is porous and often dyed ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/agate/)), so ask the seller whether the color is natural. More on [moss agate](/stones/moss-agate/).

### Jade, Green & Blue

**Mineral Fact.** Jade is a cultural name for two very durable materials, jadeite and nephrite. Jadeite can be bleached and filled with resin (Type B) or also dyed (Type C), and "Mexican jade" is green-dyed calcite ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/jade/)). Natural blue jadeite is rare. The International Gem Society names Guatemala as the source of rare blue stones, a color once favored by the ancient Maya, and lists dyeing among jadeite's typical treatments, with glass, plastic and other lookalikes sold as jade worldwide ([IGS](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/jadeite-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). Before you pay for blue jade, ask in writing whether it is jadeite or nephrite and whether the color is dyed. **Tradition.** The kingfisher-green jadeite citron that Kunz described stood for long life and riches. **Modern Association.** Green jade and blue jade are both Classic for money. Shop guides say businessmen carry jade in their pockets during negotiations ([Moonrise Crystals](https://moonrisecrystals.com/green-jade-meaning/)) and that ancient traders held it in the right hand while doing business ([CrystalVaults](https://www.crystalvaults.com/crystal-encyclopedia/blue-jade/)). **Care.** Warm soapy water is always safe for jade, but warm acids can harm it ([GIA](https://gia.edu/jade-care-cleaning)), and the resins and dyes in treated jadeite can discolor or fade with heat and light ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/jade/)). More on [jade](/stones/jade/), [green jade](/stones/green-jade/) and [blue jade](/stones/blue-jade/).

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** A stone is not a financial plan, and no crystal changes what is in your account. If debt or bills keep you up at night, a nonprofit credit counselor can go through the numbers with you. If money worries affect your sleep or mood for weeks, talk to a doctor too. And if betting or spending starts to feel out of your control, talk to your doctor or a counselor, whatever any shop guide says about lucky stones.

# Blue Amber Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Blue amber is real amber, fossilized tree resin, mostly from the Dominican Republic, that looks blue in daylight when you hold it over something dark. The blue is fluorescence at the surface, not color inside, so on a white table the same piece looks yellow to orange. Its calming and protective meanings are modern beliefs.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Real blue amber looks yellow to orange on a white background and turns blue over black in daylight. A bead that looks blue on white is not showing this effect. Amber floats in strong salt water and most plastics sink. Only a lab can confirm where it was mined.

## What Blue Amber Actually Is

**The Stone.** Amber is fossilized tree resin, an organic gem rather than a mineral. It is not a fossil in the strictest sense, since its organic material is never replaced by minerals. Instead, the resin turns into a polymer over time and pressure, a natural plastic. Amber comes in more colors than "amber," and rare pieces can even be blue or green. Our [amber](/stones/amber/) page covers the whole family.

"Blue amber" is a name from the Chinese gem trade, where this amber is highly sought after. A 2020 *Gems & Gemology* study defines it as transparent yellow amber from the Dominican Republic, Mexico and Myanmar with "a blue or greenish blue glow when viewed on a black background in normal sunlight." Almost all commercial blue amber comes from those three countries. Dominican blue amber comes from the resin of an extinct tree, *Hymenaea protera*.

### Why Does Blue Amber Look Blue?

Because of fluorescence, light given off when ultraviolet strikes the surface. Daylight carries ultraviolet, and in this amber it wakes a greenish blue glow. Amber absorbs ultraviolet completely and short-wave blue light strongly, so the glow stays at the very surface and cannot change the body color inside, a 2014 study found. A compound called perylene has been proposed as the source of the glow.

### Why Does My Blue Amber Look Yellow?

Because the glow is weak next to ordinary reflected light. Against a light background, only the yellow to orange body color is easy to see. A black background reflects less light back at you, and the blue shows through. Thickness matters too. In the 2014 study, the body color was yellow where the amber was under 3 mm thick, orangy yellow at about 3 to 6 mm, orange at 6 to 8 mm and reddish orange to orangy red beyond 8 mm.

### Where Blue Amber Comes From

The main Dominican deposits formed in a single basin 15 to 20 million years ago. Most Dominican amber comes from two zones, north of Santiago de los Caballeros and northeast of Santo Domingo. It is mined by hand, often from wells more than 10 meters (about 33 feet) deep dug inside a hillside pit, and blue amber turns up only now and then among the other kinds.

Dominican blue amber generally glows much more strongly than Mexican or Burmese material, though all three look very similar. Sicily also produces amber that sometimes shows opalescent blue or green.

### Is Dominican Amber Really Amber?

Gem references treat it as amber, though one age line says otherwise. The International Gem Society calls preserved resin younger than 30 million years copal, yet lists the Dominican Republic as an amber source. A 2014 study in GIA's journal *Gems & Gemology* dates the main Dominican deposits at 15 to 20 million years and calls the material amber. See [amber vs copal](/compare/amber-vs-copal/).

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** The name is recent, and Bellani and colleagues first described blue amber scientifically in 2005. The older stories belong to amber in general, and several are about the sun.

- **Ancient Greece.** **Tradition.** The Greeks called amber elektron, "made by the sun." In Greek poetry, grains of amber were the tears of the Heliades, grieving for their brother Phaëthon on the banks of the Eridanus, the river Po. For Nicias, amber was the essence of the setting sun's rays, congealed in the sea (Kunz, *The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, pages 55 to 56).
- **About 600 BCE.** **Tradition.** The philosopher Thales noticed amber's electric pull, the start of the study of electricity, Kunz writes (*The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, 1915, page 63).
- **Ancient Egypt.** **Tradition.** Egyptians buried amber in tombs for the afterlife.
- **Folk belief.** **Tradition.** For centuries, amber necklaces were believed to protect children from colds. Kunz's own guess was that a bare throat simply stayed at a steady temperature (1915, preface, page viii).

**Safety.** That old belief has a dangerous modern form. Never put an amber teething necklace or bracelet on a baby. The US Food and Drug Administration warned parents and caregivers against teething jewelry in December 2018. Its [consumer advice](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/safely-soothing-teething-pain-infants-and-children) still cites reports of deaths and serious injuries to infants and children, including strangulation and choking, caused by teething jewelry such as amber teething necklaces.

## What People Use Blue Amber for Today

### What Does Blue Amber Mean?

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal practice, blue amber means calm, protection, harmony and spiritual growth. In modern crystal guides it is Widely Linked with [protection](/crystals-for/protection/) and Often Linked with [anxiety](/crystals-for/anxiety/).

The "why" is mostly color and history. Blue reads as calm and cool where yellow amber reads as warmth, and amber's protective reputation goes back to ancient talismans. In Japan, amber's "stone words" are embrace, long life and prosperity.

Sellers go further. One calls blue amber a "negative ion emitter" that fights stress and boosts immune defenses, and others list sleep, depression, joint pain and teething. Nothing in the gem sources behind this page supports those claims.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Blue amber is often chosen for stress, anxiety, sleep and low moods, and amber for a teething baby. A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. Talk to a doctor or a therapist if anxiety, poor sleep or low mood lasts, and to your pediatrician about teething pain. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## How to Use Blue Amber in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** This one does not depend on believing anything. The blue is real physics.

### The One-Minute Window Break

**Try This.** Keep a square of black cloth with your blue amber. Once a day, at a moment when your attention sags, carry both to a bright window. Lay the amber on the cloth and watch the yellow stone turn blue. Then put both away and go back to work.

**What Research Says.** In a lab study, people who briefly switched to a second task during a long, dull one kept their performance steady, while those who stayed on it without a break got steadily worse (Ariga and Lleras, 2011, *Cognition*, [doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.12.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.12.007)). It was a short lab task and the break was a second task, not a stone, so treat the window minute as a personal practice.

### A Gift With the True Story

**Try This.** For Dominican blue amber, add a card. "Amber from a tree that lived 15 to 20 million years ago, now extinct. The blue is light, not dye. Hold it over something black near a window."

**Safety.** Small beads are not toys. Keep amber away from small children and pets, out of any drink, and away from candles, since amber "may be lighted like a torch," in the words of an old account Kunz quotes (1915, page 60).

## Is My Blue Amber Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone.

- **Natural, untreated.** Amber with natural blue fluorescence. Most blue amber on the market shows an even blue or greenish blue across the whole surface.
- **Natural, treated.** Heating can darken amber to desirable colors and create sparkly "star spangles," and dyeing is another common treatment for amber.
- **Lab-grown.** What is sold as synthetic amber is pressed amber, also called ambroid, made from small bits fused under heat and extreme pressure, or plastic.
- **Imitation.** Plastic and glass, plus copal, a younger resin that looks almost the same.
- **Trade name.** "Blue amber" is itself a trade term for a fluorescent look, not a separate gem, and the honest name is amber from a named country.

Four checks you can do at home, with their limits.

1. **Do the white and black test.** In daylight, set the piece on white paper, then on black cloth. Real blue amber looks yellow to orange on white and turns blue or greenish blue on black. A piece that is blue on white is not showing fluorescence, so ask about dye, a coating, plastic or glass. The limit is that some real Dominican amber shows too little glow to turn blue at all.
2. **Use a 365 nm UV flashlight in a dark room.** Dominican and Mexican blue amber glow blue, Burmese sometimes violetish blue, and short-wave ultraviolet gives a much weaker response. A glow shows fluorescence, not amber, so pair it with the next check.
3. **Float it in salt water.** Dissolve as much salt as will go into hot water, which gives a liquid of about 1.13 specific gravity, and let it cool. Drop in a loose stone, not one on a cord or in metal. Amber floats, while most plastics sink. Some plastics with a density as low as 1.05, or with air bubbles inside, float too. Take it out as soon as you see whether it floats, then rinse it in plain water and dry it at once, since salt has no care rating yet.
4. **Rub it on wool.** Real amber picks up a static charge strong enough to lift tiny bits of ash or paper (Kunz 1915, page 54). It is a safe test, but a clue rather than proof.

Skip the hot needle and the acetone drop. The hot point test damages the piece, and our care data marks amber No Chemicals.

What no home check can do is prove origin. One lab test needs slices thinner than 0.5 mm, impossible on jewelry, so researchers proposed fluorescence mapping, which leaves the stone intact.

Ask the seller four things. Is it amber or copal? Where was it mined? Has it been heated, dyed, coated or filled? Can I see it on black in daylight before I pay?

## How to Care for Blue Amber

**Mineral Fact.** At 2 to 2.5 on the Mohs scale, the 1 to 10 scratch-hardness scale gemologists use, amber is highly susceptible to scratches, so pendants, earrings and necklaces fare better than rings, which need protective settings.

- **Water.** Brief Only. A damp cloth, or a quick wipe with warm water and mild detergent.
- **Soaking.** Brief Only, which in practice means do not soak. Never use ultrasonic, steam or any other mechanical cleaner.
- **Sun.** Not Enough Data. We found no reference source on whether sunlight weakens the blue. Natural amber often darkens with age toward red-brown.
- **Heat.** Keep Away From Heat. Amber burns, and heat is also used to darken it.
- **Chemicals.** No Chemicals. Solvents and alcohol can partly dissolve the surface and cause permanent damage, so put on perfume and hand sanitizer before your jewelry.
- **Salt.** Not Enough Data.
- **Storage.** Brief Only, see the steps below.
- **Toxicity.** Brief Only. The International Gem Society's toxicity table rates amber a high risk, with the hazard given only as organic material, so keep it out of drinks and mouths.

### How to Clean Blue Amber Jewelry at Home

1. Wipe the amber with a soft cloth dampened in lukewarm water with one drop of mild soap.
2. Wipe again with a cloth dampened in plain water.
3. Dry it at once with a soft, lint-free cloth.

### How to Store Blue Amber

1. Give each piece its own soft pouch, away from harder stones and metal clasps.
2. Keep it away from radiators, sunny dashboards and hot lamps.
3. Take amber rings off for chores, the gym and anything that could scratch them.

**Where the beliefs and the care data disagree.** One guide suggests charging amber in sunlight for a few hours, then warns that long sun may affect its color. Sun has no rating yet, so the window minute above is plenty. Check your piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/), which shows OK, Brief Only, a warning that names the test such as Keep Dry, or Not Enough Data with the reason and the source.

## Blue Amber vs Look-Alikes

- **Baltic amber.** The Kaliningrad region on the Baltic is the most significant amber source in the world. See [Baltic amber](/stones/baltic-amber/).
- **Indonesian blue amber.** Real amber that glows greenish blue too, sometimes over a red body color. See [Indonesian blue amber](/stones/indonesian-blue-amber/).
- **Copal.** It usually glows whiter under short-wave ultraviolet than amber, a call that takes experience. See [copal](/stones/copal/).

## Price Guide

Amber is sold by weight in grams, and one gram is about 5 carats. Clearly visible insects command a premium, as do light colors, clarity and size. For blue amber, origin and the strength of the glow matter too. A 2020 *Gems & Gemology* study reported that in Chinese trading centers, Dominican blue amber sells for far more per gram than Mexican material that looks almost the same. Since origin cannot be seen by eye, a Dominican premium deserves a written origin on the receipt.

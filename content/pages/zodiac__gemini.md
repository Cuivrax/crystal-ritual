# Gemini Crystals & Birthstones, Plus Where the Links Come From

Gemini has no single official birthstone, because birthstones belong to months and Gemini runs from about May 21 to June 20. That gives emerald for a May birthday and pearl, alexandrite or moonstone for a June one. As a zodiac stone, a 1913 list gives Gemini the agate, and agate and its lace varieties still lead today's crystal guides, next to celestite, citrine and alexandrite, all beliefs rather than mineral facts.

## What Is the Gemini Birthstone?

The honest answer has two parts, the month stone and the sign stone.

**The month stone.** On the modern list of the [American Gem Society](https://www.americangemsociety.org/birthstones/), May's birthstone is emerald, and June has three, pearl, alexandrite and moonstone. The 1912 jewelers' list gave June the pearl with moonstone as its alternate (Kunz 1913, page 319), and alexandrite joined them in 1952, when the Jewelry Industry Council of America revised the list. Our [May](/birthstones/may/) and [June](/birthstones/june/) pages go stone by stone.

**The sign stone.** **Tradition.** In the verse list of zodiac gems that George Frederick Kunz printed in 1913, Gemini gets the agate. "Gemini's children health and wealth command, / And all the ills of age withstand, / Who wear their rings on either hand / Of agate" (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, page 346). Rings on either hand suit a sign of twins, though that is our reading of the rhyme, and its promises are a rhyme's, not a doctor's. An old Spanish list that Kunz thought probably reflected Arab tradition gives Gemini the sapphire instead (page 347).

Here is the twist. Kunz's own calendar of the months pairs May with Gemini and gives it the emerald, while June goes with Cancer and gets the agate (pages 328 to 329). The two lists in the same book swap agate and emerald between Gemini and [Cancer](/zodiac/cancer/), whose 1913 verse stone is the emerald. Give a Gemini an agate and you follow the zodiac rhyme. Give an emerald and you follow his calendar.

## What Color Is the Gemini Birthstone?

The Gemini birthstone has no single color, and June alone covers three looks. Emerald is green, pearl is best known in white and cream, moonstone glows with a white to silvery sheen, and alexandrite changes color, green to bluish green in daylight and red to purplish red under incandescent light. The 1913 agate comes in bands of brown, white, red, gray, pink, black or yellow. If you want one color for the sign, the modern picks lean pale blue and yellow, from blue lace agate and celestite to citrine.

## Gemini Crystals in Today's Guides

**Modern Association.** Guides describe Gemini as an air sign ruled by Mercury, curious, talkative and sometimes restless, and like many signs it gets stones for two opposite jobs. Some are said to feed the talk, like blue lace agate for a sign ruled by "Mercury, the planet of speech in astrology," or citrine, which one guide says "supports quick thinking and communication." Others are said to settle it. A German guide gives Gemini agate for "grounding of restless energy," and a Spanish one says citrine brings the sign's thousand ideas down to earth and gives it focus. Here are the usual zodiac stones for Gemini, with what each stone really is.

### Agate & Its Lace Varieties

**Widely Linked.** [Agate](/stones/agate/) is a translucent, usually banded chalcedony, a microcrystalline quartz, rated 6.5 to 7 and durable enough for most jewelry. [Blue lace agate](/stones/blue-lace-agate/), with its sky-blue bands, was first found on a farm in South West Africa in 1962, according to one dealer guide, and [crazy lace agate](/stones/crazy-lace-agate/) is a tightly banded agate from Chihuahua, Mexico, one of the best-known agate varieties. Most of the spectacularly colored agate in the trade is dyed, an old and usually stable treatment.

Agate is also the Gemini stone with a line from the old books to the modern lists. **Tradition.** Marbodus, an 11th-century bishop of Rennes, wrote that agates make their wearers "agreeable and persuasive" (Kunz 1913, page 51), not far from the talkative sign today's guides describe.

### Celestite

**Widely Linked.** [Celestite](/stones/celestite/) is strontium sulfate, named from the Latin for heavenly after its usual sky blue. It is very soft, 3 to 3.5 on the Mohs scale, and its blue may fade with heat or light, so it is a shelf stone, not a ring stone. Pale blue geodes come from Madagascar and Sicily. One guide says it brings "balance and tranquility" to Gemini and Cancer alike.

### Citrine

**Widely Linked.** [Citrine](/stones/citrine/) is yellow to brownish orange quartz colored by a trace of iron, and most citrine sold today is amethyst heated until it turned gold. Heated is a treatment, not a fake. It is a November birthstone, and a few guides also give it to [Sagittarius](/zodiac/sagittarius/).

### Alexandrite

**Widely Linked.** [Alexandrite](/stones/alexandrite/) is the very rare color-change variety of chrysoberyl, rated 8.5 and durable in any setting. It was discovered in Russia's Ural Mountains in the 1830s. Lab-grown alexandrite has existed since the 1960s, and a bargain-priced stone is likely not natural. Of the three June stones, it is the one modern guides tie to Gemini most, since, in one guide's words, the sign's "symbol of the twins fits a gem with two faces."

### Goshenite & Zoisite

**Widely Linked.** [Goshenite](/stones/goshenite/) is colorless beryl, named after Goshen, Massachusetts, and rated 7.5 to 8. It is the same mineral as the May emerald, minus the color. [Zoisite](/stones/zoisite/) is a calcium aluminum silicate mineral best known in its blue form, which is sold as tanzanite, and most tanzanite is heat-treated brown zoisite.

### Emerald, Pearl & Moonstone

**Rarely Linked.** The month stones of Gemini season hardly appear in modern Gemini lists. [Emerald](/stones/emerald/) is green beryl, rated 7.5 to 8, and most cut stones are oiled or filled. [Pearl](/stones/pearl/) is an organic gem made by a mollusk, only 2.5 to 3 on the Mohs scale, and cultured pearls are real pearls. [Moonstone](/stones/moonstone/) is a feldspar with adularescence, a soft glow that seems to float under the surface. Guides give moonstone, and to a lesser degree pearl, to [Cancer](/zodiac/cancer/) instead.

## Where the Gemini Links Come From

**The Story.** Gemini's old stones follow Mercury and its day, Wednesday.

- **Medieval Jewish tradition.** **Tradition.** The tribe of Zebulun was paired with Gemini (Kunz 1913, page 314).
- **Old color lore.** **Tradition.** Mercury and Wednesday, "the day of Mercury," were both "typified by green," and the typical green stone was the emerald (page 31).
- **Old print.** **Tradition.** An old print of the Roman day gods pairs Mercury with Gemini and the rock crystal, for Wednesday (page 332).
- **Old weekday rituals.** **Tradition.** In a set of occult rituals Kunz describes, Wednesday was the day for the "Works of Science," with a green robe, a necklace of hollow glass beads filled with quicksilver and a ring "adorned with an agate" (page 336).
- **1913.** **Tradition.** Kunz's verse list names the agate (page 346), and the old Spanish list the sapphire (page 347).
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** The agate family, goshenite, celestite, citrine and alexandrite lead the modern lists, most of them linked to Mercury and talk.

One possible thread, and it is our reading rather than a documented link, is that agate reached Gemini through Mercury. The 1913 rhyme gives the sign agate, and the old Wednesday ritual put an agate on Mercury's day.

## How to Use Gemini Stones in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** Whatever you believe about the stones, these give a busy mind a pause you chose and something in your hand to mark it.

### Two Stones for Two Ideas

**Try This.** Keep two small stones on your desk, an agate for the task you are on and a second stone for ideas you are parking. When a new idea interrupts, write it on a sticky note, put it under the parking stone and go back to the agate. Guides call the sign restless, and this keeps every new idea without losing the old task.

### A Wednesday Idea Review

**Try This.** The old print gave Wednesday to Mercury and Gemini. Every Wednesday at lunch, read the notes under your parking stone. Keep one idea, schedule it, and throw the rest away.

### A Birthday Card for Either Twin

**Try This.** For a May Gemini, give an emerald and explain the swap, since it is the stone of Kunz's calendar and of Cancer's 1913 verse. For a June Gemini, an alexandrite carries the two-faced story the guides love.

**Safety.** No stone goes in water you drink. Celestite is soft and light sensitive, so keep it out of the bath and off the windowsill. Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them. These are personal practices, not treatments.

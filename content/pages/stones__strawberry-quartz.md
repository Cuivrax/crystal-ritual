# Strawberry Quartz Meaning & What It Really Is

Strawberry quartz is a trade name for pink to red quartz colored by tiny red inclusions, often iron oxides such as hematite. Crystal lovers link it with love, joy and calm. The honest note is that the name has no strict gemological definition, and stones sold under it range from natural included quartz to aventurine, heated quartz and plain glass.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Look closely with a 10x loupe. Natural strawberry quartz shows red flecks, needles or platelets suspended inside the quartz. Tiny round gas bubbles mean glass. Flat, glittery flakes in a grainy pink body point to pink aventurine, a different quartz rock often sold under the same name. An even color proves nothing either way, since the finest inclusions only show under a microscope.

## What Strawberry Quartz Actually Is

**The Stone.** Strawberry quartz is a name, not a mineral. In 2021, *Gems & Gemology*, the journal of the Gemological Institute of America, stated that "strawberry quartz" is not a strictly defined term in gemology. It is a novelty gem and a marketing term for pink to red quartz or quartzite colored by visible mineral inclusions.

Underneath is quartz, silicon dioxide at 7 on the Mohs scale, and the classic material is quartz with vein-like red iron oxide inclusions.

### What Gives Strawberry Quartz Its Color?

Tiny grains of other minerals. They include small platelets of hematite, flakes of lepidolite or of alurgite (an orangy red mica), and crystals of piemontite, a rare manganese mineral. Among iron oxides, four can do the job, lepidocrocite, goethite, limonite and hematite.

Grain size decides the look. In some pieces the inclusions are so fine that only a microscope shows them, and the stone looks evenly pink. Others show bold reddish needles, flakes or glints within clearer quartz.

### Three Different Things Sold as Strawberry Quartz

1. **Quartz crystals with red inclusions.** The classic kind. Examples include a terminated crystal from near Chihuahua, Mexico, packed with tiny needles of reflective red hematite, and a very light amethyst from Ontario's Thunder Bay District with red inclusions that appear to be hematite.
2. **Pink aventurine.** Granular quartz rock, called quartzite, tinted by flakes of pink mica and often marketed as "pink aventurine" or "strawberry aventurine." A bangle sent to a Taipei lab as strawberry quartz turned out to be quartz rock with pink to red muscovite mica flakes, which the authors of a 2021 report identified as pink aventurine quartz.
3. **Heat-treated quartz.** In its Spring 2014 issue, *Gems & Gemology* reported mottled pink and white "strawberry" quartz from Minas Gerais, Brazil, seen at the 2014 Tucson shows, heat treated only, which "at first blush looks nothing like the plain brownish-gray material it once was."

### Where It Comes From & Is It Rare?

Dealers say most comes from Russia and Kazakhstan, with more from Brazil, and known examples add Mexico and Ontario. Strawberry quartz is rare, yet it remains an inexpensive gem. The name, though, is easy to find on aventurine, heated quartz and glass, so look closely.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** Strawberry quartz has no ancient lore. Kunz's two books on gem lore (1913 and 1915) never mention it, and shop guides place it in the jewelry trade only in the second half of the 20th century.

- **1960s to 1970s.** Shop guides date its discovery to Mexico in 1960, to Mexico and Africa in the 1970s or to South Africa around 1970.
- **Late 20th century.** It "first got popular in the jewellery market in the late 20th century," says one jeweler.
- **2014 and 2021.** *Gems & Gemology* reports heat-treated "strawberry" quartz, then notes that the term is not strictly defined.
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Love, joy and calm, as told in modern crystal guides.

### Is There Ancient Strawberry Quartz Lore?

No. A few guides mention "ancient healing practices" or a mythology of fertility without naming a text or a culture. Others say plainly that a stone found so recently has no myths or legends, and they have the record on their side.

It does have a young culture of its own. One Japanese power stone guide gives it the stone words love, youth and charm.

## What People Use Strawberry Quartz for Today

**Modern Association.** Modern crystal guides make strawberry quartz a Classic stone for [love](/crystals-for/love/), for calm in anxious moments and, more surprisingly, for protection. It is Widely Linked with money, focus and confidence, and only Sometimes Linked with sleep.

The "why" is mostly color. Pink-red reads as the heart, so guides place it on the heart chakra and give it rose quartz's love theme, or as one puts it, rose quartz's unconditional love plus "joy, gratitude, and emotional healing." One jeweler adds that in the East the red flecks are "seen as a symbol of good fortune," which may explain the money link. Libra is the sign named most, and one guide ties that to Venus, its ruling planet.

### Green Strawberry Quartz

Green strawberry quartz is a seller's name, not a defined variety. Strawberry quartz is described as pink to red, so a green piece sold under that name is some other material. If you buy one, ask the seller which mineral makes it green and whether the color was added.

### What Is Strawberry Quartz Good For?

As a material, jewelry, cut into cabochons, beads, bangles and faceted gems. Everything else is belief, and even one dealer calls the metaphysical claims "rooted in traditional beliefs and folklore." Some guides promise fertility, immune support, detox or rosier skin. Mineralogy and medicine do not support those claims.

## How to Use Strawberry Quartz in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** Strawberry quartz is a small, cheerful stone, with small rituals to match. None of them needs you to believe anything about energy.

### Three Good Things at Bedtime

**Try This.** Keep a tumbled piece on the nightstand. Before lights out, hold it and name three good things from the day, out loud or on paper. One shop suggests holding the stone "while listing your blessings for the day." The list is the practice. The stone makes sure you remember it.

### Count the Red Flecks

**Try This.** When a day gets loud, hold the stone under a lamp and count five red flecks, one slow breath per fleck, each breath out longer than the breath in. It doubles as practice for spotting real inclusions.

### A Valentine That Tells the Truth

**Try This.** Wrap a strawberry quartz pendant with a card that says what it is, quartz with tiny red iron oxide crystals inside, and that one Japanese guide gives it the words love, youth and charm.

**Safety.** Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

**What Research Says.** Counting good things has been tested, without stones. In three randomized studies by [Emmons and McCullough (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.84.2.377), two with college students and one with 65 adults with neuromuscular disease, people who listed what they were grateful for reported better well-being than comparison groups, most clearly in positive mood. The results were self-reported and nobody held a stone, so the stone here is only the cue.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Strawberry quartz is often chosen for anxiety, past hurt or loss. A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. If anxiety, grief or low mood lasts or feels heavy, talk to a doctor or a therapist. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My Strawberry Quartz Real?

### Is Strawberry Quartz Natural or Man-Made?

Both are sold under the name. One dealer warns that "not everything labeled as strawberry quartz is the real deal," since some is man-made, "usually just colored glass." We use the same five labels on every stone.

- **Natural, untreated.** Quartz with visible red inclusions such as hematite, the material the name was made for.
- **Natural, treated.** Heat-treated "strawberry" quartz from Brazil, reported in 2014. Colored quartz in general can also be dyed. A purple or neon pink "strawberry quartz" deserves a question.
- **Lab-grown.** Synthetic quartz comes in every color of the rainbow.
- **Imitation.** The most common imitation is glass with hematite or another red material added, and tiny gas bubbles can give it away. The "fruit quartz" names, cherry, blueberry, kiwi and pineapple quartz, cover man-made glass. See [is cherry quartz real](/real-or-fake/cherry-quartz/).
- **Trade names.** "Red fire" quartz is another name for quartz with red iron oxide, and "strawberry aventurine" is pink aventurine. One Japanese shop warns that "African strawberry quartz" is not strawberry quartz at all.

Glass sold as "imitation strawberry quartz" or "strawberry quartz glass" is an honest imitation, and glass knowingly presented as natural is a fake.

Three checks you can do at home, with their limits.

1. **Look for bubbles.** Under a loupe, tiny round gas bubbles point to glass, though well-made glass can hide them.
2. **Look at the inclusions.** Distinct red needles or flakes inside the quartz fit strawberry quartz, and flat flakes glittering together in a grainy body fit aventurine. Very fine inclusions only show under a microscope.
3. **Judge the color.** Synthetic quartz often comes in unnatural colors, so a purple or neon stone is worth a second look.

Ask the seller whether it is quartz, aventurine or glass, and whether it was heated or dyed. The [Label Decoder](/tools/crystal-label-decoder/) turns the words on a tag into what they usually mean.

## How to Care for Strawberry Quartz

**Mineral Fact.** Quartz is very resistant to chemicals and heat. The ratings below come from rose quartz and quartz in general, since no care guide covers strawberry quartz on its own.

- **Water.** OK for washing. Warm, soapy water is always a safe method for rose quartz.
- **Soaking.** Brief only. Care guidance covers washing, not a long soak.
- **Sun and heat.** OK. Rose quartz is generally stable when exposed to light and heat.
- **Chemicals.** OK, since quartz is very resistant to chemicals.
- **Salt.** Unknown. There is no reliable guidance yet, so keep it away from salt.
- **Cleaning.** OK by hand. Skip ultrasonic and steam cleaners.
- **Scratches and storage.** Unknown. There is no specific guidance yet.
- **Toxicity.** OK to wear and handle. Gem toxicity notes rate wearing quartz jewelry as negligible risk, while cutting or grinding it is high risk from dust.

### Can Strawberry Quartz Go in the Sun or in Water?

Yes to both, within limits. Several guides warn that strong sun fades it or darkens its inclusions, but our Care Checker rates sun OK, because GIA finds rose quartz, its closest pink cousin, generally stable to light, and our care data wins over the guides. If a seller cannot say whether a piece is natural, keep it out of strong sun anyway, since glass, dye and aventurine are different materials.

Wash it with soap and a soft brush, keep soaks short, skip salt, and keep every stone out of water you drink. ## Strawberry Quartz vs Look-Alikes

### Is Strawberry Quartz the Same as Rose Quartz?

No, they are two different materials. Rose quartz is pink throughout from microscopic fibers of a mineral similar to dumortierite, and it is nearly always cloudy. Strawberry quartz gets its color from inclusions you can often see, and good pieces sparkle with red specks. See [rose quartz](/stones/rose-quartz/).

### Strawberry Quartz vs Red Aventurine

Aventurine is quartz with abundant shiny inclusions of minerals such as mica or hematite, in colors that include red-brown. The pink or red kinds are grainy rock whose flat flakes catch the light together, while classic strawberry quartz is clearer quartz with red specks floating inside. Both are real quartz. See [pink aventurine](/stones/pink-aventurine/).

### Fire Quartz vs Strawberry Quartz

The names overlap. Quartz with red iron oxide is sold as strawberry quartz or as "red fire" quartz, so the two labels can cover the same material. See [fire quartz](/stones/fire-quartz/) and [hematoid quartz](/stones/hematoid-quartz/).

- **Cherry quartz.** Man-made, see-through glass sold under a fruit name.
- **Lepidolite.** A pink to purple lithium mica, pearly and only 2.5 to 3.5 on the Mohs scale, so a soft, flaky pink stone is not quartz.

## Price Guide

Strawberry quartz is very popular and abundant, with material of superb color and appearance rare, and it remains an inexpensive gem. A price follows how close a piece comes to the best.

- **Color.** The most desirable pieces have a distinctly pink or red color with no trace of other hues, even across the face of the gem.
- **Inclusions.** They should be large enough to see but not so large that surface-reaching ones leave a noticeable pit in the surface. Reflective inclusions that give a glittery or cat's-eye effect appeal to some buyers.
- **Form.** It is cut into cabochons, beads, bangles and faceted gems, so compare like with like.
- **Treatment.** Heat-treated "strawberry" quartz from Brazil was offered at the 2014 Tucson shows, so ask whether a piece was heated.
- **Glass and borrowed names.** The most common imitation is glass mixed with hematite or another red material, which should be sold as "imitation strawberry quartz" or "strawberry quartz glass," and pink quartzite is often marketed as "pink aventurine" or "strawberry aventurine." A quartz price should buy quartz.

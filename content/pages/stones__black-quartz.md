# Black Quartz Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Black quartz is usually morion, smoky quartz so dark it looks black, colored by natural radiation acting on traces of aluminum inside the crystal. Labs can darken clear quartz the same way. Crystal sellers tie it to grounding and protection, which are modern beliefs, and the stone itself is ordinary, durable quartz.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** A crystal point that is very dark brown to black fits morion. Opaque, perfectly even black beads are a different question, since most black chalcedony sold today is dyed. Nothing on a tag tells you whether the color is natural or lab-made, so ask the seller.

## What Black Quartz Actually Is

**The Stone.** Quartz is silicon dioxide, one of the most common minerals on earth. Smoky quartz is its brown variety, in every shade from light tan to nearly black. Very dark brown material is known as morion or cairngorm, the second name from the Cairngorm Mountains of Scotland. "Black quartz" is the shop label for that darkest end. Our [smoky quartz page](/stones/smoky-quartz/) covers the lighter shades, and the [quartz page](/stones/quartz/) the whole family.

### What Makes It Black?

Radiation. Natural radiation from the surrounding rock activates color centers, tiny defects in the crystal, around aluminum impurities in the quartz. Radioactive mineral deposits in many parts of the world are associated with very dark smoky quartz, which was probably colored by those minerals. Amethyst gets its purple in a similar way, with iron in place of aluminum.

### Natural or Lab-Made?

Both exist. All quartz can be irradiated to the morion color. Labs use cobalt-60 gamma rays, X-rays or electrons, the dose can be controlled to vary the shade, and the result is not always even. It is not often done, though, because natural smoky quartz is inexpensive and abundant, and synthetic smoky quartz is seldom made for the same reason.

Treatment can also go the other way. Natural smoky quartz with a very dark color is sometimes heated to lighten it.

### Is Black Quartz Radioactive?

Natural morion got its color from radiation in the rock around it. For treated gems, a 1988 *Gems & Gemology* review by Charles Ashbaugh named three laboratory sources, cobalt-60 gamma rays, linear accelerators, which use electrons, and nuclear reactors, and found that radioactivity is induced by two of them, the accelerator and the reactor. He noted that colorless quartz is irradiated to produce smoky quartz, and wrote that "2 nCi of any radioactive nuclide per gram of material can be judged to be harmless and safe," nCi being nanocuries, a unit of radioactivity. In the United States, companies that distribute newly irradiated gems must be licensed by the Nuclear Regulatory Commission and hold the stones until any radioactivity decays to a safe level. More in [is my crystal radioactive](/care/radioactive-crystals/).

### Where It Comes From

Smoky quartz grows mainly in quartz veins and pegmatite dikes, bodies of rock that cut across igneous and metamorphic rocks. Brazil is the current source of most smoky quartz used in jewelry, and Madagascar also produces it. Pikes Peak in Colorado is famous with collectors for smoky quartz crystals paired with blue-green amazonite. Smoky quartz also grows large, and the Smithsonian holds cut gems of 4,500 carats from California and 1,695 carats from Brazil, though larger stones get very dark and opaque.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** The history belongs to dark smoky quartz under its older names.

- **12th century, China.** **Tradition.** Flat pieces of smoky quartz were used to make eyeglasses, some to shield the eyes from bright light. A few were worn by judges in court to hide their facial expressions.
- **Crystal gazing.** **Tradition.** Kunz described a sphere of smoky quartz in the British Museum, which arrived there in 1700 with the Cottonian Library, as one of the crystals Dr. John Dee used in his crystal-gazing experiments. He suspected cairngorm was chosen because of Scottish superstitions about its virtues (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, page 190).
- **Victorian era.** **Tradition.** Dark brown smoky quartz from the Mourne Mountains of Ireland was often used in mourning jewelry.
- **Scotland and New Hampshire.** **Tradition.** Smoky quartz is the national gem of Scotland, under the name cairngorm, and on May 31, 1985, it became the official state gem of New Hampshire.

One shop says smoky quartz was sacred to the Druids and that black quartz was believed to offer a spiritual link to Hekate, goddess of magic, the night and the moon. It gives no source for either, so treat both as shop folklore.

## What People Use Black Quartz for Today

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal guides, black or morion quartz is a grounding and protection stone, tied to the root chakra. On our five-facet scale it is Sometimes Linked for [grounding](/crystals-for/grounding/), protection, anxiety, sleep, focus, love and confidence. Scorpio and Capricorn are the signs named most often, and the element is earth. One Swiss shop credits it with smoky quartz's virtues "in an amplified form," which is the whole logic of the meaning, darker color, stronger version.

Sellers place it by the front door against negative energy, on a desk for focus or in the bedroom for pleasant dreams. One suggests setting it near a computer or television to shield your body from electronic devices, and another sells it for detox and quitting addictions. Gem references and medicine give no support to either claim.

## How to Use Black Quartz in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** These rituals ask for no belief at all. The habit is the point.

### The End-of-Workday Shutdown

**Try This.** At the end of the workday, write tomorrow's first task on a sticky note, set the black quartz on it and close the laptop. The stone stays on the note overnight as a sign that work is parked. In the morning, lift it and start with that task.

### A Stone by the Door

**Try This.** Keep a black quartz point in the dish where your keys land. On the way out, touch it and check keys, phone and wallet. Quartz at 7 on the Mohs scale holds up well in a key dish, though it can scratch softer stones beside it.

### A Gift With Roots

**Try This.** For a friend with Scottish family or a New Hampshire address, a dark smoky quartz makes a gift with a real story. Write on the card that it is cairngorm, Scotland's national gem and New Hampshire's state gem, and ask the seller whether its color is natural.

**Safety.** Small stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

**What Research Says.** A 2018 review by Nicholas Hobson and colleagues argues that rituals help people regulate emotions, goals and social connection through both their physical steps and the meaning people give them ([*Personality and Social Psychology Review*](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868317734944)). It is a framework drawn from earlier studies, many of them lab studies with students, not a test of its own, and none involved stones.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Black quartz is sold for anxiety, sleep, detox and quitting addictions. A stone can be a cue for a habit, but it is not treatment. For anxiety, sleep problems or substance use, talk to a doctor or a therapist, and never stop or delay medical treatment because of a stone. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My Black Quartz Real?

- **Natural, untreated.** Morion darkened by radiation in the earth.
- **Natural, treated.** Clear quartz irradiated in a lab until it turns morion-dark. Ask, since a tag may not say.
- **Lab-grown.** Synthetic quartz is made in every color, but synthetic smoky quartz is seldom produced.
- **Imitation and other stones.** Opaque black beads sold as quartz may be black chalcedony, a fine-grained quartz, and most black chalcedony on the market is dyed. Solid black chalcedony should simply be called black chalcedony, since "onyx" strictly means banded material. Black obsidian is natural volcanic glass.
- **Trade names.** Morion and cairngorm are honest names for very dark smoky quartz. "Black quartz" is a loose shop name. Brown quartz sold as "smoky topaz" is mislabeled, since topaz is a different mineral.

### Two Checks at Home, With Their Limits

1. **Shine a phone flashlight through a thin edge or a point.** Smoky quartz is brown at heart, so a brown glow fits morion. Morion can be nearly opaque, so no glow proves nothing.
2. **Skip the scratch test.** Scratching glass or a knife blade damages the stone and still leaves the real questions open.

Natural versus irradiated color, and dyed chalcedony versus crystal quartz, are jobs for a gemologist.

### Three Questions for the Seller

1. Is this crystal quartz (morion) or chalcedony?
2. Is the color natural, irradiated or dyed? Ask for the answer in writing on the receipt.
3. Where was it mined?

## How to Care for Black Quartz

**Mineral Fact.** Quartz rates 7 on the Mohs scale, has no cleavage and is chemically inert in contact with most substances, so it is a durable stone. Where the rating is Not Enough Data, the advice below is the cautious choice.

- **Water and soaking.** Not Enough Data. A quick wash and dry is the cautious route. Skip long soaks.
- **Sun.** Brief Only. Smoky quartz is said to fade under UV light, so keep sun sessions short.
- **Heat.** Brief Only. Heat can lighten very dark smoky quartz, which is why treaters use it.
- **Chemicals.** Not Enough Data. Quartz resists most substances, but a dye may not, so skip household cleaners.
- **Salt.** Not Enough Data. Leave salt bowls out.
- **Scratches.** Not Enough Data. Quartz at 7 can scratch softer stones.
- **Toxicity.** OK to wear and handle. Cutting, grinding or polishing quartz is a high dust risk that calls for a respirator, ventilation and wet methods.

### How to Clean Black Quartz at Home

1. Wipe it with a soft cloth dampened in lukewarm water and a drop of mild dish soap.
2. For a crystal point or cluster, brush the crevices gently with a soft toothbrush.
3. Rinse quickly and pat dry with a lint-free cloth.
4. If the piece could be dyed, such as opaque black beads, use only the damp cloth, and stop if color comes off on it.
5. Skip ultrasonic and steam cleaners to be safe, since a damp cloth does the job.

### How to Store It

1. Keep it apart from softer stones, which it can scratch, and from harder ones such as topaz and sapphire, which can scratch it.
2. Keep it off sunny windowsills and away from radiators and hot cars.
3. Wrap clusters in a soft cloth when you move them, so the points do not knock together.

### Can Black Quartz Go in Water, Sun or Salt?

Shop guides cleanse morion with water, salt, sage, sunlight or moonlight, and one warns that salt can be corrosive and long sun can fade stones. Sun is rated Brief Only, and water and salt have Not Enough Data, so moonlight, sound and smoke are the gentle choices. Check your exact piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Black Quartz vs Look-Alikes

- **Black tourmaline.** Schorl, a different mineral colored jet black by iron, at 7 to 7.5 on the Mohs scale. See [black tourmaline](/stones/black-tourmaline/).
- **Black obsidian.** Natural volcanic glass, very shiny when polished, sometimes with trapped air bubbles, and its broken edges are extremely sharp. See [black obsidian](/stones/black-obsidian/).
- **Black onyx.** Usually dyed black chalcedony, as explained above. See [black onyx](/stones/black-onyx/).
- **Tourmalinated quartz.** Clear quartz with black tourmaline needles inside, a different look. See [tourmalinated quartz](/stones/tourmalinated-quartz/).

## Price Guide

This guide gives no dollar figures yet. Smoky quartz is relatively inexpensive because it is abundant, found in many places, and its brown color is not in high demand. Someone who wants a really big gem without a really big budget often ends up with one. Compare size, clarity, form (a crystal cluster, a tumbled stone or a cut gem) and locality, since some collector sites such as Pikes Peak are world famous. Ask whether the color is natural or irradiated before you pay a premium for it.

# White Topaz Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

White topaz is colorless topaz, the mineral's most common natural color, rated 8 on the Mohs scale. It is sold as an affordable diamond alternative and is the raw material for most blue and mystic topaz. Its meanings of clarity and honesty are modern beliefs, and next to a diamond it shows far less colored fire.

## What White Topaz Actually Is

**The Stone.** Topaz is a silicate of aluminum and fluorine, and most topaz crystals are colorless. Colorless topaz is plentiful. At 8 on the Mohs scale, topaz is the hardest silicate mineral, and diamond, corundum and chrysoberyl are the only commonly known minerals harder than it. Our [topaz page](/stones/topaz/) covers the whole family.

### What Color Is White Topaz?

Colorless. "White" is trade shorthand. Topaz takes its colors from trace elements or tiny defects in its crystal structure rather than from its basic chemistry, with chromium behind natural pink, red and violet, and structural defects behind yellow, brown and blue. White topaz is the stone without them.

### Hard but Easy to Split

Hardness is resistance to scratching, and toughness is resistance to breaking. Topaz has a perfect basal cleavage, a flat plane along which the crystal splits cleanly, and low tenacity, so it chips and cleaves easily. GIA rates its toughness poor and warns that a hard blow might split it.

### The Stone Behind Blue Topaz

In the 1960s, treaters found that irradiating colorless topaz turns it brown and that heating the brown stone gives a stable blue. Before that, colorless topaz was common enough to be bought cheaply by the ton. Today most topaz in commercial jewelry has been heated, irradiated or coated. Almost all blue topaz in stores, such as [London blue topaz](/stones/london-blue-topaz/), began as colorless topaz, and [mystic topaz](/stones/mystic-topaz/) is clear topaz with a coating.

### Where White Topaz Comes From

Brazil is the principal source of gem topaz, and Minas Gerais produces colorless crystals up to several hundred pounds. Water-clear crystals of top quality come from Klein Spitzkopje in Namibia, and colorless topaz also comes from Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Texas and Colorado. The Smithsonian holds cut colorless gems of 2,680 carats from Brazil and 325 carats from Colorado.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** White topaz has little lore of its own, because for most of history "topaz" meant a different stone.

- **77 CE, Rome.** **Tradition.** Pliny the Elder wrote that topazos "is a stone that is still held in very high estimation for its green tints" (*Natural History*, Book 37, chapter 32, Bostock and Riley translation). His translators read it as peridot, and Kunz agreed that "the topaz of the ancients was unquestionably the gem commonly called chrysolite at present," meaning peridot (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, a note on page 66). Pliny's legend put its source on Topazos, an island in the Red Sea that was hard to find (Kunz 1913, page 291).
- **Until modern gemology.** **Tradition.** All yellow, brown and orange transparent gems were called topaz.
- **13th century, India.** **Tradition.** The physician Naharari wrote that anyone who wears topaz "will be assured of long life, beauty and intelligence" (Kunz, *The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, 1915, page 158).
- **Portugal.** **Tradition.** The Braganza Diamond of the Portuguese crown jewels was actually a 1,680-carat colorless topaz. Some colorless topazes have been mistaken for diamonds, though topaz has less hardness and brilliance.

### Is White Topaz a Birthstone?

Not on its own. Topaz is the November birthstone on the American Gem Society's list, and white topaz counts only as one color of it. One jewelry shop sells it as a modern alternative to April's diamond, and one crystal guide notes that people born in November or December sometimes choose it. More on the [November birthstone page](/birthstones/november/).

## What People Use White Topaz for Today

**Modern Association.** The meaning of white topaz in modern crystal guides is awareness and mental clarity. They call it a "clean slate" stone for clearing mental clutter and a reminder to speak plainly and kindly. On our five-facet scale it is a Classic stone for [focus](/crystals-for/focus/), Widely Linked for protection, Often Linked for anxiety, Sometimes Linked for love and sleep, and Rarely Linked for money and grounding. Guides place it on the crown chakra.

The "why" is mostly transparency, read as truth and a fresh start, and one guide says that is part of why it turns up in engagement rings. One shop calls it the stone of fidelity. Western guides pair topaz with Scorpio and Sagittarius through the November birthstone, and one guide says white topaz sometimes stands in for diamond, the stone of Venus, in Vedic astrology.

Some shops promise relief from migraines and headaches, an energy boost, or stronger hair and nails. Gem references and medicine give no support to those claims.

## How to Use White Topaz in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** White topaz is mostly a jewelry stone, so its rituals live on your hand and on your desk.

### The Focus Break

**Try This.** On a long work block, keep a white topaz by your keyboard. When you notice you have reread the same line twice, pick it up, tilt it under the lamp for a few breaths and name the one thing you are working on. Then put it down and go back.

### The Ring Dish Habit

**Try This.** One hard blow might split topaz, so give your ring a home. Put a small dish by the sink and the door, and make it a rule that the ring goes in before dishes, the gym, gardening or moving furniture.

### An Honest Diamond Stand-In

**Try This.** For an [April birthday](/birthstones/april/) or a budget engagement ring, white topaz is a fair choice if the card is honest too. Write what it is (natural colorless topaz, not diamond) and add one line of history, that the Braganza Diamond in Portugal's crown jewels turned out to be topaz.

**Safety.** Loose stones and small jewelry are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

**What Research Says.** In a lab experiment on a long, dull visual task, Atsunori Ariga and Alejandro Lleras found that people who briefly switched to a second task partway through kept their performance steady, while people who stayed on the main task without a break got steadily worse ([*Cognition*, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.12.007)). It was a short lab task, the break was another task rather than rest, and nothing in it involved stones, so the focus break above is our extrapolation.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Some shops sell white topaz for headaches, migraines, anxiety and sleep. A stone can be a pleasant cue, but it is not care. For headaches that keep coming back, lasting anxiety or poor sleep, talk to a doctor. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My White Topaz Real?

- **Natural, untreated.** Colorless is topaz's most common natural color, so a white topaz needs no treatment to look white.
- **Natural, treated.** The common topaz treatments add color to colorless topaz, irradiation and heat for blue, coatings for mystic and pink. A GIA report names any detectable treatment.
- **Lab-grown.** Topaz has been synthesized in labs, but it is not usually sold. A tag reading "lab-created white topaz" deserves a question about what the stone really is.
- **Imitation.** Glass, cubic zirconia and synthetic spinel are colorless look-alikes.
- **Trade names.** White, silver and colorless topaz are the same stone. "Smoky topaz" is brown quartz, mistakenly named.

### Does White Topaz Sparkle Like a Diamond?

Not as much. Refractive index measures how strongly a stone bends light, and dispersion is how much it splits white light into rainbow colors, the colored fire. White topaz is well below diamond on both, and its dispersion is about a third of diamond's (see the table below).

### Three Checks at Home, With Their Limits

1. **Compare it with a known diamond.** Under one lamp, the diamond throws more colored flashes. You need a real diamond for this, and it does not separate topaz from white sapphire or quartz.
2. **Look at the facet edges with a 10x loupe.** Glass tends to show rounded facet junctions. It is a hint, not proof.
3. **Skip the weight and scratch tests.** Glass ranges in density from lighter to heavier than topaz, and diamond's density of 3.515 falls inside topaz's own range, so heft proves nothing. A scratch test puts pressure on a stone that can split along its cleavage.

### Three Questions for the Seller

1. Is this natural topaz, and has it been treated or coated?
2. For a ring, is the setting a bezel or other protective setting?
3. For an expensive piece, is there a lab report? GIA's identifies whether a stone is natural or lab-grown and names detectable treatments.

## How to Care for White Topaz

**Mineral Fact.** Topaz rates 8 on the Mohs scale, but its toughness is poor because of its cleavage, and extreme pressure or sharp temperature changes might cause it to break. Where a rating is Not Enough Data, the notes below follow GIA's care guide for topaz.

- **Cleaning.** OK with a soft brush, mild detergent and warm water.
- **Water and soaking.** Not Enough Data. Warm, soapy water works best for topaz, so a wash is fine. Skip long soaks.
- **Heat.** Keep Away From Heat. High heat or sudden temperature changes can cause breaks, and the vibration and heat of ultrasonic and steam cleaners could split it.
- **Sun.** Not Enough Data. Topaz color is generally stable to light, and the fading known for topaz concerns yellow to brown stones.
- **Chemicals.** Not Enough Data. Topaz is affected only very slightly by chemicals. Take it off for household cleaners anyway.
- **Salt.** Not Enough Data. Leave salt bowls out.
- **Knocks and storage.** Brief Only. It chips and cleaves easily, so store it wrapped.
- **Toxicity.** OK to wear and handle. Topaz has no known toxicity.

### How to Clean a White Topaz Ring at Home

1. Fill a small bowl with warm water and one drop of mild dish soap.
2. Dip the ring briefly and brush around and under the stone with a soft toothbrush, where lotion and skin oil collect.
3. Rinse under running water of the same temperature, with the drain closed.
4. Pat dry with a soft, lint-free cloth.
5. Never steam it, boil it or put it in an ultrasonic cleaner.

Do it when the sparkle looks dull.

### Daily Wear & Storage

1. For a ring worn every day, choose a bezel or another protective setting, which keeps the stone safe from knocks.
2. If you prefer prongs, ask the jeweler to position them so they do not stress the cleavage plane.
3. Store each piece in its own pouch. Diamond and sapphire can scratch topaz, and topaz can scratch quartz and citrine.

### Can White Topaz Go in Water, Sun or Salt?

A quick wash, yes. Skip long soaks, salt bowls and hot windowsills, since soaking, salt and sun are rated Not Enough Data and heat is Keep Away From Heat. Shop guides suggest a brief rinse under the tap, moonlight, smoke or a singing bowl, and all of these spare it heat. Check your exact piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Price Guide

This guide gives no dollar figures yet. Blue and colorless topaz are very affordable and widely available, and colorless topaz has a much lower value than the rare natural pink, red, orange and blue colors. Three things move the price.

- **Cut.** Cut is one of the most important factors in a topaz's appearance, so compare stones under the same light.
- **Size and clarity.** Colorless topaz grows large and is often transparent, so big stones are within reach.
- **The name.** If a seller prices white topaz like a diamond, or calls it one, walk away.

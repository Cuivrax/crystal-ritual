# White Sapphire Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

White sapphire is colorless corundum, the same mineral as blue sapphire and ruby, with a hardness of 9. It is sold mostly as a lower-cost alternative to diamond, and much of it is grown in a lab. Its meanings of purity and commitment are modern beliefs, and next to a diamond it shows a softer white glow with far less fire.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Look at it next to a known diamond in the same light. A diamond throws more sparkle and colored flashes, while white sapphire shows a calmer white glow. Then ask the seller two things, natural or lab-grown, and heated, coated or filled. Natural and lab-grown white sapphire share the same properties, so only a gem lab can settle that one.

## What White Sapphire Actually Is

**The Stone.** Corundum is aluminum oxide, "colorless when pure, but trace amounts of various metals produce almost any color," and when it is colorless it is called a white sapphire ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/corundum.shtml)). Red corundum is ruby and every other color counts as sapphire, so the [International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/white-sapphires-vs-diamonds/) defines white sapphire as any gem-quality corundum that is colorless, or white. Same crystal as a blue sapphire, minus the iron, titanium or chromium that tint the others.

### Is White Sapphire a Real Stone?

Yes. It is a real mineral, not glass and not a trade name. The [Gemological Institute of America](https://www.gia.edu/sapphire/gem-overview) gives sapphire a hardness of 9, a specific gravity of 4.00 and a refractive index of 1.762 to 1.770. Corundum is the third-hardest mineral after diamond and moissanite, with no cleavage (Geology.com).

How much of it is mined is less clear. Some white sapphire comes out of the ground and some is grown in a lab, says the International Gem Society. One diamond retailer calls natural white sapphire "incredibly rare" and says most are yellow or gray stones heated or chemically treated to look clear ([diamonds.pro](https://www.diamonds.pro/education/white-sapphire)). A tag alone cannot tell you, so read "natural" as a question.

Sapphires are mined all over the world, though gem quality is much rarer. Sri Lanka still produces every color, and the International Gem Society ([sapphire guide](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/sapphire-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)) pictures white rough from Montana. IGS also shows a 350-carat colorless sapphire crystal from the gem gravels of Ratnapura, Sri Lanka. We found no reference source on which mines supply most of the colorless sapphire sold today.

### What Does a White Sapphire Look Like?

Clear, with a soft white brightness. IGS describes "a white glow of color, without the fireworks of diamonds." Dealer guides add that many look slightly cloudy, and that they turn dull in some lighting or once dirt builds up ([25karats.com](https://www.25karats.com/education/glossary/White%20Sapphire), diamonds.pro). If yours looks a little sleepy next to a diamond, that is the honest difference.

### The Same Material as a Watch Crystal

Clear synthetic corundum is grown in large crystals, sawn into sheets and used for watch crystals, grocery store scanner windows and aircraft windows (Geology.com), and GIA adds spacecraft. If your watch says "sapphire crystal," you already wear lab-grown white sapphire every day.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** White sapphire has spent most of its history standing in for something else. Here is what the old texts say.

- **2nd century.** **Tradition.** Kunz quotes the Orphic poem on stones about the adamas, the ancient "diamond," and comments that it "probably refers either to colorless corundum, the so-called 'white sapphire,' or to quartz." He was "disinclined to believe that the ancients knew the diamond" (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, pages 72 to 73). The lookalike may be older than the diamond, at least in writing.
- **3rd or 4th century.** **Tradition.** A Greek list of seven amulets for sailors names an adamas of two kinds, a Macedonian one "likened to ice," probably rock crystal, and an Indian one "of a silvery hue," which Kunz thought "may possibly have been our corundum" (1913, page 39).
- **973 to 1048.** **Tradition.** The scholar Al-Berûnî listed white among the colors of the yakut, the corundum crystals, next to yellow, gray, green and black (*The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, 1915, page 403).
- **1500s.** **Tradition.** Paracelsus (1493 to 1541) made the "white sapphire" a stone of Jupiter (Kunz 1913, pages 372 to 373).
- **The Talisman of Charlemagne.** **Tradition.** This relic from the cathedral treasury at Aachen holds two large sapphires around a small cross said to be made from wood of the Holy Cross. Its band carries pearls, sapphires, amethysts, "an almost white sapphire" and "a white sapphire." Empress Josephine received it in 1804, and Empress Eugénie is said to have worn it at the birth of the Prince Imperial (Kunz 1915, pages 329 to 331).
- **Sapphire in general.** **Tradition.** Sapphire was long linked with royalty and said to protect against poison and fraud, though ancient "sapphires" may really be lapis lazuli (International Gem Society). Protection against fraud is a nice irony for a stone so often passed off as diamond.
- **Kansas City list.** **Tradition.** Kunz grumbled that the sapphire, "properly the gem for April," became the September birthstone in the list adopted in Kansas City (1913, pages 319 to 320).
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Indian astrology shops sell it as a stone of Venus, and jewelers sell it as an engagement stone.

### What About Apollo & the Oracle of Delphi?

One gem shop says the Greeks found white sapphire on Naxos, tied it to Apollo and received it from the Oracle of Delphi ([Gem Rock Auctions](https://www.gemrockauctions.com/learn/a-z-of-gemstones/white-sapphire)). No old text is on hand for that. Pliny the Elder's sapphirus, in *Natural History* Book 37, is a blue stone "refulgent with spots like gold," and his translators Bostock and Riley take it to be lapis lazuli, not corundum ([Perseus, Tufts University](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0137%3Abook%3D37%3Achapter%3D39)). So Pliny offers no white sapphire either. Since Kunz could not be sure the ancient adamas was corundum at all, we keep these stories at arm's length.

## What People Use White Sapphire for Today

**Modern Association.** Crystal guides rate white sapphire a Classic stone for love, with protection and confidence close behind, and money and anxiety linked less often. The "why" is plain. A clear stone reads as purity, and the engagement ring is its best-known use, so commitment came with it.

Western crystal sites mostly skip it. Most meaning pages come from Indian gem and astrology shops, which sell it as the stone of Venus (Shukra), the planet of love, beauty and partnership, sometimes as white pukhraj ([Navratan](https://www.navratan.com/blog/white-sapphire-benefits), [Brahma Gems](https://brahmagems.com/blog/white-sapphire-the-stone-of-beauty-luxury-guide-to-wear.html)). Libra and Taurus are the signs named most. Some shops say to wear it only after an astrologer reads your birth chart, and one describes a Friday sunrise first wearing with a mantra and a dip in Ganges water or milk. A few guides place it on the crown chakra.

### Is White Sapphire a Birthstone?

Not on its own. The [American Gem Society](https://www.americangemsociety.org/birthstones/) list gives sapphire to September and diamond to April, and GIA names sapphire the gem of the 5th and 45th anniversaries. Gem Rock Auctions calls white sapphire an alternative April birthstone, which works as a diamond stand-in and happens to echo Kunz. Treat it as a jeweler's suggestion, not an official list.

Some shops also promise help with migraines, thyroid trouble, epilepsy, depression or an easy delivery. Gem science and medicine do not back those claims.

## How to Use White Sapphire in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** White sapphire is a jewelry stone more than a pocket stone, so its rituals live on your hand, in a gift box and by the sink.

### The Sunday Ring Clean

**Try This.** White sapphire dulls as dirt and soap build up, so it needs regular cleaning anyway. Make it a two-minute Sunday habit with warm water, a drop of mild dish soap, a soft toothbrush, a rinse and a dry cloth. While you brush, name one thing you want to keep doing next week. The stone gets clean either way, and you get a pause you actually keep.

### A Stand-In for April, With the Truth on the Card

**Try This.** For an [April birthday](/birthstones/april/) on a budget, white sapphire studs are an honest gift if the card is honest too. Write what it is (colorless sapphire, the same mineral as ruby) and what it is not (a diamond). Add that a 1913 gem expert thought sapphire belonged to April all along.

### A Fifth Anniversary Note

**Try This.** Sapphire is the traditional 5th anniversary gem, and white counts if blue is not their color. Put a note in the box with one short line for each year and one for the year ahead. Jewelers and astrologers both tie the stone to commitment. The note is the part that shows it.

**Safety.** Loose stones, small studs and bead jewelry are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

**What Research Says.** In a 2010 study of 96 volunteers who each repeated one eating, drinking or activity habit daily in the same setting for 12 weeks, the habit grew more automatic along a leveling curve, taking anywhere from 18 to 254 days to reach 95 percent of each person's plateau, and missing a single day did not materially set it back ([Lally and colleagues, *European Journal of Social Psychology*](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)). The sample was small and made of volunteers, the ratings were self-reported, the habits were simple daily ones, and no stones were involved, so applying it to a weekly ring clean is our extrapolation. The fixed moment is the part that may stick, not the stone.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Some shops sell white sapphire for headaches, low mood, pregnancy or recovery from illness. A ring can be a comfort to wear, but it is not care. For health concerns, during pregnancy or if low mood lasts, talk to a doctor, a midwife or a therapist, and never use a stone in place of treatment.

## Is My White Sapphire Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone. With white sapphire the classic deception runs the other way, a real white sapphire sold as a diamond, which one diamond retailer warns about directly (diamonds.pro).

- **Natural, untreated.** Mined and never heated or filled. Dealers call it rare.
- **Natural, treated.** Heating improves color and clarity and "is an accepted practice in the gem trade," says IGS. Some stones get coatings to look whiter or a diffusion treatment that changes the outer layer. Fracture-filled stones hold leaded glass that makes them look clearer, and IGS advises against buying them.
- **Lab-grown.** Corundum was first made in a lab in the early 1900s, and many sapphires sold today are lab-grown. Natural and lab-made sapphires have the same optical and physical properties (International Gem Society).
- **Imitation.** Glass, plastic and other synthetics imitate sapphires, and cubic zirconia can be made colorless (International Gem Society). The IGS [chart of diamond simulants](https://gemsociety.org/article/diamond-and-simulant-comparison-chart) lists white sapphire beside glass, white topaz, synthetic spinel and cubic zirconia, the colorless materials to rule out. We found no reference source on which of them is most often sold as white sapphire in the US.
- **Trade name.** "Colorless sapphire" is simply another name for the same stone.

### What Does Lab-Created White Sapphire Mean?

It means real corundum grown in a laboratory instead of mined. Not a fake sapphire, a sapphire with a factory birthplace. Modern lab stones look so natural that IGS calls them hard for all but the most skilled professionals to identify. Gemologists look for clues such as curved growth lines, found only in synthetic sapphires and rubies. That is lab work. A lab-created label is fine as long as the price matches it.

### Three Checks, With Their Limits

1. **Compare it with a diamond.** In the same bright light, a well-cut diamond shows more brilliance, dispersion and scintillation than a well-cut white sapphire (International Gem Society). You need a known diamond, and it says nothing about natural versus lab-grown.
2. **Look for a window.** Look straight down through the top. If you can see through to the back, light is leaking out, a cutting flaw dealers call a window (diamonds.pro). It is a quality clue, not a sign of a fake.
3. **Ask for the paperwork.** One retailer notes that GIA does not grade white sapphires like diamonds but reports identity, color, weight and treatment (diamonds.pro). A report is only as good as the lab behind it.

Three questions for the seller. Natural or lab-grown? Heated, coated, diffused or fracture-filled? Which lab wrote the report? The [Label Decoder](/tools/crystal-label-decoder/) turns the words on a tag into what they usually mean.

## How to Care for White Sapphire

**Mineral Fact.** Hard and without cleavage, sapphire is a superb everyday stone with great resistance to scratches and blows, though a heavily included or fractured stone is less stable (International Gem Society).

- **Water.** OK for washing with warm water, detergent and a soft brush, per the IGS [care guide](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/gemstone-care-guide/). Keep soaks brief.
- **Sun, heat, salt and chemicals.** Unknown. There is no reliable guidance for white sapphire yet, so keep it away from harsh cleaners and ask a jeweler.
- **Scratches and storage.** Brief only. Durable but not scratch-proof, and dirt and small scratches build up with daily wear (diamonds.pro). Only a diamond or a moissanite can scratch a sapphire (IGS), so store it apart from diamond pieces.
- **Cleaning.** OK, and often. IGS warns that some sapphires are oiled and that inclusions can weaken the stone, so if you do not know how yours was treated, stay with warm soapy water and ask a jeweler before anything stronger.
- **Toxicity.** OK to wear and handle. IGS lists no known risk for sapphire.

### Can You Soak White Sapphire?

Briefly. One gem shop suggests soaking it in warm soapy water for 20 to 30 minutes (Gem Rock Auctions). Long soaks are rated Brief only, because the care guidance covers washing, not soaking, and a filled or oiled stone is the one you least want sitting in water. Wash, rinse, dry. Check your exact piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/), which shows OK, Brief only, Avoid or Unknown with the reason and the source.

## White Sapphire vs Look-Alikes

- **Diamond.** Harder (10 against 9) and much brighter, with a refractive index of about 2.42 against about 1.77, according to diamonds.pro. Practically speaking, the hardness gap "makes little difference," says IGS. See [white sapphire vs diamond](/compare/white-sapphire-vs-diamond/).
- **Moissanite.** The only gem besides diamond that can scratch a sapphire (IGS). It bends light far more strongly, with a refractive index of 2.648 to 2.691 against 1.757 to 1.779 for white sapphire, and its dispersion, the property behind colored fire, is listed at up to .104 against .018, per the IGS simulant chart. It is also strongly doubly refractive, and a thermal diamond tester reads it as diamond while white sapphire reads as a simulant. See [white sapphire vs moissanite](/compare/white-sapphire-vs-moissanite/).
- **Cubic zirconia.** A lab-made material that can be produced colorless. It is far denser, with a specific gravity of 5.56 to 6.00 against 3.90 to 4.10 for white sapphire, and softer at 8 to 8.5 (IGS simulant chart), so a gemologist can separate the two by specific gravity alone. Under magnification CZ may show gas bubbles or unmelted powder, while natural white sapphire may show silk, crystals or fingerprints.
- **White topaz.** Dealers say the cloudy look of many white sapphires helps tell them from diamond, topaz and moissanite (diamonds.pro). The IGS chart gives white topaz a lower refractive index (1.609 to 1.637), a lower specific gravity (3.49 to 3.57), a hardness of 8 and perfect cleavage in one direction, which white sapphire lacks. A refractometer reading tells them apart, so this is a job for a gemologist, not a home test.
- **Blue sapphire.** Same mineral, colored by trace elements. Our [sapphire page](/stones/sapphire/) covers the family.

## Price Guide

White sapphires are much less expensive than diamonds at any carat size (International Gem Society). A 2026 IGS article on [fancy-colored sapphires](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/are-fancy-colored-sapphires-expensive/) says they are "most often used as a low-cost simulant for diamonds" and that per-carat values are "quite low." Demand rose in the early 1990s, it adds, but there is not much demand for colorless sapphire on its own now that so many low-cost synthetic diamonds are available.

One diamond retailer notes that color-treated white sapphires cost less than naturally occurring ones (diamonds.pro). A natural, untreated stone with a lab report and a lab-grown one can look identical and cost very differently, so the paperwork is part of what you pay for.

Two current listings give a sense of the natural end, both loose stones from Madagascar heated to improve their color. One listing we found, a 1.50-carat oval about 7.6 by 5.9 mm, was about $598. Another, a lot of four round stones of 4.5 mm, 1.73 carats in all, was about $228 for the set. We found no current listing for lab-grown white sapphire to set beside them.

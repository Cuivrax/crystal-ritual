# Lemurian Quartz Meaning & What It Really Is

Lemurian quartz is ordinary quartz sold under a trade name that dealers tie to clear crystals from Minas Gerais, Brazil, which often show horizontal ridges called striations. Crystal lovers believe the crystals hold knowledge from Lemuria, a lost continent first proposed by a zoologist in 1864. No test can prove a crystal is Lemurian, so the higher price buys the story, not a different mineral.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Lemurian quartz is real quartz. What nobody can verify is the name, since no mineral test identifies a crystal as Lemurian. Look for natural crystal faces with ridges across the sides, ask the seller where it was mined, and never pay a Lemurian premium for a polished ball or tower.

## What Lemurian Quartz Actually Is

**The Stone.** Quartz is silicon dioxide, a mineral of silicon and oxygen. It rates 7 on the Mohs scale of scratch resistance, has a glassy luster, and breaks along curved, shell-like surfaces instead of splitting along flat planes. Lemurian is not a mineral or a variety in mineralogy. As one crystal dealer who has researched the name explains, it has no recognized geological definition, and geologists classify these crystals as quartz. The honest label is simply quartz, or striated clear quartz from Brazil. Our [quartz page](/stones/quartz/) covers the family.

### What Are the Lines on Lemurian Quartz?

They are striations, fine, slightly indented lines on a crystal's faces. Quartz crystals are usually striated across their width, on the long sides called prism faces, and that is a handy identification clue. Beryl crystals, which can look similar, lack horizontal striations. The dealer explains that the lines form as new layers spread across a face while the crystal grows, so they are a normal record of growth, not a sign of a special kind of quartz.

### Is Lemurian Quartz the Same as Clear Quartz?

Yes, as a mineral. The same dealer notes that some crystals sold as Lemurian have only one or two faint striations and some have none, and then there may be nothing visible to separate them from other [clear quartz](/stones/clear-quartz/). The difference is the name, the claimed locality and the price.

### Where Lemurian Quartz Comes From

According to the same dealer, the name was first tied to clear quartz from the Serra do Cabral in Minas Gerais, Brazil, around Joaquim Felício and Francisco Dumont. Quartz was mined nearby during the Second World War for radio oscillator plates, and a mine abandoned for having too many inclusions later reopened to supply collectors. Crystals sold as Lemurian also come from Colombia and, in small amounts, Peru, though the dealer found no geological publication confirming a Peruvian source under the name.

### Seed Crystals & Record Keepers

"Lemurian seed crystal" is used for the same kind of crystal. Some sellers claim seed crystals differ slightly, but no geological standard separates the two. Crystals with tiny triangle marks on their faces are sold as "record keepers," believed to store information. The trade calls the marks trigons, a word gemologists use mostly for etch marks on diamonds. The dealer explains that on quartz such triangles may be raised growth hillocks or small pits etched by fluids.

## Where the Name Comes From

**The Story.** Clear quartz has old lore, Lemuria has a 19th-century history, and the two met in the crystal trade only recently.

- **77 CE, Rome.** **Tradition.** Pliny the Elder believed rock crystal was a form of ice, frozen hard by extreme cold, and wrote that "crystal is only to be found in places where the winter snow freezes with the greatest intensity" (*Natural History*, Book 37, chapter 9, Bostock and Riley translation).
- **1864.** The zoologist Philip Sclater wrote "I should propose the name Lemuria!" for a lost land that could explain how lemurs spread between Madagascar and India. Plate tectonics later replaced lost continents as the explanation for patterns like this.
- **1877 to 1904.** **Tradition.** Helena Blavatsky's *Isis Unveiled* (1877) is reported to contain the first metaphysical reading of Lemuria, and Scott-Elliot's *The Lost Lemuria* (1904) helped establish the occult version. Theosophists made Lemuria the home of the earliest "root races" of humanity, and later writers moved it to the Pacific.
- **1905 to 1931, California.** **Tradition.** Frederick Spencer Oliver's *A Dweller on Two Planets*, known from a 1905 edition, became the main source of Mount Shasta's esoteric legends, and Wishar Cervé's 1931 book made the Shasta Lemuria legend widely popular.
- **1987, Brazil.** The same dealer reports that the mineral collector Rock Currier worked a small quartz mine near Joaquim Felício that year and later recalled being surprised when US customers called the crystals Lemurian. The dealer calls this the earliest dated firsthand account it has found and finds no support for the common claim of a discovery in the early 1990s.
- **Late 1990s.** One seller dates the "Lemurian Seed Crystal" label for loose, elongated Serra do Cabral crystals to this period. Some crystal sites credit the healer Katrina Raphaell with naming or popularizing the crystals. The dealer notes that who coined the name, and how Brazilian quartz came to be linked with Lemuria, is not known.
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Ancient wisdom, connection and spiritual awakening.

## What People Use Lemurian Quartz for Today

### What Does Lemurian Quartz Mean?

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal practice, Lemurian quartz means remembered wisdom. The story says the people of Lemuria foresaw their end and stored their knowledge in quartz crystals, and that the striations carry a lesson about staying united while keeping your individuality. Guides tie it to the crown chakra, and some add the third eye.

On our five-facet scale it is Often Linked with [sleep](/crystals-for/sleep/) and [focus](/crystals-for/focus/), Sometimes Linked with love and grounding, and Rarely Linked with anxiety, protection or confidence. A favorite practice is to trace the ridges with a finger during meditation. Others set a Lemurian point at the center of a crystal grid or keep one by the bed for dreams.

Some pages promise better sleep, a stronger immune response, or relief for headaches and breathing. Gem references and medicine give no support to those claims.

A story can still mean something to the person who keeps the crystal. The quartz is real either way, and Lemuria itself began as Philip Sclater's 1864 proposal to explain why lemurs lived in both Madagascar and India.

## How to Use Lemurian Quartz in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** The ridges are what make these crystals feel different in the hand, so use them.

### Trace the Ridges at Bedtime

**Try This.** Sit on the edge of the bed with the point in one hand. Breathe in through your nose, then breathe out slowly while you slide a fingertip down one ridge. Move to the next ridge on the next breath. Stop after five minutes, or when you lose count.

### A Ladder List at the Desk

**Try This.** Write the three steps of today's main task on a sticky note and set the crystal on it. Each time you finish a step, run a finger across one ridge. When the list is done, move the crystal to the edge of the desk.

### An Honest Label for a Gift

**Try This.** If you give one, add a card that says what it is, quartz from Brazil sold as Lemurian, and one line of its history, that Lemuria began as an 1864 proposal to explain where lemurs lived.

**Safety.** Crystal points have sharp tips, and small stones are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets.

**What Research Says.** In a small remote randomized study, 108 adults did 5 minutes a day of one of three breathing exercises or of mindfulness meditation for a month, and breathwork, especially exhale-focused cyclic sighing, improved mood and lowered breathing rate more than meditation did ([Balban and colleagues, *Cell Reports Medicine*, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895)). Participants knew which exercise they did, mood was self-reported, and no stones were involved. The ridge tracing is our extrapolation.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Lemurian quartz is often chosen for sleep, and some sites suggest it for anxiety, old trauma and physical healing. A stone can be a pleasant thing to hold, but it is not care. If poor sleep, worry or low mood lasts, talk to a doctor or a therapist. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My Lemurian Quartz Real?

- **Natural, untreated.** The usual case. It is natural quartz.
- **Natural, treated.** No treatment is documented for this name.
- **Lab-grown.** Most quartz used in electronics and optics is grown in laboratories, but none is documented on the market as Lemurian.
- **Imitation.** Glass is an age-old gem imitation still used today. See [quartz vs glass](/compare/quartz-vs-glass/).
- **Trade names.** Lemurian, Lemurian seed and record keeper are trade terms, not mineral names.

### How to Identify Lemurian Quartz

There is no recognized test, so these checks tell you what you have, not whether the label is true.

1. **Look for natural faces.** A crystal with its own flat faces and point can show striations. Once quartz is shaped and polished into a ball or tower, the faces and ridges are gone, and nothing can show it was ever sold as Lemurian.
2. **Run a fingernail across the sides.** Ridges you can feel are striations. They are ordinary growth marks, common on quartz from many places.
3. **Make sure it is quartz, not glass.** Our [guide to telling crystals from fakes](/real-or-fake/how-to-tell-if-crystals-are-real/) covers the checks.
4. **Ask where it was mined, in writing.** Provenance is the only basis for the name, and even it cannot be checked independently without documents.

## How to Care for Lemurian Quartz

**Mineral Fact.** Quartz rates 7, has no cleavage and is chemically inert in contact with most substances. Our care data for this trade name is mostly Not Enough Data, so the notes below are the cautious choice, not a rating.

- **Chemicals.** OK. Quartz in general resists most chemicals.
- **Water and soaking.** Not Enough Data. A quick rinse, never a long soak.
- **Sun.** Not Enough Data. Keep it off sunny windowsills.
- **Heat.** Not Enough Data. Keep it away from candle flames, radiators and hot cars.
- **Salt.** Not Enough Data. One seller warns that salt water and hot water can dull the surface.
- **Storage.** Not Enough Data. One seller suggests wrapping each crystal in soft cloth so the striated faces do not scratch against other crystals.
- **Toxicity.** Not Enough Data. Quartz's known hazard is silica dust from cutting and grinding, so never sand, grind or drill one at home without proper dust protection.

### How to Clean Lemurian Quartz at Home

1. **Dust it.** Brush along the ridges with a soft, dry makeup brush or paintbrush.
2. **Rinse only if grimy.** Hold it under lukewarm running water for a few seconds and brush gently. Never use hot water.
3. **Dry it right away.** Pat it with a towel, then let it air dry on the towel.
4. **Wrap it.** Store it in its own cloth or pouch.

### How to Cleanse Lemurian Quartz

Cleansing is a belief, not a mineral process. Sellers suggest moonlight, sound or smoke, and all three keep the crystal dry. Skip salt and long soaks. More in [how to cleanse crystals without damaging them](/care/how-to-cleanse-crystals/), and check your piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Lemurian Quartz vs Look-Alikes

- **Clear quartz.** The same mineral. A striated clear quartz from anywhere looks the same.
- **Tessin and Muzo habit quartz.** Specific tapered crystal shapes, the second first described from Colombia. Some Lemurians are compared with them, but the dealer points out that striations alone do not prove either shape.
- **Glass.** An age-old gem imitation, and a fair question for any very cheap point. See [quartz vs glass](/compare/quartz-vs-glass/).

## Price Guide

### Is Lemurian Quartz Worth Paying More For?

Only if the story matters to you. The dealer notes that crystals sold as Lemurian command considerably higher prices than comparable clear quartz, though no mineral property sets them apart, and advises caution about paying inflated prices for specimens that may be indistinguishable from much cheaper quartz. This guide gives no figures. Judge a crystal as you would any quartz point, and treat a polished Lemurian ball as plain quartz.

# Moldavite Meaning, Properties & Real-Life Uses

Moldavite is a green natural glass formed about 15 million years ago, when an asteroid impact in southeastern Germany melted rock and splashed it across central Europe. Modern crystal guides call it a stone of transformation, which is a belief. The honest note is about buying it, since much of the moldavite sold online is man-made green glass.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Look for gas bubbles and swirly flow lines, often visible to the eye and clear under a 10x loupe. Be wary of a faceted stone over 2 carats, any piece with no visible bubbles, and anything labeled "African moldavite." Only a gem lab gives a positive identification.

## What Moldavite Actually Is

**The Stone.** Moldavite is a tektite, natural glass formed when silica sand or rock was melted and thrown into the air by a meteorite impact, then cooled. By definition, only glass from the impact at Nördlinger Ries in Germany can be called moldavite ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/moldavite-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)).

The incoming asteroid is thought to have broken in two and dug the Ries and Steinheim craters. It hit hard enough to melt the target rock and splatter it northeast, and most of that splatter hardened in the air and fell in what is now the Czech Republic ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/moldavite/)). Moldavite formed up to 280 miles (450 km) from the crater (International Gem Society).

### Is Moldavite a Crystal?

No. It solidified so fast that its atoms never had time to line up into minerals, which also trapped gas bubbles in the glass. That makes it amorphous, a mineraloid rather than a mineral (Geology.com).

### Is Moldavite From Space?

Not quite. The glass is Earth rock melted by an impact (International Gem Society), and only the impactor came from space. That is why Geology.com calls "glass meteorite" a misnomer.

### Where Moldavite Is Found

Millions of pieces have been collected, mostly in the Czech Republic (Geology.com). Austria, Germany and Poland have it too (International Gem Society). Erosion, burial and extensive mining mean big, fine pieces are unusual today (Geology.com).

### Bubbles, Etching & Hedgehogs

Moldavite's green comes from a very low amount of iron. Other tektites carry more iron and are black ([Gems & Gemology](https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/spring-2015-gemnews-moldavites-natural-fake)). Acidic groundwater etched buried pieces into crinkled shapes (Geology.com). Spiky ones are nicknamed "hedgehogs," and their swirls preserve the moment the glass was still liquid (International Gem Society).

Size is a clue. Faceted moldavite tops out around 25 carats (International Gem Society), and very few real moldavites weigh more than 3.5 ounces (100 g), says *Gems & Gemology*.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** Moldavite's written history is short, and its spiritual reputation is younger still.

- **Prehistory.** **Tradition.** People used tektites, moldavite included, for ornaments, tools and religious purposes, and finds in an Austrian cave and outside the strewn field suggest they collected and traded them (International Gem Society).
- **1787.** Moldavite was first described scientifically as "chrysolite," or golden stone, a name also used for chrysoberyl, prehnite and peridot. Before it was known as natural glass, it was taken for slag from Bohemian glassmaking (International Gem Society).
- **1836.** Scientists named it moldavite, after a major source near the Moldau River in Bohemia (International Gem Society). Geology.com lists vltavín as another name.
- **Late 1800s.** Faceted moldavite was popular in Czech jewelry, often with Czech garnets or small river pearls, until its use faded in the early 1900s, as green bottle glass imitations appeared (*Gems & Gemology*).
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Geology.com notes that its impact origin gives moldavite special appeal for New Age, astrological and spiritual practices. The crystal author Robert Simmons gave it a whole book with Kathy Warner, *Moldavite*, subtitled *Starborn Stone of Transformation* (1988), and later a separate title, *The Book of Moldavite*. One moldavite jeweler cites *The Book of Stones* (Simmons with Naisha Ahsian, 2005) and Melody's *Love Is in the Earth* (1991) as its sources.
- **Folk belief.** **Tradition.** A bead dealer's guide says Czechoslovakian legend made moldavite a stone of good luck and a common betrothal gift. We found no older source for it.

### What About the Emerald That Fell From the Sky?

Shop pages say medieval Europeans called moldavite "the emerald that fell from the sky," that it surrounded the Venus of Willendorf, or that it was the Holy Grail. We found no reference work that supports those stories, and Kunz's two big books of gem lore never mention moldavite. Even GemSelect, which sells it, warns that not every popular claim about its past is documented history.

## What People Use Moldavite for Today

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal guides, one idea shows up nearly everywhere, transformation and change. Spiritual growth, intuition and protection come next. Most guides tie it to the heart chakra, with the third eye close behind, and those that mention astrology call it a stone for every sign. The "why" is the origin story. A glass born in a violent impact reads as sudden change.

### What Does Moldavite Do?

As a material, moldavite is a brittle green glass that scratches about as easily as man-made glass. Many people believe it has healing properties and pay a premium for it, but "there is no scientific evidence that moldavite has any medical effectiveness beyond being a placebo," says Geology.com.

### The Moldavite Flush & the Ruin-Your-Life Rumor

Several guides describe a "moldavite flush," a rush of heat or tingling on first touch, and one shop that sells it calls those reports personal anecdotes, not known effects. A social media rumor says moldavite will ruin your life if you are not ready. Some guides call that overblown, others advise short sessions with a grounding stone such as smoky quartz, black tourmaline or hematite, the pairing behind our [crystals for grounding](/crystals-for/grounding/).

## How to Use Moldavite in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** Moldavite's modern meaning is change, so give it a job in a change you already chose.

### Five Minutes, Then Put It Down

**Try This.** Many guides suggest short sessions with a new moldavite. Set a timer for five minutes, hold it, and think of one habit you want to drop. When the timer rings, write the habit down and put the stone away.

### The Sunday Change List

**Try This.** Keep the stone on a small notebook. Each Sunday, write one change for the week and the first small step, then set the moldavite on the page. Next Sunday, check the step before you write a new one.

### A Pendant You Touch on Purpose

**Try This.** Wear it as a pendant or earrings, where it is safe from knocks (see care below). Each time your fingers find it, ask whether what you are doing right now fits the change you picked.

**Safety.** Small stones are not toys. Keep tumbled pieces and beads away from small children and pets.

**What Research Says.** No study behind this page tested moldavite, so treat these as personal practices.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Some guides warn of dizziness or a rush of heat when you first hold moldavite. Nothing in the mineral sources behind this page explains such a reaction, so treat it as a signal from your body. If you feel unwell, anxious or low and it lasts, talk to a doctor or a therapist.

## Is My Moldavite Real? How to Spot Fake Moldavite

**The Stone.** Our five labels get simple here. The sources behind this page describe no common treatment (Natural, treated) and no lab-grown moldavite, so the question is almost always Natural, untreated versus Imitation, meaning man-made green glass, from faceted bottle glass to molded pieces with fake etching. Trade names get their own box.

> **Trade Name.** "African moldavite" is not moldavite. By definition moldavite occurs only around the Ries impact, no natural moldavite occurs in Africa, and pieces sold under that name are most likely manufactured glass (International Gem Society). Geology.com also flags "glass meteorite," "bohemian chrysolite," "false chrysolite" and "glass chrysolite" as misnomers. Bouteille stone and vltavín are genuine old names.

### Why Fake Moldavite Is Everywhere

Once moldavite's origin became known, demand outran supply. People faceted "moldavite" from green bottle glass, then glassmakers in China made glass in just the right green, and fakes even reached museum collections (Geology.com).

Geology.com tested the market. Its author bought a 6.19-carat, 13 x 11 mm faceted "moldavite" online for $29.49, and it was imitation glass, though a real stone that size would be enormous and far pricier. An imitation is sold as one, a fake is sold without disclosure, and the seller may not even know (Geology.com).

### Fake Moldavite Is Not New

The argument over glass is old. Around 1250 the German poet Volmar was so angry at a writer who claimed colored glass in a ring looked as good as a real gem that he declared killing the man would be no sin (Kunz, *The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, pages 373 to 374).

In a 2015 report, the mineralogist Jaroslav Hyršl described five 19th-century moldavite jewelry sets at the Museum of Decorative Arts in Prague. Only one set held real moldavite, a donation from Olga Havlová, first wife of Václav Havel. The other four were glass. He also saw two huge, too-perfect fakes in a high-end Hanoi jewelry shop in 2013 ([Gems & Gemology, Spring 2015](https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/spring-2015-gemnews-moldavites-natural-fake)).

### Five Checks You Can Do at Home

1. **Look for bubbles and flow lines.** Most genuine moldavite holds trapped gas bubbles, often stretched long, and swirly flow lines, visible at 10x and often to the eye (Geology.com), and almost always far more bubbles than artificial glass (*Gems & Gemology*).
2. **Hunt for lechatelierite.** Under a loupe, look for wire-shaped or lens-shaped inclusions standing out in high relief, a strong indication of moldavite (International Gem Society) that only some specimens show. Dipping a rough piece in water helps reveal the wires (*Gems & Gemology*).
3. **Check size and price.** A faceted moldavite over 2 carats, or any piece without eye-visible bubbles, has a good chance of being fake (Geology.com).
4. **Do not trust the surface alone.** Fakers now copy the natural etched skin almost exactly, likely with hydrofluoric acid, on realistic small stones of a few grams (*Gems & Gemology*). A shape that looks too perfect is a warning, and a smooth, glossy "raw" piece without the usual etched look deserves a closer look. A cut and polished moldavite can be genuine, though, since rounds, emerald cuts and cabochons are its usual cuts (International Gem Society).
5. **Read the origin.** Anything sold as African moldavite is out (see the box above). Those pieces tend to look clearer, with fewer inclusions, than real moldavite (International Gem Society).

A glassy break settles nothing, since real moldavite is glass too, and only a gem lab gives a positive identification (Geology.com).

### What a Gem Lab Checks

For a polished stone, lechatelierite and refractive index are the best tools, with density and ultraviolet light as backup. Some fakes do not fluoresce, and one gave itself away with a specific gravity of 3.60 (*Gems & Gemology*).

| Property | Natural moldavite | Recent Chinese imitation | 19th-century glass |
|---|---|---|---|
| Color | Light green to brown | Light green | Light green |
| Refractive index | 1.490 (1.480 to 1.510) | 1.520 | 1.545 to 1.580 |
| Density | 2.35 (2.27 to 2.46) | 2.52 to 2.53 | Not measured |
| UV light | Inert | Chalky in short-wave UV | Inert |

Data from *Gems & Gemology*, Spring 2015. For hard cases, labs measure chemistry with LA-ICP-MS, which helps separate moldavite from soda-lime green glass, or use FTIR spectrometry (International Gem Society).

### Where to Buy Real Moldavite

Our sources name no seller, and Geology.com warns that online, "there is a very good chance that you will receive imitation material." An authenticity card or good reviews are the seller's word, not a test. Before you pay, ask these.

1. Where exactly was it found? Real moldavite comes from the Ries strewn field, mostly southern Bohemia.
2. Can you send close-ups with light behind the stone, showing bubbles or flow lines?
3. Will you accept a return if a gem lab says it is glass?

## How to Care for Moldavite

**Mineral Fact.** At Mohs 5 to 5.5, moldavite is about as hard as man-made glass and brittle, so earrings, pins and pendants suit it better than rings, which need a protective bezel and only occasional wear (Geology.com, International Gem Society).

- **Water.** OK. A soft brush, mild detergent and warm water (International Gem Society). Our sources cover washing, not long soaks. A quick dip for the lechatelierite check is fine, but skip the overnight soak.
- **Cleaning machines.** Skip them. Moldavite is full of inclusions, so no steam or ultrasonic cleaners (International Gem Society).
- **Sun.** Unknown.
- **Heat.** Unknown. No source behind this page covers it, so as a precaution for brittle glass, keep it away from open flame and sudden temperature swings.
- **Salt and chemicals.** Unknown. Skip salt cleanses until a reference source covers them.
- **Storage.** On its own, in a soft pouch, away from harder stones that can scratch it.
- **Toxicity.** OK to wear and handle. The International Gem Society [toxicity table](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/gemstone-care-guide/) lists no known risk, and its silica warning for tektites concerns cutting dust.

Check your exact piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Moldavite vs Look-Alikes

- **Green bottle and soda-lime glass.** The main impostor, with fewer bubbles and a different refractive index and density (see the lab table).
- **Libyan desert glass.** A real tektite from Africa, but pale yellowish green, while moldavite tends to be darker green (International Gem Society). More on our [Libyan desert glass page](/stones/libyan-desert-glass/).
- **Other tektites.** Tektites from other places are black, brown or yellow (International Gem Society). See our [tektite page](/stones/tektite/).
- **Peridot.** "Chrysolite" is an old name for yellowish green olivine, or peridot, so "bohemian chrysolite" is a misnomer for moldavite (Geology.com).

## Price Guide

Rising prices are exactly why imitations spread (*Gems & Gemology*, 2015). Czech supply has been depleted, and the best rough specimens often go to collectors for more than they would fetch as jewelry (Geology.com). Most genuine faceted stones are small, so a large faceted stone at a bargain price is the red flag.

What moves the price is mostly size and quality, because large faceted stones and big rough pieces are rare (International Gem Society). Part of the premium also comes from belief, since many people pay extra for its reputed healing properties (Geology.com). We give no price figures here, since the only current listing we found gave no size or weight.

What the fakes cost buyers is the price of a real stone paid for glass. Much of the moldavite sold online is undisclosed man-made glass, so Geology.com advises anyone spending serious money on it to be knowledgeable and careful. A high price is no guarantee either, since the two huge fakes Hyršl saw were in a high-end jewelry shop (*Gems & Gemology*). The checks above matter more than the tag.

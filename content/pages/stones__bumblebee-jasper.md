# Bumblebee Jasper Meaning, Properties & Real-Life Uses

Bumblebee jasper is not a jasper. It is a trade name for a yellow, orange and black stone from a West Java volcano that a 2018 gemological study found to be mostly calcite colored by arsenic minerals. Crystal guides link it with confidence and joy. The honest note is safety, so keep it dry, out of mouths and away from children.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Real bumblebee stone shows bright yellow to orange bands with black, often in bull's-eye circles. Under magnification it looks like colored powder cemented in stone. Never scratch or acid-test it at home, since that makes dust from arsenic minerals. Only a lab can confirm what a piece is.

## What Bumblebee Jasper Actually Is

**The Stone.** Sellers usually describe bumblebee jasper as a volcanic rock with sulfur and arsenic minerals from Indonesia. The closest look comes from a 2018 paper in *The Journal of Gemmology* by Emmanuel Fritsch and Joel Ivey, summarized by the gemological association [Gem-A](https://gem-a.com/?p=7188), which calls the material Bumble Bee Stone (BBS).

A drop of dilute hydrochloric acid made the surface bubble, showing the stone is rich in carbonate. "Therefore, it is not jasper, which is an opaque form of microcrystalline quartz," the summary concludes. Electron microscope analysis showed it is mostly calcite, probably slightly magnesian (Gem-A).

Real jasper is an opaque variety of microcrystalline quartz that the [International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/jasper-gem-information/) calls trouble free to care for. Even one retailer says this stone "isn't technically a jasper at all" ([Rock Your World Gems](https://www.rockyourworldgems.com/pages/about-bumblebee-jasper)).

### What Gives Bumblebee Jasper Its Colors?

Arsenic and iron sulfides, not sulfur itself. The bright yellow comes from "an unexpected sulfide, pararealgar," a yellow form of realgar, and the orange from a mix of pararealgar and realgar, both arsenic sulfide, As₄S₄. The black is pyrite, an iron sulfide, in tiny discs (Gem-A).

Gem-A places it in "a rare category of gems coloured by micro-inclusions of sulphide pigments." The retailer above credits sulfur compounds and manganese oxides as well, but the lab analysis found arsenic sulfides and pyrite.

### How Bumblebee Jasper Forms

It grew at a solfatara, a fumarole that vents hot, sulfur-rich gases, close to the Mount Ciremai volcano. Heated groundwater picked up iron, sulfur, calcium carbonate and arsenic from the volcanic system, and as the gases escaped, minerals settled in bands inside fractures in volcanic ash and tuff (Gem-A).

The veins run nearly vertical, and single color bands rarely exceed about 2 inches (5 cm), according to Gem-A.

The famous bull's-eyes come from slicing across rounded, grape-like growths, and a typical one measures about half an inch (10 to 14 mm) across (Gem-A).

### Where Bumblebee Jasper Comes From

The mining area sits on the lower slopes of Mount Ciremai (or Cereme), an active volcano about 16 miles (25 km) southwest of the town of Cirebon in West Java. Production began in 2003 and had yielded an estimated 150 tons by the 2018 study, and no other deposit has been documented (Gem-A). Many sellers name Papandayan, another West Java volcano, instead.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** Bumblebee jasper has no ancient lore, and two shop guides say so plainly. Its whole history is recent.

- **1990s.** Shop histories disagree. One says an American rock and mineral enthusiast working in Indonesia named it. Another credits a company called IndoAgate in the mid-1990s and gives the local name as Batu Badar Blerang, translated as "fumarole jasper."
- **2003.** Production of the material begins, according to the Gem-A summary.
- **2018.** Fritsch and Ivey identify pararealgar as the yellow pigment and show the stone is mostly calcite, not jasper (Gem-A).
- **Borrowed lore.** **Tradition.** Old jasper lore belongs to real jasper. Pliny the Elder wrote that "throughout all the East, it is the custom, it is said, to wear iaspis by way of amulet" (*Natural History*, Book 37, chapter 37, Bostock and Riley translation, [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0137%3Abook%3D37%3Achapter%3D37)). Iaspis is a good, if not exact, match for modern jasper, and none of it is about this stone.
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Confidence, courage, personal power and joy lead, with the solar plexus chakra, Leo and the element fire, which one guide ties to the volcano where the stone formed.

## What People Use Bumblebee Jasper for Today

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal guides, bumblebee jasper is Widely Linked with [confidence](/crystals-for/confidence/), often phrased as self-esteem, courage and being yourself. It is Often Linked with focus, especially clarity during hard decisions, and Sometimes Linked with sleep, anxiety, protection and grounding. One guide even nicknames it the other hematite, for grounding.

The "why" starts with the color. Guides call it a bright yellow stone of the solar plexus, the energy center they tie to personal power and confidence. The volcano adds fire, and the name adds cheer, since one shop calls it "a happy stone of courage."

### What Does Bumblebee Jasper Do?

As a material, it is a soft, porous stone best for cabochons, pendants and light-wear pieces, as the retailer puts it. Everything else is belief about the person holding it.

Some guides list allergies, skin rashes, blood sugar, fertility, circulation or immunity. Nothing in the mineral sources behind this page supports those claims.

## How to Use Bumblebee Jasper in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** This is a stone to look at more than to hold. Each idea keeps handling short and the stone dry, and each is a cue for a habit, not a power of the stone.

### A Desk Stone You Look at, Not Rub

**Try This.** Set a polished piece in a small dish at eye level on your desk. Before you start the task you dread most, look at the yellow for one slow breath and say the first step out loud. Skip the worry-stone habit with this one, since constant rubbing wears a soft stone.

### The Overnight Decision Card

**Try This.** Guides suggest it for hard decisions. Write your two options on an index card, one per side, and set the stone on it on a high shelf before bed. In the morning, flip the card and notice which side you hoped to see. Your reaction holds the answer.

### A Gift for a Grown-Up, with a Care Card

**Try This.** For an adult friend who loves bold colors, wrap a polished piece with a card that says what it is, calcite from a volcanic gas vent in Java, colored by arsenic minerals. Add the care rules in one line. Keep it dry, wash your hands after handling, and keep it from small children and pets.

**Safety.** Keep it out of reach of children and pets, wash your hands after handling it, and never put it in your mouth, a drink or a bath.

**What Research Says.** In a [2025 randomized study](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1092852925100515) of 138 adults, Escolà-Gascón and colleagues gave people real rose quartz or a matching glass fake for 14 days, and anxiety scores fell only in those who believed in crystal healing, with the fake doing as well as the real stone. It was one trial in adults with low anxiety, and it did not test bumblebee stone. If a desk ritual helps you, the cue and your own expectations are doing the work.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Bumblebee jasper is often chosen for stress, worry and sleepless nights, and some guides make claims about blood sugar or fertility. A stone can be a comfort to look at, but it is not care. If stress, anxiety or poor sleep lasts or feels heavy, talk to a doctor or a therapist, and bring blood sugar or fertility questions to your doctor. If anyone swallows a piece, call the Poison Help line (details below). If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My Bumblebee Jasper Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone. The name is the first thing to correct.

- **Natural, untreated.** The material Gem-A describes, colored by natural sulfide pigments. In 2014 GIA reported that most specimens are filled (see the next label), and we found no newer figure for the untreated share.
- **Natural, treated.** The stone is porous (Gem-A). In 2014, GIA's *Gems & Gemology* reported that the porous rock is easily cut and polished and that most specimens are filled with a product called Opticon ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/summer-2014-gemnews-bumble-bee-jasper-indonesia)). We found no reference source on dyed or coated bumblebee stone.
- **Lab-grown.** We found no reference source on a lab-made version.
- **Imitation.** We found no reference source describing dyed or other stones sold as bumblebee jasper.
- **Trade names.** "Bumblebee jasper" is a trade name that hides what the stone is, since it is not jasper or quartz. Gem-A uses Bumble Bee Stone, or BBS, and GIA's 2014 report notes it was also sold at the Tucson show as Eclipse "jasper." A piece sold under the jasper name can still be the real material.

Two checks you can do at home, and one you should never do.

1. **Read the pattern.** Look for bright yellow to orange bands with black, often as bull's-eye circles (Gem-A). The limit is that colors vary, and Gem-A notes that 2017 material was more orange than earlier finds.
2. **Look under magnification.** Real material looks like colored powders cemented in a carbonate matrix, with tiny cavities that can hold orange to reddish crystals (Gem-A). This is a strong hint, not a proof.
3. **Never acid-test or scratch it.** The lab's acid drop made it fizz. At home that damages the stone, and scratching turns arsenic-bearing minerals into dust.

Three questions to ask the seller. Has it been filled, stabilized, sealed or dyed? Where was it mined? Is this piece finished, or raw?

## How to Care for Bumblebee Jasper

**Mineral Fact.** Bumblebee stone is mostly calcite, which rates 3 on the Mohs scale ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/calcite.shtml)), and one retailer puts the stone at around 4. It is porous, and it reacts to acid (Gem-A).

- **Water.** Avoid. A retailer advises against soaking or prolonged water exposure for this soft, porous stone.
- **Soaks.** Avoid, for the same reason.
- **Sun.** Unknown. No source covers it yet.
- **Heat.** Unknown. No source covers it yet.
- **Acids and chemicals.** Avoid. It fizzes in dilute hydrochloric acid (Gem-A), and the retailer says to avoid acids.
- **Salt.** Unknown. No source covers it yet.
- **Cleaning.** Unknown in our data. A dry, soft cloth is the gentle choice until a source covers it.
- **Storage.** Unknown in our data. Since calcite rates only 3, a separate soft pouch is the cautious choice.
- **Toxicity.** Avoid. A retailer says it is safe to wear but contains arsenic and sulfur minerals. Do not ingest it or let children mouth it.

### Is Bumblebee Jasper Toxic?

Treat it as toxic if swallowed or ground into dust. Our Care Checker rates its toxicity Avoid. A retailer says it is safe to wear but contains arsenic and sulfur minerals, and should not be ingested or handled by children who might put it in their mouth.

The reason is in the colors, pararealgar and realgar, both arsenic sulfides (Gem-A). For realgar on its own, the International Gem Society rates the toxicity risk High, hazard arsenic, and describes it as dangerous if swallowed ([IGS care guide](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/gemstone-care-guide/)). The IGS advises cleaning yourself properly and avoiding dust when handling realgar ([IGS realgar](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/realgar-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)), and [Minerals.net](https://www.minerals.net/mineral/realgar) recommends washing your hands afterward. Bumblebee stone is mostly calcite, not pure realgar, but we apply the same habits.

- **Handling.** Wearing a finished piece is fine by the retailer's account. Wash your hands after handling any piece, and take extra care with raw, chipped or crumbly ones.
- **Cutting.** Never cut, drill, sand or polish it at home. The International Gem Society warns that lapidaries should take care cutting potentially toxic minerals, since inhaling or swallowing particles could harm your health.
- **Children and pets.** Keep it out of reach, and do not give it to a child as a pocket stone.
- **If someone swallows a piece.** In the US, call the Poison Help line at 1-800-222-1222, toll-free and available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week ([Poison Help](https://poisonhelp.hrsa.gov/)). Call 911 right away if the person collapses, has a seizure, has trouble breathing or cannot be woken ([Poison Control](https://poison.org/need-immediate-assistance)). For a pet, call your vet at once.

Our guide to [toxic crystals](/care/toxic-crystals/) covers the other stones that need the same caution.

### Can Bumblebee Jasper Get Wet?

Keep it dry. Our Care Checker rates water Avoid, because a retailer advises against soaking or long water exposure for this soft, porous stone. Take a bumblebee ring or bracelet off before you wash your hands, shower or swim, and if a piece gets splashed, pat it dry at once.

That rules out every water cleanse, from running water to a moon water jar. One guide calls it safe for indirect gem water, but our care data wins. Its water and toxicity ratings are both Avoid, so it has no place near anything you drink, and our list of [crystals that can't go in water](/care/crystals-that-cant-go-in-water/) includes it for both reasons.

### Can Bumblebee Jasper Go in the Sun?

We do not know yet, so keep it out of strong sun. Our Care Checker rates sun Unknown because no source tests the stone itself. Realgar, one of its pigments, slowly turns into yellow pararealgar in light, according to the International Gem Society. Check your piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Bumblebee Jasper vs Look-Alikes

- **Orange calcite.** Also calcite, and our Care Checker rates its toxicity OK. A piece sold as bumblebee with no black pattern at all deserves a second look. See [orange calcite](/stones/orange-calcite/).
- **Crazy lace agate.** A banded agate with streaks of yellow, red and brown. It is quartz, harder at 6.5 to 7, and rated OK for toxicity. See [crazy lace agate](/stones/crazy-lace-agate/).
- **Realgar.** The orange pigment as a mineral on its own, rated Avoid for water, sun, heat and toxicity in our data. See [realgar](/stones/realgar/).
- **Real jasper.** Opaque quartz, hardy enough for all jewelry uses (International Gem Society).

## Price Guide

Gem-A says the most sought-after bumblebee stone is the orbicular variety with a bull's-eye pattern, and its photos show the best pieces with strong color contrast. Treatment is the other question, since GIA reported in 2014 that most specimens are filled with Opticon, so ask whether a piece is filled before comparing prices.

# Garnet vs Ruby, What Really Sets Them Apart

Garnet and ruby can both be deep red, but they are different minerals. Ruby is red corundum, colored by chromium and rated 9 on the Mohs scale. Red garnet belongs to a group of silicate minerals rated 6.5 to 7.5. Ruby is far rarer and pricier, and only a gemologist's test settles a doubtful red stone.

## What Each Stone Really Is

### Ruby, the Red Corundum

**Mineral Fact.** Ruby is the most valuable variety of corundum, the red one. Gem corundum in every other color is sapphire ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/ruby/gem-overview)). A trace of chromium makes it red, and red fluorescence makes fine ruby seem to glow in sunlight (GIA). Where ruby ends and pink sapphire begins is not agreed ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/ruby-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). Myanmar's Mogok valley was the main source for centuries (GIA).

Ruby also has a history of being something else. Many famous historic "rubies" turned out to be red spinels, the Black Prince's Ruby in the British crown jewels among them (IGS).

### Garnet, the Red Family

**Mineral Fact.** Garnet is not one mineral. It is a set of closely related minerals forming a group, with gems in almost every color ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/garnet/gem-overview)). The red ones include pyrope, almandine and blends of the two, such as the purplish rhodolite ([IGS](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/garnet-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). Garnets are never found pure, and the purest pyrope on record was about 83 percent pyrope (IGS). Most garnet forms where shale is metamorphosed at converging plate boundaries ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/garnet.shtml)).

Red garnet is one of the most common and widespread of gems, and one of the oldest in jewelry. GIA describes a red garnet bead necklace from an Egyptian grave that is more than 5,000 years old.

## How to Tell Garnet From Ruby

Color will not do it. Double refraction will, in the hands of someone with the right instruments.

### Why Color Fails

Chrome pyrope garnet can show a red that rivals fine ruby, though it is typically very dark in tone (IGS). Going the other way, iron-rich rubies from Thailand and Cambodia often have a garnet-like red, and some of their facets look blackish, much like red garnet (IGS).

The confusion is old. Blood-red cabochons were once all called carbuncles, a name gemologists now split into garnets, spinels and rubies (IGS). Kunz saw the result firsthand. At the Düsseldorf Exhibition of 1891, the "Ring of St. Elizabeth", said to hold her miraculously luminous ruby, turned out to be set with "a large almost flat carbuncle garnet of no great brilliancy" (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, page 165).

### The One Test That Separates Them

**Mineral Fact.** Ruby is doubly refractive and red garnet is not. GIA gives ruby a birefringence of 0.008 to 0.010 and lists none for garnet ([GIA ruby](https://www.gia.edu/ruby/gem-overview), [GIA garnet](https://www.gia.edu/garnet/gem-overview)), and the International Gem Society describes pyrope and almandine as generally isotropic, meaning singly refractive.

The refractive index alone will not settle it, since garnet's wide range, 1.714 to 1.888, takes in ruby's 1.762 to 1.770 (GIA). The doubled reading is what gives ruby away. A gemologist reads it with standard instruments, without harming the stone [TO CHECK a source naming the instruments, refractometer or polariscope]. One caution. Calcium garnets such as grossular and andradite can show some double refraction (IGS), another reason to leave the final call to a professional.

### Home Clues and Their Limits

1. **Tone.** A very dark red leans toward garnet, chrome pyrope especially (IGS). Tone alone proves nothing.
2. **A UV flashlight.** Rubies fluoresce red under long and shortwave ultraviolet light, and Myanmar stones can glow strongly, but Thai stones generally lack the glow (IGS). A red glow fits ruby. No glow proves nothing. [TO CHECK how red garnet and lab-made ruby react under UV.] Never shine a UV light into anyone's eyes.
3. **No scratch test.** The hardness gap, 9 against 6.5 to 7.5, is real, but scratching a gem damages it and settles less than a lab can.
4. **A lab report for anything costly.** Modern lab-made rubies can have natural-looking color and inclusions that challenge even trained professionals (IGS).

### Three Questions for the Seller

1. Is this ruby, meaning corundum, or a garnet, and which garnet?
2. Has it been heated, and has it been diffused or filled with glass? Simple heating is broadly accepted when disclosed, but diffusion coloring and polymer or lead glass filling are not (IGS).
3. Is it natural or lab-grown, and is there a lab report?

## What People Believe About Each

**Tradition.** Ruby carries the grander titles. In Sanskrit it is *ratnaraj*, "king of precious stones" (Kunz 1913, page 101), and the medieval "Lapidaire" of Philippe de Valois called it "the lord of stones" (page 102). An inextinguishable flame was thought to burn inside it (pages 101 to 102), and in Burma a ruby set into the flesh was believed to make its bearer invulnerable to spear, sword or gun (page 103).

Garnet's stories are earthier. The word carbuncle, used for certain fine garnets, meant a glowing coal (pages 292 to 293). A lion engraved on a garnet was said to guard its wearer's honors and health and protect travelers (page 134). And Kunz reports that some "Asiatic tribes" used garnets as bullets, on the theory that a blood-red stone would wound more deeply. The "rebellious Hanzas", as he calls them, fired them at British troops on the Kashmir frontier in 1892 (pages 33 to 34).

**Modern Association.** Modern crystal guides tie ruby to the heart and root chakras, with passion and protection as its themes. Garnet gets the root and heart chakras too, along with the element fire. These are modern associations, not properties of either stone.

## Which to Choose

- **For a birthday.** Garnet is the January birthstone and ruby the July one on the modern American list ([American Gem Society](https://www.americangemsociety.org/birthstones/)). See our [January birthstone guide](/birthstones/january/) and [July birthstone guide](/birthstones/july/).
- **For an anniversary.** Garnet marks the 2nd, ruby the 15th and the 40th (GIA).
- **For a budget.** Garnet includes affordable dark red varieties, while rare greens such as tsavorite and demantoid cost more (GIA, IGS). Ruby runs from a few hundred to thousands of dollars per carat (IGS), and in 2015 a 25.59-carat ruby ring sold for $1,266,901 per carat, a record for a colored gemstone at auction (GIA).
- **For daily wear.** Ruby rates 9 on the Mohs scale and garnet 6.5 to 7.5, so ruby resists scratches better. Our care data has no water, sun or salt rating for either stone yet, so check the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/) before any cleansing ritual.
- **For color.** Ruby is always red. Garnet comes in nearly every color, so a garnet lover has room to roam.

**Try This.** Giving one as a gift? Tuck in a card with the stone's real name and one line of its story, "the glowing coal" for a garnet, "king of precious stones" for a ruby. More on each in our [garnet](/stones/garnet/) and [ruby](/stones/ruby/) pages.

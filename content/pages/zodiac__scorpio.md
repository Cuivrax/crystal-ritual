# Scorpio Crystals & Birthstones, Plus Where the Links Come From

Scorpio has no single official birthstone, because birthstones belong to months and Scorpio runs from about October 23 to November 21. That gives opal or tourmaline for an October birthday and topaz or citrine for a November one. As a zodiac stone, a 1913 list gives Scorpio the beryl, while today's crystal guides favor dark stones like malachite, charoite and obsidian, all beliefs rather than mineral facts.

## What Is the Scorpio Birthstone?

The honest answer has two parts, the month stone and the sign stone.

**The month stone.** On the modern list of the [American Gem Society](https://www.americangemsociety.org/birthstones/), October has two birthstones, opal and tourmaline, and November has two, topaz and citrine. The 1912 jewelers' list gave October the opal, with tourmaline as its alternate, and November the topaz (Kunz 1913, pages 319 to 320). Citrine joined November in 1952. Our [October](/birthstones/october/) and [November](/birthstones/november/) pages go stone by stone.

**The sign stone.** **Tradition.** The traditional Scorpio zodiac stone is the beryl, from the verse list of zodiac gems that George Frederick Kunz printed in 1913. "Through Scorpio this gem so fair / Is that which every one should wear, / Or tears of sad repentance bear, / The beryl" (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, page 347). On the same page, an old Spanish list that Kunz thought probably reflected Arab tradition gives Scorpio the garnet.

Kunz also wanted the beryl for October itself. Beryl appeared for October in all eight old month lists he compared (page 316), and he complained, "For October neither the tourmaline nor the opal is as appropriate as the beryl" (page 320). In his calendar of the months, October carries the beryl, the sign of Scorpio and hops as its flower, with a verse that opens "October's child is born for woe" (page 330).

### What Color Is the Scorpio Birthstone?

There is no single Scorpio birthstone color. Opal can have any body color, with a play of color in precious opal. Tourmaline occurs in more colors than any other mineral group, black most commonly. Most natural topaz is colorless, though it also comes in yellow, orange, brown, red, pink, blue and green, and citrine runs from pale yellow to brownish orange. The 1913 beryl is colorless when pure and takes its color from traces of other elements.

Modern guides go darker, with black obsidian, deep green malachite and purple charoite. The month stones span almost every color, while the modern picks lean dark.

## Scorpio Crystals in Today's Guides

**Modern Association.** Most lists of crystals for Scorpio come from modern crystal guides. One shop guide calls Scorpio a water sign of "depth, transformation, and uncompromising truth," and another names Pluto as its ruler. No stone reaches Classic for Scorpio.

### Malachite

**Widely Linked.** [Malachite](/stones/malachite/) is a green copper carbonate mineral and a minor copper ore, always green and often banded. It rates only 3.5 to 4.5 on the Mohs scale and is brittle with perfect cleavage, so earrings and pendants suit it better than rings. Dust from grinding it is toxic to breathe, though finished jewelry and carvings should be safe. One guide notes that its links to Scorpio and Capricorn "are recent and vary from source to source."

### Charoite

**Widely Linked.** [Charoite](/stones/charoite/) is a purple ornamental material that is really a rock, made almost entirely of the mineral charoite. It comes only from the Chara River area of the Sakha Republic in Russia, and manganese gives it its color. It rates 5 to 6 on the Mohs scale and has no known enhancements. Even one guide that recommends it for Scorpio says it is "not directly related to any birth sign or the zodiac signs in the traditional sense."

### Obsidian

**Widely Linked.** [Obsidian](/stones/obsidian/) is volcanic glass, formed when molten rock cooled too fast for crystals to grow. Black is its most common color. At 5 to 6 on the Mohs scale it scratches and chips easily, and even household dust is harder. One guide ties it to Scorpio through Pluto, which it says "governs transformation, death and rebirth, and the surfacing of what is hidden."

### Unakite

**Widely Linked.** [Unakite](/stones/unakite/) is a pink and green metamorphic rock named for the Unaka range of North Carolina and Tennessee. It rates 6 to 7 on the Mohs scale, but its minerals have perfect cleavage, so it suits pieces kept away from knocks.

### Kunzite

**Often Linked.** [Kunzite](/stones/kunzite/), the best-known variety of the mineral spodumene, is named after George F. Kunz, the man who printed the 1913 list, so his own name now sits on Scorpio's modern lists.

### Beryl, the 1913 Stone

**Rarely Linked.** Modern Scorpio lists hardly mention [beryl](/stones/beryl/), though most of its varieties are familiar. Blue to blue-green beryl is [aquamarine](/stones/aquamarine/), golden beryl is heliodor, pink beryl is morganite, colorless beryl is goshenite, and green beryl colored by chromium or vanadium is emerald. Beryl rates 7.5 to 8 on the Mohs scale, but it is brittle and breaks along its cleavage. **Tradition.** Kunz notes that polished spheres of beryl were often preferred for crystal gazing (1913, page 180).

### November Stones for November Scorpios

**Rarely Linked.** [Topaz](/stones/topaz/) rates 8 on the Mohs scale but cleaves easily, and nearly all blue topaz in stores is colorless topaz that was irradiated, then heated. [Citrine](/stones/citrine/) is yellow to orange quartz, and most of it is heated amethyst. One guide still ties citrine to Scorpio through its place as a November birthstone. Opal and tourmaline, the October stones, are covered on the [Libra](/zodiac/libra/) page.

## Where the Scorpio Links Come From

**The Story.** Scorpio's oldest gem link sits beneath a Roman emperor.

- **Roman Empire.** **Tradition.** The Gemma Augustea, a cameo by the gem engraver Dioskorides that commemorates a triumph of Tiberius in 13 CE, shows the sign of Scorpio beneath the figure of Tiberius, "under which that emperor was born" (Kunz 1913, page 342).
- **Medieval Jewish tradition.** **Tradition.** The tribe of Manasseh was paired with Scorpio (page 314).
- **Old star and talisman lists.** **Tradition.** A list of fixed stars gives the magnet to the tail of the Great Bear at 8° of Scorpio (page 351). Hercules, under the sign of Scorpio, "renders the wearer victorious in all conflicts in the field," and Vexillum, a flag on a lance, also promises victory (pages 352 and 355).
- **Old planetary lists.** **Tradition.** In one list the moon controlled the beryl, and in the Lapidario of Alfonso X Venus lent it virtue (page 348). One writer on crystal gazing made Libra, not Scorpio, the sign that controlled its virtues (page 212).
- **1913.** **Tradition.** Kunz's verse list names the beryl and the old Spanish list the garnet (page 347).
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Malachite, charoite, boulder opal, obsidian and unakite lead the modern lists of zodiac stones for Scorpio.

The garnet is one place where old and new meet. One shop guide calls it "the main stone for Scorpio," just as the old Spanish list did.

## How to Use Scorpio Stones in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** Scorpio season brings darker evenings. Whatever you believe about the stones, these give a pause you chose and something in your hand to mark it.

### A Journal Stone for Darker Evenings

**Try This.** Keep an obsidian palm stone on top of a notebook by your reading chair. Each evening, lift it and write one honest sentence about how the day really went. Guides call Scorpio truth-seeking, and one true line a night is enough. Obsidian chips easily, so it stays on the notebook, not in a pocket with keys.

### A Two-Minute Gazing Pause

**Try This.** Old crystal gazers preferred beryl spheres. Borrow the pause, not the visions. Set a small aquamarine or clear quartz sphere on a dark cloth, put your phone in another room and look into the stone for two minutes before bed. It is simply a quiet place to rest your eyes.

### A Birthday Card with Two Stones

**Try This.** For an October Scorpio, give an opal or a tourmaline and copy the 1913 beryl verse inside the card. For a November Scorpio, give a topaz or a citrine and add one line about the Gemma Augustea, where Scorpio sits beneath an emperor.

**Safety.** Malachite is a copper mineral, so it never goes in water you drink. Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them. These are personal practices, not treatments.

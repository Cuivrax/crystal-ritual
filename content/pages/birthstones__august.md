# August Birthstone Meaning, History & Honest Buying Tips

The August birthstones are peridot, spinel and sardonyx, the three stones the American Gem Society lists for the month. Peridot is the yellowish green gem variety of olivine, spinel is a hard gem found in red, pink and many other colors, and sardonyx is chalcedony banded in red-brown with white or black. Sardonyx led the 1912 US jewelers' list, peridot was its alternate, and spinel joined in 2016.

## What Is the August Birthstone?

August has three, peridot, spinel and sardonyx. The American Gem Society and GIA list all three. Older lists gave August a fourth stone, carnelian.

| List | August stone | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Arabian and Polish lists | Sardonyx | Kunz 1913, page 315 |

### Modern vs Traditional August Birthstones

"Traditional" means sardonyx, which GIA calls the original August birthstone, with a history of more than 4,000 years. "Modern" means peridot and spinel. Today's charts put peridot first, though in 1912 it was only the alternate.

### Why Does August Have Three Birthstones?

When spinel was added in July 2016, the American Gem Society noted that "August now joins June and December as the only months represented by three gems." The three also cover three ways of wearing a stone. Peridot is the green one, spinel the hard one for an everyday ring, and sardonyx the opaque, carvable one for a signet or a cameo.

## Where the August Birthstone Comes From

**Tradition.** The old month lists follow the twelve foundation stones of Revelation, counted from March (Kunz 1913, pages 307 to 308). The sixth stone, the sardius, which Kunz reads as carnelian, fell to August, and the seventh, the chrysolite, to September (pages 303, 316 and 319). That is why carnelian leads the old August lists and why peridot, under its old name chrysolite, was a September stone in six of the eight lists Kunz compared.

Kunz's calendar of the months still gives August the carnelian, with a stern verse. "Wear a carnelian or for thee / No conjugal felicity; / The August-born without this stone, / 'Tis said, must live unloved, alone" (page 329).

The 1912 jewelers chose sardonyx, already August's stone in the Arabian and Polish lists, and moved peridot over from September, "another undesirable change" in Kunz's view (page 320). Our [birthstone chart](/birthstones/) tells the rest, including [September](/birthstones/september/).

## Peridot, the Green August Birthstone

**The Stone.** [Peridot](/stones/peridot/) is the gem variety of olivine, (Mg,Fe)₂SiO₄, a mineral of the Earth's upper mantle that volcanoes bring to the surface. Its yellowish green comes from iron that is part of the mineral itself, not from a trace impurity, so peridot comes only in yellowish greens to greens, some with a brownish tint. Some peridot is 4.5 billion years old, found in pallasite meteorites left from the birth of the solar system, and in 2005 peridot turned up in comet dust brought back by the Stardust space probe.

The Red Sea island of Zabargad is the oldest recorded source, lost for centuries and rediscovered in 1905. Myanmar yields the largest stones, and Arizona is the main US source.

**The Story.** Peridot spent most of its history under other names.

- **Ancient Egypt.** **Tradition.** The Egyptians called it the "gem of the sun." Greek writers said its island was guarded and that the gem could only be seen at night by its own glow, so seekers marked the spot and found it the next day (Kunz 1913, page 66).
- **The word topaz.** The Greeks called the island Topazios, and the name eventually passed to the yellow gem we call topaz. Pliny's "topazos," a stone "held in very high estimation for its green tints," is read by his translators as peridot (*Natural History*, book 37, chapter 32).
- **The Crusades.** The finest peridots reached Europe's cathedral treasures around the time of the Crusades and were generally called emeralds, among them stones nearly two inches long in the Treasury of the Three Magi at Cologne Cathedral (Kunz 1913, page 66). Some historians speculate that Cleopatra's emeralds were peridot too.
- **Middle Ages.** **Tradition.** Set in gold, the chrysolite was said to dispel "the vague terrors of the night" (page 67).

## Spinel, the Newest August Birthstone

**The Stone.** [Spinel](/stones/spinel/) is magnesium aluminum oxide, MgAl₂O₄, rated 7.5 to 8 on the Mohs scale with no cleavage. Its name comes from the Latin spina, thorn, for the shape of its crystals. Chromium makes it red and pink, iron gives most blues, and cobalt the most vivid blue. Major sources include Tajikistan, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, Vietnam, Tanzania and Pakistan. Natural spinel on the market is almost all untreated.

**The Story.** Spinel is the great impostor of the crown jewels.

- **Burma.** **Tradition.** Spinel crystals are so perfect that they are said to be nat thwe, "polished by the spirits."
- **Middle Ages.** The balas ruby, a spinel from Badakhshan in Central Asia, was one of the most admired gems of medieval times, and Dante used it as a symbol of divine joy (Kunz 1915, page 401).
- **14th century.** The Black Prince's Ruby, about 170 carats, in the British Imperial State Crown, is a red spinel.
- **1783.** The mineralogist Jean Baptiste Louis Romé de l'Isle identified spinel as a different mineral from ruby, though Geology.com dates the moment European scientists and gem dealers learned the difference to the mid-1800s. Many famous "rubies" had been spinels all along.

## Sardonyx, the Original August Birthstone

**The Stone.** [Sardonyx](/stones/sardonyx/) joins two kinds of chalcedony, a quartz made of microscopic crystals. Bands of brownish red to brown or dark orange sard alternate with white or black onyx. It rates 6.5 to 7 on the Mohs scale. India is known for sardonyx with good contrast, and it also comes from Brazil, Uruguay, Germany, Madagascar and the United States. Like other chalcedony, it is commonly dyed.

**The Story.** Sardonyx was a working stone before it was a birthstone.

- **Ancient Rome.** Romans cut seals and signet rings in sardonyx because hot wax would not stick to it, and soldiers wore rings carved with Mars for protection in battle. It remained a favorite for cameos and intaglios.
- **1st century.** **Tradition.** The historian Josephus rendered one of the high priest's breastplate stones as sardonyx (Kunz 1913, pages 290 to 291), and Revelation names it the fifth foundation stone (page 303).
- **Medieval England.** **Tradition.** The Abbey of St. Alban kept an onyx gem it lent to women in childbirth. One borrower kept it until her death, and her daughter returned the "long-lost sardonyx" from her own deathbed (Kunz 1915, pages 152 to 153).

## What August Birthstones Mean Today

**Modern Association.** GIA ties sardonyx to "courage, happiness and clear communication," an easy line to draw from Roman soldiers and their Mars rings. In our rankings sardonyx is a Classic stone for confidence and Widely Linked for protection. Peridot is Widely Linked for protection and money, a modern echo of the sun gem said to chase away the terrors of the night. Spinel is Widely Linked for love, protection, anxiety and confidence. Some sites list health benefits for these stones, which nothing in the mineral or medical sources behind this page supports.

### What Is the August Birthstone Color?

Green, mostly, from peridot's yellowish green. Spinel adds red, pink, orange, purple and blue, and sardonyx brings red-brown and white bands.

August splits between [Leo](/zodiac/leo/), until about August 22, and [Virgo](/zodiac/virgo/). Kunz's zodiac verses give Leo the onyx and Virgo the carnelian (1913, page 346), while several modern guides pair peridot and sardonyx with Leo.

## How to Choose an August Birthstone

Start with how it will be worn. The Mohs scale ranks scratch resistance from talc at 1 to diamond at 10, and cleavage means a tendency to split along flat planes. Spinel, at 7.5 to 8 with no cleavage, suits any jewelry. GIA does not recommend peridot for a ring worn daily, and household dust is hard enough to scratch it. Sardonyx at 6.5 to 7 is best in earrings or a necklace unless the setting protects it.

| Piece | Peridot | Spinel | Sardonyx |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ring worn every day | Protective setting, or save it for special days | Good choice | Protective setting |
| Pendant or earrings | Good choice | Good choice | Good choice |

- **Someone who loves red.** A red spinel looks like ruby and sells for a fraction of a ruby's price.
- **A baby's first piece.** A small spinel pendant, durable enough to last into adulthood, kept in its box as a keepsake. Small stones are a choking hazard.
- **A budget gift.** Crystal sellers describe sardonyx as affordable, and attractive small peridots exist at every price point.
- **An inherited sardonyx cameo or signet.** Assume it may be dyed and tell the jeweler before any repair, since high heat might affect the color of dyed sardonyx.

Peridot's value rests mostly on color, with a pure grass green at the top. Spinel's most valued colors are bright red, cobalt blue and vivid pink or orange, while pale lavender is more affordable. Sardonyx is judged on clean, well-defined bands, and sellers say fine carving raises its price.

### What to Ask Before You Buy

1. **Ask for the mineral name in writing.** A bright, cheap "August birthstone" may well be synthetic spinel, which is inexpensive, made in almost any color and widely used in birthstone jewelry. Ask whether it is natural or lab-grown.
2. **For peridot, ask whether it could be glass.** Green glass is the most common peridot imitation.
3. **For sardonyx, ask whether the color is natural or dyed.**
4. **Get the answers on the receipt.** Under the Federal Trade Commission's Jewelry Guides, a seller should disclose a treatment that is not permanent, creates special care needs or significantly affects value. A lab-made or imitation stone may be called a birthstone only with a word such as "laboratory-created" or "imitation" just as visible in front of it.

## Is My August Birthstone Real?

We use the same five labels on every stone. August has a twist, since lab-grown spinel, one of its own birthstones, is a favorite stand-in for other gems in low-cost birthstone jewelry.

### Real or Fake Peridot

- **Natural, untreated.** Most peridot.
- **Natural, treated.** Some cut stones are backed with metal foil, so check old pieces with closed backs.
- **Lab-grown.** Synthetic peridot exists but rarely appears in jewelry.
- **Imitation.** Green glass most often, and synthetic corundum, synthetic spinel or cubic zirconia now and then.
- **Trade name.** Chrysolite, the old name, which gemologists no longer use. Peridot has more often been sold as emerald than the other way around.

Two checks with a 10x loupe.

1. **Look through the top for doubled facets.** Peridot splits light so strongly that you see two of each back facet edge, and the stone can look slightly fuzzy. No doubling from any angle is a reason to ask more questions.
2. **Look for lily pads.** Natural peridot often holds small disk-shaped inclusions called lily pads.

Neither check proves a stone natural. For lab-grown peridot, a gem lab is the answer.

### Real or Fake Spinel

- **Natural, untreated.** Almost all natural spinel.
- **Natural, treated.** Rare. Heating or fracture filling for clarity, and some diffusion-colored stones have been reported.
- **Lab-grown.** Made since the 19th century, cheap and common in birthstone jewelry and class rings. Most colorless spinel in jewelry is probably synthetic, and a lab-grown star spinel can look too perfect.
- **Trade name.** Balas ruby is the old name for red spinel, and ceylonite and pleonaste are very dark spinels.

Rows of tiny octahedral inclusions under magnification point to natural spinel. For anything costly, ask for a lab report.

### Real or Fake Sardonyx

Dye is the question that matters, since chalcedony is commonly dyed, so ask whether the color is natural. Sard, carnelian and sardonyx are all chalcedony, and only sardonyx shows layered bands of sard and onyx.

Our data has little on spinel yet, so here is GIA's advice. Its August page calls spinel stable to light and chemicals, says warm soapy water is always safe, and warns that ultrasonic and steam cleaners can be a problem for stones with fractures and that high heat can fade some colors. The cautious choice is soap, water and a soft brush.

### How to Clean a Peridot Ring at Home

You need two small bowls of warm water at the same temperature, one drop of mild dish soap, a soft toothbrush and a lint-free cloth.

1. **Add the soap to one bowl.** Keep the water warm, never hot.
2. **Dip and brush for a minute.** Brush gently around and under the stone, where lotion and sweat collect. Keep it brief.
3. **Rinse in the second bowl,** not under the tap, so the water stays the same temperature and the ring cannot slip down the drain.
4. **Pat dry** with the cloth and let it air dry before you put it away.
5. **Never use** a steam or ultrasonic cleaner, a jewelry dip or any acid. Peridot is vulnerable to thermal shock and can be damaged by acids, even by long contact with sweat.

In summer, wipe the ring with a cloth dampened in plain water after a hot day, then dry it.

### Spinel & Sardonyx in Two Minutes

Clean either one with warm water, mild soap and a soft brush, then dry it. Keep sardonyx away from bleach and harsh chemicals, and skip machines for both unless a jeweler has checked the stone for fractures.

### How to Store August Birthstones

1. **Give each piece its own pouch.** Spinel, at 7.5 to 8, will scratch peridot and sardonyx if they rub together.
2. **Keep peridot covered.** Household dust is hard enough to scratch it.
3. **Keep all three away from heat,** such as a sunny dresser or a car in summer.

**Where the beliefs and the care data disagree.** Several crystal guides cleanse spinel and peridot under running water or in sunlight, and one seller suggests soaking sardonyx in kosher salt. Our data allows peridot and sardonyx a rinse but only a brief soak, has no rating yet for spinel in water or for any of the three in sun or salt, and marks peridot Keep Away From Heat. So skip long soaks, the salt bowl and the sunny sill, and use moonlight indoors if you like the ritual.

## Small August Rituals

**Try This.** The old lore said peridot set in gold kept away the terrors of the night. When you take off a peridot at bedtime, put it in its dish and name one thing that went right today.

**Try This.** For an August birthday, give a red spinel with a card that tells the story of the Black Prince's Ruby, the famous "ruby" that was a spinel all along.

**What Research Says.** In three archival field studies, people started goal-directed behavior such as gym visits more often right after dates that felt like a fresh start, including birthdays ([Dai, Milkman and Riis, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901)). It shows a pattern, not a cause, and has nothing to do with stones, but a birthday morning looks like a good time to start one small habit.

**Safety.** Small stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Spinel is often chosen for anxiety, and old lore credited red spinel with stopping blood loss and sardonyx with easing childbirth. A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. Talk to a doctor, a midwife or a therapist about anything that worries you. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

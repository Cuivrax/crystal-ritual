# Crystal Gift Ideas That Are Easy to Get Right

The easiest crystal gifts to get right are sturdy, inexpensive stones with a clear story, such as rose quartz, amethyst, citrine or tiger's eye, or the person's own birthstone. Choose by the person and the occasion, then add a short card that says what the stone is and how to care for it. For homes with babies or pets, skip small stones.

## Crystal Gift Ideas at a Glance

**Modern Association.** These pairings come from modern crystal guides, with our labels for how often a stone is tied to a need. The last column is what we would want to know before buying.

| For | Stone | Why People Choose It | The Honest Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| A partner or an anniversary | [Rose quartz](/stones/rose-quartz/) | Classic for love, and GIA lists it for the 5th anniversary | Hardness 7, easy to clean |
| A new job or a big interview | [Tiger's eye](/stones/tigers-eye/) | Widely Linked to confidence and focus, Classic for golden tiger's eye | Hard enough for a pocket, care data still has gaps |
| A friend who sleeps badly | [Amethyst](/stones/amethyst/) | Classic for sleep | Some fade in long, intense light |
| A new home | Amethyst | Classic for protection | Display it away from a sunny window |
| A new business or savings goal | [Citrine](/stones/citrine/) | Classic for money | Most citrine is heated amethyst, and it is still quartz |
| A student | [Clear quartz](/stones/clear-quartz/) | Classic for focus | Lab-grown quartz is common and clean inside |
| Someone grieving | [Smoky quartz](/stones/smoky-quartz/) | Widely Linked to grief | Keep it out of sun and heat, which can fade it |
| A December birthday | Tanzanite, turquoise or zircon | The modern December birthstones | Each needs its own care, see below |
| The skeptic | An amethyst geode | It grew in a hole in an old lava flow | The real story is the gift |

Every stone here is a form of quartz except the December birthstones, which is why the list leans on it. Quartz rates 7 on the Mohs scale and is chemically inert with most substances ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/quartz.shtml)), so it survives being unwrapped, dropped in a bag and handled. If you are not sure about a stone, look it up in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/) before you wrap it.

## Can You Give Crystals as Gifts?

Yes, and one of the oldest beliefs about stones says a gift is the best way to own one.

**Tradition.** Kunz records a widespread superstition that a diamond lost its talismanic power if it was bought and kept it only if it was received as a gift, and he notes the same belief about turquoise. The spirit in the stone was thought to dislike being bought and sold, but was happy to move to a new owner when the stone was given "as a pledge of love or friendship" (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, page 73). He also retells a story from Anselmus de Boot, court physician to Emperor Rudolph II. De Boot's father bought a faded turquoise cheaply and gave it to his son, saying the stone's virtues were said to exist only when it had been given. Within a month, the story goes, the color came back (pages 109 to 110). Kunz, ever the gemologist, notes that if the story were true it would be an altogether unique case, and elsewhere he writes that a faded turquoise, like an egg, can never be restored (page 114).

**Modern Association.** The idea survives. Modern guides still describe a turquoise as especially meaningful, or even lucky, when it is given rather than bought ([crystalgemstones.net](https://crystalgemstones.net/turquoise-meaning-and-spiritual-properties.html) is one example), and one suggests treating a turquoise you buy for yourself as a gift to yourself ([Crystal Vaults](https://www.crystalvaults.com/crystal-encyclopedia/turquoise/)). Some people like to cleanse a stone they receive. Others keep it exactly as it arrived, because of who it came from.

## December Birthstones for Holiday Birthdays

The modern US list from the [American Gem Society](https://www.americangemsociety.org/birthstones/) gives December three stones, tanzanite, turquoise and zircon. That is handy for gift shopping, because the three look nothing alike and suit very different styles.

**Tradition.** December has not always meant turquoise. Kunz describes an older natal-stone order, probably from Poland, that already gave turquoise to December, while the lists of his own day had moved it to July and given December the ruby (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, pages 307 to 308). Writing in 1913 about a newly revised list that swapped them back, he grumbled about the reasoning. The pale turquoise was said to suit a winter month when the sun is weak, but "it is in winter that we seek for warmth" (page 320). More history on our [December birthstone page](/birthstones/december/).

### Tanzanite

**Mineral Fact.** Tanzanite is the blue-violet variety of the mineral zoisite, named by Tiffany & Co. after Tanzania, and it is mined commercially only in the Merelani Hills of northern Tanzania ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/tanzanite/gem-overview)). It rates 6 to 7, and nearly all of it is heat treated, a stable treatment ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/tanzanite-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). It became a modern December birthstone in 2002 ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/tanzanite/)), and GIA names it the gem for a 24th anniversary. **Gift Note.** The International Gem Society warns that cleavage and brittleness make daily wear risky, so earrings or a pendant make a safer gift than an everyday ring. Clean it with warm water, mild detergent and a soft brush. More on [tanzanite](/stones/tanzanite/).

### Turquoise

**Mineral Fact.** Turquoise is a hydrated copper aluminum phosphate ([IGS](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/turquoise-jewelry-gem-information/)) that rates 5 to 6 and has been treasured for thousands of years ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/turquoise.shtml)). GIA calls it the traditional December birthstone and the gem of the 11th anniversary, and notes that ancient Egyptians called it mefkat, meaning joy and delight ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/turquoise/gem-overview)). Most turquoise on the market has been treated or stabilized, and blue-dyed howlite is a common imitation that is sometimes sold without disclosure ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/turquoise-jewelry-gem-information/)). **Gift Note.** Turquoise is porous and can absorb liquids that damage or recolor it, so store it away from bright light and heat and clean it with a soft cloth and very mild soap ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/turquoise.shtml)). Ask the seller in writing whether it is natural, stabilized or reconstituted. More on [turquoise](/stones/turquoise/).

### Zircon

**Mineral Fact.** Zircon is a natural mineral that has been used as a gem for about 2,000 years, and it is not the same thing as cubic zirconia, which is made in a lab ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/zircon.shtml)). It rates 6 to 7.5, and almost all blue and colorless zircon is heat treated ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/zircon-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). **Tradition.** Kunz notes that one Sanskrit name for hyacinth, an old name for zircon, ties it to the dragon blamed for eclipses, so the stone was believed to avert misfortune (1913, pages 344 to 345). **Gift Note.** Ultraviolet light from tanning beds or nail lamps can turn heat-treated blue zircon brown ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology/fall-2016-reversible-color-modification-blue-zircon-long-wave-ultraviolet-radiation), [Geology.com](https://geology.com/minerals/zircon.shtml)). If the person you are buying for gets gel manicures, tell them to take the ring off first. Zircon is also brittle and chips at facet edges, so skip the ultrasonic cleaner ([IGS](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/zircon-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)). More on [blue zircon](/stones/blue-zircon/).

### Crystals for Christmas Stockings

A tumbled stone and a folded card fit in any stocking. Choose from the quartz family above, since a stocking is a rough ride, and leave small stones out of stockings for young children altogether.

## Birthstones by Month for Birthday Gifts

For crystals for a birthday, the birthstone is the safest bet, because it is personal without being a guess. This is the modern US list as published by the [American Gem Society](https://www.americangemsociety.org/birthstones/).

| Month | Birthstones |
|---|---|
| January | Garnet |
| February | Amethyst |
| March | Aquamarine, bloodstone |
| April | Diamond |
| May | Emerald |
| June | Pearl, alexandrite, moonstone |
| July | Ruby |
| August | Peridot, sardonyx, spinel |
| September | Sapphire |
| October | Opal, tourmaline |
| November | Topaz, citrine |
| December | Tanzanite, turquoise, zircon |

The list is younger than it looks. In 1912 the National Association of Jewelers met to standardize the American birthstones, and in 1952 the Jewelry Industry Council of America revised the list, adding alexandrite for June, pink tourmaline for October, citrine for November and zircon for December. Tanzanite joined December in 2002 and spinel joined August in 2016 ([International Gem Society](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/history-of-birthstones/)). For February and November, a tumbled amethyst or citrine is an easy, inexpensive way to give the birthstone without buying jewelry. Our [birthstones by month guide](/birthstones/) explains where each list comes from.

## The Care Card That Makes the Gift

**The Ritual.** Whatever you believe about the stone, here is what the card does for sure. It turns a pretty rock into a gift with a story, and it keeps the stone from being ruined in its first week.

**Try This.** On a small card, write four lines.

1. **What it is.** The stone's name and the mineral it belongs to.
2. **Where the meaning comes from.** One line, labeled as a belief or a tradition.
3. **How to care for it.** Water, sun and storage, from the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).
4. **Why you chose it for them.** This is the line they will remember.

Three filled-in examples.

> **Rose Quartz.** A pink form of quartz, hardness 7, tough enough for everyday handling ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/rose-quartz/)). Modern crystal guides connect it with love above all. Rinse it in warm soapy water when it gets dusty ([IGS](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/rose-quartz/)). I picked it because you are the kindest person I know.

> **Amethyst.** Purple quartz, colored by traces of iron and natural radiation ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/amethyst/)). Its ancient Greek name means "not drunk" ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/amethyst/gem-overview)). Keep it off sunny windowsills, since long intense light can fade it ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/amethyst-care-cleaning)). For your nightstand, and for the new bedtime routine.

> **Tiger's Eye.** Quartz grown around mineral fibers ([IGS](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/tigers-eye-gem-information/)), which is why a band of light slides across it when you turn it ([Ganoksin](https://ganoksin.com/article/a-second-look-at-tigers-eye)). Safe to carry and handle, says [Deposits Magazine](https://depositsmag.com/2023/04/29/the-how-and-why-of-tigers-eye/). Many people keep one in a pocket on days that call for nerve. For your first week at the new job.

**What Research Says.** In four experiments, people who wrote letters of gratitude underestimated how happy their recipients would feel and overestimated how awkward the letter would be (Kumar and Epley, 2018, *Psychological Science*, [doi.org/10.1177/0956797618772506](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618772506)). The study was about letters, not stones or gifts. It does suggest that the fourth line on the card matters more than you think.

### For the Skeptic

**Try This.** Give an amethyst geode with a card that tells its real story. Large amethyst deposits form in cavities of old basalt lava flows in Brazil and Uruguay ([Geology.com](https://geology.com/gemstones/amethyst/)), and in places like Brazil the crystal-lined geodes can be big enough to stand in ([GIA](https://www.gia.edu/amethyst/gem-overview)). No belief required.

## Gifts to Rethink

Most crystal gifts go wrong in a few predictable ways.

- **Small stones for babies, toddlers and pets.** Tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys and can be swallowed. For a new baby, give the parents a stone for a high shelf, or the baby's birthstone to keep for later.
- **Amber teething necklaces.** In December 2018 the US Food and Drug Administration warned against teething necklaces and bracelets after reports of serious injuries, including an 18-month-old strangled by an amber teething necklace during a nap and a 7-month-old who choked on a bead from a teething bracelet, according to [Mount Nittany Health's summary](https://kids.mountnittany.org/resources/details/safety-hazards-of-teething-jewelry). The FDA's own [teething guidance](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/safely-soothing-teething-pain-infants-and-children) also lists strangulation and choking from amber teething necklaces. Do not give one.
- **Crystal water bottles.** Skip them as a gift. If someone wants a stone near water, our [moon water page](/rituals/moon-water/) keeps it beside the jar, never in it. Never put malachite, azurite, pyrite or any dyed or stabilized stone in water someone will drink.
- **Fragile stones for rough lives.** A fluorite ring is not recommended, since fluorite is soft and cleaves easily ([IGS](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/fluorite-jewelry-gemstone-information/)). Selenite is a form of gypsum, which is water soluble, so a selenite lamp is a poor fit for a steamy bathroom ([IGS](https://www.gemsociety.org/article/gypsum-jewelry-and-gemstone-information/)).
- **A label that promises a cure.** If the tag says a stone heals a condition, find a different seller. No stone treats illness, and a gift should not imply it does.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** A stone can be a kind gift for someone who is grieving, ill, pregnant or caring for a new baby. It is comfort to hold, not care. If the person you are buying for is struggling, the most useful thing in the box may be your note saying you will check in, and a gentle push to talk to a doctor, midwife or therapist. If someone is in crisis in the US, they can call or text 988 or chat at 988lifeline.org to reach the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline ([988 Lifeline](https://988lifeline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/988Day_FactSheet_988-2026_EN.pdf)).

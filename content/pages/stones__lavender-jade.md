# Lavender Jade Meaning, Properties & Real-Life Uses

Lavender jade is jadeite, one of the two minerals sold as jade, in pinkish to bluish purple shades colored by manganese. After green, lavender is jadeite's most valued color. Crystal lovers link it with calm and compassion, which are modern beliefs, and the honest note is that pale or grayish jadeite is sometimes dyed to look lavender.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Look between the grains with a 10x loupe, since color pooled there points to dye. Jadeite feels heavy for its size. An even, bright lavender at a low price is a reason to ask for the A, B or C grade in writing, and only a gem lab can confirm it.

## What Lavender Jade Actually Is

**The Stone.** "Jade" covers two different materials, jadeite and nephrite, which a Frenchman, Alexis Damour, showed were distinct in 1863. Lavender is a jadeite color. Nephrite usually runs from white and cream to dark green. Our [jade page](/stones/jade/) covers the whole family, and [jadeite vs nephrite](/compare/jadeite-vs-nephrite/) explains the difference.

In the trade, lavender jadeite loosely covers a broad range, from pinkish and purplish to violetish and bluish, and jadeite that might be called pink is sold as lavender too. Like all jade, it is exceptionally tough and resists breaking and chipping.

### What Makes Jade Lavender?

Manganese. A 2012 study in *Gems & Gemology* by Ren Lu of GIA weighed the explanations proposed over the years, from iron and titanium pairs to several forms of manganese, and confirmed that manganese makes lavender and chromium makes green. It also has a practical line. When natural manganese is too low for a saturated lavender, a dye is introduced.

### Where Lavender Jade Comes From

The study's samples came from mines of well-known origin. Burmese material from Myanmar was pinkish to purple in a whitish matrix, while the Japanese and Guatemalan pieces were bluish, with virtually no lavender. Deep lavender to purple jade from Turkey has also been reported with high manganese.

### What Is Turkish Purple Jade?

A different rock. A distinctive purplish jadeite and quartz rock occurs only in Bursa, Turkey. In a 2013 lab note, GIA described a mottled purple bead of this kind of rock, its own origin unconfirmed, whose lavender and white parts were a mix of jadeite and quartz, with reddish orange cinnabar and fine needles of aegirine inside. It read lower than jadeite on a gemologist's refractometer and weighed less for its size. It is natural and contains jadeite, but it is not the same as lavender jadeite, and the label should say what it is.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** The old gem books say nothing about lavender jade itself, so its story is jade's.

- **Ancient China.** **Tradition.** Jade was "the musical gem par excellence." Chimes were made of 12 to 24 jade pieces, the "stone chime" of court and religious ceremonies had 16, and Confucius, troubled that his efforts to reform the morals of his day were failing, consoled himself by playing the "musical stone." A passing peasant said, "Full indeed is the heart of him who beats the musical stone like that!" (Kunz, *The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, page 87).
- **Early 1900s, the Muslim world.** **Tradition.** Jade talismans were so prized as heirlooms among the Turks that they were hard to buy, Kunz wrote, and members of an orthodox Muslim sect called the Pekdash carried a flat piece of jade all their lives "as a protection against injury or annoyance of every kind" (1913, page 246).
- **Spanish America.** **Tradition.** The word jade comes from the Spanish *piedra de hijada*, "stone of the flank," said to be given because the Indians used it for kidney diseases. Kunz wondered whether the Spaniards only suggested that virtue to raise the value of their jade (1913, pages 382 to 383).
- **2000s.** **Modern Association.** Compassion. One seller credits Robert Simmons and Naisha Ahsian's *The Book of Stones* (first published 2005) with tying lavender jade's soft color to Kwan Yin, the goddess of compassion.
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Calm, gentleness, compassion and dreams.

## What People Use Lavender Jade for Today

### What Does Lavender Jade Mean?

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal practice, lavender jade means calm, gentleness and quiet clarity. It is a Classic stone for [anxiety](/crystals-for/anxiety/) in modern crystal guides, Widely Linked with focus, Often Linked with [sleep](/crystals-for/sleep/) and love, Sometimes Linked with protection and confidence, and only Rarely Linked with money and grief. Guides call jade a "dream stone" and suggest lavender jade for meditation, retreats and the bedside.

The "why" has three parts. Pale violet reads as soft and spiritual, so most guides place it on the crown chakra, at the top of the head, and some on the heart. Jade carries centuries of Chinese reverence, and the compassion link comes from the Kwan Yin association above. Taurus and Libra, the signs named for it, are borrowed from jade in general, and some guides give it the earth element.

Some pages also claim it treats the kidneys, spleen, adrenal glands or heart rhythm. The kidney idea is the old flank stone story, and no reliable evidence supports any of these claims.

## How to Use Lavender Jade in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** The stone will not calm you down. It can sit in the moments where you decide to slow down yourself.

### A Flat Stone for the Waiting Room

**Try This.** Borrow the Pekdash habit Kunz describes and carry a flat lavender jade worry stone in your pocket. In a waiting room or before a hard phone call, rest your thumb on it, breathe in for a count of four and out for a count of six, three times. Then make the call.

### The Nightstand Notebook

**Try This.** Keep a small lavender jade on a notebook by the bed. Before lights out, pick up the stone, write three things that went right today, however small, and set the stone back on the closed notebook. In the morning, it is the first thing you see.

### A Gift That Tells the Truth

**Try This.** For a friend going through a tender season, wrap a lavender jade pendant with a card that says what it is (jadeite, colored by manganese), what people associate with it (calm and compassion) and its grade if the seller put it in writing, "A jade, untreated" or "dyed."

**Safety.** Small beads and tumbled stones are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets, and never put lavender jade in water you drink, since a piece may be dyed or filled with resin.

**What Research Says.** In three randomized studies, two with college students keeping weekly or daily records and one with 65 adults living with neuromuscular disease, people asked to list things they were grateful for reported better well-being than comparison groups, most clearly in positive mood (Emmons and McCullough, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2003, [doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.84.2.377](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.84.2.377)). The outcomes were self-reported, the gains did not show on every measure, and no stones were involved. The nightstand notebook is our extrapolation.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Lavender jade is often chosen for anxiety, stress and emotional hurt, and some sites suggest it for the kidneys or heart rhythm. A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. If anxiety, sadness or poor sleep lasts or feels heavy, talk to a doctor or a therapist, and see a doctor about any kidney or heart concern. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is Lavender Jade Dyed?

Sometimes, and you cannot always tell by eye. When natural manganese is too low for a saturated lavender, a dye is introduced, and grayish jadeite may be dyed to mauve. The trade grades jadeite with three letters.

- **Natural, untreated.** "A" jade is jadeite that is natural or polished with wax only, the traditional finish, which buyers accept.
- **Natural, treated.** "B" jade is bleached in acid and then filled with polymer resin, and it can start to deteriorate in a few years. "C" jade is dyed, usually on top of B treatment, and its color can fade over time or with heat and light.
- **Lab-grown.** General Electric researchers made synthetic jadeite, documented in 1984, but it is not produced commercially. A "synthetic jade" tag probably means a look-alike.
- **Imitation.** Glass and plastic both serve as jade simulants.
- **Trade names.** Lavender, lilac and mauve jade are color names for jadeite. "Purple jade" from Turkey is a jadeite and quartz rock.

### How to Check Lavender Jade at Home

You need a 10x loupe, a 365 nm UV flashlight (an inexpensive long-wave model) and a stone you know for comparison.

1. **Look at the grain edges.** Under the loupe, color pooled between the grains points to dye. Today's dyes are often far less obvious than older ones, especially in fine-textured stones, so a clean look proves nothing.
2. **Shine the UV flashlight.** In a dark room, older dyed jadeite often glowed orangy under long-wave UV. Natural Burmese lavender in the 2012 study was inert or showed a very weak reddish glow. But one dyed ring in the same study showed no reaction at all, so a glow is a clue and no glow is not a pass.
3. **Weigh it in your palm.** Jadeite is dense, 3.3 to 3.5 times the weight of the same volume of water, so it feels heavy for its size next to a quartz stone of the same size. Heft can rule a stone out, never in.
4. **Read the price.** B jade normally sells for a small fraction of a similar A piece, and C jade for a small fraction of B, so a bright lavender bangle at a bead-shop price calls for questions.
5. **Leave the rest to a lab.** B treatment often needs a gem laboratory to detect, and a lab report usually costs a tiny fraction of what an A jade costs.

Ask the seller four things. Is this jadeite, nephrite or another stone? Is it A, B or C jade? Is there a lab report? Where was it mined? The [Label Decoder](/tools/crystal-label-decoder/) translates jade names like these.

## How to Care for Lavender Jade

**Mineral Fact.** Our care data rates lavender jade Keep Out of Sun and Keep Away From Heat because treated jade suffers from both. The other fields read Not Enough Data, so GIA's published advice for jade in general fills the gap. Heat from a jeweler's torch can harm jade, warm acids can affect it, and jade itself is stable to light.

- **Water and soaking.** Not Enough Data. GIA calls warm, soapy water always a safe choice for jade. Wash it, do not leave it in a bowl.
- **Sun.** Keep Out of Sun. Polymer resin in B jade can discolor in sunlight, and dye in C jade can fade.
- **Heat.** Keep Away From Heat. The same resins and dyes suffer in heat. Tell a jeweler before any repair that uses a torch.
- **Chemicals.** Not Enough Data. Warm acids can affect jade, and acid-treated jadeite may develop cracks, so keep cleaning sprays off it.
- **Salt.** Not Enough Data. Skip it.
- **Knocks and storage.** Not Enough Data. Give it its own pouch, since some care against scratches is still needed.
- **Cleaning.** Not Enough Data. Ultrasonic and steam cleaners are safe only for untreated jade, so warm soapy water is the cautious choice.
- **Toxicity.** Not Enough Data.

### How to Clean a Lavender Jade Bangle at Home

1. Fill a small bowl with warm water and one drop of mild dish soap.
2. Dip the bangle briefly and wipe it with a soft cloth or a soft toothbrush.
3. Rinse in plain water of the same temperature.
4. Pat dry with a soft, lint-free cloth.
5. Skip the ultrasonic and the steamer unless a lab report calls it A jade, and never use vinegar or acidic cleaners.

### Does Lavender Jade Fade in the Sun?

Natural lavender jadeite should not. GIA calls jade stable to light, and the color comes from manganese built into the stone. Dyed lavender can fade over time or with heat and light, and resin in B jade can discolor, and you rarely know which you own without a lab report. So skip the sunny windowsill, take a bangle off before a day at the beach, and keep it out of a hot car. Check your piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Price Guide

Color is jadeite's most important value factor, then transparency, then texture. Lavender is the next most valuable color after green, and intense colors command a substantial premium over lighter and weaker ones. The finest jadeite pieces can surpass top ruby and sapphire at auction.

- **Grade.** A jade is the price benchmark. B and C sell for small fractions of it, and C should be bought on the assumption that it will look its best for only a few years.
- **Transparency.** The best jadeite is semitransparent, so text read through it looks slightly blurred.

# Green Opal Meaning, Properties & Real-Life Uses

Green opal is a color name, not one material. Most often it means green common opal, the kind without rainbow flashes, most likely tinted by nickel or chromium, and the best-known kind is prase opal from Tanzania, a near twin of chrysoprase. Crystal lovers tie it to the heart and to fresh starts, which are modern beliefs, and like all opal it holds water and dislikes heat.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** Green common opal shows no rainbow flashes. Under a 10x loupe, round bubbles and swirl marks point to glass, and "opalite" on a tag usually means glass or plastic. Chrysoprase looks almost the same, and only a gemologist can tell the two apart for sure.

## What Green Opal Actually Is

**The Stone.** Opal is silica with up to 20% water trapped in its structure, built from microscopic silica spheres. Uniform, neatly stacked spheres split light into flashes of color and make precious opal. Random ones make common opal. In green common opal, the color sits in the body of the stone instead of dancing across it.

With no crystal structure, opal is a mineraloid rather than a true mineral, and its water makes it softer, lighter and more brittle than quartz. Our [opal page](/stones/opal/) covers the whole family.

### Prase Opal, the Green Opal Gemologists Mean

Prase opals are green to yellowish green common opals, translucent to opaque, that resemble chrysoprase. A golden green variety is called chrysopal.

A mine on Iyobo Mountain near Haneti, central Tanzania, yields chrysoprase and prase opal side by side, though chrysoprase makes up the vast majority of the output. Better pieces become cabochons and some lower-grade rough becomes beads, and both stones have served as jade substitutes, according to a 2009 *Gems & Gemology* study by James Shigley and colleagues. The same journal had described a bright green, nickel-bearing Tanzanian opal in 1984.

### What Makes Green Opal Green?

In Tanzanian prase opal, most likely nickel. The 2009 study presumes that, as in chrysoprase, dispersed particles of nickel-containing minerals color it.

In 2020, GIA's lab examined a dark green common opal colored by chromium, possibly from minute inclusions of chromium-colored serpentine, and found inclusions of chromite inside.

### Green Flashes Mean Precious Opal

Precious opal can flash green among other colors, and lechosos opal is a trade term for opal with green fire. That is a different purchase, priced as precious opal, so if a "green opal" label sits on a stone that flashes, ask which one you are buying.

### Where Green Opal Comes From

Opal forms when silica-rich water seeps into cracks in rock and leaves its silica as tiny spheres. Besides Tanzania, Kenya has produced fine green common opal.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** The old gem books say nothing about green common opal itself, so its story is opal's.

- **77 CE, Rome.** **Tradition.** Pliny the Elder wrote that opal shows at once "the piercing fire of carbunculus, the purple brilliancy of amethystos, and the sea-green of smaragdus," that is, emerald (*Natural History*, Book 37, chapter 21, Bostock and Riley translation). His green was a flash of precious opal. Because opal carries the colors of other gems, the Romans thought it the most precious of all.
- **Bedouin lore.** **Tradition.** Opals were believed to hold lightning and to fall from the sky during thunderstorms.
- **Middle Ages.** **Tradition.** Opal was called the eyestone, thought to cure eye diseases and to make its wearer invisible, hence "patron of thieves" (Kunz, *The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, pages 146 and 148). In the East it was worn in amulets against disease (a note on page 143).
- **1829.** **Tradition.** Walter Scott's novel *Anne of Geierstein* gave opal its unlucky reputation. Kunz blamed a careless reading, and cutters who sometimes broke the brittle stone and came to see it as unlucky (1913, pages 143 to 144 and 152).

One seller calls blue-green "Incan opals" the national gemstone of Peru, and a French shop retells a legend of opals born where a rainbow touched the ground. Neither comes with a source, so treat both as shop folklore.

## What People Use Green Opal for Today

### What Does Green Opal Mean?

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal practice, green opal means renewal, emotional rest and starting again after a hard season, and guides tie it to the heart chakra above all. On our five-facet scale it is Widely Linked for [anxiety](/crystals-for/anxiety/), Often Linked for sleep, and Sometimes Linked for love, protection, money, confidence and grief.

The "why" is color and water. Green reads as new leaves, and because opal holds water, some practitioners give it the water element. Guides suggest it at thresholds, a move, a new job, the end of a relationship. Libra is the sign named most often, though lists vary.

Some sites list blood pressure, kidneys, insulin, the immune system and skin. Gem references and medicine give no support to those claims.

## How to Use Green Opal in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** The stone will not restart anything. It can mark the day you do.

### A Fresh-Start Marker

**Try This.** On the first morning of something new, a job, a lease, the week after a breakup, write one small daily action on an index card and set the green opal on it on your desk, out of direct sun. Each day you do the action, slide the stone along the card. On Sunday, write next week's.

### The Nightstand Stone

**Try This.** Keep the stone and a notebook on your nightstand, away from the lamp, the radiator and the afternoon sun. Before lights out, write one line about what you are letting rest until morning, then set the stone on the closed notebook.

### A Gift for Someone Starting Over

**Try This.** For a friend moving cities, wrap a green opal with a card saying what it is (a common opal colored by nickel minerals, if the seller confirms prase opal), what people associate with it (renewal) and one care line, "no sun, no soaking."

**Safety.** Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

**What Research Says.** In three archival field studies tracking Google searches for "diet," gym visits and goal commitments, Hengchen Dai, Katherine Milkman and Jason Riis found that people started goal-directed behavior more often right after dates that feel like a new beginning, such as a new week, month, year or birthday ([*Management Science*, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901)). The studies were observational, so they show a pattern, not a cause, and none involved stones.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Green opal is often chosen for anxiety, poor sleep, grief and recovery after illness. A stone can be a comfort to hold, but it is not care. If worry, sadness or poor sleep lasts or feels heavy, talk to a doctor or a therapist. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My Green Opal Real?

- **Natural, untreated.** Haneti prase opal reaches the market as cabochons and beads, and the 2009 study of that material describes no treatment.
- **Natural, treated.** Opal can be impregnated with oil, wax or plastic, or sugar and smoke treated, all of which should be disclosed. Hydrophane opal, a kind that turns transparent when wet, absorbs water and chemicals, and GIA has reported Ethiopian hydrophane opal dyed vivid blue and pink with an organic dye.
- **Lab-grown.** Lab-created opals are real opal. They tend to show strong play-of-color in a mosaic pattern, unlike a plain green body.
- **Imitation.** Usually glass or plastic. Dyed green agate and green glass have also imitated chrysoprase, prase opal's twin.
- **Trade names.** Prase opal and chrysopal are honest names. "Opalite" is the trap, since it usually means a glass or plastic simulant, even though a natural green, lavender or purple opal sometimes goes by it. See [opal vs opalite](/compare/opal-vs-opalite/).

### Three Checks at Home, With Their Limits

1. **Tilt it under a lamp.** Prase opal shows no flashes. Strong mosaic patches of rainbow color point to precious or lab-grown opal. A plain green body does not prove opal, though.
2. **Look with a 10x loupe.** An inexpensive jeweler's magnifier is enough. Round bubbles and swirl marks point to glass, and color concentrated in cracks points to a treatment. Both take practice to spot.
3. **Skip the needle and the water.** A needle scars what you probe, and weighing a stone in water adds moisture opal dislikes. Leave density and refractive index to a gemologist.

### Three Questions for the Seller

1. Is this opal, or chalcedony such as chrysoprase?
2. Where was it mined?
3. Has it been dyed, oiled, waxed or impregnated with plastic? Ask for the answer in writing on the receipt.

### Green Opal or Chrysoprase?

Prase opal is hydrated silica, while [chrysoprase](/stones/chrysoprase/) is chalcedony, a quartz made of crystals far too small to see. A gemologist separates them by refractive index and density (see the table below), and by eye it is guesswork. It matters at home, because opal is softer and fussier about water.

## How to Care for Green Opal

**Mineral Fact.** Opal is softer and more easily chipped than most popular gems, and it suits earrings, pendants and brooches better than rings. The ratings below are for opal in general.

- **Water and soaking.** Brief Only. Opal is sensitive to moisture.
- **Sun.** Keep Out of Sun. Heat from intense light can cause fine fracture lines called crazing.
- **Heat.** Keep Away From Heat, including sudden changes in temperature.
- **Chemicals.** No Chemicals. Hydrofluoric acid and caustic alkaline solutions attack opal.
- **Salt.** Not Enough Data. Leave salt bowls out.
- **Knocks.** Handle Gently. Opal scratches easily.
- **Cleaning.** OK with warm, soapy water and a soft brush.
- **Toxicity.** OK to handle. Opal is a low risk, and its silicosis hazard concerns cutting dust.

### How to Clean Green Opal at Home

1. Fill a small bowl with lukewarm, not hot, water and one drop of mild dish soap.
2. Dip a soft toothbrush in it and brush the stone gently, instead of dropping the stone in.
3. Rinse quickly under water of the same temperature, with the drain closed.
4. Pat dry with a soft, lint-free cloth and let it air dry away from the radiator.
5. Never use an ultrasonic or steam cleaner. GIA calls warm, soapy water the only safe way to clean opal.

Do it when skin oil dulls the surface.

### How to Store & Wear It

1. Give it a soft pouch of its own, since quartz and many other stones in a crystal bowl can scratch it.
2. Keep it out of sunny windows, hot cars and the top of the radiator.
3. Take rings and bracelets off for dishes, gardening, the gym and household cleaners.
4. For daily wear, choose a pendant or earrings, or ask for a bezel that wraps the edge of a ring stone.

### Can Green Opal Go in Water, Sun or Salt?

A quick wash, yes. A soak, a salt bowl or a sunny windowsill, no. Moonlight, sound and smoke, the methods shop guides suggest, all keep it dry. Kunz noted in 1913 that opals slowly lose their water and that some transparent Mexican opals lost their color within a few years (page 146). Check your exact piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Green Opal vs Look-Alikes

| | Green opal (prase opal) | Chrysoprase |
|---|---|---|
| Material | Common opal, hydrated silica | Chalcedony, microcrystalline quartz |
| Mohs hardness | 5 to 6.5 | 6.5 to 7 |
| Specific gravity | 2.09 to 2.13 | 2.57 to 2.63 |
| Refractive index | About 1.455 | About 1.549 |
| Look | Slightly more translucent | Slightly less translucent |
| Sun | Keep Out of Sun | Keep Out of Sun, some stones fade |

- **Green aventurine.** A quartz rock that glitters with flakes of a green mica called fuchsite. See [green aventurine](/stones/green-aventurine/).
- **Jade.** Both prase opal and chrysoprase have stood in for jade, a different material. See [green jade](/stones/green-jade/).

## Price Guide

This guide gives no dollar figures yet. Common opal is frequently cut as a gem and can command reasonable prices, while the best precious opals rival expensive diamonds per carat.

- **Opal or chalcedony.** Chrysoprase ranks as the second most valuable chalcedony, so a stone should be priced as what it is.
- **Form and grade.** At Haneti the better material becomes cabochons and some lower-grade rough becomes beads, so compare like with like.
- **Treatment and name.** Ask about treatment before you pay, and never pay opal prices for "opalite," which usually means glass or plastic.

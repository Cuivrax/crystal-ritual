# Pink Topaz Meaning, Properties & What It Really Is

Pink topaz is topaz, a hard aluminum silicate mineral, in a pink color that comes from traces of chromium when it is natural. Natural pink is extremely rare, so most pink topaz in stores was heated, irradiated or coated with a thin colored film. Crystal lovers tie it to love and gentle self-esteem, which are modern beliefs.

> **Is It Real? Quick Checks.** A large, vivid, inexpensive pink topaz is almost always treated, often coated. With a 10x loupe, look at the facet edges and corners for spots where the color has worn thin. Ask the seller in writing whether the color is natural, heated, irradiated or coated, because heating cannot be detected even by a lab.

## What Pink Topaz Actually Is

**The Stone.** Topaz is a silicate of aluminum and fluorine, and at 8 on the Mohs scale of scratch resistance it is the hardest silicate mineral. Most natural topaz is colorless. Our [topaz page](/stones/topaz/) covers the whole family.

Hard is not the same as tough. Topaz has a perfect basal cleavage, a flat plane along which it splits cleanly, so it chips and cleaves easily, and a hard blow might split it.

### What Makes Pink Topaz Pink?

In natural stones the cause is chromium, the element behind natural pink, red and violet to purple topaz. The reds and pinks are the most highly regarded topaz colors, and a vivid purplish pink is the rarest of all.

### Where Pink Topaz Is Found

A locality does not prove natural color.

- **Pakistan.** Fine, cuttable pink crystals grow in limestone at Ghundao Hill, near Katlang in Mardan.
- **Russia.** The Sanarka area produces pink topaz, and the Ural Mountains have yielded magenta material.
- **Brazil.** A reddish purple topaz from the Capão mine shows the gem's top color.
- **Myanmar.** Pink is one of the colors found in its gem gravels.

Pink gems over 5 carats from Pakistan are rare, and the largest known pink topaz is an oval of a little over 79 carats from Russia. A 58.8-carat Russian pink oval is flawless, and the Smithsonian holds a 34-carat deep pink gem from Brazil.

## What Was Done to Your Pink Topaz?

This sets both the price and the care. Pink topaz reaches stores four ways.

| Route | Starting stone | Where the color sits | Stable? | Can a lab tell? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Natural | Chromium-bearing topaz | Throughout | Yes | Not from a heated stone |
| Heated | Some yellow, orange or brown topaz | Throughout | Yes | No |
| Irradiated | Colorless topaz | Throughout, pale pink only | Not documented | Not by routine tests |
| Coated | Colorless topaz | A surface film | Wears where it rubs | Yes, with gem testing |

### Heated Pink Topaz

Some yellow, orange and brown topaz turns pink or red when heated, a step sometimes called burning. Some orange crystals from Ouro Preto, Brazil, contain chromium and turn pink in the heat, and some look reddish even before. The treatment is common, stable and undetectable, so a heated pink keeps its color and no one can prove it was heated.

### Irradiated Pink Topaz

Irradiation alone can turn colorless topaz a pale pink. A 1985 article in GIA's journal *Gems & Gemology* reported that no routine gemological test could say for sure whether a topaz color was natural or treated.

### Coated Pink Topaz

Vivid pink is also made from colorless topaz, which is first heated, then coated with a film of metallic oxide. Most topaz sold in department and mall stores at low to moderate prices has been treated in a laboratory.

In a pink coated topaz studied by Harald Gabasch and colleagues in *Gems & Gemology* in 2008, the color came from gold embedded in silica, in a distinct layer with a sharp boundary, about 400 nanometers thick in all (a nanometer is a billionth of a meter). Irradiation changes the whole stone, while coatings work only near the surface. That is why a coating can wear thin or through at points where the stone rubs.

### How to Tell Which One You Have

You need a 10x loupe, an inexpensive jeweler's magnifier, and a lamp.

1. **Start with price and size.** A big, bright, clean pink at a modest price is almost certainly treated, and coating is the way vivid pink is made from cheap colorless topaz.
2. **Check the edges with the loupe.** Look at the facet edges and the girdle, the thin outer rim. Pale or colorless spots where the pink has rubbed off mean a coating. A new stone may show no wear yet.
3. **Look for fine scratches.** Topaz itself rates 8, but topaz coatings are not very durable. Scuffs or a dull surface on a fairly new stone point to a film.
4. **For an expensive stone, get a report.** Heated and natural pink look the same, and no home test separates them. A GIA Colored Stone Identification Report says whether a stone is natural or lab-grown and names any detectable treatment.

### What to Ask Before You Buy

1. **Is the color natural, heated, irradiated or coated?** Under the Federal Trade Commission's Jewelry Guides, a seller should disclose a treatment that is not permanent, creates special care requirements or significantly affects value. Coated topaz has such a care need, since it should be cleaned only with mild soapy water. Online, the disclosure belongs in the product description.
2. **Is it topaz, or another pink stone?** The same Guides call it deceptive to sell a lab-made or imitation stone as "topaz" without a clear qualifier, or to call a manufactured product "natural."
3. **Will you write it on the receipt?** "Pink topaz, coated" or "pink topaz, natural color" on paper settles it later.

The [Label Decoder](/tools/crystal-label-decoder/) explains words you may see on the tag, such as natural, treated and imperial topaz.

## Where the Meaning Comes From

**The Story.** Pink topaz has no lore of its own, and older topaz lore comes with a catch, because "topaz" did not always mean topaz.

- **77 CE, Rome.** **Tradition.** Pliny the Elder wrote that topazos "is a stone that is still held in very high estimation for its green tints" (*Natural History*, Book 37, chapter 32, Bostock and Riley translation). His translators read it as peridot, and George Frederick Kunz agreed that the topaz of the ancients was the gem then called chrysolite (*The Curious Lore of Precious Stones*, 1913, a note on page 66).
- **Probably 10th century.** **Tradition.** Andreas, bishop of Caesarea, described "the topaz, which is of a ruddy color, resembling somewhat the carbuncle," as the stone of the apostle Matthew (Kunz 1913, pages 311 to 312). Which gem he meant is not known.
- **India.** **Tradition.** In the Hindu nine-gem ornament, topaz stood for Jupiter (Kunz 1913, pages 242 to 243). Kunz notes that the Sanskrit name for this "yellow stone" indicated a corundum, the gem we now call yellow sapphire (page 344).
- **13th century, India.** **Tradition.** The physician Naharari wrote that anyone who wears topaz "will be assured of long life, beauty and intelligence" (Kunz, *The Magic of Jewels and Charms*, 1915, page 158).
- **Today.** **Modern Association.** Love, compassion and self-worth, which sellers tie to its soft pink color.

## What People Use Pink Topaz for Today

### What Does Pink Topaz Mean?

**Modern Association.** In modern crystal guides, pink topaz means love that includes yourself. It is a Classic stone for [love](/crystals-for/love/), Often Linked with confidence, Sometimes Linked with anxiety, and Rarely Linked with protection, money, focus or grief. Sellers add compassion, hope and honesty, and one calls its meaning "giving one's self permission to be happy."

The reason sellers give is the color, its "soft pink energy," so guides place it on the heart chakra and suggest wearing it as a pendant close to the heart. One seller names Libra and Scorpio as its signs and ties topaz to Jupiter, as the Hindu nine-gem ornament did. Topaz itself is the [November birthstone](/birthstones/november/) on the American Gem Society's list, and sellers market pink topaz as a November gift.

Some pages say it eases stress or heals the emotions, claims that gem references and medicine do not support.

## How to Use Pink Topaz in Everyday Life

**The Ritual.** As a jewelry stone, its rituals live on your hand.

### A Gift With a Real Note

**Try This.** For an anniversary or a November birthday, give pink topaz with a handwritten card. Write one specific thing you are grateful for in that person, then one honest line about the stone, "natural pink" or "topaz, coated pink," as the receipt says.

### The Ring Dish Habit

**Try This.** One hard knock can split topaz, and a coating wears where it rubs. Put a small dish by the sink, and the ring goes in before dishes, the gym, gardening and hand cream.

**Safety.** Loose stones and small jewelry are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

**What Research Says.** In three experiments, Amit Kumar and Nicholas Epley found that people who wrote gratitude letters underestimated how good the recipients would feel and overestimated how awkward it would seem ([*Psychological Science*, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618772506)). The letters were written because the study asked for them, and none involved stones. The card above is our extrapolation.

> **When a Stone Is Not Enough.** Pink topaz is sometimes chosen for heartbreak, grief and anxiety. A stone can be a comfort to wear, but it is not care. If sadness or worry lasts or feels heavy, talk to a doctor or a therapist. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text 988, or chat at [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org/), any time, day or night, in the United States. In an emergency, call 911.

## Is My Pink Topaz Real?

- **Natural, untreated.** Extremely rare, and priced that way.
- **Natural, treated.** The usual case. Heated, irradiated or coated, as explained above. All are real topaz.
- **Lab-grown.** Topaz has been made in labs but is not usually sold.
- **Imitation.** Glass is an age-old imitation, cubic zirconia comes in almost any color, pink included, and synthetic spinel is a common choice for imitation birthstone jewelry.
- **Trade names.** "Imperial topaz" has no official standard. Some dealers use it for orange to pink to red to purple, others only for certain saturated shades, and it is the color, not the word, that gives topaz its value. "Spanish topaz" is a misleading name for reddish heated quartz.

## How to Care for Pink Topaz

**Mineral Fact.** Topaz resists scratches but splits easily. Jewelers prefer protective mountings for valuable topaz, or use it in pendants and pins that see less wear.

- **Water.** OK for washing with warm water, mild detergent and a soft brush. Keep soaks brief.
- **Cleaning.** Brief Only. No ultrasonic or steam cleaners, since vibration and heat can split topaz.
- **Heat.** Keep Away From Heat. High heat or sudden temperature changes can break it, so tell your jeweler it is topaz before any repair.
- **Sun.** Brief Only. Long exposure to heat or sunlight can fade yellow to brown and reddish brown topaz.
- **Chemicals.** Not Enough Data. GIA says topaz is affected only very slightly by chemicals.
- **Salt.** Not Enough Data. Skip salt bowls.
- **Knocks and storage.** Brief Only. Prongs can stress the cleavage plane, so choose a protective setting for daily wear.
- **Toxicity.** OK to wear and handle. Topaz has no known toxicity.
- **Coated stones.** Mild soapy water only. Abrasive cleaners and buffing wheels remove the coating.

### How to Clean a Pink Topaz Ring at Home

1. Fill a small bowl with warm water and one drop of mild dish soap.
2. Dip the ring briefly and brush around and under the stone with a soft toothbrush, where lotion collects.
3. Rinse under running water of the same temperature, with the drain closed.
4. Pat dry with a soft, lint-free cloth.
5. Never steam it, boil it or put it in an ultrasonic cleaner.

### Can Pink Topaz Go in the Sun?

Briefly. Topaz color is generally stable to light, and heated pink is a stable treatment, but our rating stays Brief Only, so keep it off a sunny windowsill. Check your exact piece in the [Care Checker](/tools/crystal-care-checker/).

## Pink Topaz vs Look-Alikes

These pink gems look alike but differ in density and refractive index, so a gemologist can usually tell them apart with standard tests.

| | Mohs hardness | Specific gravity |
|---|---|---|
| Pink topaz | 8 | 3.4 to 3.6 (topaz in general) |
| Pink sapphire | 9 | 4.00 (sapphire in general) |
| Pink spinel | 8 | 3.60 (spinel in general) |
| Morganite | 7.5 to 8 | 2.80 to 2.91 |
| Pink tourmaline | 7 to 7.5 | 2.8 to 3.3 (tourmaline in general) |
| Kunzite | 6.5 to 7 | 3.18 |
| Rose quartz | 7 | 2.66 |

- **Pink tourmaline.** A different mineral, slightly softer. See [pink tourmaline](/stones/pink-tourmaline/).
- **Morganite.** A pink beryl colored by manganese, often light in tone. See [morganite](/stones/morganite/).
- **Pink sapphire.** Corundum, harder and denser. See [pink sapphire](/stones/pink-sapphire/).
- **Mystic topaz.** Colorless topaz with a multicolored coating. See [mystic topaz](/stones/mystic-topaz/).

## Price Guide

### How Much Is Pink Topaz Worth?

It depends mostly on what was done to it, so this guide gives no single figure. Color is the most important quality factor for topaz, and it matters more than country of origin. Four things move the price.

- **Treatment.** Coated pink starts from colorless topaz, which has a much lower value. Natural pink is extremely rare.
- **Size.** Pink gems from Pakistan over 5 carats are rare, and for imperial topaz the price per carat rises dramatically with size.
- **Clarity.** Rare colors such as pink may show inclusions and still be valuable.
- **Cut.** It is one of the most important factors in how the stone looks.

If a seller prices pink topaz as natural, ask for a lab report first.

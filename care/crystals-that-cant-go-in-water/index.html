Which Crystals Can Go in Water? Full Safety Table

By Iris Holloway

Hard crystals such as amethyst, citrine, rose quartz and clear quartz can go in water for a quick rinse, but should not be left to soak. Fluorite, opal, halite, angelite and marcasite should stay out of water, while selenite, celestite, turquoise and lapis lazuli can take only brief contact. No crystal belongs in water you drink.

Every rating on this page comes from the same care data as our Care Checker, and each one rests on a quote we checked against its source page. OK means the cited source says the contact is fine. Brief Only means keep it quick. Keep Dry and No Soaking mean the cited source warns against it. Not Enough Data means we have no sourced answer yet, and we would rather tell you that than guess from a stone's hardness.

Why Water Hurts Some Stones

Water does damage in five different ways, and knowing which one applies tells you how careful to be.

It dissolves them. Halite is rock salt and dissolves in water, faster when the water is warm. Gypsum, the mineral behind selenite, is water soluble, and fluorite is slightly soluble. Water soluble gems need extra care.

They soak it up. Turquoise is porous and absorbs liquids that can damage it or change its color. Opal already holds water in its structure, 3% to 21% of it, and hydrophane opal takes in more. In Ethiopian hydrophane opal that can end in a crack.

They react with it. Marcasite reacts with moisture and releases sulfuric acid, and realgar can give off toxic fumes. Magnetite can rust. Pyrite has no care rating in our data yet, but it oxidizes when exposed to air and moisture and produces acids, which is why rock used for concrete must be free of it, and it tarnishes easily.

The surface is not the stone. Dyes, wax and metal films sit in or on the surface. Lapis lazuli is often dyed and waxed, gypsum takes dyes and coatings very easily, and some dyed agate bleeds in water. Not sure if yours is dyed? The Label Decoder lists the questions to ask the seller.

Glue and thin layers. An opal doublet is a thin layer of precious opal glued to a black base, which is why a fire opal dealer says never to wet doublets or triplets. Ammolite's thin layer can crack with frost and weather.

Modern Association

Why People Put Healing Crystals in Water

Holding a stone under running water is one of the most common ways modern crystal guides suggest to cleanse it, next to moonlight, smoke and sound. Some guides go further. One crystal blog tells readers to drop citrine into a water bottle for energy, and a German stone encyclopedia marks citrine as suitable for direct contact with water. Those are beliefs about energy. They say nothing about what water does to the stone, and on that question the care data wins every time.

Tradition Swallowing stones is far older than water bottles. George Frederick Kunz records the old belief that powdered malachite, dissolved in milk and drunk as a potion, would cure colic, and he adds that "if not very carefully used, the effect would have been anything but favorable." The physician De Boot was told that six grains of it worked as a purgative and, Kunz notes, never ventured to test it (The Magic of Jewels and Charms, 1915, pages 150 to 151). Malachite is a copper mineral, and it heads our list of stones to keep out of drinking water.

Can You Put Crystals in a Water Bottle?

No stone should sit in water you drink, and that includes a water bottle. Our care data rates how a stone handles water, not whether the water that touched it is safe to swallow, and it clears no stone for that. Crystal healers should avoid making potions with toxic gems and avoid putting such gems in anyone's mouth.

Try This Keep the stone beside your bottle instead, on the desk next to your keyboard or in the bag pocket next to the bottle. Every refill becomes the reminder, and nothing touches what you drink.

Some bottles hold a stone in a sealed compartment so it never meets the water. That is the indirect method, and we will only name stones for it once a reference source clears them for a long soak and our data clears them for toxicity. So far the Care Checker clears a long soak for three agates only through one dealer's table for agate in general, which is not enough for anything you drink, so we name no stone for a sealed bottle yet. Keeping the stone beside the bottle, as in the Try This above, works with any stone.

How Long Should You Cleanse Crystals in Water?

As long as a rinse, and no longer. The cited sources describe washing a stone, not leaving it to soak, so the Care Checker shows Brief Only for a long soak on every stone except those three agates. Here is the rinse the cited sources support for stones rated OK.

  1. Check the stone first

    Put it in the Care Checker. If it says Keep Dry or Not Enough Data, skip water and pick a dry method.

  2. Use lukewarm water, not hot

    Abrupt temperature changes can fracture citrine.

  3. Add a drop of mild soap and use a soft brush

    That is the recommended method for amethyst, citrine and rose quartz.

  4. Rinse, then pat it dry with a soft cloth

    Do it straight away, before you set the stone back on the shelf.

  5. For Brief Only stones, use a damp cloth instead of the tap

    Wipe and dry at once, the usual advice for turquoise.

Prefer to keep a stone dry? Moonlight, sound, smoke, a selenite plate and brown rice are the dry methods the Care Checker lists, and it records no physical contact risk for any of them. Planning a jar under the full moon? Read how to make moon water without ruining your stones, where the stone sits beside the jar.

Stones with No Water Rating Yet

Black tourmaline, labradorite, tiger's eye, carnelian, smoky quartz, howlite, lepidolite, malachite and pyrite have no sourced water rating yet, so the Care Checker shows Not Enough Data for them. That does not mean water will ruin them. It means no reference we trust has said, and until one does, a dry method is the gentle choice.

The same goes for any stone you cannot name. If a gift or a market find has no label, treat it as Not Enough Data, keep it dry and keep it out of anything you drink. For the full method by method picture, including sun and salt, see how to cleanse crystals without damaging them.

Zodiac Crystals for Every Sign

Zodiac birthstones are stones tied to the twelve astrological signs instead of the calendar months. A verse list printed in 1913 gives each sign one gem, from bloodstone for Aries to amethyst for Pisces, while today's crystal guides favor other stones, such as jet for Capricorn and angelite for Aquarius. Both are astrology traditions, not mineral facts, and the lists rarely agree.

Zodiac Birthstones vs Month Birthstones

A birthstone belongs to a month. A zodiac stone belongs to a sign. Every sign straddles two months, so each sign inherits two sets of month birthstones.

Kunz saw the overlap as a feature, since zodiac stones widen the choice while the month list keeps its old order (1913, pages 321 to 322). The month list was standardized in August 1912 by the National Association of Jewelers, meeting in Kansas City, and Kunz disliked several of its changes (pages 317 to 320). The list has been updated since, and our birthstones by month page covers that side.

So "the Capricorn birthstone" has a two-part answer. The month stone depends on the birthday, and the sign stone depends on the tradition you follow.

Tradition

Where Zodiac Stones Come From

The Story. The idea is old, but the lists keep changing.

  1. Ancient Mesopotamia Tradition

    The zodiac constellations appeared in ancient Mesopotamia, and the ancient Greeks adopted many aspects of Mesopotamian astrology, constellations included. In astrology, the sign of Aries begins with the Sun's position at the March equinox.

  2. Roman Empire Tradition

    Some engraved gems carried the zodiac sign of the wearer's birth constellation, usually cut on onyx, carnelian and similar stones, according to Kunz (1913, pages 124 to 125).

  3. 1st century CE Tradition

    The historian Josephus tied the twelve stones of the Hebrew high priest's breastplate to the months or to "the twelve signs of what the Greeks call the zodiac." St. Jerome adapted the passage about three centuries later, and Kunz calls it the foundation of both birthstones and zodiac stones (1913, pages 309 to 310).

  4. Medieval Jewish tradition Tradition

    Medieval Jewish tradition paired the twelve tribes of Israel with the twelve signs, Judah with Aries through Asher with Pisces (Kunz 1913, pages 313 to 314).

  5. 1762 Tradition

    The writer Poujet fils described rings set with the birth-month stone and engraved with the zodiac sign, a fashion he dated two centuries back (Kunz 1913, pages 316 to 317).

  6. 1913 Tradition

    Kunz printed the verse list in our chart, plus an old Spanish list that he thought probably reflected Arab tradition, with chalcedony for Capricorn and amethyst for Aquarius (1913, page 347).

  7. Today Modern Association

    Crystal guides add their own picks, often by color or by a stone's modern meaning, and one openly calls jet's Capricorn link a newer association.

Astronomers also count a thirteenth zodiac constellation, Ophiuchus, between Scorpius and Sagittarius. Astrology's twelve signs leave it out, and so do all these lists.

Why the Lists Disagree

The lists come from different systems. In old astrology, each planet had its stones, and the signs had ruling planets. An old print of the Roman day gods, reproduced by Kunz, pairs Saturn with Capricorn and Aquarius and gives Saturday the turquoise (1913, page 332), and Saturn got "all dark, black, and brittle stones" (page 348). Modern guides lean on color and on names, so aquamarine, named from the Latin for seawater, drifts toward Aquarius.

Guides also copy each other and then drift. They tie smoky quartz to Capricorn, Sagittarius or Scorpio, and one lumps Sagittarius and Capricorn together as "winter signs." No list is the correct one. Pick the tradition you like, or wear the month stone and keep the sign stone on a shelf.

Which Zodiac Stone Should I Choose?

Try This Choose by where the stone will live. A ring or bracelet takes knocks, so pick a hard stone for it. Many zodiac picks are soft. Angelite rates 3 to 3.5 on the Mohs scale, according to one dealer guide, and celestite is just as soft. Both suit a nightstand better than a hand.

For a gift, write both stones on the card, the month birthstone and the sign's 1913 stone. Before any stone goes on a sunny sill or near water, run it through the Care Checker. Malachite, a favorite for Scorpio and Capricorn, is a copper mineral, so it never goes in water you drink.

Safety. Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them.

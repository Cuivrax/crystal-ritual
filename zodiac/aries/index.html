Aries Stones

Aries Crystals & Birthstones, Plus Where the Links Come From

Aries has no single official birthstone, because birthstones belong to months and Aries runs from about March 21 to April 19. That gives aquamarine or bloodstone for a March birthday and diamond for an April one. As a zodiac stone, a 1913 list gives Aries the bloodstone, while today's crystal guides favor blue kyanite, fire agate and red jasper, all beliefs rather than mineral facts.

By Iris Holloway

What Is the Aries Birthstone?

The honest answer has two parts, the month stone and the sign stone, and for Aries they meet in one stone.

The month stone. On the modern list of the American Gem Society, March has two birthstones, aquamarine and bloodstone, and April has one, diamond. All three were on the 1912 jewelers' list, with aquamarine as March's alternate (Kunz 1913, page 319). Our March and April pages go stone by stone.

The sign stone. Tradition In the verse list of zodiac gems that George Frederick Kunz printed in 1913, Aries gets the bloodstone. "Who on this world of ours his eyes / In Aries opens shall be wise / If always on his hand there lies / A bloodstone" (The Curious Lore of Precious Stones, page 345). Two pages later, an old Spanish list that Kunz thought probably reflected Arab tradition gives Aries crystal instead, most likely rock crystal, the old name for clear quartz (page 347).

Kunz's calendar of the months makes the same match. March carries "jasper, bloodstone" as its natal stones and Aries as its sign, and its verse borrows the zodiac rhyme's first line, "Who on this world of ours her eyes / In March first opens may be wise" (page 327). For a March Aries, bloodstone covers the birthday, the sign and both rhymes.

What Color Is the Aries Birthstone?

There is no single Aries birthstone color. Bloodstone is dark green spattered with red, aquamarine is greenish blue to blue, and diamond is best known colorless, though most diamonds are actually brown or yellow. If you want one color for the sign, the modern lists lean red and orange, from red jasper and carnelian to the flashes of fire agate, with blue kyanite as the odd one out.

Modern Association

Aries Crystals in Today's Guides

"Aries is a fire sign ruled by Mars the planet of action, drive and competition," one guide puts it. Then the guides split. Some zodiac stones for Aries are said to feed that fire, like red jasper, which a French guide says strengthens the sign's courage and initiative. Others are said to cool it. One jewelry guide credits blue kyanite with a "calming effect on impulsive communication," and another says carnelian helps Aries be "less impulsive." No stone reaches Classic for Aries. Here are the usual picks.

Blue Kyanite

Widely Linked. Blue kyanite is an aluminum silicate mineral, and most gem kyanite is blue, colored by iron and titanium. Its old name, disthene, means two strengths, because its hardness changes with direction, from about 4.5 to 7 on the Mohs scale. A steel needle scratches a crystal along its length but not across it. Guides usually name it for Aries, Taurus and Libra together, and it rates even higher for Taurus.

Fire Agate

Widely Linked. Fire agate is a brown agate that flashes iridescent yellow, red, orange and green. Thin layers coated with iron oxide minerals make the flashes. It rates 6.5 to 7 on the Mohs scale and comes from Mexico and the southwestern US, where Arizona has the most reported localities. One French guide calls Aries the sign "par excellence" of fire agate.

Red Jasper

Widely Linked. Red jasper is an opaque red form of jasper, a microcrystalline quartz colored by iron oxide, and it rates about 6.5 to 7 on the Mohs scale. It is tough enough for a pocket, and our care data rates undyed jasper OK for water, sun and heat. According to one dealer guide, many stones labeled jasper are not jasper at all, so ask the seller what you are buying.

Bloodstone

Often Linked. Bloodstone is a dark green chalcedony spattered with red, and the red spots are iron oxide, most likely hematite. It rates 6.5 to 7, has no cleavage and suits any kind of wear. Tradition In Christian legend the red spots stood for the blood of Christ (Kunz 1913, page 267). Modern guides rank it below their red favorites, though it is the 1913 Aries stone and a March birthstone. One German guide still calls it the main Aries stone, said to grant "endurance and calm in conflict."

Carnelian & Pyrite

Often Linked. Carnelian is a translucent orange to red chalcedony colored by iron. Heating darkens the red, so assume any piece was heated. Its courage lore is old. Tradition The Lapidario of Alfonso X recommended carnelian to people who were "timid in speech," since the warm-colored stone would give them the courage to "speak both boldly and well" (Kunz 1913, page 63).

Pyrite is iron sulfide, the brass-yellow mineral nicknamed fool's gold, and it is brittle. One guide gives it to Leo above all and "to Aries for its Mars and Fire nature."

Diamond for April Aries

Rarely Linked. Diamond is pure carbon, 10 on the Mohs scale, formed deep in the mantle, not from coal. It is April's birthstone, yet few crystal guides tie it to Aries. Kunz wanted it off the month list, since the ancients did not seem to know it, and suggested it as a border around the month stone instead (1913, page 321).

Aquamarine, the other March stone, is greenish blue to blue beryl, and modern guides give it to Pisces far more often.

Tradition

Where the Aries Links Come From

The Story. In astrology, Aries begins with the Sun's position at the March equinox, which makes it the first sign of the cycle. Its stones carry some of the oldest sign lore in Kunz.

  1. Medieval Jewish tradition Tradition

    The tribe of Judah was paired with Aries (Kunz 1913, page 314).

  2. Engraved gems Tradition

    A sard in the Metropolitan Museum of Art shows Aries carrying a long caduceus, a design also struck on coins of Antioch, a city founded in the month of Aries (page 342).

  3. Old talisman lists Tradition

    A list of constellation figures for engraved gems, reprinted by Kunz, puts Pegasus under Aries and promises that it "makes the wearer swift, cautious, and bold" (page 353).

  4. Old planetary lists Tradition

    Mars took the diamond, the jacinth and the ruby, the last "according with the ruddy hue of our neighbor planet" (page 348). In an old print of the Roman day gods, though, Mars stands with Capricorn and the jasper for Tuesday (page 332).

  5. 1913 Tradition

    Kunz's verse list gives Aries the bloodstone (page 345). His calendar adds jasper to March, promising that whoever wears one "will meet all dangers brave and wise and strong" (page 327).

  6. Today Modern Association

    Blue kyanite, fire agate and red jasper lead the modern lists, most of them red and most of them explained by Mars.

One possible thread, and it is our reading rather than a documented link, is that the red picks echo an old belief Kunz describes, "the stimulating and animating effects produced by red stones" (page 63).

Try This

How to Use Aries Stones in Everyday Life

The Ritual. Aries season starts the astrological year. Whatever you believe about the stones, these give a pause you chose and something in your hand to mark it.

Daytime

A First Day of Spring List

Try This On the March equinox, write one thing you want to start before your next birthday and set a bloodstone or a red jasper on the page. Every Sunday evening, read the line and name the next small step.

A Pause Before You Hit Send

Try This Keep a blue kyanite next to your laptop. When a message makes you want to fire back, pick up the stone, take one slow breath and read your reply once more before you send it. The breath is the part you control. Kyanite scratches easily along its length, so it lives on the desk, not in a pocket full of keys.

Daytime

A Birthday Card with Two Verses

Try This For a March Aries, give a bloodstone and copy both 1913 verses on the card, since they share a first line. For an April Aries, tuck the Aries verse in with the diamond.

Safety. Pyrite never goes in water you drink, and neither does any other stone. Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them. These are personal practices, not treatments.

Capricorn Stones

Capricorn Crystals & Birthstones, Plus Where the Links Come From

Capricorn has no single official birthstone, because birthstones belong to months and Capricorn runs from about December 22 to January 19. That gives tanzanite, turquoise or zircon for a December birthday and garnet for a January one. As a zodiac stone, a 1913 list gives Capricorn the ruby, while today's crystal guides favor jet, tourmalinated quartz and smoky quartz, all beliefs rather than mineral facts.

What Is the Capricorn Birthstone?

The honest answer has two parts, the month stone and the sign stone.

The month stone. On the modern list of the American Gem Society, December has three birthstones, tanzanite, turquoise and zircon, and January has one, garnet. So a Capricorn born on December 28 and one born on January 8 have different birthstones. Turquoise was December's stone on the 1912 jewelers' list (Kunz 1913, page 319), zircon joined it in 1952 and tanzanite in 2002. Our December and January pages go stone by stone.

The sign stone. Tradition In the verse list of zodiac gems that George Frederick Kunz printed in 1913, Capricorn's stone is the ruby. "Those who live in Capricorn / No trouble shall their brows adorn / If they this glowing gem have worn, / The ruby" (The Curious Lore of Precious Stones, page 347). On the same page, an old Spanish list that Kunz thought probably reflected Arab tradition gives Capricorn chalcedony instead.

Ruby is July's birthstone today. Kunz himself complained that the 1912 list moved the ruby from December to July and turquoise the other way (1913, page 320). In his own calendar of the months, December still carries the ruby and the sign of Capricornus, with holly as its flower (page 331).

Modern Association

Capricorn Crystals in Today's Guides

In modern crystal guides, Capricorn's stones are mostly black, brown or very dark. Guides describe the sign as steady and hard-working, sometimes stubborn, and some pick stones to soften that. One shop guide calls smoky quartz Capricorn's main stone because it helps the sign relax, and another guide says malachite encourages Capricorns to step outside their comfort zone. Here are the usual picks, with what each stone really is.

Jet

Classic. Jet is a black organic gem, a variety of lignite coal that forms from fossilized wood, and the finest comes from Whitby, England. It became a mourning stone in Victorian Britain, in the style of Queen Victoria herself. Jet is brittle and fairly soft, so rings need protective settings, and it burns like coal. One modern guide calls the Capricorn link a newer association, though jet had a planet long before it had a sign. In the old planetary lists Saturn took "all dark, black, and brittle stones" (Kunz 1913, page 348), a description jet fits.

Tourmalinated Quartz

Classic. This is usually colorless rock crystal holding long, slender, usually black tourmaline needles. The host is quartz, which rates 7 on the Mohs scale and is chemically inert with most substances. It is two minerals in one stone, and both turn up in Capricorn lists.

Smoky Quartz

Widely Linked. Smoky quartz is brown to nearly black quartz. Its color forms when natural radiation from the surrounding rock activates color centers around aluminum in the crystal. It is the national gem of Scotland, where it is called cairngorm, and on May 31, 1985, it became the official state gem of New Hampshire. Guides also tie it to Sagittarius and Scorpio, and one groups Sagittarius and Capricorn together as the winter signs. Labs can give rock crystal the same smoky color by irradiation, so ask the seller whether a stone is natural.

Black Tourmaline

Widely Linked. The black tourmaline of the trade is schorl, the most common tourmaline species. It rates 7 to 7.5 on the Mohs scale, and tourmaline in general suits everyday jewelry. That makes it one of the hardest pocket stones on this list.

Onyx

Often Linked. Onyx is chalcedony with black and white layers. It occurs in nature in thin bands, and what you find in stores is almost always dyed. Dyed is not fake, it is a treatment, so ask the seller whether the color was enhanced. White onyx gets its own Widely Linked spot in Capricorn lists.

Malachite

Widely Linked. Malachite is a green copper mineral, soft at 3.5 to 4.5 on the Mohs scale. It is the odd one out by color, and one guide notes that its pairing with Scorpio and Capricorn is recent. Dust from grinding it is toxic to breathe, though finished jewelry and carvings should be safe.

Garnet for January Capricorns

Rarely Linked. Garnet is not a single mineral but a group of closely related minerals, with gems in almost every color, and it is January's birthstone and the gem for a second anniversary. Few crystal guides tie it to Capricorn as a sign. For anyone born between January 1 and January 19, though, it is the one official birthstone, and that is reason enough.

Some links stay rare and you can skip them, like ruby in modern guides. It survives mostly as the 1913 verse.

Tradition

Where the Capricorn Links Come From

The Story. Capricorn's oldest gem links run through one Roman emperor.

  1. Roman Empire Tradition

    The Gemma Augustea, a cameo by the gem engraver Dioskorides that commemorates a triumph of Tiberius in 13 CE, shows the sign of Capricornus above the figure of Augustus as "the constellation of his nativity," and the sign of Scorpio beneath Tiberius. In Kunz's day it was in the Austrian Imperial Collection in Vienna (1913, pages 342 to 343).

  2. Roman era Tradition

    Kunz illustrates a carnelian engraved with Taurus, Leo and Capricorn around a six-rayed star, referred to the nativity of Augustus (1913, page 322). He also describes a green jasper in the Metropolitan Museum of Art bearing Luna, Capricorn and Taurus, read as its owner's horoscope (page 342).

  3. Old print Tradition

    An old print of the Roman gods of the days of the week pairs Saturn with Capricorn and Aquarius and gives Saturday the turquoise. The same print pairs Mars with Capricorn and gives Tuesday the jasper (Kunz 1913, page 332).

  4. Medieval Jewish tradition Tradition

    The tribe of Dan was paired with Capricorn (Kunz 1913, pages 313 to 314).

  5. Old astrology Tradition

    Saturn, the planet of that print, had to be content with "all dark, black, and brittle stones," and a Saturnian stone was little desired because the planet's influence was thought baleful (Kunz 1913, page 348).

  6. 1913 Tradition

    Kunz's verse list names the ruby (page 347).

  7. Today Modern Association

    Jet, tourmalinated quartz, blue tiger's eye, smoky quartz and black tourmaline lead the modern lists.

One possible thread, and it is our reading rather than a documented link, is that the dark modern picks echo Saturn's old dark stones.

Try This

How to Use Capricorn Stones in Everyday Life

The Ritual. Capricorn season holds New Year's Day, which makes it a natural time for planning rituals. Whatever you believe about the stones, these give a pause you chose and something in your hand to mark it.

A New Year Planning Stone

Try This On January 1, set a smoky quartz or a piece of tourmalinated quartz on your planner. Write three goals for the year on one page and leave the stone on top. Every Monday morning, lift it, read the page and pick one task for the week. Then put it back.

Daytime

A Coat Pocket Stone for Winter Commutes

Try This Drop a tumbled black tourmaline into your coat pocket. When your hand finds it on a cold platform or in traffic, drop your shoulders and take one slow breath out. A hard stone suits a pocket full of keys.

Daytime

A Birthday Card with Two Stones

Try This For a December Capricorn, give the month stone and write the 1913 ruby verse inside the card. For a January Capricorn, give a garnet and add one line about the Gemma Augustea, where Capricorn floats above an emperor. The card does half the work.

Daytime

A Desk Shutdown at the End of the Day

Try This Keep a small onyx or jet piece by your keyboard. When you finish work, set it on top of the closed laptop. It marks the line between work and home. Jet is soft, so a desk suits it better than a pocket.

Safety. Small tumbled stones and bead bracelets are not toys. Keep them away from small children and pets who might swallow them. These are personal practices, not treatments.
